## Executive position — safe to say publicly

> Other Australian Repair Cafés use a range of arrangements, commonly a sponsoring or auspicing not-for-profit, community house, council, Men's Shed-linked group or stand-alone policy. Many combine visitor registration/house rules with public liability and voluntary workers accident policies, although coverage is different in each case. Repair Café Dubbo is investigating its own organising structure, venue permission, volunteer protections and insurance, including whether an AMSA-linked arrangement could be suitable. **No insurance or auspice for Repair Café Dubbo has been confirmed; waivers cannot be assumed to replace insurance or eliminate legal responsibility.** No public repair event should commence until written evidence of cover for the exact activities, people and venues is obtained.

**High-priority discovery (7 Nov):** The 2026/27 *AMSA/Everest Risk Group Insurance FAQ v12.2* says third-party sublessees are not insured by a Shed's policies and that the **standard AMSA policy does not insure testing and tagging equipment NOT owned by the Shed**. A Shed hosting a separate community Repair Café therefore does **not** automatically solve the organiser's insurance problem. An approved Shed-operated program might be differently assessed, but MUST be confirmed in writing with the current insured organisation, broker and policy schedules. [P1–P3]

**Do not say:** “The library is too risky; the Men's Shed's insurance will cover us”, “everyone signs a waiver so there is no liability”, “a test-and-tag volunteer makes all electrical repair legal and insured”, “we already have permission to host a Café at the Shed or The Destination”, “Council has approved the scheme”, or “the app's insurance and waiver forms mean we are insured”.

## 3. Insurance: what each term does and does not establish

| Term | Working meaning and risk | The actual Dubbo question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Public liability (PL)** | Protects the insured organiser against certain third-party injury/property damage claims caused by its insured activities, subject to wording. **Visitors themselves are not “insured persons”** under PL merely by walking in. [P1, P2, P4] | Is the correct organiser insured for volunteers and public interaction at the specified venue(s), including workshops/tools, furniture/bike/computer tasks, setup/pack-down? |
| **Products liability / completed operations** | Potentially relevant where work on a customer item later causes harm. **Do not infer cover from a PL dollar limit or the word “products” alone**. | After a customer takes a repaired item home, what kinds of loss/defective work are covered or excluded? Do “products” include repairs to customer-owned property? |
| **Voluntary workers personal accident** | Financial benefits for eligible volunteer's own injury (different from PL responding to claims by a third party). Not automatically workers compensation or full medical coverage. [P4] | Which volunteers, ages, travel, roles and equipment are included? Are volunteers of a separate Repair Café insured? |
| **Association/office bearers liability** | May help a committee/directors with governance-related claims, subject to conditions. | Whose committee is exposed, and who controls safety, funds, personal data and complaints? |
| **Care, custody & control / customer goods** | Public liability may exclude damage to items under the organiser's control even when third-party losses otherwise covered. | Does the policy pay for a customer's item ruined on the workbench, lost/stolen, or unrepairable after disassembly? |
| **Venue property and equipment** | Venue's premises policy and organiser's liability are different; hiring a venue does not automatically insure repair work. | What happens if a volunteer damages a Shed workbench, trips a visitor with a cable or starts a fire? Whose insurance responds? |
| **Professional/trade/electrical** | Higher-risk work may require qualifications, separate cover and explicit approval. Test/tag is **not** a universal electrical repair licence. | Is ANY mains appliance repair authorised? Are repairs to customer-owned electrical items and their testing separately covered? |
| **Cyber/privacy** | Technology advice may involve confidential devices and account credentials; PL may not cover data loss/cyber claims. | Will the Café open computers, access user accounts or copy data? What policy/consent/loss protections apply? |

### 3.1 Most important 2026/27 AMSA primary-source facts

The official [AMSA insurance overview](https://mensshed.org/for-mens-sheds/shed-insurance/) and insurer broker's [2026/27 FAQ, version 12.2](https://everestrg.com.au/amsa-faq/) (issued **9 February 2026**, policy **28 February 2026–28 February 2027**) give the following:

1. Package classes include **$40m public and products liability**, voluntary workers personal accident, association liability, and optional asset protections — **limits do not define activity eligibility**. [P1]
2. Current published access price **$28.50/member**, with FAQ minimum **15 members for premium calculation**; if eligible, minimum illustrated base amount **15 × $28.50 = $427.50**, **not a quote or promise of cover**. [P1, P3] Membership counting and organisation eligibility must be answered by AMSA/Everest.
3. The accepted business description includes certain non-certifiable workshop projects, community work and machinery/equipment repairs. **It is NOT a blanket authorisation for any customer item, electrical repair or a separately organised Repair Café.** [P3]
4. [AMSA's public explanation of *outside groups*](https://mensshed.org/the-shed-online/shed-insurance-a-deep-dive-into-public-liability-insurance-insuring-the-shed-visitors-and-outside-groups/) says outside groups should carry **their own insurance**. Broker FAQ Q19 specifically says sublessees/third parties do not receive cover under Shed policy. A joint Shed-owned activity would need **written insurance endorsement/confirmation**, not an assumed exception. [P2, P3]
5. Broker FAQ Q39 specifically **excludes testing and tagging of property not owned by the Shed** under the standard programme. This is critical for a public Café repairing visitor-owned electrical items. Other separately written policies, licensed tradespeople and conditional approval may be possible, but **not established**. [P3]
6. FAQ distinguishes a liability claim from visitors and direct visitor personal accident coverage; **public liability protects the insured against qualifying third-party claims**, not each visitor's own personal policy. [P2, P3]
7. FAQ's accepted list includes instruction/mentoring for students **aged 12 and older**; do not infer cover for under-12 or general family events without checking current FAQ children, supervision and venue requirements. [P3]
8. Ask **Everest Risk Group**, the appointed broker, for precise written answers and relevant PDS/schedule/endorsements. **AMSA being a group purchasing body is not proof Repair Café Dubbo can join or that South Dubbo will be the policyholder.**

## 4. Waivers and NSW legal responsibilities — important distinctions

- **Visitor registration and signed house rules are useful evidence** that participants understood the collaborative/free nature of the event, that volunteers may refuse unsafe work, that parts are owner-funded and no result is guaranteed. They do **not** by themselves transfer all responsibility for defective work or remove an organiser's duty to act safely.
- **Australian Consumer Law** and mandatory protections cannot simply be overridden by broad “no liability” wording. Whether particular consumer guarantees apply to a genuine free volunteer event is fact-sensitive; avoid saying “no charge means no commercial or consumer obligations”. [P5]
- NSW **Civil Liability Act 2002, Part 9**, contains a qualified personal liability protection for **good-faith volunteers** of certain community organisations doing authorised work. It has exceptions, including conduct outside authorised activities or contrary to instructions; it is **not immunity for an unstructured group**, not accident insurance, and not a blanket protection for the organising entity. Check eligibility and facts with NSW legal advisers. [P6]
- A **signed waiver does not bind unrelated third parties** (for example a person injured after a repaired item causes a fire) merely because the owner signed it.
- Safety records, competent repair boundaries, photos with separate consent, visitor item tracking, incident response and owners retaining custody are necessary controls independent of insurance.
- Require a solicitor/Not-for-profit Law review of a NSW-specific **visitor acknowledgement and house rules**, including accessible wording, parents/guardians, privacy and non-excludable rights, before adopting interstate templates.
- In general, **public liability and volunteer accident are complementary**. Not-for-profit Law's October 2026 guidance says organisations should choose based on activities and actual people, and volunteer injury may require separate accident insurance. [P4]

### Suggested interim non-legal house-rules principles (NOT a finished waiver)

1. This is collaborative, volunteer-led learning and diagnosis, not a guaranteed repair.
2. Visitor owns and normally stays with item, follows volunteer instructions, discloses known hazards and confirms authority to present it.
3. Volunteers may reject dangerous, dirty, recalled, unsafe, out-of-scope or data-sensitive work.
4. Work begins only if appropriate volunteers, tools and precautions are available; no unsupervised visitors in tool areas.
5. No mains/appliance/gas/refrigeration/safety-critical/battery-pack work without explicit approved scope. No fire-risk or recalled battery items.
6. Owner buys any necessary replacement parts and removes all unrepairable items on the day.
7. Clearly explain the repair result and limitations and give advice for a professional repairer when appropriate.
8. Separate **consent to service**, **privacy notice**, **waiver/acknowledgement**, **photo consent** and **incident/repair notes**. Do not imply a waiver is absolute.

## 5. Electrical, batteries, computer and other safety boundaries

- **NSW SafeWork** distinguishes appropriate **competence for inspection/testing** from broader electrical work. Test-and-tag checks specific electrical safety properties and must be performed by competent persons in required settings; being able to test/tag does **not automatically authorise rewiring, mains appliance diagnosis or repairs**, nor make them insured. [P7–P8]
- Before adding customer-owned mains appliances, obtain **NSW regulator/qualified electrical professional confirmation** on exact task legality and required competency; and obtain broker confirmation on the customer's item and pre/post-repair testing, not merely whether “electrical” appears in a general policy.
- Distinguish **low voltage** from **mains-fed device internals**: a computer with an external PSU may still contain lithium cells and personal data; opening a laptop is not automatically risk-free. **Do not open laptop batteries, repair swollen packs, puncture cells, solder dangerous items or power on equipment suspected of unsafe charging.**
- Proposed **Phase 0 accepted only after risk/insurance sign-off**: hand sewing, simple detached wooden fixtures, non-powered small household objects, basic mechanical bike adjustment (exclude critical braking/structural work pending competence), digital education, external inspection and limited owner-assisted computer troubleshooting.
- **Phase 0 default exclusions**: electrical appliances/mains internals, portable appliance test/tag on visitor-owned property, microwave ovens, gas, refrigeration, electrical installations, power-tool safety-critical refurbishment, damaged/swollen lithium battery work, medical devices, vehicle-critical components, major children's product safety repairs, unsupervised soldering/hot work and any work requiring statutory certification. This is a cautious **organiser proposal**, not a statement that every activity is illegal.
- For technology: get explicit owner consent, recommend backup, avoid taking passwords or copying private data, use an intake device-owner verification and stop-work rules. Don't accept unwiped personal-data devices for donation or commercial refurbishment through Café.
- Require first aid, extinguisher/fire readiness suitable to activities, trip/extension cord control, safety station separation, qualified leaders where needed, incident log and agreed emergency exits.

## 6. Legal structures / who is accountable

Three viable structures are NOT equivalent:

**A. Separate Repair Café with its own insurance; hire venue(s).** An independent legal/unincorporated organiser and policy must be identified. Allows flexibility at The Destination and Shed, but can involve premiums and admin.

**B. Repair Café formally adopted/auspiced as the programme of an existing incorporated community group.** Potentially the lowest administrative overhead, but only with a written agreement, explicit insurer disclosure, volunteer roles, data controller, money/bank handling, supervision and termination/asset plan. Being “auspiced” for grants **does not prove automatic insurance cover**.

**C. Independent NSW incorporated association.** May offer durable governance and ability to contract, bank, apply for grants and procure insurance; requires members, officers, rules and recurrent compliance. Earlier repo research identified the five-member NSW minimum and committee requirements; **verify current Fair Trading conditions/fees before applying**. [P9]

**Recommended course:** Seek an eligible auspice and compare documented multi-venue cover with a standalone quote. Do **not** incorporate purely to obtain a low anecdotal AMSA price without determining eligibility, coverage, minimum people and annual governance cost. The Men’s Shed could instead simply be a source of individual volunteers.

**Commercial boundary, from existing repo:** Run the Café and private e-waste/ITAD/repair-resale work as separate programmes. The visitor retains item ownership, no automatic recycling/resale transfer, no use of volunteer contact data as commercial leads, separate funding/stock/insurance, neutral referrals to local businesses, record any conflict of interest. Tools donated for community use must not automatically become private resale tools. [R4]

## 9. Suggested reusable insurer questionnaire (send as written, keep answers)

Describe: *Monthly three-hour, free, donation-supported skill-sharing event in Dubbo NSW with owner present; volunteers, not paid tradespeople; perhaps 10–25 visitors at a small pilot; sewing/hand tools/wood small objects/low-risk bikes/basic computer troubleshooting; no high-risk electrical or damaged batteries initially; at South Dubbo Men's Shed and/or The Destination, Wongarbon; visitor form and approved risk controls.*

Request a **question-by-question** reply:

1. What is the insured **legal entity** and is an unincorporated organising team eligible? Could an incorporated parent or AMSA member association act as named insured?
2. Would an independently operated Café at a Shed be an excluded **outside group**? What if the Shed committee formally operates the event?
3. Do guest members of the public, women, teens, visitors, external volunteers and paid venue staff affect cover? Who is covered for **their own injury**?
4. Does PL respond to visitors' **injury/property-damage claims** caused by negligent station work or temporary flooring/electrical cords?
5. Are **customer-owned items under repair** covered for accidental damage, fire, theft or loss? Which relevant exclusions/deductibles?
6. Does **product/completed operations** respond to a repaired item causing damage **after it leaves**, including negligence allegations? Does it apply to repair/services or only products sold?
7. Does cover include **computer repairs, laptop batteries, low-voltage electronics, hand sewing, furniture, bikes, sharp tools**, and only regulated categories if licensed?
8. What restrictions exist on test/tag for **customer-owned** equipment (AMSA FAQ Q39), and on any repair of mains-fed appliances?
9. Can a single policy cover an occasional programme at **both venues**? Are addresses pre-nominated or can off-site pop-ups be approved?
10. Does volunteer personal accident cover include setup/pack-down, transport, pre-existing conditions, age limits, injuries to helpers not on member list, and non-Medicare gap (if any)?
11. Must volunteers have special training, police checks, WWCC, risk induction, incident logs, or first aid?
12. Is hired/loaned equipment and venue property included? What contractual indemnities or sublease exceptions apply?
13. What amount is actually payable in year one including premium, broker fee, minimum insured member count, excesses, add-ons and renewals?
14. Please provide **written confirmation**, schedule, coverage wording, certificate, endorsements and **every exclusion** relevant to the disclosed task list; advise what changes trigger re-underwriting.

**Policy comparison rubric:** Don't choose the lowest dollar price before comparing coverage for customer items, completed repairs, volunteer injuries, venue portability, excluded work, insurance excess, and public event attendance.
