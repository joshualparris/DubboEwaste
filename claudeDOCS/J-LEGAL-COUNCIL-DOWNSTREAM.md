## 58. What does the 2026 NSW second-hand-dealer regime say about recycling and the 14-day hold?

**Status: ✅ Substantially answered from current NSW Government guidance implementing the Act/2026 Regulation.**

Current NSW Government guidance, updated **1 Sep 2026**, says:

### Recycling exemption
A second-hand dealer licence is **not required** to:

> engage in recycling and rubbish collection programs.

That wording matters, but it does not automatically mean every business that acquires reusable devices and resells them can call itself a “recycling program”. If DubboEwaste purchases prescribed electronic goods for resale, the ordinary second-hand-dealer rules may still apply depending on the actual business model.

Source:
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers/pawnbroker-and-second-hand-dealer-licences

### 14-day retention
For regulated goods, current NSW guidance says second-hand dealers generally must:
- retain goods for **14 days** after receiving them;
- keep them on notified premises during that period;
- **not alter the goods until the retention period has ended**.

Exceptions include certain goods such as consignment goods and goods purchased by the licensee at auction.

Source:
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers

**DubboEwaste implication:** if the operation falls within licensed second-hand dealing, “triage immediately” must not silently become dismantling, upgrading, wiping in a way that legally constitutes alteration, or parts harvesting during the retention period. Get Fair Trading's written interpretation for the intended workflow.

NSW Government says an applicant may seek exemption from provisions of the Pawnbrokers and Second-hand Dealers Act.

Process:
1. download and digitally complete the exemption application;
2. clearly state **which provision(s)** you seek exemption from and why;
3. email it to **licensing.exemptions@customerservice.nsw.gov.au**.

Source:
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers

For example, **Alegre Pty Ltd** has exemptions tied to conditions such as serial/IMEI-level inventory tracking and circumstances around the statutory retention period. This is useful evidence that technology-device businesses can receive tailored exemptions rather than only all-or-nothing treatment.

## 60. Home business vs home industry under Dubbo/NSW planning rules

**Status: ✅🟡 Rules identified; application to a specific premises still depends on zoning/site constraints.**

The NSW standard definition of **home business** permits a business carried on in a dwelling or ancillary building by permanent residents, provided it does not:
- employ more than 2 non-residents;
- adversely affect neighbourhood amenity through noise, fumes, waste, traffic, etc.;
- expose unsightly matter to neighbours/public;
- display more than permitted business signage;
- conduct ordinary on-site retail of goods not produced there, except online retail.

**Home industry** uses similar limits but is an **industrial activity** carried on at the dwelling/ancillary building; it is a type of light industry.

Primary legislation/definition source:
- https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/

NSW Planning says home businesses/home industries can be exempt development if all State Policy and local standards are met. Dubbo Regional Council likewise says exempt development needs no DA only where every applicable standard/site limitation is satisfied.

Sources:
- https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/the-planning-system/exempt-and-complying-development-policy
- https://www.smallbusiness.nsw.gov.au/help/common-questions/getting-approval-for-a-home-business
- https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/Builders-Developers/Overview-of-Application-Process/do-i-need-a-da
- https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/Builders-Developers/Overview-of-Application-Process/types-of-das

**For e-waste/refurbishing:** the biggest planning risks are likely to be storage volume/appearance, customer and collection traffic, noise, hazardous batteries/materials, waste generation and whether the activity is properly characterised as home business or home industry.

**Not solved generically online:** whether a particular Dubbo address can legally run the proposed operation. That needs the exact zoning/property constraints and, prudently, written Council planning advice.

Sources:
- https://www.chubb.com/au-en/business/technology-liability-insurance.html
- https://ftainsurance.com.au/products/information-technology

No responsible current premium figure can be quoted from public pages because pricing depends on turnover, activity description, stock/customer-goods value, premises, claims history, limits/excesses and whether data destruction/recycling activities are included.

**Actionable quote description:** “small electronics/computer refurbishment, repair and resale; handling customer/client devices and data-bearing assets; lithium-ion batteries; occasional collection/delivery; used-product sales.” Do not obtain a cheap generic “IT consultant” policy without disclosing the physical repair/resale/e-waste work.
