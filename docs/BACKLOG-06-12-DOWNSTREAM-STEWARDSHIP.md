# 10. NTCRS opportunities

## 10.1 Current approved co-regulators

**VERIFIED, DCCEEW updated 20 May 2026:**

Approved National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme co-regulatory arrangements are:

1. Activ Group Solutions Pty Ltd — Ecoactiv
2. Australia and New Zealand Recycling Platform Ltd — TechCollect
3. Ecocycle Pty Ltd
4. Ecycle Solutions Pty Ltd
5. Sustainable Product Stewards Pty Ltd

Source:  
https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/protection/waste/product-stewardship/products-schemes/television-computer-recycling-scheme/coreg-arrangements

## 10.2 Who gets free services

**VERIFIED:** households and **small businesses** have access to free NTCRS collection services for covered products at designated collection points/services.

Source:  
https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/protection/waste/product-stewardship/television-computer-recycling-scheme

The government page does not set a simple universal “X devices per small business” quantity cap; service-specific acceptance can vary.

## 10.3 Covered products

**VERIFIED covered categories include:**

### Televisions
- colour TVs
- black-and-white TVs

### Computers
- laptops
- notebooks
- palmtops
- tablets
- personal computers
- PC CPUs
- monitors/projectors
- CRT and flat-screen monitors

### Printers
- multi-function
- single-function

### Computer parts/peripherals
- keyboards
- joysticks/game pads
- mice/trackballs
- scanners
- CD/DVD drives
- hard drives
- memory/network/sound/video/IDE/SCSI and similar cards.

Source:  
https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/protection/waste/consumers/recycling-drop-off

This is broader than the Phase 0 acceptance list and creates a potential free residual route for eligible products.

## 10.4 CRTs

**VERIFIED:** CRT monitors are expressly within the Commonwealth covered-product list. Televisions are also covered.

This does not mean every local collection point will accept a CRT without conditions; check the actual service.

## 10.5 AS 5377

**VERIFIED:** NTCRS co-regulatory arrangements are expected to contract recycling only to facilities currently certified to **AS 5377**.

The government's material-recovery target is **90% by weight** sent for further processing into usable materials.

## 10.6 Becoming a collection partner

TechCollect publicly invites:
- local councils
- waste management organisations
- selected retail/business partners.

It says partnership can be **free** and include:
- signage/marketing
- staff training
- collection units
- transport
- recycling.

Source:  
https://techcollect.com.au/our-partners/partner-with-us/

### Dubbo eWaste opportunity

This is worth approaching **after** the planning status of the shed operation is resolved.

Possible model:
- Dubbo eWaste removes viable reuse devices first where permitted
- eligible residual NTCRS products go into a co-reg-funded collection stream
- no direct downstream disposal fee.

But confirm that the co-regulator permits reuse triage and how ownership/data responsibilities work.

## 10.7 TechCollect current NSW list

TechCollect's current public NSW collection-partner list includes multiple Council/waste sites but **does not list Dubbo, Matthews or AMR**.

That weakens any claim that the AMR yard is currently a public TechCollect collection partner.

Source:  
https://techcollect.com.au/our-partners/

## 10.8 Certification distinction

Do not conflate:
- **collection site/partner**
with
- **contracted recycler**.

AS 5377 certification obligations in Commonwealth guidance apply to the recycling facilities/service providers used for NTCRS recycling. A small local collection partner may participate through a co-regulator's logistics/recycler network rather than becoming an industrial recycler itself.

# 11. MobileMuster

## 11.1 What it is

**VERIFIED:** MobileMuster is the official Australian mobile-industry product stewardship scheme for mobile phones.

## 11.2 Free pickup

Registered sites can book a **free pickup**. Non-registered organisations can also contact MobileMuster for a one-off pickup, or join for ongoing collections.

Source:  
https://www.mobilemuster.com.au/book-a-pickup/

## 11.3 Standard accepted items

The standard program accepts:
- mobile phones
- chargers
- mobile accessories.

Selected carrier stores may also take some specific smart devices.

## 11.4 What is not accepted in the ordinary program

MobileMuster explicitly says it does **not** accept:
- loose mobile-phone batteries
- iPads/tablets
- other electronics.

This means Dubbo eWaste needs a separate tablet/downstream route.

## 11.5 Partner model

MobileMuster says it works with:
- retailers
- service providers
- repair stores
- councils
- charities
- workplaces.

It can supply collection units and offers free pickup/reporting support.

This makes it a realistic future Phase 0/1 partner for dead phones once the premises model is approved.

## 11.6 Public versus organisation-only site

MobileMuster's join process can support:
- a public drop-off point
- organisation-only collection.

For a residential startup, **organisation-only/non-public collection** is the safer concept until Council/insurance approve public visitation.

## 11.7 Data

MobileMuster says data is destroyed as part of recycling. For Dubbo eWaste, retain the stronger internal rule:
- data wipe before downstream handoff where practicable
- do not rely on generic scheme marketing for client-level destruction certification.

# 12. B-cycle / damaged lithium batteries

## 12.1 B-cycle is not a simple “buy a bin” solution

**VERIFIED:** to become a B-cycle drop-off point the organisation must:

1. find an accredited B-cycle Collector
2. choose an appropriate container with the Collector
3. complete a Site Risk Assessment and Safety Plan
4. meet the scheme commitments/code
5. have authority to register the site.

B-cycle does **not** simply provide a free public battery bin.

Sources:
- https://bcycle.com.au/register/
- https://bcycle.com.au/register/form/

## 12.2 Who pays/gets rebates

B-cycle says drop-off points are **not eligible for collection rebates**. Rebates are aimed at accredited Collectors transporting batteries in bulk.

A drop-off point works commercially with its accredited Collector.

## 12.3 Badly damaged or swollen batteries

**VERIFIED:** badly damaged, swollen or bulging batteries must **not** be put into an ordinary B-cycle drop-off point.

B-cycle says:
- use a fire-resistant, non-airtight container
- contact Council/transfer station
- if they cannot assist, contact an accredited B-cycle Collector for advice.

Source:  
https://bcycle.com.au/

## 12.4 Ordinary battery transport/preparation

For ordinary undamaged loose batteries, B-cycle advises:
- tape terminals
- prevent terminals touching
- transport in a suitable container.

## 12.5 Nearest Dubbo accredited commercial collector

**UNRESOLVED from indexed web search.**

The B-cycle network uses an interactive location/collector finder rather than exposing a simple indexed Dubbo list in search results.

Use B-cycle's “Find a Collector” service and ask specifically for:
- commercial pickup in Dubbo
- damaged/swollen lithium batteries
- minimum pickup
- packaging/UN-approved container rules
- dangerous-goods transport
- charges.

## 12.6 Phase 0 recommendation

Do **not** add public battery collection.

Accepting ordinary phones/laptops with healthy embedded batteries is a different question from advertising a loose-battery service.

At startup:
- reject visibly swollen/damaged/wet/fire-affected battery devices
- no loose battery intake
- have one identified specialist route for a battery discovered damaged after intake.

# Priority conclusions from items 6–12

1. **AMR is now clearly the current operator of the former Matthews Dubbo yard**, but the precise transaction with InfraBuild is not publicly documented.
2. Council's last published Matthews e-waste contract expired **30 June 2025**; no public successor award has yet been identified.
3. AMR publicly accepts broad e-waste for free, but **lithium wording is the critical ambiguity** for modern devices.
4. AMR's exact downstream processor remains unverified; do not invent a Sircel/TechCollect chain.
5. The **NTCRS is the strongest potential zero-cost residual stream** for TVs/computers/printers/peripherals.
6. TechCollect explicitly offers free collection-partner infrastructure to selected partners, making it worth a direct approach.
7. MobileMuster is a strong free pathway for **phones/chargers/accessories**, but not tablets or loose batteries.
8. B-cycle adds risk/administration that Phase 0 does not need. **No public battery collection at launch.**
