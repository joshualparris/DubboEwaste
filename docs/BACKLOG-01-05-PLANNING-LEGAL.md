## 1.4 Exempt home-business pathway

**VERIFIED:** NSW Planning says a home-based business may be **exempt development** if all applicable Codes SEPP standards and land requirements are met. It can occur in part of a dwelling or an ancillary structure such as a garage/studio.

This can remove the need for a DA where all exempt-development requirements are satisfied.

Key constraints in the statutory home-business definition include:
- operated by permanent residents
- no more than 2 non-resident employees
- no interference with neighbourhood amenity through noise, vibration, smell, fumes, smoke, vapour, steam, soot, ash, dust, wastewater, waste products, grit, oil or otherwise
- no excessive traffic generation
- no unsightly matter visible from neighbouring premises/public places
- only a permitted business-identification sign
- no ordinary retail sale/display of goods not produced at the dwelling, **except online retailing**

Dubbo LEP also caps home-business floor area at **30 m²**.

NSW Planning source:  
https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/development-and-assessment/planning-approval-pathways/exempt-development/home-based-enterprises

### Consequence for the Phase 0 model

The most defensible cheap-start design remains:

- under 30 m²
- one resident operator
- no staff initially
- pre-approved intake
- no unattended public bin
- no outdoor stockpiles
- no walk-in retail showroom
- online sales
- low vehicle movements
- rejected items leave with the person bringing them
- small stock cap

This does **not** guarantee Council will classify the activity as an exempt home business. It simply removes several facts that would push the use toward a separate commercial/waste classification.

# 2. Planning-law classification of Dubbo eWaste

## 2.1 Home business

**VERIFIED:** the Standard Instrument defines home business by reference to:
- business carried on in a dwelling/building ancillary to a dwelling
- resident operation
- max 2 non-resident workers
- low neighbourhood impact
- limited signage
- limits on retail activity at the dwelling

The definition focuses on **how the activity actually operates**, not its brand name.

## 2.2 Home industry

**VERIFIED:** a home industry is a resident-operated industrial activity carried on in a dwelling or ancillary building and is also constrained by neighbourhood amenity, employment and scale.

If the business moves from testing/repair into regular dismantling, processing or fabrication, this category may become more relevant, but it is not automatically the correct classification.

## 2.3 Goods repair and reuse premises

**VERIFIED:** the Standard Instrument defines a **goods repair and reuse premises** as premises whose **principal purpose** is to:
- collect goods, including appliances, and
- repair or refurbish them
- for sale, hire or swap.

This definition is strikingly close to a mature “collect electronics → repair/refurbish → sell” business.

The phrase **principal purpose** matters. Incidental repair/reuse inside a genuine low-impact home business is not necessarily the same thing as premises principally used as a goods repair and reuse premises.

## 2.4 Resource recovery facility

**VERIFIED:** a resource recovery facility includes premises used for:
- recovery of resources from waste
- sorting and separation
- processing/treating waste
- temporary storage
- transfer or sale of recovered resources

Again, this could describe a mature e-waste processing operation far more readily than a small repair/resale pilot.

## 2.5 Why “free donations” do not solve waste classification

**VERIFIED:** NSW's POEO definition of waste is deliberately broad. It includes:
- discarded
- rejected
- unwanted
- surplus
- abandoned substances
- including material intended for sale, recycling, processing, recovery or purification.

The legislation expressly says a substance is **not precluded from being waste merely because it can be processed, recycled, reused or recovered**.

Therefore:
- charging $0
- calling something a donation
- intending to refurbish it
- intending to sell it later

does **not by itself** prove it is not legally waste.

This is why selective pre-approval and the actual purpose of intake matter.

Official legislation source:  
https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/pdf/asmade/act-2005-96

## 2.6 What appears to determine classification

Public legislation strongly points to these facts being relevant:

- **principal/dominant purpose** of the premises
- what material is accepted
- whether incoming material is selected goods or indiscriminate waste
- volume stored
- whether processing/dismantling is routine
- whether the site is advertised as a public drop-off
- customer traffic
- visible stock/waste
- whether material routinely leaves as whole reused goods or as processed waste fractions

No one factor (for example “open Tuesdays only”) creates a different planning category on its own.

## 2.7 Tue/Sat appointment intake

**No statutory rule found saying two intake days automatically changes classification.**

It is relevant primarily because it affects:
- traffic
- amenity
- intensity of use
- public-drop-off character.

Appointments and pre-screening make the operation more home-business-like but do not guarantee that classification.

## 2.8 Case law / comparable Council decisions

**UNRESOLVED:** web research did not locate a NSW Land and Environment Court judgment squarely deciding the classification of a small home electronics-refurbishment business receiving donated unwanted devices.

That absence is useful: do not manufacture a precedent. Seek written Council planning advice on the exact facts.

# 3. EPA licensing and NSW waste law

## 3.1 Published EPL thresholds

**VERIFIED:** Dubbo is outside the Sydney/Newcastle/Wollongong regulated area. NSW EPA's current reduced-licensing-threshold page gives the following outside-regulated-area thresholds for relevant scheduled waste activities:

### Resource recovery
Licence threshold includes:
- **2,500 tonnes or 2,500 m³** stored on site at any time; or
- **more than 12,000 tonnes/year** processed.

### Non-thermal waste processing
The same published outside-area scale thresholds apply:
- **2,500 tonnes or 2,500 m³** on site; or
- **more than 12,000 tonnes/year** processed.

### Waste storage
Published thresholds are likewise far above a Phase 0 shed operation.

Source:  
https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/Your-environment/Waste/waste-overview/licensing/reduced-licensing-thresholds

### Meaning for Phase 0

At a 20–30-device pilot, an EPA environment protection licence is **not the realistic first blocker**.

But EPA licensing thresholds do **not** grant planning permission.

## 3.2 Being under the EPL threshold does not make a waste facility lawful

**VERIFIED principle:** a site can be below EPA licensing thresholds and still require development consent or otherwise be unlawful as a waste facility.

Therefore “we are nowhere near 2,500 tonnes” is **not** a planning answer.

## 3.3 Waste definition

See section 2.5 above. Unwanted equipment can remain “waste” despite reuse/recycling plans.

## 3.4 When recovered equipment stops being waste

**UNRESOLVED for a universal rule.**

No simple public rule says, for example, “after testing a laptop it ceases to be waste”.

Whether a particular object remains waste is fact-specific. A clearly refurbished, tested product placed back into ordinary commerce is a much stronger non-waste position than an untested discarded device in a mixed e-waste pile, but do not turn that practical distinction into a claimed statutory bright line.

## 3.5 Section 143 — lawful destination

**VERIFIED:** POEO Act s143 requires waste to be transported to a place that can **lawfully receive it**. EPA says both the waste owner and transporter can be responsible.

This is why Dubbo eWaste must verify downstream acceptance before carrying residual waste to AMR, a Council facility or another recycler.

EPA source:  
https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/Your-environment/Waste/industrial-waste/construction-demolition

## 3.6 Resource Recovery Orders / Exemptions

**VERIFIED:** EPA publishes general RRO/RREs for specified recovered materials. The current list does **not** show a generic e-waste/electronics order or exemption.

RRO/RREs are generally aimed at lawful reuse of particular waste-derived materials; they are not a generic licence to run an e-waste refurb business.

Source:  
https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/Your-environment/Recycling-and-reuse/resource-recovery-framework/current-orders-and-exemptions

## 3.7 Waste tracking

**VERIFIED:** NSW mandatory tracking applies to specified hazardous/trackable wastes under the Waste Regulation. The consignor must determine whether the waste appears in the relevant schedule/table and, where required, obtain a consignment authorisation.

Ordinary intact laptops/PCs are **not automatically described on the EPA page as trackable hazardous waste simply because they are e-waste**.

Source:  
https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/Your-environment/Waste/tracking-transporting-hazardous-waste/waste-must-tracked

## 3.8 Lithium batteries

**UNRESOLVED at the item-specific legal-classification level.**

Do not state “all lithium batteries are hazardous trackable waste”. Classification depends on the waste's characteristics and the applicable regulation/code.

For Phase 0 this uncertainty is avoidable:
- no public loose-battery service
- reject swollen/damaged/wet/fire-affected battery devices
- use an accredited/specialist downstream path if damaged batteries arise.

## 3.9 Transporter licence

No evidence found that transporting a handful of ordinary non-trackable e-waste devices creates a standalone “waste transporter licence” requirement.

However:
- loads must be safely contained
- trackable hazardous waste has separate requirements
- s143 lawful-destination responsibility still applies.

# 4. NSW second-hand dealer law

## 4.1 Current source

**VERIFIED:** NSW Fair Trading's guidance was updated **1 September 2026** and refers to the current **Pawnbrokers and Second-hand Dealers Regulation 2026**.

Source:  
https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers/pawnbroker-and-second-hand-dealer-licences

## 4.2 Electronic goods

**VERIFIED:** a second-hand dealer licence is generally required to buy, sell or exchange prescribed second-hand goods, including electronic goods.

## 4.3 Recycling/rubbish-program carve-out

**VERIFIED wording from current Fair Trading guidance:** a licence is not required to:

> engage in recycling and rubbish collection programs.

### What the web does NOT settle

Public guidance does not clearly answer:
- what makes an activity a “recycling program”
- whether collecting goods free and then refurbishing/reselling viable units remains within that carve-out
- whether parts harvested from such units remain covered
- whether a resale-heavy business stops being a recycling program.

**This remains the single most important Fair Trading written-confirmation question.**

## 4.4 Free acquisition

**UNRESOLVED:** no public authority located says that receiving an item free necessarily avoids the Act. The regime regulates dealing in prescribed goods; do not rely on “I didn't buy it” as the whole compliance theory.

## 4.5 If a licence applies — operating obligations

Current Fair Trading guidance confirms:

- notify Fair Trading of **all NSW premises** used to conduct the business or store goods
- display required signage
- verify supplier identity
- obtain a signed ownership statement
- maintain electronic transaction records
- prescribed acquisition records are transmitted to NSW Police within **3 days**
- transaction records retained for **3 years**
- regulated goods generally held for **14 days**
- during the holding period the goods **must not be altered**

Source:  
https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/pawnbrokers-and-second-hand-dealers

## 4.6 What does “altered” mean for wiping/repair?

**UNRESOLVED:** current public Fair Trading guidance says goods cannot be altered during the 14-day hold but does not answer whether:
- secure wiping
- opening a laptop
- swapping RAM
- changing SSD
- repair
- parts harvesting

counts as prohibited alteration in each case.

If licensed, obtain a written answer before building a 14-day workflow.

## 4.7 Supplier records

Current guidance requires supplier identification and an ownership statement describing:
- licensee
- supplier
- goods in sufficient detail.

It also requires electronic transaction records. The exact prescribed record schema should be implemented only after the licensing question is settled.

## 4.8 IMEI / MAC

Current Fair Trading material includes device identifiers such as IMEI/MAC in prescribed record contexts. Do not generalise the pawnbroker pledge examples to every Dubbo eWaste transaction without checking the current 2026 Regulation.

Practical Phase 0 policy remains sensible regardless:
- record serial
- IMEI for phones/tablets
- MAC where useful
- source/ownership authority.

## 4.9 Game consoles

Fair Trading's public exemptions mention **video games**, but the research did not establish that this phrase automatically exempts **console hardware**. Treat console hardware as unresolved rather than assuming an exemption.

## 4.10 Existing IT-business licence search

Public web searches established the corporate identity of the relevant local IT business searched, but did **not** locate indexed evidence of a NSW second-hand dealer licence for it.

Likewise, no indexed evidence was found proving a relevant licence for the other local computer business searched.

**No result is not proof of no licence.** Use NSW Fair Trading/licence verification or ask the entity directly.

# 5. NSW scrap-metal registration

## 5.1 Exact definition

**VERIFIED:** the Scrap Metal Industry Act defines **scrap metal** as an object of commercial value that:

1. is made from or contains metal; and
2. is **sold or obtained as scrap for recycling or reprocessing of the metal**.

This is narrower than “anything electronic contains metal”.

Official legislation:  
https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/pdf/inforce/current/act-2016-042

## 5.2 “Dealing in”

**VERIFIED:** dealing in scrap metal includes:
- buying
- receiving
- obtaining.

## 5.3 Residential premises

**VERIFIED:** carrying on a scrap-metal business can occur from **any location**, explicitly including residential premises.

Being home-based does not create an exemption.

## 5.4 Six-day presumption

**VERIFIED:** the 2024 Regulation sets the prescribed number for the rebuttable business presumption at **more than 6 days in a 12-month period**.

A person dealing in scrap metal on more than that number of days is presumed to be carrying on a scrap-metal business unless rebutted.

## 5.5 Registration fee

**VERIFIED current NSW Police guidance:** **$260 for three years**.

Source:  
https://www.police.nsw.gov.au/online_services/scrap_metal_industry

## 5.6 How this maps to Dubbo eWaste

### Whole devices accepted for reuse/refurbishment

A laptop accepted as a computer for testing/refurbishment is **not automatically “scrap metal” merely because it contains metal**. The statutory definition also requires that it be sold/obtained **as scrap for metal recycling/reprocessing**.

That supports keeping the startup intake purpose explicit:
- reusable electronics
- repairable electronics
- donor parts
- selected resale stock.

### Devices accepted specifically to strip for commodity metal

If Dubbo eWaste routinely obtains dead equipment **as scrap** in order to recover and sell/recycle the metal, the scrap-metal regime becomes substantially more relevant.

### Residual carcasses

The legislation does not give a simple public answer for the point at which residual metal from a reuse business turns the whole business into a scrap-metal dealer. If regular metal sales/rebates become a revenue stream, get SLED confirmation.

## 5.7 Paying a recycler versus being paid

If Dubbo eWaste merely pays an authorised downstream recycler to accept its waste, that looks materially different from operating as a scrap dealer.

If Dubbo eWaste:
- acquires scrap
- sorts metal
- regularly sells separated metal
- receives commodity payments

the case for registration becomes stronger.

This boundary should be confirmed with NSW Police SLED before adding “scrap” as an advertised service/revenue stream.

## 5.8 Transaction / payment obligations if registered

NSW Police confirms registered scrap-metal dealers:

- cannot pay for scrap with cash
- cannot pay by cheque payable to cash
- cannot pay in kind with goods/services
- must keep approved transaction records
- records include supplier identity, transaction date, description/quantity/weight and payment method
- records must be retained **3 years**
- suspicious transactions must be reported.

Source:  
https://www.police.nsw.gov.au/online_services/scrap_metal_industry

# Priority conclusions from items 1–5

1. **Planning classification, not EPA tonnage, is the biggest early legal question.**
2. Current evidence strongly points to **R2**, but obtain the authoritative property report/s10.7 certificate before treating it as final.
3. If R2 is confirmed, **home business/home industry are listed consent uses**, while goods-repair/reuse and waste/resource facilities are not listed principal uses.
4. A genuine low-impact home business may still qualify as **exempt development** under the Codes SEPP if all standards are met.
5. “Free donations” do **not** automatically stop unwanted electronics being waste under NSW environmental law.
6. The Fair Trading recycling-program carve-out is real, but its application to **collect → refurbish → resell** remains unresolved.
7. Scrap-metal registration is not triggered merely because computers contain metal; purpose matters. Keep Phase 0 focused on **electronics reuse**, not commodity scrap.
8. Do not spend on a DA, second-hand licence or scrap registration until the exact classification questions are answered.
