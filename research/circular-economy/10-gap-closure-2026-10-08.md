# Gap Closure — Dubbo Circular Economy

Date: 8 October 2026

This document revisits the gaps identified in the uploaded research pack against newer public evidence and research already present in the DubboEwaste repository.

## Executive result

The research now supports a clearer picture:

1. **Dubbo Regional Council is actively planning circular-economy/reuse infrastructure**, including reuse-shop provision and a Whylandra Resource Recovery Park/Circular Economy Hub concept.
2. **Whylandra is not currently verified as operating a public reuse shop.** Public-facing material still describes disposal and recycling.
3. **Public scavenging is not a lawful workaround** at Council waste facilities. Council pages explicitly prohibit scavenging/searching discarded material for reuse at relevant facilities.
4. **The historical e-waste processor/collector is known, but the current post-June-2025 Council contract and exact downstream material destinations are still not publicly proven.**
5. **Council's broad waste mass is now quantified** from the adopted Waste Strategy.
6. **The polystyrene pathway is substantially closed:** Whylandra now processes polystyrene on-site and sells densified blocks onward to manufacturers.
7. **Local repair/reuse capacity exists but is fragmented.** Computer/mobile, appliance, bike, machinery, clothing/shoe and community-workshop capability can all be named; a coordinated “front door” is missing.
8. The most valuable remaining questions are now operational and contractual, so more generic web searching has diminishing returns.

## Gap 1 — Does Whylandra have a reuse-shop / pre-disposal diversion plan?

**Status: VERIFIED PLAN; OPERATING STATUS NOT VERIFIED**

Council's October 2024 meeting outcome states that staff were preparing a business case for two reuse shops, one at Whylandra and one at Wellington.

A Council response to the 2024/25 Operational Plan consultation goes further: both the Wellington and Whylandra facility master plans include provision for a tip/reuse shop, and Council explicitly recognises these shops as important to the local circular economy and landfill diversion.

The adopted 2025–35 Waste Strategy adds:
- Action 6.11: support innovative circular solutions/pilots;
- Action 6.12: investigate deconstruction for problem wastes within a Circular Economy Hub concept;
- Action 6.13: review and assess a Resource Recovery Park at Whylandra, including a community-run Circular Economy Hub.

The 2025–29 Delivery Program also says Council is to adopt the Whylandra Waste Facility master plan and commence implementation.

**But:** the current Whylandra public page (edited July 2026) still describes a recycling/disposal facility, not a functioning reuse shop. Therefore the evidence supports **planned / under assessment**, not “open”.

### Important legal/operational boundary

Current Council waste-facility pages prohibit scavenging/searching discarded material for reuse. That means a reuse-first ITAD pilot cannot be based on informal retrieval from the public tip floor. It would require a formal pre-disposal diversion point, ownership-transfer rule, Council permission and/or contractual arrangement.

### What is still unknown

- Has the Whylandra master plan actually been adopted after the planned 2026 process?
- Is the reuse-shop component funded?
- Is an operator procurement planned?
- Would electronics be admitted?
- At what point does ownership pass to Council?
- Can Council legally/contractually divert accepted devices to a third-party refurbisher before recycling?

**Next evidence method:** Council Resource Recovery & Efficiency + procurement records + formal GIPA if required.

## Gap 2 — What does Dubbo's waste stream actually look like?

**Status: VERIFIED AT COUNCIL-WIDE AGGREGATE LEVEL**

The adopted 2025–35 Waste Strategy reports an average of **100,926 tonnes/year** managed across the reference period 2017/18–2021/22.

Generation by sector:
- Municipal solid waste: **28,176 t/year (28%)**
- Commercial & industrial: **29,275 t/year (29%)**
- Construction & demolition: **43,475 t/year (43%)**

Outcome:
- Disposed: **55,562 t/year (55%)**
- Diverted: **45,364 t/year (45%)**

Location:
- Managed within Council area: **97,031 t/year (96%)**
- Managed outside Council area: **3,895 t/year (4%)**

The strategy identifies self-haul mixed waste of more than 35,000 t/year as a major diversion opportunity.

### Useful interpretation

The opportunity is not just “more recycling”. The strategic gap is **front-end sorting, reuse and repair before mixed material loses value**.

### Still missing

The published strategy does not give the local computer/laptop-specific surplus volume required for an ITAD business case. That remains a primary-data problem:
- devices retired per school/business per year;
- percentage reusable;
- percentage data-bearing;
- average age/spec;
- current disposal/reuse route.

## Gap 3 — What happens to e-waste after Whylandra / NTCRS collection?

**Status: PARTIAL; CURRENT CONTRACT/DOWNSTREAM STILL UNVERIFIED**

Verified historical/current pieces:

- Council's Waste Strategy lists **Matthews Metals** for collection of e-waste, commencing 30 June 2023 and expiring 30 June 2025.
- The strategy also identifies Matthews Metals, Dubbo as local e-waste processing infrastructure.
- A later NSW regional infrastructure study likewise describes e-waste processing by Matthews Metals in Dubbo.
- The address associated with the former Matthews operation at **34 Mountbatten Drive, Dubbo** is now publicly presented by **Australian Metal Recycling (AMR)** as its Dubbo site, “formerly Matthews Metal Management”.
- The NSW Police Scrap Metal Industry Register lists **Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd / Australian Metal Recycling** at that address in 2026.
- NetWaste states Sims Metal Management collects e-waste from the majority of member councils, while explicitly noting that some councils maintain individual arrangements. This cannot be used to prove the Dubbo route.

### What this means

It is reasonable to say the **local processor/site transition is strongly evidenced**, but it is not responsible to state that AMR currently holds Dubbo Regional Council's e-waste contract without a current Council award/contract source.

The following remain unverified:
- Council's post-30-June-2025 e-waste contractor;
- whether Whylandra material first goes to AMR, Sims or another contractor today;
- the exact downstream facilities after first processing;
- recovery fractions for metals/plastics/boards/drives;
- reuse vs destruction policy for functional computers;
- data-bearing handling requirements.

**Next evidence method:** Council contract register/procurement record, then ask the contractor for downstream material-flow and data-destruction evidence.

## Gap 4 — Current polystyrene / “Foam Muncher” pathway

**Status: CLOSED / UPDATED**

Older research focused on a Sydney contractor pathway. Council's 2025 update changes the picture.

Whylandra now has an **on-site polystyrene processing machine**, co-funded by the NSW EPA. Council reported that:
- 5.7 tonnes were diverted in its first 15 months;
- the material is compacted into dense blocks;
- blocks are sold to manufacturers for use in building products;
- the new machine removes the previous need to send material to a Sydney contractor for melting.

Council's 2026 information continues to list separated polystyrene recycling.

**Research implication:** archive “Foam Muncher” as a historical pathway unless new evidence shows a continuing role.

## Gap 5 — Charity/op-shop acceptance and unsold pathways

**Status: PARTIAL**

Current evidence shows different local organisations operate different material scopes.

### Vinnies Dubbo
The official Vinnies shop directory currently places the Dubbo shop at **151 Macquarie Street**, so older addresses in the pack should not be treated as current. Public Vinnies information confirms typical donated categories such as clothing, accessories, shoes, toys, homewares and books.

**Still unknown locally:** electrical acceptance, test/tag process, furniture limits and exact route for unsold stock.

### Orana Support Service
Its official material explicitly advertises **electrical & whitegoods and furniture** among op-shop categories, and its donation information separately seeks furniture/whitegoods for clients moving into housing.

This is the strongest current evidence in the local charity sector that bulky/electrical household goods are actively handled.

**Still unknown:** electrical safety testing, failure rates, unsold-stock destination and whether repair occurs before resale.

### Emmanuel Care Centre
Official information confirms a donation-based op shop selling clothing, bric-a-brac, books, toys and other goods.

**Still unknown:** electrical/furniture scope and unsold route.

### Council textile fallback
Council provides charity shops and residents a free textile recycling service: unsaleable textiles can go to Whylandra, where they are bagged and sent for recycling.

**Still unknown:** current named downstream textile recycler and material recovery outcome.

### Conclusion

The “op shops” question is not one pathway. It needs a **shop-by-shop material acceptance matrix** and direct-contact evidence for:
- electricals;
- furniture;
- test/tag;
- repairs;
- unsold textiles;
- unsold hardgoods;
- transport/pickup;
- recycler used.

## Gap 10 — Procurement route for a local pilot

**Status: VERIFIED ENTRY MECHANISM**

Dubbo Regional Council's current procurement page directs suppliers to **VendorPanel** and states that an ABN is required for tender submissions. It also publishes recently received/awarded procurement information and a small-business procurement guide.

For a reuse/ITAD pilot, the realistic paths are:
1. small pilot/quotation under Council's procurement thresholds;
2. EOI/RFQ/tender if scope/value requires it;
3. collaboration under a formally authorised Council program;
4. Council-supported but independently operated pilot off-site.

Do not approach active tender matters informally through councillors/staff outside the permitted process.
