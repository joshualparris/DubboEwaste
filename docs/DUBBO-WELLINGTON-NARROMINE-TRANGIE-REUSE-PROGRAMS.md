# Official e-waste, reuse and resale programs — Dubbo, Wellington, Narromine and Trangie

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)

## Executive finding

Across Dubbo, Wellington, Narromine and Trangie, the official systems are still much stronger at **collecting/recycling e-waste** than at **testing, wiping, repairing and reselling working electronics**.

No current official council program was found that routinely performs this full electronics-reuse workflow:

**device arrives → functional test → secure data erase → repair/refurbish → resale/redeployment → recycle only failures**

The towns do have different levels of reuse activity, but most reuse is encouraged **before** the waste facility takes custody.

---

## 1. Dubbo

### Official e-waste route

Dubbo Regional Council's Whylandra Waste and Recycling Centre accepts separated domestic e-waste and old TVs free from residents.

Council has also said recyclable items including domestic e-waste can be segregated free at Whylandra/Wellington.

Current regional research also identifies Whylandra as a NetWaste e-waste collection point.

Sources:
- Dubbo Regional Council Whylandra page
- https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/News-and-Media/News-and-Resources/Council-News/2024/waste-facilities-cashless-from-1-july-2024
- https://www.netwaste.com.au/ewaste/

### Reuse/resale

Dubbo Regional Council has formally investigated **reuse shops**.

In October 2024 Council said staff were preparing a business case for:
- one reuse shop at Whylandra Waste Facility;
- one reuse shop associated with Wellington.

Council said the business case would assess environmental, economic and social benefits, followed by community/stakeholder consultation.

Source:
- https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/News-and-Media/News-and-Resources/Council-News/2024/outcomes-from-the-october-ordinary-council-meeting

Earlier Council material also said the Whylandra and Wellington master plans included provision for tip/reuse shops and recognised their role in the local circular economy and landfill diversion.

As of this research date, current facility pages did **not** verify that an electronics-focused reuse shop is operating.

### Practical conclusion

Dubbo Council clearly recognises reuse as desirable, but its current public e-waste service remains primarily a **collection/recycling pathway**.

---

## 2. Wellington

### Official e-waste route

Wellington Waste Transfer Station accepts segregated domestic e-waste/old TVs under Dubbo Regional Council's waste system.

It is also a listed regional e-waste collection point.

### Reuse/resale

Wellington was explicitly included in Dubbo Regional Council's reuse-shop planning.

The 2024 business-case update described:
- Whylandra reuse shop;
- Wellington reuse shop.

Current research did not verify an operating electronics reuse/refurbishment shop in Wellington.

### Practical conclusion

Wellington has a full official e-waste route and a **planned reuse-shop concept**, but no verified current electronics testing/refurbishment/resale system was found.

---

## 3. Narromine

### Official e-waste route

Narromine Shire Council's 2026–27 fees identify **domestic e-waste as no charge** at the waste facility.

NetWaste also lists Narromine as an e-waste collection point.

### Official reuse activity

Narromine has taken a **low-infrastructure reuse approach** through programs such as Garage Sale Trail.

Narromine Shire Council's 2024–25 Annual Report records:
- **47 garage sales/stalls** promoted through the 2024 Garage Sale Trail;
- **4,231 items reused or sold**.

This is important because it achieves reuse without Council needing to:
- take custody;
- test products;
- run a store;
- provide warranties;
- manage unsold stock.

### Electronics-specific reuse

No current official Narromine Council program was found that:
- tests computers;
- securely wipes drives;
- refurbishes laptops/phones;
- resells or redistributes them.

### Practical conclusion

Narromine promotes reuse, but its model is mainly **resident-to-resident reuse**, not council-operated electronics refurbishment.

---

## 4. Trangie

### Official electronics-recycling activity

Current Narromine Shire information identifies small-item recycling through Trangie Library for categories including:
- mobile phones;
- printer cartridges;
- household batteries;
- smoke detectors;
- selected lamps.

For full computers/TVs, Narromine's main waste facility is the more clearly documented e-waste route.

### Reuse/resale

No current official Trangie electronics-reuse/refurbishment/resale program was found.

### Practical conclusion

Trangie has **small-electronics recycling access**, but no verified council-run device-rescue operation.

## 5. Regional downstream context

All four towns sit within or adjacent to the broader **NetWaste** regional system.

NetWaste currently says **Sims Metal Management collects e-waste from the majority of NetWaste councils**, but it does not publicly map every member council to Sims.

Other documented regional routes include:
- Parkes → Sircel;
- Mudgee Tip Shop → reuse opportunity before recycling;
- historical NetWaste material → Sims St Marys shredding;
- historical/current Dubbo/Matthews/AMR pathways, with Dubbo's current post-2025 processor still unresolved.

Therefore do not assign a current downstream company to a specific town unless the council/contract identifies it.

## 6. Reuse hierarchy actually visible in the region

The current pattern is:

### Dubbo/Wellington
Council collection + recycling, with reuse shops planned/business-cased.

### Narromine
Council collection + recycling, plus person-to-person reuse via Garage Sale Trail.

### Trangie
Small-electronics recycling, but no verified specialist reuse layer.

### Parkes
Council collection with Sircel as a documented current regional processor.

### Mudgee
Tip Shop gives a clearer example of local reuse before final recycling.

---

## 7. What is missing

The missing regional service is not another generic e-waste cage.

It is a specialist layer that can do:

1. provenance/ownership check;
2. serial capture;
3. safe battery inspection;
4. secure data sanitisation;
5. power-on and hardware test;
6. repair/value assessment;
7. refurbishment;
8. commercial resale or social redeployment;
9. donor-parts recovery;
10. verified recycling for failures.

None of the four local council systems was found publicly providing this complete electronics-specific service.

---

## 8. Implication for Dubbo eWaste

A regional Dubbo-based operation could potentially serve:

**Dubbo + Wellington + Narromine + Trangie**

without trying to replace council waste services.

The role would be:

**intercept selected reusable electronics before they enter waste contracts**

while councils/NetWaste remain the downstream system for material that genuinely cannot be reused.

That model needs:
- direct council permission/partnership before diverting council-owned material;
- written ownership transfer from private/business donors;
- secure data practices;
- strict intake;
- known downstream routes;
- no assumption that public waste facilities will hand over items after disposal.

## 9. Questions to resolve with each council

### Dubbo Regional Council
- Current status of Whylandra and Wellington reuse-shop business cases.
- Is either reuse shop funded, designed or scheduled?
- Would electronics be included?
- Can reusable e-waste be diverted **before** it becomes council waste?
- Who currently owns e-waste after drop-off?
- Does the current e-waste contract prohibit diversion?
- Could a pilot reuse partner operate by appointment rather than inside the waste facility?

### Narromine Shire Council
- Exact current route for computers/TVs after Narromine drop-off.
- Whether any reusable equipment is currently separated.
- Whether Council would support a pre-disposal electronics reuse pilot.
- Whether Garage Sale Trail or community-reuse programs could include secure electronics reuse.
- Whether Trangie residents can take full e-waste locally or must use Narromine for certain categories.

### Trangie
- Exact current full e-waste acceptance at the transfer/waste facility.
- Whether laptops/desktops/monitors/TVs are accepted locally.
- Whether a mobile/periodic reuse collection would be useful.
