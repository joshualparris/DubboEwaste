# Good360, Compnow, National Device Bank, WorkVentures & Good Things Australia — deep dive and Dubbo connections

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)

This document traces the Australian reuse ecosystem around **Good360 Australia, Compnow/SustainIT, the National Device Bank, WorkVentures and Good Things Australia**, then maps the strongest **specific connections to Dubbo and regional NSW**.

The most important finding is that there are several related but **not identical** device-reuse pathways. They should not be collapsed into one chain.

---

## Executive findings

### 1. Good360 and Sircel are genuinely connected

Good360's 2024 Annual Report names **Sircel** among organisations contributing to its digital-access work. The same report names **Compnow** separately.

This proves Sircel has been a product donor into the Good360 digital-inclusion ecosystem.

It does **not** prove:

- all Sircel reuse devices went to Good360;
- that Sircel devices specifically went through Compnow;
- how many devices Sircel donated;
- that Sircel is currently a formal National Device Bank donor.

Those links need to remain separate unless a partner confirms them.

### 2. Compnow is a very detailed working example of how Good360 gets corporate laptops ready for second life

Good360 and Compnow published a 2024 case study showing an unusually detailed refurbishment chain:

**corporate fleet -> Compnow pickup -> certified wipe -> condition assessment -> hardware test -> repair/battery swap/parts matching -> OS reinstall -> source missing charger -> individual box/label -> Good360 -> disadvantaged school/student**

Compnow became Good360's official Education Partner in 2022.

### 3. The National Device Bank is a newer, broader system

The **National Device Bank (NDB)** was officially launched on 7 August 2025 by:

- **WorkVentures**
- **Good360 Australia**
- **Good Things Australia**

Its model is:

**corporate/government donor -> secure refurbishment -> free community distribution -> connectivity/digital skills/support**

WorkVentures is the technical/ITAD/refurbishment anchor; Good360 brings distribution/logistics and charity reach; Good Things brings community digital-literacy capability.

### 4. NSW now has a separate government-device pilot

The **NSW Device Bank** is a distinct 12-month state pilot announced in May 2026.

It uses **surplus NSW Government laptops** and is delivered by:

- NSW Telco Authority;
- WorkVentures;
- Good Things Australia.

The first publicly identified rollout was at **The Place Charlestown** in September 2026: 30 refurbished laptops, 12 months of data, digital-skills training and IT support.

The NSW program says it will expand through registered charities/community organisations across NSW.

### 5. Dubbo already has strong Good Things infrastructure

Two especially important local connections were found:

- **Dubbo Support Center** is a Good Things Australia partner, was selected for **Digital Sisters**, received multiple Good Things/Be Connected grants, and is now an **ACNC-registered charity**.
- **Digital Nebula – Dubbo** received Good Things/Be Connected digital-skills and Get Online Week funding.

This matters because the NSW Device Bank's published eligibility says registered NSW charities/NFPs on the ACNC register can request devices or apply as digital-literacy providers.

**Dubbo Support Center appears to meet that published organisation-level requirement. No public evidence was found that it has already applied or been selected.**

### 6. No publicly announced Dubbo Device Bank rollout was found

As of 2 October 2026, this research found:

- no named National Device Bank deployment in Dubbo;
- no named NSW Device Bank deployment in Dubbo;
- no public Compnow device-refurbishment project specifically in Dubbo;
- no public evidence that Good360's digital-device program has already distributed laptops in Dubbo.

That is not evidence that nothing has happened privately. It means there is **no public proof yet**.

# 1. How the organisations fit together

## The ecosystem is better understood as two overlapping pipelines

### Pipeline A — Good360 Digital Divide / Laptop Launchpad

**Corporate or government donor**
↓  
**Good360 donation program**
↓  
**trusted refurbishment partner such as Compnow**
↓  
secure wipe / test / repair / preparation
↓  
**Good360**
↓  
registered charity or disadvantaged school
↓  
**person who needs the device**

This pathway predates the National Device Bank and remains active.

### Pipeline B — National Device Bank

**Corporate or government donor**
↓  
**National Device Bank**
↓  
**WorkVentures**
- secure logistics
- asset tracking
- Blancco/data sanitisation
- testing/grading
- repair/refurbishment
- configuration
↓  
**Good360 / Good Things / WorkVentures networks**
↓  
trusted community organisation
↓  
**recipient + device + skills/support/connectivity**

### Pipeline C — NSW Device Bank

**NSW Government surplus laptop**
↓  
**NSW Telco Authority program**
↓  
**WorkVentures**
- secure collection
- wipe
- refurbish
- quality test
↓  
**Good Things Australia**
- trusted local community partner
- digital-skills layer
↓  
**eligible NSW resident**
↓  
laptop + internet/data + skills + support

The NSW Device Bank is therefore especially relevant to Dubbo because it is explicitly statewide and prioritises people facing digital exclusion including **regional and remote residents**.

# 2. Good360 Australia in detail

## What Good360 actually does

Good360 is primarily the **matching/distribution layer**, not the industrial ITAD/refurbishment specialist.

Its Digital Divide program says it:

1. **reclaims** used laptops, mobiles and tablets from organisations;
2. sends them through trusted refurbishment partners;
3. securely wipes/tests/prepares them;
4. recycles devices that fail;
5. **rehomes** successful devices through its charity/school network.

Its current donor page targets:

- laptops;
- mobile phones;
- iPads/tablets;
- generally **3–5 years old**;
- laptops **less than 5 years old and in good working condition**.

Good360 says recipient devices are supplied **free**.

### Program economics

Good360's current donor information says it asks participating donors to contribute toward program costs:

- **$100 per device** for device processing/distribution;
- **$150 per device** for device + 12 months of data.

Good360 says the recipient pays nothing.

This is useful for Dubbo E-Waste because it shows that a charitable reuse pathway still has real costs:

- data sanitisation;
- labour;
- testing;
- repairs;
- cleaning;
- logistics;
- matching;
- connectivity;
- support.

A "free laptop" to the end user is not a zero-cost system.

# 4. Compnow's Good360 refurbishment process — step by step

The 2024 Good360/Compnow case study is particularly valuable because it describes the practical workflow rather than just saying "refurbished".

## Step 1 — corporate fleet sourcing

Good360 specifically seeks corporate fleets rather than one-off individual devices.

The case study explains why:

- businesses can provide **bulk quantities of identical models**;
- schools can support and maintain a common device type more easily;
- teachers/students learn on the same platform;
- corporate devices are often higher specification;
- they often leave corporate service before physical end-of-life.

This is highly relevant to Dubbo E-Waste.

**Ten same-model ex-business laptops are operationally much better than ten random household laptops.**

## Step 2 — nationwide collection

Compnow's Lifecycle team collects redundant fleets **direct from donor organisations anywhere in Australia**.

That means Compnow does not need a Dubbo branch to service a Dubbo corporate donor.

Compnow's current physical locations are:

- Melbourne;
- Adelaide;
- Brisbane;
- Perth;
- Sydney.

No Dubbo Compnow office was found.

## Step 3 — secure data erasure

Good360 says corporate donors were concerned about legal/data risk.

Compnow's SustainIT system provides certified data erasure, and Good360 says this capability was essential in persuading businesses to allow equipment to be reused rather than destroyed.

Compnow's broader SustainIT material says:

- all data/identity is cleansed;
- processing is auditable;
- data-destruction certificates are available.

This is the same commercial insight found in the Sircel research:

> **trusted reuse requires credible data destruction/sanitisation evidence.**

## Step 4 — physical and cosmetic assessment

The case study says Compnow checks each device for:

- damage;
- scratches;
- wear and tear;
- missing keys.

So "working" is not enough. Presentation and completeness matter for a device intended to support equal access in education.

## Step 5 — hardware testing

Named tests in the case study include:

- battery life;
- speakers;
- camera.

The full internal test matrix is not published.

## Step 6 — repairs and parts matching

Compnow has multi-brand equipment repair credentials.

The case study specifically says it can:

- replace batteries;
- "mix and match" to maximise the life of devices.

This implies a donor-parts strategy: one marginal device can help make another complete and reliable.

That is directly applicable to a small Dubbo refurb operation.

## Step 7 — operating system reinstall

The case study says the operating system is reinstalled after wipe/test.

The published case study specifically refers to Mac/iOS in its worked example; it should not be interpreted as saying Good360 only distributes Apple devices.

## Step 8 — missing accessories are sourced

Compnow sources items such as:

- power adapters.

This matters because a donated laptop without a charger is not actually ready for a student.

## Step 9 — individual presentation

Each successful laptop is:

- individually boxed;
- labelled;
- delivered to Good360.

Again, this shows the difference between "technically powers on" and **deployment-ready**.

## Step 10 — unsuitable items can still create value

One of the most interesting details in the case study is that unsuitable donated products, such as certain keyboards or VoIP phones, may be **on-sold by Compnow and the proceeds transferred to Good360**.

So the social model is not necessarily:

> every donated object must itself be donated.

It can be:

> recover the highest value, then use that value to fund social outcomes.

This is an important possible model for Dubbo E-Waste.

# 5. Compnow's wider SustainIT business model

Outside the Good360 case study, Compnow's current SustainIT/buy-back material describes this lifecycle:

1. identify end-of-life/unneeded assets;
2. receive an indicative buy-back quote;
3. secure collection;
4. detailed auditable assessment;
5. secure removal of data and identity;
6. formal itemised valuation;
7. supplier payment;
8. refurbish, resell, reuse or recycle.

Compnow says **more than 98%** of equipment it receives is refurbished/repurposed and less than 2% is recycled.

That is a **Compnow company claim across its lifecycle service**, not an audited figure for the Good360 partnership specifically.

## Why this matters for Dubbo

Compnow demonstrates that reuse can be commercially self-supporting:

- sell high-value devices;
- return value to the asset owner;
- repair worthwhile equipment;
- donate some equipment;
- recycle the tail.

Dubbo E-Waste could use the same logic at much smaller scale.

# 6. National Device Bank — what it is now

The National Device Bank officially launched on **7 August 2025**.

It is a national collaboration between:

- **WorkVentures**
- **Good360 Australia**
- **Good Things Australia**

Its purpose is to keep good corporate/government devices in Australia and put them into productive use rather than allowing all refresh equipment to be exported, shredded or landfilled.

## Current recipient model

The NDB says registered not-for-profits can apply.

It does not operate as:

> individual person fills out a form and receives a laptop directly.

Instead:

**community organisation -> requests devices -> identifies/supports people -> device reaches recipient through trusted local channel**

## Current device quality benchmark

The NDB's current standard free laptop specification is:

- Intel Core **i5 6th generation or higher**;
- **8 GB+ RAM**;
- **128 GB+ SSD**;
- Wi-Fi;
- built-in webcam/microphone;
- minimum **13.3-inch screen**.

This is an extremely useful benchmark for Dubbo E-Waste's future **social-reuse grade**.

It says, effectively:

> a donated device should still be useful, not merely technically functional.

## Current direct-donation threshold

The NDB's current donor page says it currently accepts contributions of **more than 50 devices** from private/public organisations.

This means a 20–30-device Dubbo pilot would **not currently meet the NDB's published direct corporate donation threshold** on its own.

However, WorkVentures' broader CircularIT operation says clients can donate as much or as little of their fleet as they wish and scopes logistics case by case.

So there are two different entry points:

- **direct NDB bulk donation**: current public threshold >50 devices;
- **WorkVentures CircularIT/ITAD engagement**: potentially smaller/flexible, subject to commercial/logistics scope.

---

# 7. How the National Device Bank is funded

The NDB is not simply "take every computer and give it away".

Industry reporting on the NDB says **a subset of donated devices can be commercially sold** to help fund the devices provided free to people in need.

This is another version of the cross-subsidy principle:

**high-value resale**
→ funds
**free community devices + refurbishment + logistics + support**

That makes the model particularly relevant to Dubbo E-Waste.

A local operation does not need to choose between:

- "business"
- and "charity".

It can potentially use commercial resale to subsidise social reuse.

# 8. WorkVentures in detail

WorkVentures is the National Device Bank's main technical/refurbishment engine.

Its current CircularIT workflow is unusually transparent.

## 1. Inventory and assessment

WorkVentures:

- catalogues assets;
- assesses condition;
- assesses residual value.

## 2. Secure collection

It advertises:

- secure logistics;
- chain of custody;
- secure containers;
- locked transport.

WorkVentures says its logistics partners cover **major cities and regional centres nationally**.

So Dubbo is geographically within the type of coverage it says it can service, although no specific Dubbo collection was publicly found.

## 3. Data sanitisation

WorkVentures says:

- storage media is sanitised to standards such as **NIST 800-88 or equivalent**;
- it uses **Blancco** to sanitise and grade controlled assets;
- serialised certificates/reporting can be issued;
- media that cannot be securely erased is physically destroyed.

It also says processing operates under ISO 27001-aligned controls and chain-of-custody tracking.

## 4. Grade/refurbish

Working equipment can be:

- refurbished;
- redeployed;
- resold;
- donated.

Other equipment is sent to responsible recycling.

## 5. Community delivery

WorkVentures says it can manage:

- logistics;
- onboarding;
- training;
- delivery;
- support;
- verification.

That means the service continues **after the bench repair is finished**.

## 6. Reporting

Corporate clients can receive:

- asset tracking;
- data-destruction evidence;
- reuse vs recycling outcomes;
- environmental/impact reporting;
- social-impact information.

This is an excellent template for the records Dubbo E-Waste should build from the start.

# 9. What happens to WorkVentures failures?

WorkVentures says devices that cannot be reused go through **R2-certified recycling partners**.

This mirrors the ideal Dubbo hierarchy:

**reuse first -> recover value second -> certified recycling last**

It is substantially different from sending all retired IT directly to a general recycling cage.

# 22. What this means for Dubbo E-Waste

There are potentially **three roles** for a local reuse operation.

## Role A — local feeder of business devices

Dubbo E-Waste could source retired fleets from:

- businesses;
- schools;
- professional services;
- community organisations.

Then:

- triage locally;
- retain commercially viable stock where agreements allow;
- potentially aggregate high-quality social-use devices;
- route them into a recognised community device program.

The NDB's current >50-device direct-donation threshold means a very small pilot may need to aggregate devices or work through WorkVentures/Good360 rather than apply as a direct donor.

## Role B — local refurbishment partner

This would be more ambitious.

Dubbo E-Waste could eventually ask whether WorkVentures, Good360 or another program needs:

- first-pass local triage;
- charger matching;
- RAM/SSD upgrades;
- cleaning;
- hardware testing;
- local repairs;
- serial-level inventory;
- final-mile deployment support.

There is **no current evidence they are seeking a Dubbo subcontractor**, so this should be explored rather than assumed.

## Role C — local social-reuse + commercial cross-subsidy

The strongest self-controlled model may be:

1. collect good retired IT locally;
2. wipe/test/repair;
3. sell high-value devices commercially;
4. use margin to support lower-value but useful social devices;
5. donate through an ACNC/local service partner;
6. track outcomes;
7. recycle failures.

That mirrors the core economics visible in:

- Compnow;
- Good360;
- National Device Bank;
- WorkVentures.

# 24. Why commercial resale should not be viewed as contrary to the social model

All three major models researched use some form of value recovery.

### Compnow
Buys back assets, refurbishes/resells/reuses them, and recycles failures.

### Good360/Compnow
Some unsuitable donated categories can be sold and proceeds returned to Good360.

### National Device Bank / WorkVentures
A subset of residual-value equipment can be remarketed to financially sustain free-device distribution.

Therefore a Dubbo model can legitimately say:

> **We sell some computers so we can save and redistribute more computers.**

That is closer to the mature Australian social-enterprise model than attempting to donate every asset regardless of value.

↓

↓  
secure wipe + test + repair

two streams:

**commercial machines**
→ resale
→ fund the operation

→ ACNC community partner such as Dubbo Support Center

with failures going to a verified recycler.

## Final conclusion

The national infrastructure Dubbo E-Waste would otherwise have to invent already exists in pieces:

- **Compnow** shows how to turn ex-corporate laptops into deployment-ready devices.
- **WorkVentures** shows how to run secure, auditable ITAD and refurbishment at scale.
- **Good360** shows how to match devices to trusted community organisations.
- **Good Things Australia** already has digital-inclusion partners in Dubbo.
- **The National Device Bank** joins those functions nationally.
- **The NSW Device Bank** now provides a live government-backed route into regional NSW.

The strongest local discovery is **Dubbo Support Center**: it is already inside Good Things' digital-inclusion ecosystem and now appears to satisfy the published ACNC eligibility requirement for the NSW Device Bank.

The most useful immediate investigation is therefore not another generic e-waste contact.

It is:

> **Can Dubbo Support Center + Good Things + WorkVentures bring the NSW Device Bank to Dubbo, and can Dubbo E-Waste become the complementary local reuse/refurbishment/supply layer?**
