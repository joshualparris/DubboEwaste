I've checked our existing DubboEwaste research. We've already identified the recycling programme exemption, but we haven't established exactly how to qualify for it.

The most promising discovery is that the 2021 Regulation, clause 6, explicitly exempted three activities:

- Conducting a recycling or rubbish collection programme.

- Selling goods collected through that programme.

- Contracting to transfer ownership of those goods.

That second provision is particularly relevant to your refurbishment and resale activities. NSW Fair Trading's current September 2026 guidance still lists recycling programmes as exempt, but we need to establish the precise wording of the new 2026 Regulation. legislation.nsw.gov.au +1

Deep Research: Qualifying Dubbo E-waste for the NSW Recycling Programme Licensing Exemption

Primary objective

Determine the cheapest, simplest and fully lawful way for Dubbo E-waste to collect, repair, refurbish, dismantle and resell unwanted electronic equipment without paying for a second-hand dealer licence, if a legitimate exemption applies.

The project is a small-scale, reuse-first initiative with limited revenue. The $692 annual licence application cost represents a significant financial barrier.

Do not assume an exemption applies merely because the organisation describes itself as a recycling programme.

2. Examine the exact current legislation

Compare the historical recycling exemption under clause 6 with the current legislation.

Determine whether the exemption still covers selling goods collected through a recycling programme, including repaired or refurbished goods.

3. Define a recycling programme

Investigate:

- Can an individual or sole trader establish one?
- Can it operate commercially?
- Can it generate profit?

- Can devices and harvested components be sold online?
- Can proceeds fund operating expenses or compensate the operator?
- Are written policies, public collection arrangements or environmental reporting required?
- Can purchased equipment form part of the programme?
- Does the programme require an approved downstream recycling partner?

Identify the actual legal criteria rather than inventing additional requirements.
