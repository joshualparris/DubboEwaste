# Dubbo eWaste — master research synthesis

**Updated:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)

## Purpose

This is the master project index for the research already captured in this repository.

It consolidates the major findings, evidence boundaries, operating decisions and unresolved questions across the Dubbo eWaste project without duplicating every supporting detail.

The exact residential street address is intentionally omitted because this repository is public.

---

## 1. Core project

Dubbo eWaste is being tested as a **small regional electronics reuse, refurbishment and resale operation**, not an industrial recycling plant.

The Phase 0 concept is:
- selective intake;
- Tuesdays and Saturdays;
- pre-approval before arrival;
- no unattended dumping;
- high-value/reusable devices first;
- online resale first;
- secure data handling;
- reuse first;
- repair second;
- parts recovery third;
- verified recycling last.

## 2. Core commercial thesis

The regional waste system already has strong collection and material-recycling infrastructure.

The missing layer is:

> **Who checks whether a device still has whole-device value before it becomes e-waste?**

The project therefore aims to capture:

**whole-device reuse value > repair value > parts value > commodity scrap value**

before a machine enters a destructive recycling stream.

## 3. Dubbo's current e-waste chain

### Council
Whylandra and Wellington accept domestic e-waste.

Historical Council documents named Matthews Metal Management as an e-waste contractor, with a listed contract ending 30 June 2025.

The current post-2025 Dubbo Regional Council e-waste processor remains unresolved publicly.

### AMR / former Matthews yard
The strongest current evidence identifies:
- Australian Metal Recycling;
- Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd;
- 34 Mountbatten Drive, Dubbo;
- NSW scrap-metal registration SM-0759;
- AMR describes Dubbo as formerly Matthews Metal Management.

AMR currently lists TVs, computers/laptops, monitors, smartphones/tablets and printers/scanners as accepted e-waste.

Its published material also excludes lithium-ion material/batteries, creating an unresolved question for whole laptops/phones/tablets.

## 4. Who does AMR Dubbo send e-waste to?

Deep research tested:
- AMR Rutherford;
- Sircel Parkes;
- ACE Recycling Group;
- Sims;
- TechCollect/ANZRP;
- historical Matthews pathways.

Result:

> **No public source currently identifies AMR Dubbo's first downstream e-waste facility.**

Important evidence:
- historical Matthews had NTCRS involvement;
- historical Matthews material referenced TechCollect product rules;
- current TechCollect NSW recyclers include Sircel and ACE;
- Sircel Parkes is a strong regional processing lead;
- no direct AMR→Sircel, AMR→ACE or AMR→Sims link has been found;
- AMR Rutherford is a major hub, but routing of Dubbo e-waste there is not proved.

## 5. TechCollect and reuse

TechCollect's public recycling stream is **not a second-hand reuse stream**.

TechCollect says collected products are recycled in Australia and not sold as second-hand products for reuse.

It advises owners of still-useful equipment to pass it on before recycling.

ANZRP supports reuse separately through programs such as:
- PonyUp for Good;

## 7. PonyUp → SecondBite → Dubbo

The correct relationship is:

**PonyUp reuse/resale → PonyUp profit → SecondBite nationally**

Separately:

**SecondBite / Coles → Connecting Community Services Dubbo → food relief**

Important boundary:
- computers do not flow from PonyUp to SecondBite;
- money/profit does.

Connecting Community Services is a concrete Dubbo SecondBite partner and also provides free computer/internet access and basic digital help.

No public evidence has been found that PonyUp currently processes Dubbo equipment or that Connecting Community Services currently receives refurbished devices through PonyUp/SecondBite.

- `PONYUP-SECONDBITE-DUBBO.md`

## 11. Why councils have not done more electronics reuse

The evidence supports a structural explanation rather than lack of interest.

Recycling can be handled as:

**collection → logistics → contracted processor → tonnage/material recovery**

Electronics reuse requires:

**triage → provenance → secure storage → data erase → test → repair → safety → pricing → retail → returns → unsold-stock management → final recycling**

Key barriers:
- labour;
- staffing;
- security;
- dumping;
- storage;
- electrical safety;
- product liability;
- Australian Consumer Law;
- data security;
- battery/fire risk;
- stolen-goods/provenance;
- repair skill;
- POS/customer service;
- warranty/returns;
- inventory depreciation;
- transport distance;
- small regional markets;
- limited council rate bases/economies of scale.

Current NSW comparative evidence from Cessnock documents exactly these kinds of barriers for a council reuse shop.

Canonical:
- `WHY-COUNCIL-ELECTRONICS-REUSE-IS-HARD.md`

## 13. Planning / legal position

The largest current legal uncertainty is planning/classification.

Key findings:
- home-business framework exists;
- Dubbo LEP home-business floor-area cap identified as 30 m²;
- goods repair/reuse premises is a separate planning concept;
- below-threshold waste activity can still have planning implications;
- exact site classification/consent remains unresolved.

Second-hand dealer research:
- electronics are prescribed goods;
- 2026–27 licence fees researched as $692 / 1 year, $1,574 / 3 years, $2,456 / 5 years;
- records/ownership/ID obligations can apply;
- 14-day hold/no-alteration rule can apply;
- recycling-program exemption exists but applicability to this mixed refurb/resale model remains unresolved.

## 19. Social reuse and commercial resale can coexist

Research examples show that resale is not incompatible with social outcomes.

Examples:
- PonyUp converts resale value into SecondBite funding;
- Reconnect mixes repairs, sales and social distribution;
- Compnow/Good360 combine value recovery with device access;
- WorkVentures/Device Bank uses structured asset recovery/refurbishment.

Therefore a Dubbo model can plausibly use:

**commercial resale → fund more reuse/social devices**

instead of trying to donate every valuable asset.

## 22. Best evidence-based positioning

> **Dubbo eWaste is a proposed regional electronics reuse and IT asset-recovery service designed to catch usable equipment before it enters material-recycling streams.**

The value proposition is not another drop-off cage.

It is:
- provenance;
- secure data handling;
- technical assessment;
- refurbishment;
- resale;
- optional social redeployment;
- parts recovery;
- verified recycling for genuine failures.

## Final synthesis

The same conclusion has strengthened across every research stream:

> **The opportunity is not to become another e-waste collection point. It is to intercept good electronics before they become e-waste.**

The surrounding system already provides collection and recycling.

The project is testing whether Dubbo can support the missing specialist layer:
**secure reuse, refurbishment, resale, social redeployment and parts recovery before final recycling.**
