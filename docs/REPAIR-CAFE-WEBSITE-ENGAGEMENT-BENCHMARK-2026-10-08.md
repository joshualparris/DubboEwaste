# Repair Café Website Engagement Benchmark

<!-- DOCUMENTATION-SYNC-2026-10-09 -->

**Research date:** 8 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Identify website/engagement patterns that should be built into a public Dubbo Repair Café participation site.

## Key conclusion

The most engaging Repair Café websites do not behave like sustainability reports.

They make four things immediately obvious:

1. **What is this?**
2. **What can I bring?**
3. **Can someone like me help?**
4. **What do I do next?**

The strongest sites reduce the psychological threshold to participation. They show that:
- you do not need to be an expert;
- repair is social and practical;
- not every item will be fixed;
- diagnosis/advice still has value;
- there are non-repair volunteer roles;
- learners can shadow experienced fixers;
- events are concrete, local and time-bounded.

# Benchmark 1 — Repair Café Silicon Valley

Website:
https://repaircafesv.org/

Volunteer page:
https://repaircafesv.org/volunteer-opportunities/

## Evidence of engagement

Repair Café International reported in May 2026 that Silicon Valley's rotating pop-up events commonly attract:
- **30–40 volunteers per event**;
- around **150 visitors with broken items**.

Source:
https://www.repaircafe.org/en/pop-up-repair-cafes-in-unusual-venues-from-museums-to-universities/

## What the website does well

### Human hero promise
“Bring your broken items. Leave with them fixed.”

It tells the visitor what they get before explaining organisational structure.

### Concrete repair categories
The homepage names electronics, appliances, clothing, bikes, jewellery, computers and toys.

### Normalises imperfect outcomes
The site explicitly explains:
- sometimes it is fixed;
- sometimes the problem is identified and the visitor leaves with a plan;
- sometimes recycling is the right answer.

This reduces fear of “wasting the volunteer's time”.

### Makes volunteering inclusive
It speaks to:
- experienced tinkerers;
- organisers;
- learners;
- students.

The volunteer page has distinct roles:
- general fixer;
- textile fixer;
- jewellery fixer;
- bike fixer;
- coordinator;
- student apprentice.

### Clear recurring action
Upcoming events are prominent, with location/time and sign-up buttons.

### Host venue partnership
Libraries, museums, community centres, makerspaces and other hosts become part of outreach.

## Dubbo implementation
- lead with a simple promise;
- list repair types visually;
- explicitly welcome learners and non-technical volunteers;
- make “I can host a venue” a first-class role;
- allow interest even before the first event date is set;
- use pop-up/rotating venue language.

# Benchmark 2 — Repair Café Toronto

Website:
https://repaircafetoronto.ca/volunteer/

## What it does well

The volunteer form asks people to choose actual skills:
- small appliances;
- computers;
- electronics;
- furniture;
- clothing;
- jewellery;
- books/paper;
- bikes.

It separately asks about non-repair roles:
- event support;
- promotion;
- refreshments.

It also includes an **Apprentice Fixer** option.

That is an important engagement pattern: the form itself teaches people that “volunteer” does not mean “qualified repair technician”.

## Dubbo implementation
The public form should capture:
- fix skills;
- learner/apprentice interest;
- event-host/admin/media roles;
- venue offers;
- tools/parts donations;
- local business/organisation partnership.

# Benchmark 3 — Bendigo Share and Repair Shed

Website:
https://bendigoshareandrepairshed.org/

FAQ:
https://bendigoshareandrepairshed.org/faqs

## Evidence of durable engagement

The organisation began as Bendigo Repair Café in **2017** and expanded into:
- Repair Café;
- Tool Library;
- Useful Bin/material exchange;
- Food is Free table;
- recycling drop-off;
- swap table;
- workshops/education;
- Remakery/upcycled retail;
- maker-space ambitions.

This is evidence of a repair initiative growing into a broader community hub rather than remaining a one-off event.

## What the site does well

### Positive framing
“Things are fixed and friends are made.”

### Shows a pathway beyond repair
Visitors can borrow, swap, learn, reuse and volunteer.

### Concrete opening times
People know when they can participate.

### FAQ lowers uncertainty
Membership, tools, safety and volunteering are clearly explained.

## Dubbo implementation
The initial site should leave room for future community ideas such as:
- tool library;
- useful-materials exchange;
- workshops;
- refurbished computers;
- swap table.

But it should label them as ideas, not promise them.

# Benchmark 4 — Waverley Council Repair Café

Current event:
https://www.waverley.nsw.gov.au/recreation/events/events/repair_cafe3

Overview:
https://www.waverley.nsw.gov.au/secondnature/get_involved/circular_economy_opportunities/repair_cafe

## What it does well

### Clear boundaries
It says what is and is not accepted.

### Time expectation
Repairs are handled in roughly 30-minute slots; unresolved items receive diagnosis/next-step advice.

### Specific categories
Computers/phones, electronics, textiles, bikes, toys, sharpening and wood items are named.

### Honest expectations
Volunteer availability means repair cannot be guaranteed.

## Dubbo implementation
The website should explain:
- this is not a commercial drop-off service;
- owners stay and participate;
- volunteers can refuse unsafe/unsuitable jobs;
- first pilot categories depend on available skills;
- complex/mains/battery-danger work may be excluded.

# Benchmark 5 — Repair Café Belfast

FAQ:
https://repaircafebelfast.wordpress.com/2017/11/30/how-does-it-work-faqs/

## What it does well

It makes the event sound social rather than clinical:
- tea/coffee;
- home baking;
- crafts/board games;
- watching and learning;
- one item at a time.

The owner stays with the fixer rather than dropping the item off.

## Dubbo implementation
Use language such as:
- “Bring something broken, bring your curiosity, or just come meet people.”
- “You do not need to know how to repair anything to belong.”

Family-friendly/community connection should be visible, while tool areas remain supervised.

About:

House rules:

## What it does well

### Strong global identity
The site makes a local Repair Café feel part of a worldwide movement.

### Clear concept
Free meeting places where people repair things **together**.

### Multiple roles
Repair experts, hosts/refreshments and communications volunteers.

### Established house rules
The repair relationship, liability expectations, parts costs and right to refuse jobs are transparent.

### Measures impact
RepairMonitor provides a model for recording repairs and outcomes.

## Dubbo implementation
- link to Repair Café International;
- do not imply official registration until it exists;
- use “proposed Repair Café Dubbo” until formal network requirements are completed;
- plan outcome measurement from the first pilot.

# Engagement design principles for the Dubbo site

## 1. One primary action
The first screen should ask:
**Help shape Repair Café Dubbo**

Secondary action:
**I could volunteer**

Do not present six equally weighted buttons.

## 2. Do not require expertise
Use:
“You can fix, organise, welcome people, teach, learn, host a venue, take photos, make tea or simply tell us what Dubbo needs.”

## 3. Show categories, not abstract mission
People recognise:
- laptop;
- bike;
- shirt;
- chair;
- drill;
- toy.

They do not necessarily respond to “resource circularity”.

## 4. Make the project status truthful
Use a visible label:
**Community idea being tested — not yet an operating repair service.**

This prevents people arriving with broken items before an event exists.

## 5. Invite the community to choose the venue
Show researched candidates but include:
**Suggest somewhere better.**

## 6. Capture skill confidence
Do not only ask “would you volunteer?”
Ask:
- curious / want to learn;
- hobbyist;
- experienced;
- trade/professional.

## 7. Separate repair and non-repair volunteering
People can help with:
- welcome desk;
- tea/coffee;
- setup;
- bookings;
- photography;
- social media;
- data/impact;
- venue hosting.

## 8. Ask the counterfactual
For residents with an item:
“What would you probably do if a Repair Café did not exist?”
- pay for repair;
- try myself;
- keep it broken;
- replace it;
- give it away;
- recycle it;
- throw it out.

This creates valuable evidence about actual waste prevention.

## 9. Keep the form short enough to finish
Use progressive visual groups and optional fields.
Only collect email if a person wants follow-up.

## 10. Show safety boundaries without making the page frightening
Simple language:
“Some repairs need licensed specialists or safer facilities. Volunteers can say no to unsafe jobs.”

## 11. Build social proof later from real data
After pilot events, show:
- items brought;
- items fixed;
- items diagnosed;
- kilograms kept in use;
- volunteer hours;
- stories/photos with consent.

Never invent launch metrics.

## 12. Mobile first
Many community participants will arrive from Facebook, local-news links or QR posters on phones.

Required:
- large tap targets;
- no horizontal scrolling;
- readable font sizes;
- short sections;
- sticky/visible CTA;
- form labels above fields;
- no required account/login.

# Public form fields recommended

## Why are you here?
- I would attend.
- I have something I would bring.
- I could volunteer as a fixer.
- I could volunteer in another role.
- I represent a local organisation/business.
- I may be able to offer a venue.
- I can donate/lend tools or materials.

## Repair interests
- computers/laptops;
- phones/tablets;
- electronics;
- small appliances;
- bikes;
- textiles/clothing;
- furniture/wood;
- tools/mechanical;
- toys;
- jewellery;
- other.

## Volunteer skills
Same repair categories plus:
- welcoming/intake;
- event setup;
- tea/coffee;
- accessibility support;
- admin;
- communications/social media;
- photography/video;
- data/impact;
- venue host.

## Confidence
- want to learn;
- beginner;
- hobbyist;
- experienced;
- trade/professional.

## Venue preference
- Western Plains Cultural Centre / Community Arts Centre;
- Pipe Band Hall;
- Connecting Community Services;
- Dubbo Library;
- Men's Shed partnership;
- rotating pop-ups;
- other.

## Timing
- Saturday morning;
- Saturday afternoon;
- Sunday;
- weekday evening;
- flexible.

## Open questions
- venue suggestion;
- repair types Dubbo needs most;
- other idea;
- accessibility need.

## Contact
- first name optional;
- email optional unless requesting updates;
- postcode optional;
- consent to contact.

# Recommended public tone

Avoid:
- ITAD;
- waste diversion percentages in the hero;
- compliance language;
- corporate jargon;
- “technicians required”.

Prefer:
- Fix it together.
- Learn something.
- Meet people.
- Keep useful things in use.
- Everyone has something to contribute.

# Recommended hero

> **Fix it. Learn it. Keep it in use.**  
> We're exploring a Repair Café for Dubbo: a welcoming place where people bring broken everyday items, sit alongside volunteers and try to repair them together.  
> **No event is operating yet. Help us shape the first one.**

# Website success criteria

The first version succeeds if it can answer:
1. How many people would attend?
2. What do they want repaired?
3. How many people might volunteer?
4. What skills do volunteers have?
5. Which venues are preferred/offered?
6. Which times work?
7. Which organisations want to partner?
8. What would residents otherwise do with broken items?
9. Are there accessibility barriers?
10. Is there enough demand for a three-month pilot?
