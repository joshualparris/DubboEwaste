## 1. What happens to Dubbo e-waste today

- Dubbo Regional Council's Whylandra Waste and Recycling Centre takes household e-waste (anything with a cord or battery) free from residents. It is a Community Recycling Centre and an NTCRS participant (via DHL Drop Zone). Council also publishes a separate commercial fee schedule; the e-waste rate for business is unknown.
- Historical public trail: Whylandra -> Matthews Metals (Dubbo) -> downstream recovery. Council's 2025-2035 Waste Strategy and a late-2025 NSW Planning study name Matthews Metals in Dubbo as the e-waste processor, but Council's listed Matthews e-waste contract expired 30 June 2025.
- **2026 update:** the strongest current evidence points to **Australian Metal Recycling (AMR), operated by Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd (ABN 51 640 765 091), at 34 Mountbatten Drive**. NSW Police's 19 Feb 2026 Scrap Metal Industry Register lists that address under AMR registration SM-0759, while AMR calls Dubbo “Formerly Matthews Metal Management”.
- InfraBuild still has an online Matthews Dubbo branch page and OneSteel Recycling (an InfraBuild subsidiary) still holds the Matthews Metal Management business name. A March 2025 tender listed InfraBuild Recycling and Scrap Metal Services as separate bidders, so AMR should not be treated as simply another InfraBuild brand.
- AMR currently advertises **free e-waste recycling** and explicitly lists **TVs, computers/laptops, monitors, smart phones/tablets and printers/scanners** as accepted e-waste. Its materials list says **electronic waste excluding lithium ion**, and lithium batteries are separately listed as not accepted. CRT-specific rules, battery removal from whole devices, commercial limits and downstream certificates still need direct confirmation.
- **What happens after drop-off:** AMR does not publicly name the downstream e-waste processor used by Dubbo. Its published equipment/capabilities look primarily like regional collection, weighing, sorting, consolidation and transport rather than a specialist electronics dismantling/refining line. The normal Australian downstream chain is dismantling/shredding → separation into steel, aluminium/copper, glass, plastics, batteries, PCBs and toner → specialist processing in Australia and, for some fractions, approved overseas facilities. This benchmark is documented by NTCRS operators, but **must not be presented as AMR's exact chain until AMR names its processor(s)**.
- **Data rule:** ordinary AMR recycling is not currently evidenced as a secure data-destruction service. Wipe/remove HDDs, SSDs and NVMe storage before handover unless AMR provides a written, serialised destruction process.
- **Deepest downstream finding:** no public source currently names AMR Dubbo's first downstream e-waste recipient. Historical Matthews records show NTCRS involvement and a TechCollect-linked intake scope. Current TechCollect NSW recyclers include **Sircel** and **ACE Recycling Group**, but its current public collection-partner list does not identify AMR/Matthews/Dubbo. **Sircel Parkes is the strongest regional research lead** because of its current Central West/NetWaste/Narromine/Parkes role, but there is still **no evidence that AMR Dubbo sends material there**.
- **TechCollect reuse finding:** TechCollect explicitly says equipment already surrendered to its collection stream is **recycled in Australia and not sold second-hand for reuse**. It asks owners to give still-useful equipment to family, friends or charities *before* using TechCollect. ANZRP supports reuse separately through upstream partnerships such as **PonyUp for Good, The Reconnect Project and The Laptop Initiative**, with non-reusable equipment then flowing to ANZRP recycling. This makes the closest model for Dubbo eWaste **reuse/refurbish first, recycling second**, not a standard TechCollect drop-off.
- **Reuse-model benchmark:** **PonyUp** targets recent corporate fleets (roughly 2–5 years), performs secure erasure/asset reporting, refurbishes and remarkets viable equipment, and sends failures to ANZRP; its published impact says about **32–32.5%** of collected equipment has been reused. **The Reconnect Project** accepts much rougher gear, including phones/tablets of any age/condition and laptops under about 8 years, then triages, repairs, harvests donor parts, securely erases and either sells affordably or distributes devices through social-service partners; its current site reports **4,800+ devices distributed**. **The Laptop Initiative** is deliberately strict: usually good working laptops ≤4 years old, already prepared/wiped, moved rapidly from corporate donors to charity partners with little warehousing and no normal repair program. Full comparison: [docs/REUSE-MODELS-PONYUP-RECONNECT-LAPTOP-INITIATIVE.md](docs/REUSE-MODELS-PONYUP-RECONNECT-LAPTOP-INITIATIVE.md).
- Full operator evidence: [docs/MATTHEWS-INFRABUILD-AMR.md](docs/MATTHEWS-INFRABUILD-AMR.md). Full downstream trace: [docs/WHAT-HAPPENS-TO-EWASTE.md](docs/WHAT-HAPPENS-TO-EWASTE.md). Exact-recipient forensic trace: [docs/AMR-DUBBO-DOWNSTREAM-FORENSIC.md](docs/AMR-DUBBO-DOWNSTREAM-FORENSIC.md).
- **NSW public-school eWaste finding:** Department public schools use a separate dedicated EDConnect/eWaste-vendor process rather than simply sending old laptops to Council/Whylandra. The vendor schedules collection, sanitises collected hard drives, provides a sanitisation certificate and provides a Settlement Report. The current vendor and the physical downstream/reuse outcome for Dubbo school laptops remain unverified. Full trace: [docs/NSW-EDUCATION-EWASTE-DUBBO-LAPTOPS.md](docs/NSW-EDUCATION-EWASTE-DUBBO-LAPTOPS.md).
- **Dubbo device-bank finding:** Good Things Australia already has direct digital-inclusion partners in Dubbo. Dubbo Support Center was selected for Digital Sisters, received multiple Good Things/Be Connected grants and is now ACNC-registered; on the published NSW Device Bank rules it appears to meet the organisation-level eligibility requirement to request devices or deliver digital-literacy support, although no Dubbo Device Bank rollout was publicly found. Digital Nebula – Dubbo also received Good Things digital-skills/Get Online Week funding. Full deep dive: [docs/GOOD360-COMPNOW-NATIONAL-DEVICE-BANK-DUBBO.md](docs/GOOD360-COMPNOW-NATIONAL-DEVICE-BANK-DUBBO.md).
- **Closest-town reuse finding:** Dubbo/Wellington/Narromine/Trangie all have recycling access of varying scope, but no verified council program routinely tests, securely wipes, repairs and resells working electronics. Dubbo/Wellington have reuse shops in planning/business-case history; Narromine's 2024 Garage Sale Trail recorded 47 sales/stalls and 4,231 items reused/sold; Trangie's clearest documented electronics route is small-item recycling. See [docs/DUBBO-WELLINGTON-NARROMINE-TRANGIE-REUSE-PROGRAMS.md](docs/DUBBO-WELLINGTON-NARROMINE-TRANGIE-REUSE-PROGRAMS.md).
- **Why reuse stalls:** NSW comparison evidence shows council reuse shops carry staffing, security, dumping, test/tag, product-liability, data, lithium, POS, capital and ongoing compliance costs. A 2026 Cessnock report specifically said minimum two staff, security/patrols, electrical test/tag, POS and dedicated infrastructure would be needed. See [docs/WHY-COUNCIL-ELECTRONICS-REUSE-IS-HARD.md](docs/WHY-COUNCIL-ELECTRONICS-REUSE-IS-HARD.md).
- **PonyUp/SecondBite Dubbo connection:** PonyUp's computers do **not** flow to SecondBite; PonyUp donates 50% of profits. Connecting Community Services is a real SecondBite partner in Dubbo and separately provides free computer/internet access, but no current device-supply link from PonyUp/SecondBite was found. See [docs/PONYUP-SECONDBITE-DUBBO.md](docs/PONYUP-SECONDBITE-DUBBO.md).
- **Fliptech Dubbo feasibility:** Fliptech is a Brookvale-based secure ITAD/e-waste company with hardware buybacks, paid/subsidised data erasure, asset reporting, refurbishment, donation and official resale channels. Historical material shows national expansion ambition and later nationwide clients, but no current franchise/branch program or announced Dubbo site was found. Dubbo is explicitly promoted by NSW Government as an e-waste investment location and has a 125,000+ catchment / 4,500+ businesses, so a **regional collection/ITAD pilot or authorised hub** is more evidence-based than immediately proposing a full processing branch. See [docs/FLIPTECH-DUBBO-BRANCH-FEASIBILITY.md](docs/FLIPTECH-DUBBO-BRANCH-FEASIBILITY.md).

## 2. The business model

Not a recycling plant: **a refurb and resale business with e-waste as the supply line.**

1. **Refurbished working gear** (best margin): laptops, desktops, monitors, phones, tablets, resold online.
2. **Parts**: RAM, SSDs, chargers, screens.
3. **Scrap** (worst margin): only if someone takes it.

Bendigo E-Waste's **mature** operation now accepts a broad range of complete, broken and raw e-waste. The useful benchmark is Joe's published hierarchy: keep devices complete, attempt **restoration/reuse first**, use donor parts and repairer pathways next, and send material to scrap/resource recovery only as a last resort. Dubbo should **not** copy the mature operation's broad intake yet; the pilot should stay selective until each low-value category has a proven downstream exit. See [the early-days deep dive](docs/bendigo-early-days-deep-dive.md).

## 3. The three main legal regimes

1. **EPA licence:** shed-scale volumes are far below the published waste licensing thresholds, but that does **not** settle planning law. NSW EPA has explicitly warned that accepting and sorting unwanted material can constitute a waste facility, and a below-threshold operation can still require development consent.
2. **Council planning (biggest risk):** NSW home-business rules permit low-impact home businesses and Dubbo's LEP caps home-business floor area at **30 m²**. Online retail is expressly contemplated; neighbourhood impact from traffic, waste, noise and visible stock is restricted. **Tue/Sat intake is not automatically prohibited**, but the collection/refurbishment activity could instead be characterised as a goods repair/reuse premises or waste/resource activity. The exact property zoning and Council classification remain **unconfirmed and gating**.
3. **NSW second-hand dealer licence (VERIFIED from NSW Fair Trading, updated Sept 2026):**
   - Needed to buy, sell or exchange prescribed goods, which include **electronic goods** (phones, laptops, tablets etc.).
   - **$692** for a new 1-year licence (2026-27); **$1,574** for 3 years; **$2,456** for 5 years. Renewals from $500 (1 yr). Fees may rise with CPI each 1 July.
   - Can be held by an individual, partnership or corporation. The **licence belongs to the legal entity**, not a brand name. A "fit and proper person" check applies to the person or each director.
   - Must notify Fair Trading of premises used for business **and storage**; display signs; check supplier ID and get an ownership statement; keep electronic records (reported to police within 3 days); **hold regulated goods 14 days and not alter them until the hold ends**.
   - **No licence is needed to "engage in recycling and rubbish collection programs"** (Fair Trading page). Whether a free-collection -> refurb -> resell -> recycle-the-rest model counts is **not confirmed**. Fair Trading (13 32 20) or a formal exemption application is the way to get that answer in writing.

### Licence-sharing idea

Only works if Dubbo eWaste genuinely trades under the **same legal entity that holds a licence**, with the shed added as a notified premises. Branding alone does nothing. It has not been verified whether Avance or any related business holds a second-hand dealer licence. Using an employer's licence would also raise liability, conflict-of-interest and who-profits questions that need written agreement.

### Other checks retained from the earlier research
