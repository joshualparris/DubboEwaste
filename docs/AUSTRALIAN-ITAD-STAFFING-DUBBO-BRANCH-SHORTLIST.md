# Australian ITAD staffing comparison — Dubbo branch shortlist

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)

## Purpose

This file takes the broader Australian IT asset disposition (ITAD) / refurbishment comparison and filters it to companies that have **public evidence making a Dubbo branch, regional hub, secure satellite facility or regional operating presence genuinely plausible**.

The screen is intentionally stricter than "does this company do e-waste?"

To stay on the shortlist, a company needed at least one of:

1. multiple physical Australian ITAD/processing locations;
2. documented branch/facility expansion;
3. explicit service coverage across regional Australia supported by a logistics/facility network;
4. public national-expansion intent plus a business model that could sensibly support a Dubbo pilot;
5. current growth/acquisition activity specifically expanding ITAD capability.

Important limitation:

> **Nobody on this list has publicly announced a Dubbo branch.**

"Shortlisted" means there is public evidence that a regional physical presence is credible enough to investigate. It does **not** mean the company has decided to open in Dubbo.

# 11. What the staffing comparison actually tells us

The most interesting result is not that bigger firms have more branches.

It is that **G1 contradicts the assumption that a company must be large before it can operate multiple ITAD facilities**.

Both Fliptech and G1 sit in LinkedIn's:
> **11–50 employee band**

Yet G1 publicly operates processing facilities in:
- Queensland;
- New South Wales;
- ACT;
- Victoria.

That materially strengthens the proposition that Fliptech could operate:
- a small Dubbo satellite;
- partner-run hub;
- collection/aggregation facility

without first becoming a 200-person company.

The key variable is not employee count alone.

It is:
- contract volume;
- recurring regional clients;
- resale/recovery value;
- transport savings;
- data-security requirements;
- facility utilisation.

# 14. The most important questions to ask all seven

1. Do you currently collect IT assets from Dubbo/Central West NSW?
2. How many current regional NSW clients do you have west of the Blue Mountains?
3. Do you service them using your own fleet, freight partners or local subcontractors?
4. What monthly asset count/tonnage would justify a local secure hub?
5. Would you consider a collection/consolidation facility without full recycling processing?
6. Would you consider an authorised local operating partner?
7. Could secure data sanitisation be performed locally under your systems?
8. Which asset categories carry enough resale value to offset regional transport?
9. How much of your regional intake is typically resold/reused versus recycled?
10. What would you need to see in a Dubbo pilot before considering permanent premises?
