# Dubbo computer repair, reuse and e-waste directory

**Research date:** 6 October 2026  
**Scope:** Dubbo NSW options for broken, unwanted or obsolete laptops/desktops; affordable repair; DIY help; low-income access; reuse; recycling; and small-business ITAD.

## Evidence labels

- **VERIFIED CURRENT** — current primary/authoritative source supports the claim.
- **LIKELY CURRENT** — current business/directory evidence supports operation but an exact service detail is not confirmed.
- **HISTORICAL** — useful context, not proof of today's service.
- **UNVERIFIED** — organisation exists but this specific service/access condition could not be established.
- **PROPOSED/FUTURE** — discussed/planned, not an operating service.

## Executive answer

Dubbo has multiple commercial computer-repair businesses and several free household e-waste routes. The research did **not** identify a verified, inclusive, free/subsidised community computer-hardware repair service or a public hands-on DIY laptop-repair workshop.

The clearest gaps are therefore narrower than "Dubbo has no options":

1. no verified inclusive Repair Café / DIY computer-repair program;
2. no verified means-tested/subsidised hardware-repair labour program;
3. no obvious universal walk-in pathway that promises to take a usable/repairable household computer, sanitise it, refurbish it and redistribute/remarket it before recycling;
4. no publicly described computer reuse-screening step at Whylandra before e-waste recycling;
5. no clearly advertised local small-batch ITAD package combining collection, serialised sanitisation evidence, remarketing and downstream reporting.

Dubbo **does** have local repair businesses, local refurbished-device supply, No Interest Loans for eligible computer purchases, free household e-waste recycling, and statewide/national commercial ITAD providers.

There is **not** enough local evidence to claim that large numbers of repairable computers are currently being prematurely recycled. The evidence supports a plausible reuse-leakage risk, not a quantified outcome.

## 5. Low-income and digital-inclusion pathways

### HopeStreet Dubbo / No Interest Loans

**VERIFIED CURRENT:** BaptistCare HopeStreet Dubbo, **111 Darling Street**, **02 5804 7350**, can connect eligible people with **No Interest Loans (NILS)** for essential technology such as computers/laptops.

Good Shepherd's current NILS eligibility includes people who:
- have a Health Care Card or Pension Card; or
- earn under the published household thresholds; or
- experienced family/domestic violence in the past ten years;
and who meet address/repayment-capacity requirements.

Technology can be financed within the program limit, with no interest, fees or charges.

This is a **replacement/purchase pathway**, not a free repair scheme.

Sources:
- https://baptistcare.org.au/hopestreet/locations/dubbo/
- https://goodshep.org.au/services/nils/

### NSW / National Device Bank

The NSW Government launched a 12-month Device Bank pilot in May 2026 to refurbish surplus government laptops and distribute them through community organisations to people facing digital-access barriers, including regional communities.

The National Device Bank works through organisations rather than direct individual applications. Its current access page invites organisational expressions of interest and says devices are not presently available.

Sources:
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/nsw-device-bank-to-help-people-and-communities-get-online
- https://nationaldevicebank.org.au/access-devices/

No universal Dubbo charity program was verified that currently provides free laptop hardware repair or guarantees a donated/refurbished computer to any low-income resident.

## 6. Working or repairable unwanted computers

A reuse-first household sequence is:

1. keep the device in use if it still meets a real need;
2. securely remove personal data, then give/sell directly where appropriate;
3. use an eligible trade-in if offered;
4. ask a local refurbisher/reseller whether it wants the exact model;
5. recycle only when genuinely end-of-life.

**Orana Business Solutions** demonstrably refurbishes pre-loved devices, and **Leading Edge** advertises refurbished computers. That proves Dubbo has refurbished-device supply. It does **not** establish a universal public donation intake.

No current service was found that publicly promises all of: walk-in household computer donation, secure sanitisation, refurbishment, local redistribution/remarketing, and transparent recycling of failures.

## 7. Whylandra e-waste

**VERIFIED CURRENT:** Whylandra Waste and Recycling Centre accepts clean, separated domestic e-waste free. Its embedded-battery trial includes whole phones, tablets and laptops; residents are told not to remove embedded batteries. Those devices are sent for specialised recycling to recover materials such as lithium, cobalt and nickel.

Current site rules also state **unauthorised scavenging is strictly prohibited**.

That means residents cannot simply salvage usable computers from the e-waste area.

No current public source reviewed says Whylandra performs computer-specific reuse/refurbishment triage before recycling.

The free household pathway should not be assumed to cover business IT disposal at scale.

Source:
https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/Households-Residents/Rubbish-Recycling-and-Sustainability/whylandra-waste-recycling-centre

## 9. Commercial ITAD for Dubbo organisations

Dubbo does not appear to have a clearly advertised local specialist ITAD operator publishing an enterprise package of chain of custody, serialised sanitisation evidence, remarketing and downstream reporting.

National providers can service NSW/regional clients subject to quote.

### Providers worth quoting

- **PonyUp for Good:** corporate collections, data sanitisation through partners, serialised registers on request, reuse/remarketing and recycling fallback.
  https://www.ponyupforgood.com/partners-data-security
- **Greenbox:** collection, tracking, sanitisation, remarketing and recycling; secure NSW/ACT facilities and broader service capability.
  https://www.greenbox.com.au/services/asset-recovery/
- **ITC Asset Management:** says it services all NSW including regional locations, with NIST 800-88-aligned/Blancco sanitisation, certificates, chain of custody and refurbishment/resale.
  https://www.itcassetmanagement.com.au/locations/nsw/

Practical rule:
- ~5 devices: local sale/refurb assessment/trade-in or ask an ITAD provider whether value covers logistics.
- ~20 devices: obtain at least two ITAD quotes.
- ~100 devices: formal serialised chain-of-custody, sanitisation evidence, reuse/remarketing and final disposition reporting should be expected.

NIST SP 800-88 **Rev.2** superseded Rev.1 in September 2025. Media-aware sanitisation, validation and evidence matter more than simply naming a wipe utility.

- https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final
- https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/asds-cyber-security-frameworks/ism/cyber-security-guidelines/guidelines-for-media

## 12. Real-world scenarios

### A — low-income parent, laptop stopped charging
Call a commercial repairer and ask the diagnostic/quote fee before travelling. No verified subsidised hardware repair was found. If repair is uneconomic and replacement is essential, HopeStreet can assess NILS eligibility.

### B — pensioner wants an SSD upgrade and explanation
CBM, Right Click Go, Custom Computer Creations and CR&T have current evidence supporting upgrade/SSD-memory related help. Library Tech Help can build confidence but cannot perform the repair.

### C — woman wants to learn to repair her own laptop
**No verified Dubbo service currently meets that exact need.** This is the strongest case for an inclusive Repair Café-style pilot.

### D — teenager with suspected gaming-PC PSU failure
Use commercial diagnosis before buying parts. No verified youth subsidised repair/DIY hardware workshop was found.

### E — family has two working laptops and one broken
Securely erase the working units and prioritise direct reuse/trade-in/refurbisher assessment. Have the broken unit assessed if economically sensible. Recycle only the genuinely end-of-life device.

### F — business replaces 25 Windows laptops
Get at least two formal ITAD quotes. Require chain of custody, media-appropriate sanitisation, serialised evidence, failed-wipe handling, reuse/remarketing terms, downstream details and final reporting.

## 14. Premature recycling: what the evidence actually supports

No local device-condition waste audit was found.

What is established:
- Whylandra provides free household e-waste recycling and sends embedded-battery devices to specialised recycling;
- public salvage is prohibited;
- no public computer reuse triage is described;
- retailer channels emphasise recycling, although some separate trade-in/reuse pathways exist;
- Council policy broadly prefers avoidance/reuse before recycling.

So there is a **plausible leakage risk** if a resident chooses the easiest disposal route for a usable machine. There is no evidence-based percentage or count.

A pilot should measure it.

## 15. Three realistic improvement models

### Model 1 — inclusive monthly Tech Repair & Reuse Café
Best first test:
- 6–8 booked devices;
- 30–45 minute owner-assisted sessions;
- laptops/desktops;
- owner supplies parts;
- no repair guarantee;
- no exposed mains-PSU work;
- no swollen/damaged battery work;
- explicit data/backup consent;
- commercial referral for jobs beyond scope;
- existing recycler for genuine end-of-life devices.

### Model 2 — triage/referral desk with no device custody
15-minute assessment; identify likely issue; direct to simple DIY, commercial repair, or recycling; help with safe data backup/wipe; keep nothing overnight.

This is lower risk and tests demand.

### Model 3 — reuse intake/community refurbishment hub
Later stage only: accept donated household/business PCs, sanitise, repair/upgrade, redistribute/resell, harvest parts and recycle failures.

This has materially greater data-custody, battery, insurance, consumer-law, premises, second-hand dealing and downstream obligations.

## 16. Recommended first pilot

Run **one pre-booked inclusive diagnosis and owner-assisted repair session** before creating a permanent organisation.

Boundary:
- 6–8 bookings;
- 30 minutes;
- laptops/desktops only;
- no unattended drop-off or overnight storage;
- no damaged/swollen batteries;
- no board-level/mains electrical work;
- owner stays with device;
- written consent for reboot/reset/software changes;
- owner supplies replacement parts;
- commercial referral list;
- verified e-waste destination.

Measure:
- fault type;
- fixed / part-needed / commercial referral / recycle;
- time used;
- estimated avoided replacement;
- whether owner otherwise would have replaced/recycled;
- demand for follow-up.

## Research limitations

This is a web-based current-service investigation, not telephone mystery-shopping. Where a fee, acceptance rule or demographic access condition is unpublished, it is marked unverified rather than guessed.

Opening hours and service rules can change. Negative findings such as "no Repair Café found" mean no current publicly discoverable service was identified through the sources reviewed, not that no informal volunteer repair activity exists.

No local device-condition waste audit was found, so this report does not quantify how many reusable computers enter recycling.

Eligibility for financial assistance, disability support, school programs and charity services is case-specific.

This research is informational and is not legal, electrical, insurance or data-security advice.
