# 03 — Tax and accounting treatment of second-hand ITAD/refurb stock

**Questions answered:** 9–12  
**Research date:** 6 October 2026  
**Boundary:** tax research, not tax advice. The actual treatment depends on entity, GST registration, acquisition facts and whether a transfer is genuinely a gift.

## 9. GST Division 66 and second-hand goods

The GST Act contains a specific second-hand-goods regime.

Division 66 can allow a GST-registered entity to claim an input tax credit on eligible second-hand goods bought from a supplier who did not make a taxable supply, where the goods were acquired for sale or exchange in the ordinary course of business.

Primary sources:
- A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999, Division 66:
  https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00446/latest
- ATO GSTD 2013/2:
  https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=%22GSD%2FGSTD20132%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001%22

### Why this matters to ITAD

A refurbisher buying a used laptop from a private/unregistered seller is not in the same GST position as buying new stock from a GST-registered wholesaler.

**Do not claim a Division 66 credit merely because an item is second-hand.** Eligibility, timing and records matter.

### Dismantling into parts

The GST law expressly has a **global accounting method** for eligible second-hand goods acquired and then divided for re-supply.

This matters for:

> one laptop acquired as a unit → RAM + screen + keyboard + board + chassis sold separately.

The ATO's explanation confirms the global method exists to deal with second-hand goods divided for re-supply.

That is one of the strongest reasons to establish the bookkeeping method with an accountant before significant parts harvesting.

## 10. Free/donated stock and personal stock introduced to business

### Personally owned item moved into trading stock

ITAA 1997 **s70-30** applies when you start holding as trading stock something you already owned.

The law treats you as if you sold and immediately reacquired it for an elected amount:

- its cost; or
- its market value just before it became trading stock.

The election can have CGT consequences depending on the choice/facts.

Primary source:
https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A05138/latest
(search s70-30)

### Item previously acquired for no consideration

s70-30(4) contains specific cost rules for property last acquired for no consideration. In the residual “other” case, statutory cost can be **nil**.

That means “someone gave it to me free, therefore its tax cost is today's eBay value” is not a safe assumption.

### Practical rule

For every non-purchased device retain:

- date;
- source category;
- transfer/title evidence;
- cash consideration ($0 if genuinely free);
- intended business route;
- valuation evidence if a tax/accounting treatment depends on market value;
- date it became trading stock.

Ask the accountant how this should feed the ledger for the actual entity.

## 11. Trading stock, parts harvesting and scrap revenue

ITAA 1997 defines trading stock broadly as things produced, manufactured or acquired and held for manufacture, sale or exchange in ordinary business.

For Dubbo ITAD, likely trading-stock categories can include:

- whole devices held for resale;
- harvested parts held for sale/use in customer repair where title passes;
- resale accessories;
- potentially sorted commodity stock held for sale.

Primary law:
https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A05138/latest
(Division 70)

### Important separation

Use distinct ledger categories for:

1. whole-device inventory;
2. harvested parts inventory;
3. consumables;
4. capital tools;
5. recycling/scrap sale proceeds;
6. service labour.

A physical laptop can change route, but the ledger should preserve the parent asset and the value/cost pathway.

### Private withdrawal of stock

ATO guidance says trading stock taken for private use must be accounted for and records kept. For a sole trader where the same person continues to own the item, accepted treatment can use cost under the relevant conditions.

Source:
https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf?DocId=PSR%2FGA20043%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001

## 12. Donating refurbished devices

A business giving devices away is not automatically entitled to a charitable deduction.

The core rules in ITAA 1997 s30-15 require the recipient to be an eligible deductible gift recipient (DGR) and specify how deductible property/trading-stock gifts are valued.

For an item of trading stock disposed of outside the ordinary course of business, the deduction rules can use market value, subject to the statutory conditions.

Primary source:
https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A05138/latest
(s30-15)

There is also an income-side rule: disposal of trading stock outside the ordinary course of business can bring **market value into assessable income** under s70-90.

That means:

> “Donate a $500 laptop = automatically get a $500 tax benefit”

is an unsafe simplification.

The deduction and assessable-income consequences must be considered together.

## Recommended accounting design

AssetFlow/accounting export should eventually capture:

- acquisition type: PURCHASE / FREE_TRANSFER / PERSONAL_TO_BUSINESS / CUSTOMER_OWNED;
- consideration paid;
- GST status of source if relevant;
- tax invoice available Y/N;
- second-hand Division 66 candidate Y/N;
- parent asset → harvested parts;
- inventory cost basis;
- sale proceeds;
- scrap proceeds;
- charitable recipient + ABN/DGR check;
- donation market-value evidence;
- private withdrawal;
- downstream fee.

Do **not** make AssetFlow calculate tax liability. Export evidence to accounting software/accountant.

## Accountant question pack

Before GST registration or meaningful stock volumes, ask:

1. For this entity, when can Division 66 credits be claimed and what records are required?
2. Should global accounting be used for devices split into parts?
3. How should genuinely free devices acquired for resale be entered?
4. How should personally owned devices moved into trading stock be valued under s70-30?
5. How should devices harvested for parts be re-costed?
6. How should scrap rebates/proceeds be classified?
7. How should charitable/DGR device gifts be accounted for under ss30-15 and 70-90?
8. What stocktake method/year-end valuation should be used?
9. What is the cleanest separation between Parris Tech Services, Dubbo ITAD and any non-commercial Repair Café?
