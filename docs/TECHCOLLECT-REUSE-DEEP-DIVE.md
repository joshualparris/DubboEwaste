# TechCollect deep dive — what happens to reusable e-waste?

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)  
**Question:** Does TechCollect test, refurbish or resell working electronics, and how does reuse fit into the ANZRP/TechCollect system?

## Executive finding

The most important distinction is:

> **TechCollect's public drop-off recycling stream is not a reuse/refurbishment stream. Once equipment is handed to TechCollect for recycling, TechCollect says it is recycled in Australia and is not sold as second-hand equipment for reuse.**

TechCollect explicitly tells people that if a TV/computer is still in good working order and current enough to remain useful in Australia, they should **give it to family, friends or a charity instead of bringing it to TechCollect**.

At the same time, TechCollect's parent/manager, the **Australia and New Zealand Recycling Platform (ANZRP)**, now supports reuse through **separate upstream partnerships** including:

- PonyUp for Good
- The Reconnect Project
- The Laptop Initiative

Those pathways triage, repair, refurbish, redeploy, donate or resell viable devices **before** unreusable equipment is handed to ANZRP/TechCollect for material recycling.

So there are effectively two different paths:

**Reuse path:** usable equipment → reuse/refurbishment partner → repair/refurbish → redeploy/resell/donate → recycling only for failures

**TechCollect recycling path:** equipment surrendered for recycling → approved recycler → dismantling/shredding/sorting → commodity/material recovery

That distinction is extremely relevant to Dubbo eWaste.

---

## 1. What TechCollect actually is

TechCollect is the public-facing recycling program managed by **ANZRP**, an approved co-regulatory arrangement under the Australian Government's **National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme (NTCRS)**.

It provides households and small businesses with free recycling for in-scope products including:

- desktop computers
- laptops
- tablets/notebooks/palmtops
- monitors
- computer parts and peripherals
- printers/faxes/scanners/multifunction devices
- televisions

The service is funded by manufacturers/importers liable under the NTCRS.

TechCollect is primarily designed to meet a **recycling obligation**, not an obligation to maximise whole-device reuse.

---

## 2. TechCollect's reuse policy is unusually explicit

TechCollect's current Safety & Environment page says all computers, computer accessories and TVs collected by TechCollect are **recycled within Australia and not sold as second-hand products for reuse**.

It explains the reason in the context of preventing inappropriate export of ostensibly "working" electronics that may quickly become e-waste overseas.

TechCollect's advice is:

- if a product is still in good working order;
- and current enough to be useful in Australia;
- give it to a family member, friend or charity **before** using TechCollect.

TechCollect says it seeks to recycle products that are **truly end-of-life**.

### Practical meaning

If someone carries a perfectly usable laptop into a normal TechCollect collection point and surrenders it as e-waste, TechCollect's published process does **not** describe:

- powering it on;
- benchmarking its condition;
- checking whether Windows still boots;
- assessing resale value;
- wiping it for refurbishment;
- listing it for sale;
- donating it to a reuse charity.

Its published route is **recycling**.

This means the decision about reuse is intended to happen **before the TechCollect drop-off**, not after.

---

## 3. What happens after something enters TechCollect

ANZRP's 2024–25 annual report states that **all e-waste collected through TechCollect undergoes first-stage recycling in Australia**.

First-stage recycling includes:

- dismantling
- shredding
- sorting

into:

- metals
- plastics
- leaded/non-leaded glass
- batteries
- printed circuit boards
- cartridges/toner
- other material fractions

Each TechCollect delivery receives a **unique identification number** so it can be tracked:

**collection site → logistics provider → recycler**

At the recycler, loads are weighed and sorted into NTCRS in-scope and out-of-scope material.

For NSW in 2024–25, ANZRP lists:

- **ACE Recycling Group**
- **Sircel**

as its recycling partners.

### 2024–25 material outcomes

ANZRP reported:

- **18,853 tonnes** of in-scope e-waste recycled
- **17,237 tonnes** of material recovered
- **91.43% material recovery rate**

Recovered material included approximately:

- metals — **64.2%**
- plastics — **17.8%**
- circuit boards — **4.6%**
- non-leaded glass — **1.1%**
- leaded glass — **0.6%**
- other — **11.7%**

This is material recovery, not whole-device reuse.

---

## 4. TechCollect does not wipe your data for you

TechCollect's current data guidance is also explicit:

> **Data deletion is the owner's responsibility before drop-off.**

TechCollect says its recycling process **does not include deletion of data** and it cannot guarantee the safety of data that was not erased before equipment was surrendered.

It says that once products are dropped off, they cannot later be retrieved because they are dismantled and broken down for recycling.

### Implication

A TechCollect drop-off should not be confused with:

- secure IT asset disposition;
- certified data erasure;
- refurbishment;
- a second-hand recovery program.

For a business client wanting serialised sanitisation records, those steps need to happen before TechCollect receives the device, or through a separate ITAD provider.

---

## 5. "Reuse" in TechCollect language often means materials, not devices

Some TechCollect education pages use language such as "re-use as much of our e-waste as possible to make new things."

That wording refers to **recovered material being reused in new manufacturing**, not necessarily reuse of the original computer/TV.

Examples from TechCollect's downstream process include:

- metals becoming new metal products;
- television/monitor glass becoming concrete/building products;
- toner/ink being incorporated into asphalt;
- plastic becoming new plastic products;
- laptop battery fractions entering new material streams.

This is **materials circularity**.

It is different from:

**device circularity:** keep the laptop intact → repair it → sell/give it to another user.

For Dubbo eWaste research, these two concepts should not be mixed.

---

## 6. ANZRP now has a separate reuse strategy

ANZRP's current e-stewardship page has a dedicated **Product Reuse** section.

It describes reuse through partnerships that operate **ahead of recycling**.

This is important because ANZRP appears to recognise that ordinary TechCollect collection does not itself provide a reuse-first triage layer.

### PonyUp for Good

PonyUp for Good provides business IT asset services.

Its workflow includes:

- collecting retired business technology;
- data erasure;
- debadging;
- assessing equipment;
- refurbishing viable products;
- remarketing them for reuse;
- directing only non-reusable equipment to ANZRP for recycling.

ANZRP's 2024–25 annual report says PonyUp:
- erases data;
- debadges;
- refurbishes;
- remarkets usable technology for reuse;
- sends equipment that **cannot be reused** to ANZRP for recycling.

ANZRP provides logistics and recycling certificates for those non-reusable devices.

This is almost exactly the **reuse-first / recycle-last** model being considered for Dubbo eWaste.

### Key structural point

PonyUp performs the reuse triage **before** the TechCollect/ANZRP material-recycling step.

It is not evidence that a laptop already deposited in a TechCollect cage is pulled back out for refurbishment.

---

## 7. The Reconnect Project

ANZRP also affiliates with **The Reconnect Project**.

Its model is strongly reuse-focused:

- phones, tablets and laptops are donated;
- technicians/volunteers assess them;
- viable devices are repaired/refurbished;
- parts may be harvested;
- repaired devices are sold affordably or distributed through support agencies;
- unrepairable products are recycled.

ANZRP describes this as extending the useful life of devices and supporting digital inclusion.

Again:

> reuse occurs through a dedicated reuse organisation, not by treating a standard TechCollect recycling cage as a source of resale inventory.

---

## 8. The Laptop Initiative

ANZRP supports **The Laptop Initiative**, founded in 2024.

It invites businesses to donate **functioning laptops**, which are redistributed to groups including:

- refugees and asylum seekers;
- disadvantaged young people;
- women escaping domestic violence.

ANZRP explicitly contrasts this with its core NTCRS work, saying its normal scheme operations focus on recovery/recycling of end-of-life electronics while it separately seeks opportunities to extend product life through reuse.

---

## 9. Reuse was not originally an NTCRS obligation

This is a crucial policy point.

ANZRP itself says:

> reuse obligations have **never formed part of ANZRP's remit under the regulations governing the scheme**.

The current NTCRS has targets for:

- collection/access;
- tonnes recycled;
- material recovery;
- recycling standards.

It does **not** currently impose a whole-device reuse target comparable to the 90% material recovery target.

ANZRP says it has nevertheless been exploring ways to maximise reuse through voluntary/beyond-compliance partnerships.

### Why this matters

The scheme's incentives are fundamentally designed around **recycling measured by weight**.

That means a working 3 kg laptop surrendered into the scheme contributes to recycling tonnage/material recovery when dismantled.

There is no equivalent statutory NTCRS reuse target saying:

- "test every device";
- "save every working laptop";
- "resell X%";
- "repair before recycling."

That helps explain why TechCollect explicitly asks users to divert good equipment to reuse **before** it reaches the recycling scheme.

---

## 10. TechCollect's anti-export reasoning

TechCollect gives a specific reason for not selling collected devices as second-hand equipment.

It warns that electronic products exported as "working" second-hand goods may:

- actually be too old or poor quality to remain useful;
- rapidly become e-waste in the destination country;
- end up processed under unsafe environmental and labour conditions.

Its response is conservative:

**everything TechCollect collects is first-stage recycled in Australia rather than sold out of the collection stream as used equipment.**

This reduces the risk of sham reuse/export.

But it also means a genuinely usable Australian laptop can lose its whole-device resale value if it is mistakenly deposited as e-waste.

## 11. TechCollect versus a true reuse-first operator

| Step | Standard TechCollect drop-off | Reuse-first operator such as PonyUp / proposed Dubbo eWaste |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | Accept NTCRS-covered e-waste | Accept selectively |
| Test whether device works | Not described as standard TechCollect step | Yes |
| Assess resale value | No published TechCollect process | Yes |
| Data sanitisation | Owner responsibility | Managed/documented |
| Repair | Not TechCollect's normal collection path | Yes |
| Refurbish | Not normal TechCollect path | Yes |
| Resell/redeploy | TechCollect says collected products are not sold second-hand | Yes |
| Donate usable device | User encouraged to do this **before** TechCollect | Yes |
| Harvest reusable parts | Recycling may separate components/materials, but not equivalent to refurbishment | Yes, where worthwhile |
| Material recycling | Core purpose | Final fallback |

## 12. The most important opportunity for Dubbo eWaste

This creates a potentially valuable gap in the local chain:

**Owner/business**
→ **Dubbo eWaste reuse triage**
→ working/economic device: wipe → test → repair/refurbish → resell/donate/redeploy  
→ uneconomic device: dismantle useful parts if appropriate  
→ actual end-of-life remainder: AMR / TechCollect / another verified recycler

versus:

**Owner**
→ ordinary recycling collection
→ dismantling/shredding/material recovery

The first path captures **whole-device value before commodity value**.

A functional business laptop can have hundreds of dollars of use/resale value while its raw metal/plastic/glass content is worth much less.

This does not make TechCollect "bad". TechCollect is doing the job its scheme was designed and funded to do: safe, traceable, high-recovery recycling of **end-of-life** products.

The loss occurs when equipment that is **not actually end-of-life** enters an end-of-life recycling stream.

## 13. Could Dubbo eWaste work alongside TechCollect?

Potentially, but the relationship needs to be structured carefully.

A strong model would be:

1. customer/business offers equipment to Dubbo eWaste;
2. ownership/authority is documented;
3. Dubbo eWaste triages for reuse;
4. reusable assets are sanitised, refurbished and resold/redeployed;
5. only confirmed end-of-life material is passed to a compliant recycler.

### What Dubbo eWaste should NOT do without agreement
- remove products after customers have already surrendered them into an official TechCollect collection cage/site;
- assume TechCollect collection partners are permitted to divert NTCRS material to resale;
- represent refurbished equipment as having passed through TechCollect if it did not;
- count reused devices as TechCollect/NTCRS recycling tonnage.

### Potential partnership concept

The more natural ANZRP comparison is **PonyUp**, not TechCollect itself:

**reuse organisation first → ANZRP recycling for the failures.**

That could make ANZRP/TechCollect a potential **downstream recycling partner**, while Dubbo eWaste supplies the upstream reuse triage.

Whether ANZRP would contract with a small regional reuse operator would need direct discussion.

---

## 14. Questions worth asking ANZRP/TechCollect

Call TechCollect / ANZRP and ask:

1. "If a perfectly functional laptop is dropped into a TechCollect collection site, is it ever tested for reuse, or is every collected device committed to first-stage recycling?"
2. "Your website says collected devices are not sold second-hand. Does that policy apply to 100% of normal TechCollect public drop-offs?"
3. "Can collection partners perform reuse triage **before** an item is formally accepted into TechCollect?"
4. "Do you have a formal pathway for councils/businesses to divert suitable devices to PonyUp, The Reconnect Project or The Laptop Initiative before recycling?"
5. "Could a regional refurbisher act as an upstream reuse partner and send only unreusable NTCRS-covered equipment to ANZRP?"
6. "How do you define the point at which ownership/custody enters the TechCollect scheme?"
7. "Would reuse-diverted devices simply fall outside NTCRS reported recycling tonnage?"
8. "Do ACE or Sircel perform any whole-device reuse triage on TechCollect loads, or are those loads contractually committed to material recycling?"
9. "Are there contractual rules preventing reuse/resale once a device is collected under NTCRS?"
10. "Is ANZRP actively seeking additional regional reuse partnerships during 2026?"

These answers would tell us whether Dubbo eWaste could sit formally in front of TechCollect rather than just using it as an independent downstream outlet.

## 16. Bottom line for Dubbo eWaste

**TechCollect is a recycling back end, not the model to copy for reuse triage.**

The stronger comparison is:

> **PonyUp for Good → reuse first → ANZRP/TechCollect-style recycling for what cannot be reused.**

That is remarkably close to the proposed Dubbo eWaste value proposition.

The key local opportunity is to stop viable electronics **before** they enter a system that correctly assumes they are end-of-life.
