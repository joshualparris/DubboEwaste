# NSW public-school eWaste — what happens to old Dubbo education laptops?

**Research date:** 2 October 2026  
**Scope:** NSW Department of Education public schools in Dubbo/Orana, not private or independent schools.  
**Status:** detailed public-source trace. The current contracted vendor and physical downstream facility for Dubbo-collected school laptops remain unpublished.

**2 October 2026 qualification:** the operational guide's school-wiping wording differs from the current policy's pre-disposal wiping requirement. Confirm the applicable preparation steps with Education's Digital Field/EDConnect team; the guide is not authority to override policy or release unwiped equipment. A later open of the guide URL redirected to Technology for Learning, so its current location/applicability also needs confirmation. See the [prospect research](DUBBO-DEVICE-DISPOSAL-PROSPECTS.md) and evidence `EVD-DISPOSAL-023`–`024` in [data/evidence.yaml](../data/evidence.yaml).

---

## Executive answer

Old laptops from NSW public schools in Dubbo do **not** simply go into the Dubbo Council/Whylandra/AMR household e-waste stream.

The Department has a **separate, dedicated eWaste disposal process** for school digital assets.

The current public workflow is:

**school identifies equipment for disposal → correct approvals → eWaste PowerApp/EDConnect request → vendor creates a cost-efficient regional collection schedule → vendor collects from the school → vendor sanitises all collected hard drives → vendor issues a data-sanitisation certificate → vendor issues a Settlement Report → asset outcome after that is not publicly disclosed in the school guide**

Two important things follow:

1. **The operational guide assigns final hard-drive sanitisation and evidence to the vendor, while saying schools need not perform hard-drive wiping/destruction.** The current policy also requires pre-disposal wiping. The applicable school preparation steps therefore need Department confirmation; do not infer permission to release unwiped equipment.
2. **The public documents do not prove that every collected laptop is destroyed.** NSW Government's current end-user-device contract allows reuse by sale/donation, reinsertion into supply chains, or recycling. But no public source currently proves which outcome is applied to Dubbo public-school laptops under the dedicated Education contract.

So the most important missing link is:

> **Who is the current NSW Education eWaste vendor, where do Dubbo school laptops physically go, and what percentage are reused/remarketed versus recycled or destroyed?**

# 1. Current Department policy

The NSW Department of Education's **Technology in schools procedures** were updated on **18 June 2026**.

Under section 3.2, principals must:

- recycle devices responsibly
- keep records of disposed/deactivated equipment
- ensure memory/data is correctly wiped.

The policy then says:

> The department has a dedicated eWaste contract in place to manage the secure disposal of department digital assets.

Schools access that service through **EDConnect** to request an eWaste collection.

The same policy also tells schools to arrange redistribution of **unused department-allocated devices for students in need** by contacting the Department's ICT refresh team.

That establishes two distinct pathways:

### Before disposal
If a Department-allocated device is still useful and unused, it can be **redistributed within the education system**.

### Once being disposed
The device goes into the Department's dedicated eWaste/secure-disposal process.

Source:  
https://education.nsw.gov.au/policy-library/policies/pd-2024-0481-01

# 2. The eWaste PowerApp / EDConnect workflow

The Department's current **eWaste Application User Guide** gives the most detailed public description of what the school and vendor actually do.

The guide says the school/support office must:

1. take stock of the items to be eWasted
2. complete the eWaste form
3. liaise with the vendor about collection date
4. move the equipment to a central accessible location
5. provide vendor access on collection day
6. sign off once everything is collected
7. keep the vendor's **Settlement Report**.

The guide then explicitly states:

> **Wiping / Destruction of Hard Drives is no longer required**

for the school/support office.

The vendor is required to:

1. receive the collection request
2. **plan a cost-efficient collection schedule**
3. communicate with the school
4. collect the eWaste
5. **data sanitise all hard drives collected**
6. provide a **Certificate of data sanitisation**
7. provide a **Settlement Report**.

The form asks schools to estimate quantities so that the correct-size **team and truck** can be arranged.

That looks like a centrally managed regional asset-disposition/logistics service, not an ad-hoc trip to a local Council tip.

Source:  
https://t4l.schools.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/doe/sws/schools/t/t4l/pdfs/DOE25-eWasteApplicationInstructions.pdf

Program contact shown in the public guide:

cor0835r8406.schoolsinfra@det.nsw.edu.au

# 3. Apparent wording tension: school wipe vs vendor wipe

There is a genuine public-document tension:

### Technology in schools policy
Says configuration and storage must be securely wiped prior to disposal and principals must ensure memory/data is correctly wiped.

### eWaste Application User Guide
Says:

> Wiping / Destruction of Hard Drives is no longer required

for the school, while the **vendor** must sanitise all collected hard drives and provide a certificate.

The guide describes vendor sanitisation, but the public wording does not resolve which preparation steps the school must complete under the current policy. Confirm these with Education's Digital Field/EDConnect team before collection. The school remains accountable for authorised decommissioning and Department configuration/data handling.

Do **not** interpret the operational guide as meaning schools can casually hand untracked equipment to anyone.

The dedicated contract and settlement/certification workflow is what provides the controlled disposal pathway.

# 4. 2025 backlog proves the service is actively used

A June 2025 Technology for Learning update told schools that:

- many schools were confused about available eWaste collection
- storerooms had filled with old/outdated computer equipment
- the Department had formalised the process
- there was a **backlog of schools requesting eWaste collection**
- the Department was working with **the vendor** to clear the backlog.

This confirms:
- the dedicated service is not merely a policy placeholder
- there is meaningful statewide volume
- collections are being managed centrally through a vendor.

The update still did **not** name the vendor.

Source:  
https://education.nsw.gov.au/teaching-and-learning/technology-for-learning/news-t4l/2025/issue122

# 5. What happens after sanitisation?

This is the main unresolved step.

The school eWaste guide stops after:

**collection → sanitisation certificate → settlement report**

It does not publish:
- testing/grading
- resale
- donation
- parts harvesting
- destruction
- material-recycling destination
- residual-value credit
- percentage reused
- percentage recycled.

Therefore it is incorrect to say either:

> “Dubbo school laptops are shredded”

or:

> “Dubbo school laptops are all refurbished and resold.”

Neither has been proven publicly.

# 6. NSW Government contract 9826 shows the permitted disposal options

The current **ICT End User Devices and Services Contract 9826** runs:

**1 October 2023 – 30 September 2028**

and was updated on 23 September 2026.

It is the mandatory whole-of-government end-user-device contract.

Its disposal-service scope includes:

- validating whether equipment is operational/non-operational
- recovery from customer sites
- confirming sanitisation
- updating asset registers
- environmentally appropriate disposal.

Critically, the contract says disposal methods may include:

- **reuse through donation or sale**
- **direct recovery and reinsertion into a supplier's supply chain**
- **recycling through a proven third-party channel**
- environmental certification for recycled items.

It also says devices cannot be sent offshore for service/disposal without sanitisation first.

Source:  
https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/contracts/ict-end-user-devices-and-services

## Important qualification

The Department of Education says it has a **dedicated eWaste contract**.

Public sources do **not** currently prove:
- that the school eWaste arrangement is an order under Contract 9826
- or that its dedicated vendor is one of the standalone disposal suppliers on 9826.

Therefore 9826 proves the NSW Government's accepted ITAD/disposal methods, but not the exact Education downstream outcome.

# 9. Could Sims or WorkVentures be the vendor?

Both are also approved standalone-service suppliers on Contract 9826.

### Sims Lifecycle Services
Has large-scale ITAD/recycling capability and appears elsewhere in NSW Government/regional e-waste arrangements.

### WorkVentures
Has secure refurbishment/social-reuse capability and now operates the NSW Device Bank pilot.

But again:

**no public source found identifies either as the current EDConnect schools eWaste vendor.**

The vendor remains unknown.

# 10. NSW Device Bank — similar process, separate program

The NSW Device Bank launched in 2026 to take surplus NSW Government laptops and:

- securely sanitise them
- test/grade/refurbish suitable devices
- redistribute devices to people facing digital exclusion
- appropriately dispose of non-viable equipment.

WorkVentures is the delivery partner for the pilot.

The public tender disclosed approximately:

- **2,800 laptops already in storage** at the start
- **2,500 additional laptops expected during the 12-month term**
- mostly HP EliteBook x360 1030 G3/G4
- some G7/G8
- most expected to be five years old or older.

Source:  
https://buy.nsw.gov.au/prcOpportunity/9A339589-49D9-448D-9A06E56260BBFA42

## Critical gap

No public evidence found that normal NSW public-school devices booked through the **school EDConnect eWaste contract** are routed to the NSW Device Bank.

These may be separate government asset streams.

This is a highly worthwhile question to ask Education.

# 11. What probably happens physically to a Dubbo school collection?

Only the first part can be stated with confidence.

### Verified

A Dubbo public school:

1. inventories the devices
2. obtains the required approvals
3. submits the eWaste application
4. gives estimated quantities
5. the vendor plans a **cost-efficient collection schedule**
6. the vendor arranges a suitably sized collection team/truck
7. vendor collects from the school
8. vendor sanitises all hard drives
9. vendor issues certification and settlement report.

### Strong operational inference

Because the vendor explicitly plans cost-efficient schedules and teams/trucks, regional schools are likely **batched into regional collection runs** rather than each school selecting its own local Dubbo recycler.

### Not verified

The public record does not identify:
- regional consolidation depot
- truck operator
- Dubbo/Orange/Sydney transfer point
- first processing warehouse
- testing/refurbishment facility
- recycler
- final commodity destinations.

Do not draw a line from a Dubbo school to AMR/Whylandra/Matthews unless Education or the vendor confirms it.

# 12. Does a school principal have authority to give Dubbo eWaste the old laptops?

For Department digital assets going through the official disposal process, the answer should be treated as **no, not casually**.

The Department has:
- asset/equipment records
- disposal approval requirements
- a dedicated eWaste contract
- an EDConnect/PowerApp collection process
- controlled data sanitisation/certification.

A local school should not simply hand Department devices to an outside recycler/refurbisher outside that framework.

Therefore the Phase 0 pitch to a NSW public school should **not** be:

> “Give Dubbo eWaste your old laptops instead.”

The future opportunity is more likely:

- become an approved/contracted service provider
- become a subcontractor to the existing vendor
- propose a Department-approved regional reuse pilot
- or integrate with an approved Device Bank/reuse pathway.

# 13. What this means for Dubbo eWaste

The schools finding changes the supply strategy.

## Not a Phase 0 free-stock source

NSW public schools are not a simple donation source.

## But a high-value future institutional market

A credible future Dubbo eWaste service could offer exactly the attributes the Department already requires:

- asset inventory
- chain of custody
- secure data sanitisation
- certificate/report
- testing/grading
- reuse-before-recycling
- environmentally appropriate residual recycling.

The long-term opportunity is to show that a **regional reuse-first provider** can:

1. reduce freight
2. identify reusable equipment before it becomes material recycling
3. create local refurbished-device supply
4. provide transparent asset-level reporting
5. hand only genuinely non-viable material to an approved recycler.

That is an institutional ITAD proposition, not ordinary public e-waste collection.

# 14. The Settlement Report may contain the missing answer

The public guide requires the vendor to provide a **Settlement Report**, but no sample was found publicly.

That report is potentially the best evidence for:

- asset ID
- serial number
- model
- sanitisation outcome
- accepted/rejected status
- grade
- resale/remarketing value
- recycling outcome
- destruction outcome
- residual-value credit.

Those fields are **not confirmed** because the template has not been published.

A redacted/blank Settlement Report should be specifically requested.

# 17. What is VERIFIED vs UNKNOWN

## VERIFIED

- NSW public schools use a dedicated Department eWaste contract.
- Schools request collections through the Department/EDConnect process.
- A public eWaste PowerApp/user guide exists.
- Schools inventory equipment and coordinate collection.
- The vendor creates cost-efficient collection schedules.
- Quantity estimates are used to arrange the right team/truck.
- The operational guide says school-side hard-drive wiping/destruction is no longer required, but its applicability and relationship to the current policy's pre-disposal wiping rule require Department clarification (`EVD-DISPOSAL-023`–`024`).
- Vendor sanitises all collected hard drives.
- Vendor provides sanitisation certificate.
- Vendor provides settlement report.
- Department policy permits redistribution of unused Department-allocated devices before disposal.
- Whole-of-government Contract 9826 allows reuse by sale/donation, supply-chain recovery, or third-party recycling.
- ACT Logistics, G1, Greenbox, Lifecycle Plus, Sims Lifecycle Services and WorkVentures are current standalone-service suppliers on C9826.
- NSW Device Bank refurbishes other surplus NSW Government laptops through WorkVentures.

## UNKNOWN / NOT PUBLICLY VERIFIED

- current dedicated Education eWaste vendor name
- whether the Education contract is under C9826
- exact Dubbo collection contractor
- physical collection route from Dubbo
- first depot
- first refurbishment/testing facility
- first recycler
- whether AMR is involved
- whether Sircel is involved
- whether Sims is involved
- whether WorkVentures is involved
- whether ACT Logistics is involved
- whether school laptops enter NSW Device Bank
- percentage reused
- percentage resold
- percentage donated
- percentage recycled
- percentage destroyed
- residual-value payments
- exact contents of the Settlement Report.

These should remain labelled unknown until primary evidence closes them.

## Bottom line

For Dubbo public schools, the verified chain currently ends publicly at:

**school → Department eWaste request → contracted vendor collection → vendor data sanitisation → sanitisation certificate + Settlement Report**

Everything after that — **reuse, resale, Device Bank, parts recovery, shredding, downstream recycler and physical destination** — still needs the contractor/settlement-report evidence.

That is now the most important next question if Dubbo eWaste wants to understand or eventually participate in the public-school laptop stream.
