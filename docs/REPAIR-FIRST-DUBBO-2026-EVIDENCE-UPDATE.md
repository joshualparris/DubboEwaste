# Repair First Dubbo — 2026 evidence update

**Research date:** 8 October 2026  
**Question:** How do you actually incentivise people in Dubbo to repair their stuff instead of automatically buying new?  
**Scope:** electronics, appliances and household goods; behaviour change; repair economics; repair subsidies; local repair capacity; community repair; current Dubbo/NSW opportunities.

This document adds newer evidence to:
- [REPAIR-FIRST-DUBBO-DEEP-RESEARCH.md](REPAIR-FIRST-DUBBO-DEEP-RESEARCH.md)
- [REPAIR-FIRST-DUBBO-IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md](REPAIR-FIRST-DUBBO-IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md)

---

# Executive conclusion

The best answer is **not one incentive**.

People repair when five things line up:

1. **Price** — repair is clearly cheaper than replacement.
2. **Convenience** — it is easy to find, book and complete.
3. **Confidence** — the quote, repairer and outcome feel trustworthy.
4. **Capability** — parts, skills and repair information actually exist.
5. **Normality** — repair is the obvious first step, not a niche hobby.

For Dubbo, the most evidence-based behaviour target is:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

The strongest local package is:

> **free/cheap diagnosis + instant repair rebate + trusted local repair directory + warranty/clear quote + regular Repair Café/Fix-It sessions + donor parts/loaners + visible local repair stories.**

Environmental messaging helps, but the public-facing reason should usually be:

> **save money, save time, keep the thing you already own.**

# 1. Australian evidence: repair is wanted, but the system gets in the way

## 1.1 Australian attitudes do not automatically become behaviour

The Productivity Commission's circular-economy work cites CHOICE research showing:

- **85%** of Australians say durability matters;
- **73%** say repairability matters;
- only **39%** regularly choose products that last longer;
- only **46%** choose easily repairable products all or most of the time.

That is a large **attitude-behaviour gap**.

Source:  
https://assets.pc.gov.au/2025-10/circular-economy.pdf

### Dubbo implication

Do not assume that telling residents “repair is good” will change decisions.

People already broadly agree.

The real task is to make the repair choice:
- cheaper;
- easier;
- lower risk;
- visible at the exact moment something breaks.

---

## 1.2 A 2,717-household Australian study shows repair is real household work

A 2024 Monash-led study surveyed **2,717 Australian households** about repair and maintenance of:
- clothing/accessories;
- furniture/homewares;
- appliances/whitegoods.

The authors describe repair as a form of **consumption work** that takes:
- time;
- skills;
- tools;
- household capacity.

Source:  
https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/repair-and-maintenance-in-australian-households-what-drives-this-/

### Dubbo implication

A repair scheme should not demand extra unpaid work from people who are already busy.

Make it:
- one booking;
- one diagnosis;
- one quote;
- one repair decision.

A scheme that requires people to research parts, compare six repairers and chase reimbursements has built the barrier back into the program.

# 2. New 2026 electronics-repair evidence: cost is important, but uncertainty may be even more important than it looks

A 2026 review/interview study of the broken-electronics repair system identified more than 100 barriers/enablers across the repair journey.

Across the literature:
- price appeared in **48%** of studies;
- social norms in **42%**;
- product design in **36%**;
- repair knowledge/capability in **33%**;
- values in **31%**.

For Australian appliances, the review cites repair cost as the main barrier for **51%** of cases in Australian research.

But the study adds a critical insight:

> People may assume repair will be too expensive without first obtaining an objective diagnosis or quote.

It also finds:
- repair is often **not front of mind**;
- uncertainty about the result discourages repair;
- past repair experiences strongly affect future behaviour;
- local repair networks are important.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667378925000616

### Dubbo implication

The first incentive should not necessarily be “pay half the repair”.

It may be:

> **remove the uncertainty before the consumer decides to replace.**

That supports a **Repair Check**:
- quick diagnosis;
- price estimate;
- repairability;
- replacement comparison;
- expected extra useful life.

# 3. Australian small-electronics research supports awareness + repairability information

A 2025 Australian study on small electrical and electronic equipment surveyed **401 consumers** and also surveyed repairers.

It found:
- consumers were generally willing to participate in circular behaviours;
- safety concerns were a barrier;
- products not designed for repair were a major barrier;
- consumers need better repair/durability information;
- awareness campaigns should target all education levels;
- product durability information at point of sale could improve decisions.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666188825006148

### Dubbo implication

A repair strategy should act at **two moments**:

## When something breaks
> Get one repair check before buying new.

## When buying something new
> Choose the model most likely to be repairable later.

A future **Repairable Choice** guide should include:
- battery replacement availability;
- spare parts;
- software support;
- local repair option;
- service manual availability;
- expected major repair cost;
- warranty.

# 4. Repair subsidy evidence has become stronger in 2026

## 4.1 Austria's current scheme

Austria replaced its earlier Repair Bonus with the **Geräte-Retter-Prämie** in January 2026.

Current official design:

- **50%** of eligible repair/service/maintenance cost;
- maximum **€130** per device;
- up to **€30** toward a repair quote/estimate;
- applies to household electrical/electronic products including laptops, TVs, tools and appliances;
- participating repair businesses are searchable publicly.

Source:  
https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/umwelt_und_klima/energie_und_ressourcen_sparen/geraete-retter-praemie

### Early uptake

By 26 March 2026:
- **65,300 vouchers** had been generated;
- **59,000 had been redeemed**.

One month after launch the government reported:
- **39,000 vouchers generated**;
- **31,100 redeemed**;
- **2,191 participating repair businesses**.

Sources:
- https://www.bmluk.gv.at/service/presse/klima-umwelt/2026/geraeteretterpraemie-zeigt-wirkung-hohe-einloesequote-bestaetigt-erfolg.html
- https://www.bmluk.gv.at/service/presse/abfall-und-kreislaufwirtschaft/2026/bons-geraete-retter-praemie.html

### What this demonstrates

A subsidy can create strong demand when:
- the support is large enough to matter;
- the scheme is simple;
- a broad repairer network exists;
- diagnosis/quotes can also be supported.

### Dubbo lesson

The **€30 diagnosis support is almost as strategically interesting as the 50% repair subsidy**.

It acknowledges that people need to know whether repair makes sense before committing.

## 4.2 France makes the discount immediate

France's current Repair Bonus is designed to reduce the repair price directly through an accredited repairer.

Current official information says:
- the discount varies by product;
- current amounts are roughly **€10–€65**;
- the consumer receives the benefit through the repair transaction;
- a repair using a circular-economy/reused part can receive an additional **20% bonus**.

Sources:
- https://www.economie.gouv.fr/particuliers/mes-droits-conso/bien-consommer/bonus-reparation-comment-ca-marche
- https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/politiques-publiques/bonus-reparation-quest-ce-que-cest

### Dubbo lesson

Two strong design ideas:

1. **instant discount**, not a paperwork-heavy reimbursement;
2. an extra incentive for **reused/donor parts** where safe and appropriate.

That second idea fits Dubbo eWaste particularly well because genuinely end-of-life devices can supply useful:
- chargers;
- RAM;
- cases;
- screens;
- fans;
- boards;
- connectors;
- compatible parts.

# 5. 2026 research on Austria shows vouchers change behaviour, but not for everyone

A September 2026 peer-reviewed study interviewed Austrian consumers about repair incentives.

It found three response patterns:

1. **no effect** — when people do not know about the scheme or non-financial barriers dominate;
2. **nudge effect** — subsidy/awareness makes repair enter consideration;
3. **actual behaviour change** — more repairs, more types of items repaired, or switching to participating repair providers.

It also found:
- a windfall effect for some people who would have repaired anyway;
- scheme design matters;
- marketing/awareness is essential;
- the interaction between repair price, subsidy and product value matters;
- long-term habit change after subsidies end remains uncertain.

Source:  
https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/consumer-perceptions-of-financial-incentives-for-repair-informati/

### Dubbo implication

A successful pilot must measure **additionality**:

> Would this person have repaired the item without the program?

Otherwise a voucher scheme can report lots of claims while paying for repairs that were going to happen anyway.

# 6. Vouchers do not build a repair ecosystem by themselves

A 2026 Journal of Cleaner Production study comparing repair-voucher policy with longer-term repair infrastructure concluded:

- vouchers can increase demand;
- vouchers increase awareness/willingness;
- repair-sector investment does not automatically follow;
- community repair sites may see limited benefit;
- structural barriers remain, including:
  - repair cost;
  - product design;
  - skills shortages.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652626003975

### Dubbo implication

Do not create demand faster than local capacity can absorb it.

Sequence:

1. map capacity;
2. recruit repairers;
3. agree service/warranty standards;
4. create directory/referral;
5. start Repair Check;
6. launch small subsidy;
7. measure;
8. scale.

# 7. Repair Cafés are useful because they create positive repair experiences

Repair Café International's 2024 RepairMonitor recorded:

- more than **37,000 repair attempts**;
- **62% successfully repaired**.

Source:  
https://www.repaircafe.org/en/repairmonitor-factsheet-provides-insight-into-repair-data-for-2024/

An Australian Repair Café survey also reported benefits beyond waste reduction:
- social connection;
- reduced isolation;
- learning;
- innovation;
- positive wellbeing;
- community empowerment.

Source:  
https://cfsd.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/RC-conference-2024-21-6-24.pdf

### Why that matters behaviourally

The 2026 repair-system research finds **past experience** strongly influences future repair.

A successful Repair Café visit can therefore do more than fix one item:

> it teaches the person that repair is a realistic option next time.

### Dubbo model

Use Repair Café for:
- diagnosis;
- simple fixes;
- maintenance;
- teaching;
- referral;
- repair confidence.

Do not use it to replace commercial specialists.

# 11. The highest-impact incentives for Dubbo, ranked

## 1. Repair Check — highest priority

### Offer

A free or very cheap initial diagnosis.

Output:

- fault;
- repairable? yes/no;
- estimated price;
- replacement equivalent;
- expected extra useful life;
- specialist referral if needed.

### Why #1

It attacks:
- uncertainty;
- assumed high cost;
- not thinking of repair;
- search friction.

### Campaign

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

---

## 2. Instant Repair Bonus

### Pilot concept

A participating repairer deducts the subsidy from the amount the resident pays.

Test:
- percentage contribution;
- category caps;
- repair-to-replacement ratios.

Do not lock policy to one number before collecting local price data.

### Starting design worth testing

For electronics/small appliances:
- up to **50%**;
- capped around **$50–$100**.

For larger appliances:
- test a higher cap only after local quotes are mapped.

### Why

It directly changes the price calculation at the decision point.

---

## 3. Repair Dubbo directory

One trusted page should answer:

- who repairs this category?
- current contact details;
- brands/models;
- diagnostic fee;
- typical turnaround;
- call-out/pickup;
- repair warranty;
- parts;
- accessibility;
- next route if not repairable.

### Why

Search effort is a hidden repair price.

---

## 4. Warranty + transparent quote standard

Participating repairers should clearly state:

- diagnosis fee;
- labour;
- parts;
- maximum authorised amount;
- expected turnaround;
- warranty on workmanship/part where applicable;
- privacy/data handling for electronics.

### Why

A new product competes with repair partly through certainty.

The repair program has to offer certainty too.

---

## 5. Monthly Repair Café / Fix-It event

Functions:
- simple repair;
- diagnosis;
- skill-sharing;
- social proof;
- volunteer recruitment;
- commercial referral.

Do not attempt safety-critical work beyond capability.

---

## 6. Donor-parts network

Dubbo eWaste can route genuine end-of-life electronics into a controlled parts inventory before residual recycling.

Potential uses:
- laptop RAM/SSDs;
- chargers;
- fans;
- screens where economically sensible;
- keyboards;
- housings;
- cables/connectors;
- tested compatible components.

A future bonus could reward safe reuse of circular parts, borrowing from France's current design.

---

## 7. Loaner devices

Phones/laptops are harder to repair when the owner cannot be without them.

A small loan pool reduces downtime.

Best targets:
- students;
- families;
- small business;
- essential communications.

---

## 8. Repairable Choice label/checklist

Before a resident buys:
- battery replaceable?
- parts available?
- local repairer?
- software support length?
- repair manual?
- typical repair cost?
- warranty?

This changes the market slowly by rewarding repairable products.

---

## 9. Local repair stories

Show real examples:

> Laptop slow, not dead. SSD upgrade + service: $X. Replacement: ~$Y.

> Phone battery replaced: $X. New comparable phone: ~$Y.

> TV power fault repaired: $X. New TV: ~$Y.

This makes repair:
- visible;
- normal;
- financially concrete.

Obtain customer permission and do not publish identifiable data without it.

---

## 10. Point-of-disposal interception

Before a resident reaches the recycling/disposal stream, provide:

> **Could this be repaired first? Scan for a Repair Check.**

Do this **before legal surrender/ownership transfer into a waste stream**, not by scavenging afterward.

# 13. A realistic 100-job pilot

Do not begin with a citywide unlimited subsidy.

Test a bounded pilot.

## Stage 1 — 100 Repair Checks

Recruit participating local repairers.

Collect:
- product category;
- age;
- diagnosis;
- quoted repair price;
- replacement price;
- expected extra life;
- customer's intended action before diagnosis.

## Stage 2 — Repair Bonus

Offer a limited number of instant repair rebates.

Test different categories/caps rather than assuming one amount fits everything.

## Stage 3 — Monthly Repair Café

Use the Café for:
- diagnosis;
- simple repairs;
- education;
- referrals;
- community skill-building.

## Stage 4 — follow-up

At 3–6 months ask:
- is the repaired item still in use?
- would you repair again?
- did you repair another item?
- would you recommend the repairer/program?

# 14. What success should mean

Do **not** use:

> number of vouchers claimed

as the headline KPI.

Measure:

## Additionality
How many repairs happened **because of** the intervention?

Ask before the benefit:

> What were you most likely to do without this program?

- repair anyway;
- buy new;
- buy used/refurbished;
- leave broken;
- dispose/recycle;
- unsure.

## Conversion
- diagnosed;
- quoted;
- approved;
- repaired.

## Failure reasons
- too expensive;
- no part;
- no local skill;
- too slow;
- unsafe;
- unrepairable design;
- software unsupported;
- customer preferred replacement.

## Economic value
- repair cost;
- subsidy;
- replacement estimate;
- estimated household spend avoided;
- local repair labour.

## Longevity
- estimated additional life;
- still working at follow-up.

## Local capacity
- repairer jobs generated;
- turnaround;
- parts delays;
- categories with no local repairer.

That data will tell Dubbo what the real barriers are.

# 15. Messaging: what to say and what not to say

## Strong

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

> **Repair first. Replace when the numbers actually make sense.**

> **Keep your money local and your gear working.**

> **A $70 repair can beat a $700 replacement.**

## Weak

> Save the planet by repairing.

> Don't be wasteful.

> Good people repair.

The environmental benefit is real, but guilt is a poor service design.

Make repair the:
- financially smart;
- low-hassle;
- trusted;
- normal

choice.

# 16. Role of Dubbo eWaste

Dubbo eWaste should not try to personally repair:
- every appliance;
- every phone;
- every TV;
- every bike;
- every electrical item.

Its strongest role is:

> **triage + routing + electronics expertise + parts recovery + refurbished alternative + downstream recycling.**

Flow:

**broken item**  
→ Repair Check  
→ easy fix / DIY  
→ specialist local repairer  
→ refurbish/upgrade  
→ donor parts  
→ refurbished replacement  
→ recycle only when genuinely end-of-life.

That makes Dubbo eWaste part of the infrastructure that **prevents waste**, not just a business that receives it afterward.

# 17. One recommended starting experiment

If only one thing can be trialled:

## **Repair Check Month**

For one month:

1. recruit 5–10 local repair businesses across categories;
2. offer a simple subsidised/free diagnosis;
3. use one booking/referral page;
4. provide repair-vs-replace comparison;
5. give a small instant bonus to a limited subset of repairable jobs;
6. track every outcome;
7. run one Repair Café alongside it.

Why this is strong:

- cheap compared with a full rebate scheme;
- tests demand;
- tests capacity;
- finds gaps;
- teaches residents the habit;
- creates local evidence for Council/NetWaste/sponsors;
- does not require building new repair infrastructure blindly.

---

# Bottom line

The best way to stop:

> **broken → buy new**

is to make the local default:

> **broken → one trusted repair check → repair if sensible → reuse/refurbish → buy refurbished or new only if repair genuinely loses.**

The evidence says repair behaviour changes when:

> **price drops + hassle drops + uncertainty drops + trust rises + repair becomes visible and normal.**

For Dubbo, the most useful first public promise is still:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**
