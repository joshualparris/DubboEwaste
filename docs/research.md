# Research notes — Dubbo eWaste

Last checked: 2 October 2026 (AEST)

These notes capture the feasibility research completed so far. They are not legal advice.

## 1. What happens to e-waste in Dubbo now?

Dubbo Regional Council accepts separated domestic e-waste at the Whylandra Waste and Recycling Centre.

Council's current Whylandra page says domestic recyclables including e-waste can be accepted free when delivered separately and uncontaminated, with Community Recycling Centre problem-waste quantities generally limited to domestic quantities.

A 2025 NSW Department of Planning regional waste infrastructure study states:

- Whylandra is Dubbo's principal waste-management site.
- specialty waste streams are handled through different partners.
- **e-waste is processed by Matthews Metals in Dubbo**.
- scrap metal is processed by InfraBuild.
- some batteries and oils are aggregated and transported to Newcastle and Sydney.

Dubbo Regional Council's adopted 2025–2035 Waste Strategy lists:

- **Matthews Metals — Collection of E-Waste**
- commencement: 30 June 2023
- listed expiry: **30 June 2025**

Those references describe the established 2024-25 pathway, but they are no longer enough to identify the current yard operator.

**2026 operator update:** NSW Police's 19 February 2026 Scrap Metal Industry Register lists **Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd / Australian Metal Recycling (AMR)**, registration **SM-0759**, at **34 Mountbatten Drive, Dubbo**. AMR's current website calls the Dubbo yard **“Formerly Matthews Metal Management.”** AMR says e-waste is recycled free and explicitly includes computers, monitors and printers, while listing lithium batteries as not accepted.

InfraBuild still has an online “Matthews Metal Management – Dubbo” page at the same address, and its subsidiary OneSteel Recycling still holds the Matthews Metal Management business name. A March 2025 regional waste tender listed InfraBuild Recycling and Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd as separate bidders, confirming they were separate operators by then.

The exact transaction/transition date, current Council contract after 30 June 2025, TVs/CRTs and detailed commercial conditions remain unresolved. See [MATTHEWS-INFRABUILD-AMR.md](MATTHEWS-INFRABUILD-AMR.md).

### Implication for Dubbo eWaste

There is already a disposal/recycling pathway. The business opportunity is therefore not simply "a place to dump electronics".

The stronger proposition is:

**collect → document → wipe → triage → reuse/refurbish → resell/redeploy → parts recovery → responsibly recycle remainder**

That creates value before material reaches the commodity-recycling stream.

## 2. Proposed business model

### Primary inputs

Potential sources:

- local households
- small businesses
- farms
- schools
- professional offices
- IT providers/MSPs
- organisations replacing fleets of PCs, monitors and peripherals

### Services

Possible services:

- free or low-cost collection of suitable unwanted IT
- paid business pickup for larger jobs
- asset inventory/reporting
- secure data erasure records
- physical drive destruction where appropriate
- refurbishment
- resale
- parts harvesting
- equipment donation/redeployment
- downstream recycling of non-reusable material

### Revenue possibilities

Potential income streams:

- sale of refurbished PCs/laptops
- sale of tested parts
- paid collection for business clear-outs
- paid data-erasure / asset-reporting service
- possible scrap/material recovery value

No revenue assumptions should be treated as validated until actual Dubbo collection volumes, resale rates, repair costs and downstream fees are measured.

## 3. Why reuse-first matters

A functioning or repairable computer is generally more valuable as a whole device than as scrap.

The triage hierarchy should therefore be:

1. reuse as-is
2. refurbish
3. repair
4. redeploy/donate
5. harvest usable parts
6. recycle remaining material

This also aligns with Council's broader circular-economy and resource-recovery direction.

## 4. Home-shed launch model

The preferred initial model is deliberately small and low-overhead.

### Start with

- existing home shed/workspace
- existing vehicle
- existing repair/test tools where suitable
- controlled collections by appointment
- online listings rather than a retail shopfront
- low stock levels
- clear inventory and chain-of-custody records
- safe separation of batteries/problem items

### Do not start with

- a public walk-in e-waste depot
- unrestricted public dumping
- warehouse lease
- employees
- industrial shredding
- bulk hazardous-waste storage
- large stockpiles of unprocessed devices
- large volumes of damaged lithium batteries

The private home address is intentionally excluded from this public repository.

## 5. Planning / home-business issue

Dubbo Regional Local Environmental Plan 2022 says that where a **home business** is permitted, it must not involve more than **30 m² of floor area**.

That does not by itself settle whether this exact e-waste/refurbishment activity is permitted. Council needs to consider the proposed use, including:

- number of pickups/drop-offs
- storage volume
- vehicle movements
- noise
- signage
- waste generation
- battery storage
- whether the activity is characterised as home business, home industry, waste activity, or something else
- whether development consent is required

Practical starting position: operate as a collection/refurbishment business with controlled appointments, not as an open public waste facility.

## 6. Second-hand dealer licence

NSW Government guidance says a second-hand dealer licence is generally needed to run a business that buys, sells or exchanges prescribed used goods.

**Electronic goods are prescribed second-hand goods.**

2026–27 new-licence fee:

- 1 year: **$692**
- 3 years: **$1,574**
- 5 years: **$2,456**

However, the same NSW Government page expressly says a licence is not needed to:

> engage in recycling and rubbish collection programs.

This creates an important unresolved question:

**Does a business that collects e-waste, refurbishes viable equipment, resells it and recycles the remainder qualify for that exemption?**

Do not assume either answer. Obtain written confirmation from NSW Fair Trading for the proposed model.

### Existing-business possibility

If the operation sits under another existing business/legal entity, any relevant licence or exemption would need to apply to **that legal entity and the actual activity/premises**.

Potential association with an existing IT business does not automatically provide coverage.

Questions to ask:

- Does the entity already hold a second-hand dealer licence?
- If so, does it cover the proposed activity?
- Does Fair Trading need the Dubbo operating/storage premises notified?
- Could Dubbo eWaste operate as a business/trading name of that entity?
- Does the recycling-program exemption make a licence unnecessary anyway?

## 7. Scrap-metal registration

NSW Police regulates businesses dealing in scrap metal.

Current registration cost:

- **$260 every three years**

NSW Police guidance says there is a legal presumption that someone who deals in scrap metal on more than six days in a 12-month period is carrying on a business dealing in scrap metal.

Whether Dubbo eWaste needs this will depend on what it actually does with recovered metal and whether that activity constitutes dealing in scrap metal.

Do not register prematurely; first define the operating model and ask NSW Police/SLED if necessary.

## 8. Business waste and downstream recycling

NSW EPA says businesses are **not eligible to use Community Recycling Centres as their business waste-disposal service**.

Therefore a commercial Dubbo eWaste operation should not rely on free household CRC disposal for its downstream waste.

It needs a legitimate commercial arrangement for:

- dead electronics
- circuit boards
- mixed e-waste
- batteries
- damaged batteries
- scrap metals where applicable
- other problem components

Potential local/downstream parties should be asked for:

- accepted categories
- minimum quantities
- fees or rebates
- whether pickup is available
- proof of recycling / certificates
- battery rules
- data-bearing-device handling
- whether they are certified/accredited for relevant programs

## 9. Lithium-ion battery safety

Fire and Rescue NSW describes lithium-ion batteries as a major and growing fire risk.

Core practices for the initial shed operation:

- working smoke or heat alarm in battery/device charging or storage area
- charge on hard, non-combustible surfaces
- do not charge while asleep or away
- disconnect once charged
- never charge swollen, leaking, punctured, crushed or overheating batteries
- keep damaged batteries away from structures/combustibles in accordance with official guidance
- do not put batteries in general waste or recycling bins
- maintain a specific downstream process for damaged/problem batteries

Battery handling needs to be treated as a core operating control, not an afterthought.

## 10. Startup costs

### Verified government costs

| Cost | Amount | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| ABN | $0 | if starting as sole trader |
| Business name | $47/1 year or $108/3 years | verified 2026 fee |
| Second-hand dealer licence | $692/1 year | only if required |
| Scrap-metal registration | $260/3 years | only if activity requires it |

### Planning estimates, not quotes

Earlier feasibility discussion estimated:

- battery/fire-safe storage: roughly $100–$250 initially
- tools/bench/shelving: potentially $0 if existing setup is sufficient
- website/social listings: potentially $0 initially
- stock: potentially $0 if sourced through collection/donation
- data-wiping software: can start with free/open tooling
- insurance: must be quoted
- parts/SSDs/RAM/chargers: buy only as demand requires

A rough **~$1,000 lean launch** is plausible only if:

- home use is approved/permitted
- existing tools/vehicle/workspace are used
- no warehouse is leased
- major licensing costs are avoided through a genuine exemption or existing compliant business structure
- insurance and downstream costs remain manageable

This is a target to validate, not a guaranteed total.

## 11. Competitive position

Dubbo already has:

- Council domestic e-waste drop-off
- commercial waste/recycling operators
- Australian Metal Recycling at the former Matthews Dubbo yard, plus InfraBuild's broader metal-recycling network
- retail take-back options for some electronics

Dubbo eWaste should therefore differentiate on:

- collection convenience
- reuse before destruction
- transparent data handling
- refurbishment
- local resale
- asset reporting
- traceability
- responsible downstream recycling

Potential positioning:

> **Dubbo eWaste — reuse first, recycle second.**

## 12. Regional benchmark

An established regional e-waste operator in Bendigo may be a useful real-world benchmark.

Questions worth asking an operator:

- What percentage of incoming devices are reusable?
- What percentage are sold as complete machines?
- Average sale value?
- Average repair/parts cost?
- What categories create losses?
- Who pays for collection?
- Are business pickups charged?
- How are data-bearing devices handled?
- What records do business clients expect?
- Which downstream recyclers are used?
- What does downstream disposal cost?
- How much storage is actually needed?
- What insurance is carried?
- What licences/approvals were needed?
- What volume made a dedicated site worthwhile?

Real operating numbers from a comparable regional city are more valuable than theoretical margins.
