# Repair First Dubbo — 2026 evidence update

**Research date:** 8 October 2026  
**Question:** How do you actually incentivise people in Dubbo to repair their stuff instead of automatically buying new?  
**Scope:** electronics, appliances and household goods; behaviour change; repair economics; repair subsidies; local repair capacity; community repair; current Dubbo/NSW opportunities.

This document adds newer evidence to:
- [REPAIR-FIRST-DUBBO-DEEP-RESEARCH.md](REPAIR-FIRST-DUBBO-DEEP-RESEARCH.md)
- [REPAIR-FIRST-DUBBO-IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md](REPAIR-FIRST-DUBBO-IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md)

# Executive conclusion

The best answer is **not one incentive**.

People repair when five things line up:

1. **Price** — repair is clearly cheaper than replacement.
2. **Convenience** — it is easy to find, book and complete.
3. **Confidence** — the quote, repairer and outcome feel trustworthy.
4. **Capability** — parts, skills and repair information actually exist.
5. **Normality** — repair is the obvious first step, not a niche hobby.

For Dubbo, the most evidence-based behaviour target is:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

The strongest local package is:

> **free/cheap diagnosis + instant repair rebate + trusted local repair directory + warranty/clear quote + regular Repair Café/Fix-It sessions + donor parts/loaners + visible local repair stories.**

Environmental messaging helps, but the public-facing reason should usually be:

> **save money, save time, keep the thing you already own.**

# 1. Australian evidence: repair is wanted, but the system gets in the way

## 1.1 Australian attitudes do not automatically become behaviour

The Productivity Commission's circular-economy work cites CHOICE research showing:

- **85%** of Australians say durability matters;
- **73%** say repairability matters;
- only **39%** regularly choose products that last longer;
- only **46%** choose easily repairable products all or most of the time.

That is a large **attitude-behaviour gap**.

Source:  
https://assets.pc.gov.au/2025-10/circular-economy.pdf

### Dubbo implication

Do not assume that telling residents “repair is good” will change decisions.

People already broadly agree.

The real task is to make the repair choice:
- cheaper;
- easier;
- lower risk;
- visible at the exact moment something breaks.

---

## 1.2 A 2,717-household Australian study shows repair is real household work

A 2024 Monash-led study surveyed **2,717 Australian households** about repair and maintenance of:
- clothing/accessories;
- furniture/homewares;
- appliances/whitegoods.

The authors describe repair as a form of **consumption work** that takes:
- time;
- skills;
- tools;
- household capacity.

Source:  
https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/repair-and-maintenance-in-australian-households-what-drives-this-/

### Dubbo implication

A repair scheme should not demand extra unpaid work from people who are already busy.

Make it:
- one booking;
- one diagnosis;
- one quote;
- one repair decision.

A scheme that requires people to research parts, compare six repairers and chase reimbursements has built the barrier back into the program.

# 2. New 2026 electronics-repair evidence: cost is important, but uncertainty may be even more important than it looks

A 2026 review/interview study of the broken-electronics repair system identified more than 100 barriers/enablers across the repair journey.

Across the literature:
- price appeared in **48%** of studies;
- social norms in **42%**;
- product design in **36%**;
- repair knowledge/capability in **33%**;
- values in **31%**.

For Australian appliances, the review cites repair cost as the main barrier for **51%** of cases in Australian research.

But the study adds a critical insight:

> People may assume repair will be too expensive without first obtaining an objective diagnosis or quote.

It also finds:
- repair is often **not front of mind**;
- uncertainty about the result discourages repair;
- past repair experiences strongly affect future behaviour;
- local repair networks are important.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667378925000616

### Dubbo implication

The first incentive should not necessarily be “pay half the repair”.

It may be:

> **remove the uncertainty before the consumer decides to replace.**

That supports a **Repair Check**:
- quick diagnosis;
- price estimate;
- repairability;
- replacement comparison;
- expected extra useful life.

# 3. Australian small-electronics research supports awareness + repairability information

A 2025 Australian study on small electrical and electronic equipment surveyed **401 consumers** and also surveyed repairers.

It found:
- consumers were generally willing to participate in circular behaviours;
- safety concerns were a barrier;
- products not designed for repair were a major barrier;
- consumers need better repair/durability information;
- awareness campaigns should target all education levels;
- product durability information at point of sale could improve decisions.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666188825006148

### Dubbo implication

A repair strategy should act at **two moments**:

## When something breaks
> Get one repair check before buying new.

## When buying something new
> Choose the model most likely to be repairable later.

A future **Repairable Choice** guide should include:
- battery replacement availability;
- spare parts;
- software support;
- local repair option;
- service manual availability;
- expected major repair cost;
- warranty.

# 4. Repair subsidy evidence has become stronger in 2026

## 4.1 Austria's current scheme

Austria replaced its earlier Repair Bonus with the **Geräte-Retter-Prämie** in January 2026.

Current official design:

- **50%** of eligible repair/service/maintenance cost;
- maximum **€130** per device;
- up to **€30** toward a repair quote/estimate;
- applies to household electrical/electronic products including laptops, TVs, tools and appliances;
- participating repair businesses are searchable publicly.

Source:  
https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/umwelt_und_klima/energie_und_ressourcen_sparen/geraete-retter-praemie

### Early uptake

By 26 March 2026:
- **65,300 vouchers** had been generated;
- **59,000 had been redeemed**.

One month after launch the government reported:
- **39,000 vouchers generated**;
- **31,100 redeemed**;
- **2,191 participating repair businesses**.

Sources:
- https://www.bmluk.gv.at/service/presse/klima-umwelt/2026/geraeteretterpraemie-zeigt-wirkung-hohe-einloesequote-bestaetigt-erfolg.html
- https://www.bmluk.gv.at/service/presse/abfall-und-kreislaufwirtschaft/2026/bons-geraete-retter-praemie.html

### What this demonstrates

A subsidy can create strong demand when:
- the support is large enough to matter;
- the scheme is simple;
- a broad repairer network exists;
- diagnosis/quotes can also be supported.

### Dubbo lesson

The **€30 diagnosis support is almost as strategically interesting as the 50% repair subsidy**.

It acknowledges that people need to know whether repair makes sense before committing.

## 4.2 France makes the discount immediate

France's current Repair Bonus is designed to reduce the repair price directly through an accredited repairer.

Current official information says:
- the discount varies by product;
- current amounts are roughly **€10–€65**;
- the consumer receives the benefit through the repair transaction;
- a repair using a circular-economy/reused part can receive an additional **20% bonus**.

Sources:
- https://www.economie.gouv.fr/particuliers/mes-droits-conso/bien-consommer/bonus-reparation-comment-ca-marche
- https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/politiques-publiques/bonus-reparation-quest-ce-que-cest

### Dubbo lesson

Two strong design ideas:

1. **instant discount**, not a paperwork-heavy reimbursement;
2. an extra incentive for **reused/donor parts** where safe and appropriate.

That second idea fits Dubbo eWaste particularly well because genuinely end-of-life devices can supply useful:
- chargers;
- RAM;
- cases;
- screens;
- fans;
- boards;
- connectors;
- compatible parts.

# 5. 2026 research on Austria shows vouchers change behaviour, but not for everyone

A September 2026 peer-reviewed study interviewed Austrian consumers about repair incentives.

It found three response patterns:

1. **no effect** — when people do not know about the scheme or non-financial barriers dominate;
2. **nudge effect** — subsidy/awareness makes repair enter consideration;
3. **actual behaviour change** — more repairs, more types of items repaired, or switching to participating repair providers.

It also found:
- a windfall effect for some people who would have repaired anyway;
- scheme design matters;
- marketing/awareness is essential;
- the interaction between repair price, subsidy and product value matters;
- long-term habit change after subsidies end remains uncertain.

Source:  
https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/consumer-perceptions-of-financial-incentives-for-repair-informati/

### Dubbo implication

A successful pilot must measure **additionality**:

> Would this person have repaired the item without the program?

Otherwise a voucher scheme can report lots of claims while paying for repairs that were going to happen anyway.

# 6. Vouchers do not build a repair ecosystem by themselves

A 2026 Journal of Cleaner Production study comparing repair-voucher policy with longer-term repair infrastructure concluded:

- vouchers can increase demand;
- vouchers increase awareness/willingness;
- repair-sector investment does not automatically follow;
- community repair sites may see limited benefit;
- structural barriers remain, including:
  - repair cost;
  - product design;
  - skills shortages.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652626003975

### Dubbo implication

Do not create demand faster than local capacity can absorb it.

Sequence:

1. map capacity;
2. recruit repairers;
3. agree service/warranty standards;
4. create directory/referral;
5. start Repair Check;
6. launch small subsidy;
7. measure;
8. scale.

# 7. Repair Cafés are useful because they create positive repair experiences

Repair Café International's 2024 RepairMonitor recorded:

- more than **37,000 repair attempts**;
- **62% successfully repaired**.

Source:  
https://www.repaircafe.org/en/repairmonitor-factsheet-provides-insight-into-repair-data-for-2024/

An Australian Repair Café survey also reported benefits beyond waste reduction:
- social connection;
- reduced isolation;
- learning;
- innovation;
- positive wellbeing;
- community empowerment.

Source:  
https://cfsd.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/RC-conference-2024-21-6-24.pdf

### Why that matters behaviourally

The 2026 repair-system research finds **past experience** strongly influences future repair.

A successful Repair Café visit can therefore do more than fix one item:

> it teaches the person that repair is a realistic option next time.

### Dubbo model

Use Repair Café for:
- diagnosis;
- simple fixes;
- maintenance;
- teaching;
- referral;
- repair confidence.

Do not use it to replace commercial specialists.

# 11. The highest-impact incentives for Dubbo, ranked

## 1. Repair Check — highest priority

### Offer

A free or very cheap initial diagnosis.

Output:

- fault;
- repairable? yes/no;
- estimated price;
- replacement equivalent;
- expected extra useful life;
- specialist referral if needed.

### Why #1

It attacks:
- uncertainty;
- assumed high cost;
- not thinking of repair;
- search friction.

### Campaign

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

---

## 2. Instant Repair Bonus

### Pilot concept

A participating repairer deducts the subsidy from the amount the resident pays.

Test:
- percentage contribution;
- category caps;
- repair-to-replacement ratios.

Do not lock policy to one number before collecting local price data.

### Starting design worth testing

For electronics/small appliances:
- up to **50%**;
- capped around **$50–$100**.

For larger appliances:
- test a higher cap only after local quotes are mapped.

### Why

It directly changes the price calculation at the decision point.

---

## 3. Repair Dubbo directory

One trusted page should answer:

- who repairs this category?
- current contact details;
- brands/models;
- diagnostic fee;
- typical turnaround;
- call-out/pickup;
- repair warranty;
- parts;
- accessibility;
- next route if not repairable.

### Why

Search effort is a hidden repair price.

---

## 4. Warranty + transparent quote standard

Participating repairers should clearly state:

- diagnosis fee;
- labour;
- parts;
- maximum authorised amount;
- expected turnaround;
- warranty on workmanship/part where applicable;
- privacy/data handling for electronics.

### Why

A new product competes with repair partly through certainty.

The repair program has to offer certainty too.

---

## 5. Monthly Repair Café / Fix-It event

Functions:
- simple repair;
- diagnosis;
- skill-sharing;
- social proof;
- volunteer recruitment;
- commercial referral.

Do not attempt safety-critical work beyond capability.

---

## 6. Donor-parts network

Dubbo eWaste can route genuine end-of-life electronics into a controlled parts inventory before residual recycling.

Potential uses:
- laptop RAM/SSDs;
- chargers;
- fans;
- screens where economically sensible;
- keyboards;
- housings;
- cables/connectors;
- tested compatible components.

A future bonus could reward safe reuse of circular parts, borrowing from France's current design.

---

## 7. Loaner devices

Phones/laptops are harder to repair when the owner cannot be without them.

A small loan pool reduces downtime.

Best targets:
- students;
- families;
- small business;
- essential communications.

---

## 8. Repairable Choice label/checklist

Before a resident buys:
- battery replaceable?
- parts available?
- local repairer?
- software support length?
- repair manual?
- typical repair cost?
- warranty?

This changes the market slowly by rewarding repairable products.

---

## 9. Local repair stories

Show real examples:

> Laptop slow, not dead. SSD upgrade + service: $X. Replacement: ~$Y.

> Phone battery replaced: $X. New comparable phone: ~$Y.

> TV power fault repaired: $X. New TV: ~$Y.

This makes repair:
- visible;
- normal;
- financially concrete.

Obtain customer permission and do not publish identifiable data without it.

---

## 10. Point-of-disposal interception

Before a resident reaches the recycling/disposal stream, provide:

> **Could this be repaired first? Scan for a Repair Check.**

Do this **before legal surrender/ownership transfer into a waste stream**, not by scavenging afterward.

Do not begin with a citywide unlimited subsidy.

Collect:

- age;
- diagnosis;
- quoted repair price;
- replacement price;
- expected extra life;
- customer's intended action before diagnosis.

## Stage 2 — Repair Bonus

Offer a limited number of instant repair rebates.

## Stage 3 — Monthly Repair Café

Use the Café for:
- diagnosis;

- education;
- referrals;
- community skill-building.

At 3–6 months ask:
- is the repaired item still in use?
- would you repair again?
- did you repair another item?
- would you recommend the repairer/program?

# 14. What success should mean

Do **not** use:

> number of vouchers claimed

as the headline KPI.

Measure:

## Additionality
How many repairs happened **because of** the intervention?

Ask before the benefit:

> What were you most likely to do without this program?

- repair anyway;
- buy new;
- buy used/refurbished;
- leave broken;
- dispose/recycle;
- unsure.

## Conversion
- diagnosed;
- quoted;
- approved;
- repaired.

## Failure reasons
- too expensive;
- no part;
- no local skill;
- too slow;
- unsafe;
- unrepairable design;
- software unsupported;
- customer preferred replacement.

## Economic value
- repair cost;
- subsidy;
- replacement estimate;
- estimated household spend avoided;
- local repair labour.

## Longevity
- estimated additional life;
- still working at follow-up.

## Local capacity
- repairer jobs generated;
- turnaround;
- parts delays;
- categories with no local repairer.

That data will tell Dubbo what the real barriers are.

# 15. Messaging: what to say and what not to say

## Strong

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

> **Repair first. Replace when the numbers actually make sense.**

> **Keep your money local and your gear working.**

> **A $70 repair can beat a $700 replacement.**

## Weak

> Save the planet by repairing.

> Don't be wasteful.

> Good people repair.

The environmental benefit is real, but guilt is a poor service design.

Make repair the:
- financially smart;
- low-hassle;
- trusted;
- normal

choice.

# 16. Role of Dubbo eWaste

Dubbo eWaste should not try to personally repair:
- every appliance;
- every phone;
- every TV;
- every bike;
- every electrical item.

Its strongest role is:

> **triage + routing + electronics expertise + parts recovery + refurbished alternative + downstream recycling.**

Flow:

**broken item**  
→ Repair Check  
→ easy fix / DIY  
→ specialist local repairer  
→ refurbish/upgrade  
→ donor parts  
→ refurbished replacement  
→ recycle only when genuinely end-of-life.

That makes Dubbo eWaste part of the infrastructure that **prevents waste**, not just a business that receives it afterward.

# 17. One recommended starting experiment

If only one thing can be trialled:

## **Repair Check Month**

For one month:

1. recruit 5–10 local repair businesses across categories;
2. offer a simple subsidised/free diagnosis;
3. use one booking/referral page;
4. provide repair-vs-replace comparison;
5. give a small instant bonus to a limited subset of repairable jobs;
6. track every outcome;
7. run one Repair Café alongside it.

Why this is strong:

- cheap compared with a full rebate scheme;
- tests demand;
- tests capacity;
- finds gaps;
- teaches residents the habit;
- creates local evidence for Council/NetWaste/sponsors;
- does not require building new repair infrastructure blindly.

# Bottom line

The best way to stop:

> **broken → buy new**

is to make the local default:

> **broken → one trusted repair check → repair if sensible → reuse/refurbish → buy refurbished or new only if repair genuinely loses.**

The evidence says repair behaviour changes when:

> **price drops + hassle drops + uncertainty drops + trust rises + repair becomes visible and normal.**

For Dubbo, the most useful first public promise is still:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

## 18. Behaviour-change model: repair is a capability + opportunity + motivation problem

A 2026 New Zealand study applied the **COM-B** behaviour-change model to repair.

Study 1 surveyed **1,512 consumers**. Study 2 examined repair-café participants. The results found that:

- **motivation** strongly predicts repair intention;
- **social opportunity** matters — repair is easier to choose when people around you, local services and institutions make it normal and accessible;
- for collaborative repair, **physical capability and physical opportunity** also matter;
- different groups need different interventions depending on how often they already repair.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1108/JCM-01-2025-7575

### Dubbo implication

There is no single “repair consumer”.

Segment the program:

### Group A — already repairs
Needs:
- easier parts;
- trusted directory;
- donor parts;
- occasional subsidy;
- specialist referrals.

Avoid spending most subsidy money here if they would have repaired anyway.

### Group B — persuadable but uncertain
This is the strongest behaviour-change target.

Needs:
- one repair check;
- price range;
- repair success estimate;
- warranty;
- quick booking;
- modest instant discount.

### Group C — replacement-default
Needs:
- repair to become socially normal;
- visible local examples;
- low-friction first experience;
- Repair Café exposure;
- repairability information when buying the next product.

This is why the best Dubbo program is a **system**, not a slogan.

## 19. Uncertainty itself is a repair barrier — and it can be reduced cheaply

A 2025 Journal of Industrial Ecology experiment with **237 consumers** tested repair offers with different levels of information.

The results are unusually actionable.

When a repair offer provided **no information** on repair cost or repair success:

- only **27.7%** chose the repair offer;
- **72.3%** chose the outside option, such as replacement.

When even a very broad repair-cost range was provided:

- repair preference rose to **53.2%**.

That is nearly double.

The study also found:

1. **total repair cost** was the most important service attribute;
2. **warranty** was second;
3. **repair success rate** was close behind;
4. shorter repair times were preferred;
5. a **regional repair service** was preferred over a nationwide network;
6. people with prior repair experience were, on average, about **11% more likely** to choose repair.

Source:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1111/jiec.70064

### Dubbo implications

The Repair Check should provide, where reasonably possible:

- likely cost or cost range;
- likely success / whether the fault is normally repairable;
- turnaround;
- warranty;
- replacement comparison.

Even imperfect information is better than:

> “Leave it with us and we’ll tell you later.”

This also gives a powerful local-business advantage:

> **local + transparent + warrantied**

can compete with “just order a new one online”.

## 20. Do not use one rigid repair-vs-replace percentage

European policy work often cites an average acceptable repair price around **20% of original purchase price**, and price relative to replacement is consistently a leading barrier.

Source:
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:52023SC0059

But current research also shows willingness varies substantially by:

- product value;
- age;
- expected remaining life;
- urgency;
- attachment;
- availability of replacement;
- confidence in the repair;
- repair warranty.

A 2025 choice experiment around a three-year-old washing machine found a much higher average willingness to pay under a transparent, credible service scenario.

Source:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1111/jiec.70064

### Dubbo rule

Do not create a policy saying:

> “Only repair if quote is under 20% of new price.”

Instead use a **repair-value conversation**:

- repair cost;
- cost of a like-for-like replacement;
- expected extra life;
- warranty;
- condition of the rest of the item;
- energy/safety implications;
- customer preference.

For a low-value toaster, replacement may rationally win.

For a $1,200 laptop with a $180 fault, repair may be obvious.

---

## 21. Repair experience creates a positive feedback loop

The same 2025 repair-choice research found people with recent paid repair experience were substantially more likely to choose repair again.

This suggests the first successful repair has value beyond the saved item.

### Dubbo implication

A Repair Café, Repair Check or voucher can create:

> **one good repair experience → higher chance of repairing the next thing**

That makes the first-contact experience critical.

Measure:

- first-ever repair customer?
- previous repair in last five years?
- would they repair again?
- did they subsequently repair another item?

The pilot should therefore value **confidence-building**, not just kilograms diverted.

---

## 22. Financial incentives work best when awareness, design and repair capacity work together

A September 2026 peer-reviewed study of Austria's repair voucher found three broad responses:

1. **no effect** where people did not know about the scheme or non-financial barriers dominated;
2. **nudge effect** where the incentive made repair enter consideration;
3. **behaviour change** where people repaired more often, repaired more product categories, or changed repair provider.

It also found:

- some subsidy goes to people who would have repaired anyway;
- awareness/marketing is essential;
- funding level must make sense relative to repair price and product value;
- long-term habit persistence after a subsidy ends is not guaranteed.

Source:
https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/consumer-perceptions-of-financial-incentives-for-repair-informati/

### OECD evidence on Austria

The OECD reports Austria's scheme provided a **50% repair discount**, initially capped at €200 per repair plus support for cost estimates.

By mid-2024:

- **more than 1 million repair vouchers** had been used;
- around **3,900 repair businesses** participated.

Demand was strong enough that the scheme later paused when available funds were exhausted.

Source:
https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-environmental-performance-reviews-austria-2026_520533a1-en/full-report/promoting-the-circular-economy_3cdb4cb8.html

### Dubbo implication

A voucher must be paired with:

- a visible directory;
- repairer capacity;
- diagnosis;
- easy redemption;
- publicity at the moment of failure;
- measurement of whether the repair would have occurred anyway.

---

## 23. France shows both the scale and the limits of repair bonuses

France's repair fund provides an instant repair discount through accredited repairers.

A 2026 consumer-organisation evaluation reported **1,888,208 subsidised electrical/electronic repairs** from scheme launch through the end of 2025, across thousands of accredited repair points.

But earlier official/oversight evidence also found:

- funding utilisation was initially much lower than expected;
- repairer coverage was geographically uneven;
- some subsidies were still too small to make high-cost repairs attractive;
- awareness and repairer accreditation were barriers.

Sources:
- https://www.clcv.org/articles/bilan-de-lobservatoire-du-fonds-reparation-des-equipements-electriques-et-electroniques-eee
- https://www.senat.fr/questions/base/2025/qSEQ250303925.html

### Dubbo lesson

A rebate with no nearby repairer is almost worthless.

The regional-city question is not just:

> “How big should the rebate be?”

It is:

> “Can the resident get a trustworthy repair without a two-week wait, a trip to Sydney, or impossible parts?”

That is why local capacity mapping comes first.

## 24. Repairability information changes buying behaviour too

Repair policy should not begin only after something breaks.

The European Commission's behavioural research found that providing repairability information can steer consumers toward more repairable products.

One study found products with repairability information were selected more often than otherwise equivalent products without it.

Sources:
- https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/46076b42-669a-11eb-aeb5-01aa75ed71a1
- https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/sites/default/files/ec_circular_economy_final_report.pdf

Australia's Productivity Commission has also recommended piloting repairability/durability labelling for whitegoods and consumer electronics.

Source:
https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/repair/report/

### Dubbo opportunity

Create a simple local **Repairable Choice** guide for common purchases:

- phone;
- laptop;
- TV;
- washing machine;
- vacuum;
- mower.

Score practical questions:

- battery replaceable?
- common spare parts locally available?
- local repair option?
- repair manuals?
- software support?
- proprietary locks?
- common repair cost?
- warranty?

This helps residents avoid buying tomorrow's e-waste.

## 25. Repair Cafés work best as a gateway, not as the whole system

The Australian Repair Network currently describes around **110 repair cafés/community repair initiatives** across Australia.

Source:
https://www.griffith.edu.au/arts-education-law/griffith-law-school/research/australian-repair-network

Repair Café International's historical RepairMonitor data found **65% of presented items were repaired** in the 2018 dataset; electrical/electronic repairs were less successful than simpler mechanical/textile repairs.

Source:
https://repaircafe.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/RepairMonitor_analysis_2018_ENGLISH-1.pdf

A 2026 study of two scalable Repair Café adaptations found:

### Weekly appointment repair hub
- 75 sessions;
- 234 devices;
- **42% repaired**;
- small user contribution;
- paid + volunteer repairers.

### Workplace Repair Cafés
- 109 devices assessed;
- **41% repaired**;
- repair brought directly to employees.

Source:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212827126007031

### Dubbo design lesson

Do not make a once-a-month Repair Café carry the entire repair strategy.

Use three layers:

1. **Repair Café** — culture, diagnosis, simple fixes, learning;
2. **booked Repair Hub / Repair Check** — predictable triage and referrals;
3. **commercial repair network** — skilled, warrantied, safety-critical work.

A workplace version could be piloted with a large Dubbo employer to reduce the inconvenience barrier.

## 26. Inclusion matters: do not make repair feel like a men's technical club

Research on Repair Cafés has documented inclusion problems where volunteer cultures become overly male-dominated or technically intimidating.

Source:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42854-022-00031-x

### Dubbo implication

Design the Repair Café deliberately around:

- welcoming hosts;
- plain language;
- textiles/sewing as well as electronics;
- family-friendly activities;
- older and younger volunteers;
- no expectation that the visitor must know tools;
- volunteers explaining rather than showing off;
- clear disability/accessibility consideration.

The public message should be:

> **You do not need to know how to repair anything to come.**

## 29. Recommended Dubbo incentive stack — evidence-ranked

### Tier 1 — do first

#### 1. Repair Check
**Evidence strength: very high**

Free/low-cost diagnosis with:
- fault;
- likely success;
- cost/range;
- turnaround;
- warranty;
- repair-vs-replace comparison.

Why first:
- directly addresses uncertainty;
- cheap compared with full subsidy;
- creates data;
- creates the habit of considering repair.

#### 2. Repair Dubbo directory
**Evidence strength: high**

One route into local repair.

Why:
- reduces search cost;
- makes fragmented capacity visible.

#### 3. Transparent quote + warranty standard
**Evidence strength: very high**

Participating repairers display:
- diagnosis fee;
- expected cost/range;
- turnaround;
- warranty;
- approval threshold for extra work.

Why:
- cost information and warranty are among the highest-value attributes.

### Tier 2 — pilot once capacity is mapped

#### 4. Instant Repair Bonus
**Evidence strength: high**

Test:
- 30–50% support;
- category-specific caps;
- immediate invoice reduction.

Do not reimburse residents weeks later.

#### 5. Monthly Repair Café
**Evidence strength: high for culture/access; moderate for repair volume**

Purpose:
- simple repairs;
- diagnosis;
- skill-sharing;
- social proof;
- commercial referral.

#### 6. Donor-parts network
**Evidence strength: practical/structural**

Use safe, tested recovered parts where appropriate to reduce cost and parts scarcity.

### Tier 3 — add if pilot identifies the need

#### 7. Loan devices
Phones/laptops.

#### 8. Workplace Repair Days
Bring repair to residents instead of asking residents to travel.

#### 9. Repairable Choice guide
Influence purchase decisions before products become waste.

#### 10. Point-of-disposal intervention
Prompt a Repair Check before legal surrender into recycling/disposal.

## 30. What not to do

### Do not lead with guilt
“Save the planet” is supportive, not the main service proposition.

Lead with:
- save money;
- know the price;
- keep the thing you know;
- support local skills.

### Do not subsidise every repair
Measure additionality.

### Do not make residents pay upfront and claim later
Friction kills uptake.

### Do not launch vouchers before repairer capacity is mapped
Demand without capacity creates long waits and teaches the wrong lesson.

### Do not force every repair through volunteers
Commercial repair businesses are part of the circular economy.

### Do not promise every product should be repaired
Some products are genuinely unsafe, obsolete or uneconomic.

### Do not judge success only by kilograms diverted
Measure:
- behaviour;
- confidence;
- local spend;
- product life;
- repeat repair.

## 31. A practical 12-week Dubbo experiment

Before a six-month citywide rebate, run a smaller behaviour experiment.

### Weeks 1–4 — map + baseline

Recruit 8–15 repair businesses/repairers.

Collect:
- categories;
- diagnosis prices;
- repair price examples;
- wait time;
- warranties;
- spare-parts problems;
- weekly spare capacity.

Run baseline survey:

> What would you normally do when this item breaks?

### Weeks 5–8 — Repair Check

Offer 50–100 subsidised/free Repair Checks.

Randomly or deliberately test:
- cost range only;
- cost + success estimate;
- cost + warranty.

Measure repair approval.

### Weeks 9–12 — add small instant bonus

For eligible jobs, add a capped instant subsidy.

Measure:

- extra conversion caused by subsidy;
- average subsidy per additional repair;
- repairer workload;
- waiting time.

Run one Repair Café during the same period.

### Core KPI

> **How many replacement decisions were converted into sensible repairs that otherwise would not have happened?**

That is more meaningful than raw voucher count.

---

## 32. Final answer

The strongest way to incentivise Dubbo residents is not:

> “Convince them to care more about waste.”

It is:

> **remove the reasons repair loses.**

Repair loses when it is:
- hard to find;
- uncertain;
- expensive;
- slow;
- risky;
- socially unusual.

So build the opposite:

> **one trusted first check + transparent price + quick local pathway + warranty + modest instant support + visible successful repairs.**

The recommended public habit remains:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

# 8 October 2026 — deeper evidence update: what actually changes repair behaviour?

This section adds comparator evidence and behavioural research gathered after the first Repair First Dubbo draft. It strengthens the case for a **friction-first, trust-first intervention**, with financial incentives added only after local repair capacity is mapped.

## A. Price is the biggest lever, but convenience and certainty materially change the decision

A 2023 peer-reviewed conjoint study of smartphone and washing-machine repair found **repair price was the most important attribute**, but repair time, warranty/authorisation and other convenience attributes also affected willingness to pay. For higher-value products, convenience mattered more.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921344923002987

A 2026 repair-economics study likewise found repair price relative to replacement price can make entire low-cost product categories economically unattractive to consumers.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S221282712600702X

### Dubbo implication

Do not treat the resident's choice as:

**repair price vs new-product price**

It is really:

**repair price + uncertainty + search effort + travel + downtime + perceived risk**  
versus  
**new product + certainty + immediate availability + warranty**

So Repair First Dubbo should compete on the **whole experience**, not only price.

---

## B. Trust, availability and effort are measurable repair barriers

A 2026 cross-country circular-consumption study found repair behaviour was strongly reduced by:

- lack of trust;
- poor availability;
- perceived effort;

while belief in product durability increased repair engagement.

Source:  
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43615-026-01147-z

A 2026 COM-B study of 1,512 New Zealand consumers found **motivation and social opportunity** were key predictors of repair intention; collaborative repair interviews reinforced that repair behaviour depends on the surrounding opportunity/capability system, not just attitudes.

Source:  
https://doi.org/10.1108/JCM-01-2025-7575

### Dubbo implication

A good program should deliberately build:

**Capability**
- diagnosis;
- skills;
- repair information;
- tools/parts.

**Opportunity**
- visible repairers;
- easy booking;
- local access;
- loan devices;
- parts availability;
- Repair Café.

**Motivation**
- lower effective price;
- visible savings;
- warranty;
- local success stories;
- social proof.

This is a better framework than a generic sustainability campaign.

---

## C. Repair Cafés attract people because they lack skills/materials, not simply because they want a social event

A 2024 study of Repair Café visitors found key drivers included:

- environmental concern;
- access to knowledge, skills and materials they did not have;
- demonstrating the importance of repair;
- connection to a broader repair movement.

Meeting people or experimenting with repair techniques was not a major driver for every visitor.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666791624000162

### Dubbo implication

Do not advertise a Dubbo Repair Café primarily as:

> “Come learn electronics and socialise.”

A stronger front-door message is:

> **Bring the broken thing. You do not need repair skills. We will help you understand whether it can be fixed and what the next step is.**

Learning and community can happen naturally after the resident has a useful reason to attend.

---

## D. NSW comparator: North Sydney Fix-It Hub proves a free booked repair service can achieve high repair rates

North Sydney Council's Fix-It Hub trial recorded:

- **182 items** brought in from November 2023 to May 2024;
- an **80% repair success rate**;
- free booked Saturday workshops;
- volunteer fixers;
- repairs ranging from appliances/electronics to furniture, clothes, garden tools and toys.

Council made the program ongoing after the trial.

Source:  
https://www.northsydney.nsw.gov.au/news/article/202/get-it-fixed-at-the-coal-loader

Later North Sydney reporting says the Fix-It Hub achieved about **85% successful repairs** and estimated **1,176 kg CO2-e avoided** through lamp/toaster repairs, while the adjacent Sydney Library of Things demonstrated direct household savings from borrowing rather than buying.

Source:  
https://www.northsydney.nsw.gov.au/nsn

### Dubbo implication

A Repair Café/Fix-It Hub is not merely symbolic. A booked, well-supported service can resolve a large share of presented items.

The North Sydney model also shows the value of co-locating or closely linking:

**repair + borrowing + practical circular-economy education**

rather than running each initiative in isolation.

---

## E. Australian comparator: Sunshine Repair Cafe shows trust and habit-building take time

Sustainability Victoria reports that Sunshine Repair Cafe:

- diverted **6.2 tonnes** of household waste;
- reached **239 people**;
- used **277 volunteer hours**;
- runs on a regular monthly schedule;
- uses appointments to match volunteers to likely repair categories;
- promotes through Facebook/Instagram and word of mouth;
- explicitly wants residents to adopt a **learning-to-fix** habit.

Source:  
https://www.sustainability.vic.gov.au/news/news-articles/fix-dont-toss-repair-cafes-teach-community-to-repair-re-use-refurbish

### Dubbo implication

A repair culture is built through **repeat visibility and good experiences**, not a single launch event.

A monthly predictable event is more useful than irregular one-off sustainability days.

---

## F. Repair bonuses clearly influence behaviour when the discount is simple and immediate

### Vienna

Vienna's city repair voucher covered **50% of eligible repair cost up to €100** and could cover repair estimates up to **€55**.

The city's 2022/23 environmental report says its earlier voucher program successfully repaired **more than 35,000 items**, with an estimated **2,700 tonnes CO2 saved**.

Source:  
https://www.wien.gv.at/spezial/umweltbericht/ressourcenschonung-kreislaufwirtschaft-starken/wiener-reparaturbon-ein-zweites-leben-fur-alltagsgegenstande/

The design is important:
- discount is applied through participating repairers;
- residents do not have to recover the subsidy through a slow reimbursement process;
- repair estimates themselves can be subsidised.

Current Vienna scheme information:  
https://www.wien.gv.at/umwelt/wiener-reparaturbon

### France

France's national Repair Bonus is deducted directly from repair invoices through accredited repairers.

In 2024, ecosystem reported **715,227 repairs** under the bonus, and reported that **50% of beneficiaries** said the bonus had a strong influence on their decision to repair.

Source:  
https://www.ecosystem.eco/document/350

By 19 May 2026, ecosystem reported **2,232,845 repairs** had benefited from the Repair Bonus, with more than 1,200 accredited repair businesses in the network.

Source:  
https://pro.ecosystem.eco/profil/reparateur

### Dubbo implication

Financial incentives are not theoretical. They can change behaviour at scale.

But the useful design lesson is:

> **Make the benefit immediate and make diagnosis/quotes affordable too.**

Do not require residents to:
1. pay full price;
2. submit paperwork;
3. wait for reimbursement.

---

## G. A subsidy should not be the first thing Dubbo launches

The evidence suggests this sequence:

### Step 1 — map repair capacity

Before generating extra demand, determine:
- who repairs what;
- current wait times;
- diagnosis fees;
- common parts constraints;
- repair warranties;
- spare weekly capacity;
- safety/licensing boundaries.

### Step 2 — launch Repair Check

Offer one trusted first diagnosis/referral.

Output:
- fault/likely fault;
- repairable yes/no;
- indicative price/range;
- likely turnaround;
- warranty where applicable;
- repair-versus-replace recommendation.

Public habit:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

### Step 3 — make the local repair market visible

Create a trusted Dubbo directory covering:
- electronics/computers;
- phones;
- appliances;
- bikes;
- textiles;
- shoes;
- furniture/tools;
- specialist categories.

### Step 4 — add transparent quote/warranty standards

Repairers in the network should make clear:
- diagnosis fee;
- authorised maximum before re-approval;
- expected turnaround;
- warranty;
- data-handling conditions for digital devices.

### Step 5 — pilot a small instant Repair Bonus

Only once spare capacity is known.

Test:
- a percentage discount;
- category-specific caps;
- direct discount at the repairer;
- extra support for diagnostic quotes if appropriate.

Measure whether the subsidy **caused an additional repair** rather than paying for one that was already going to happen.

- parts shortages;
- customer trust/satisfaction;
- estimated extra product life.

---

# What Dubbo should not do

- Do not lead with guilt or generic environmental messaging.
- Do not launch a large voucher before mapping repair capacity.
- Do not require full upfront payment followed by reimbursement paperwork.
- Do not make volunteer repair compete unfairly with commercial safety-critical work.
- Do not claim every broken item should be repaired.
- Do not measure only kilograms diverted.
- Do not call a venue/organisation a partner until it has agreed.
- Do not make a Repair Café feel like a technical club for people who already know tools.

Public-facing promise:

> **You do not need repair skills. Bring the item and get a clear next step.**

---

# Final evidence-based answer

To make repair beat replacement in Dubbo, make repair:

**cheaper enough + easier + faster + more certain + more trusted + more visible.**

The strongest starting behaviour is not:

> “Become a repair person.”

It is:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

Then build the local system around that habit.
