**Research date:** 8 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Identify realistic venues for a small, inclusive Dubbo Repair Café pilot and longer-term community repair program.

## Decision standard

A good Repair Café venue needs more than an empty room. The research scores venues on:

- inclusive public access;
- central/easy-to-find location;
- tables and chairs;
- power availability;
- toilets and accessibility;
- ability to tolerate tools, parts and minor mess;
- safe separation of repair stations;
- public liability / booking requirements;
- cost;
- weekend/after-hours availability;
- storage/setup constraints;
- whether the host organisation is aligned with community learning, repair, sustainability or inclusion.

The first pilot should **not** require permanent storage, heavy machinery or exposed mains-electrical repair.

## Recommended operating model

The strongest model is a **rotating pop-up Repair Café** rather than committing immediately to one permanent venue.

Repair Café Silicon Valley provides a useful current benchmark: it uses libraries, community centres, museums, a university, a Buddhist centre and a hackerspace. Repair Café International reports that this pop-up approach commonly attracts 30–40 volunteers and about 150 visitors per event there.

For Dubbo, rotating venues could:
- test different neighbourhoods and audiences;
- let host organisations bring their own community;
- avoid early rent/storage commitments;
- discover which venue type actually works;
- keep the project inclusive instead of making it feel owned by one demographic or organisation.

Source:
https://www.repaircafe.org/en/pop-up-repair-cafes-in-unusual-venues-from-museums-to-universities/

# Ranked Dubbo venue candidates

## 1. Western Plains Cultural Centre / Community Arts Centre
**Address:** 76 Wingewarra Street, Dubbo  
**Overall pilot fit:** **9/10 — strongest all-round public venue lead**  
**Evidence:** VERIFIED CURRENT

### Why it fits

The Western Plains Cultural Centre publicly offers spaces for:
- community meetings;
- hands-on creativity;
- workshops/classes;
- community and cultural groups.

The Community Arts Centre includes:
- a classroom;
- an **Art Studio with sinks, drying racks and preparation benches**;
- a courtyard;
- toilets within the building.

The main facility has multiple meeting rooms and an auditorium. The overall site is explicitly managed as general community-use land for social connection, cultural expression and recreation.

The centre is also already associated with Waste 2 Art and other sustainability/community programming, so repair/reuse would not be culturally alien to the venue.

### Best space to inspect first

**Community Arts Centre Art Studio**

Why:
- sinks;
- preparation benches;
- designed for hands-on activity;
- more tolerant of workshop-style use than a carpeted meeting room;
- allows separate tables/stations.

The **Classroom** is the fallback for computer/textile/bike-triage sessions if the Art Studio is unavailable.

### Constraints / unknowns

- Repair Café-style tool use must be explicitly approved by WPCC.
- Need to clarify whether soldering, battery-powered tools and bike repairs are permitted.
- Need current 2026/27 community-group hire rates.
- After-hours staffing may add cost.
- No assumption of storage between events.
- Public liability / risk-management requirements need confirmation.
- Need site inspection for power outlet count, trip-hazard management and accessible entry.

### Evidence
- Hire a Space: https://www.westernplainsculturalcentre.org/hireaspace
- WPCC Plan of Management: https://yoursay.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/plan-management-western-plains-cultural-centre
- Council Book a Space: https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/community-groups/community-awards-and-tours/book-a-space

### Further investigation
Ask WPCC:
1. Would the Art Studio permit a community Repair Café?
2. Are low-voltage electronics, laptops, sewing, bikes and hand-tool work acceptable?
3. What activities are prohibited?
4. How many power outlets/circuits are available?
5. What is the 2026/27 not-for-profit/community rate?
6. What public-liability cover is required?
7. Can tables be rearranged into repair stations?
8. Is Saturday/Sunday recurring monthly use possible?

## 2. Dubbo Pipe Band Hall
**Address:** 116 Darling Street, Dubbo  
**Overall pilot fit:** **8.5/10 — best low-cost hall lead if eligibility is met**  
**Evidence:** VERIFIED CURRENT venue availability; exact 2026/27 fee/facilities require confirmation.

### Why it fits

Council explicitly says the Pipe Band Hall is available **only to not-for-profit community groups** for:
- workshops;
- training;
- meetings.

That is unusually close to a Repair Café's intended use.

The hall has recently hosted inclusive workshops/community gatherings, showing it is used for public community programming rather than only Pipe Band activity.

Historical/current-adjacent Council fees show it has been a low-cost venue. The adopted 2025/26 community-group fee was **$37.50**; the exact 2026/27 rate should be confirmed before budgeting.

Council booking terms also provide a clear insurance framework: regular groups/incorporated bodies are expected to provide evidence of public liability insurance, historically at not less than $20 million.

### Strengths
- central location;
- workshop/training use explicitly allowed;
- low-cost community-hall model;
- likely enough open floor area for multiple tables;
- can feel neutral and inclusive if branded as a public event;
- suitable for a 2–4 hour Saturday pilot.

### Constraints / unknowns
- Need current 2026/27 fee.
- Need exact capacity, accessibility, toilets, table inventory and power layout.
- Need to establish whether a new Repair Café group can qualify before incorporation, or whether a partner not-for-profit must be the hirer.
- Need explicit permission for repair tools/electronics.
- Need risk-management/public-liability confirmation.
- Council is reviewing community halls in 2026, so future arrangements may change.

### Evidence
- Council Book a Space: https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/community-groups/community-awards-and-tours/book-a-space
- Council hall booking conditions: https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/ArticleDocuments/11106/Community%20Halls%20Booking%20Application%202022.pdf.aspx?Embed=Y
- 2025/26 fees: https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/ArticleDocuments/290/Dubbo%20Regional%20Council%20-%2020252026%20Fees%20and%20Charges%20%28adopted%2024%20June%202025%29.PDF.aspx
- Current hall review: https://yoursay.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/review-community-halls
- Recent public event evidence: https://events.humanitix.com/a-place-to-call-home-campaign-collaboration-workshop-dubbo

### Further investigation
- Inspect room layout.
- Confirm current tables/chairs.
- Count safe power points.
- Confirm wheelchair access and accessible toilet.
- Confirm kitchen/tea facilities.
- Ask whether Council will permit soldering and small tool use.
- Ask whether Council or a partner organisation could sponsor the booking for a pilot.

## 3. Connecting Community Services / Dubbo Neighbourhood Centre
**Address:** 31–33 Church Street, Dubbo  
**Overall pilot fit:** **8/10 — strongest community-inclusion partner venue**  
**Evidence:** VERIFIED CURRENT

### Why it fits

Connecting Community Services is a community-based not-for-profit that explicitly works across:
- seniors;
- youth and families;
- disability;
- Aboriginal family wellbeing;
- women's safety;
- migrant support;
- general community information/referral.

Its venue-hire offer includes:
- **Conference Room:** about 14 people U-shaped with tables/chairs or 22 theatre style;
- **Board Room:** 9 around a table;
- TV/HDMI;
- Wi-Fi;
- kitchen use on request;
- whiteboard/lectern in the Conference Room.

This is a particularly good fit if the Repair Café wants to reach people beyond the usual technically confident/older-male repair audience.

### Strong use case
A **small first pilot** focused on:
- laptops/computers;
- phones/tablets diagnosis;
- textiles;
- small mechanical items;
- repair advice/triage;
- volunteer information session.

### Constraints
- Normal hire availability is Monday–Friday.
- Conference/Board Rooms can be considered for after-hours access only by special request.
- The room is relatively small for bikes, furniture or multiple noisy stations.
- Public liability evidence is required.
- Need approval for tools, soldering or any activity beyond ordinary training/workshops.
- Exact current hire rate should be requested.

### Evidence
- Room hire: https://ccsd.org.au/community/room-hire
- Room hire terms: https://ccsd.org.au/_MediaDownloads/E-PR02%20Room%20Hire%20Application%20Form%2C%20CCS%20Dubbo%20V.%2028.06.2024.pdf?downloadable=1
- Organisation/services: https://ccsd.org.au/

### Further investigation
- Ask whether a Saturday or evening pilot is possible.
- Ask whether room hire could be partnered/sponsored.
- Confirm power, workbench/table protection and tool restrictions.
- Explore whether CCS would help recruit residents who face cost/digital-access barriers.

## 4. Dubbo Library meeting rooms
**Address:** corner Macquarie and Talbragar Streets, Dubbo  
**Overall pilot fit:** **8/10 for light/computer repair; 5/10 for full mixed Repair Café**  
**Evidence:** VERIFIED CURRENT

### Why it fits

Macquarie Regional Library makes meeting rooms available for public community activities.

For not-for-profit groups:
- **meeting-room hire is free**.

The current listed commercial rates are:
- small meeting room: $22/hour;
- medium meeting room (Dubbo): $44/hour.

Dubbo Library already runs free Tech Help, so residents are used to seeing it as a friendly place for technology learning. The Library also has adaptive computer-access equipment and current accessibility initiatives.

### Best use
- volunteer recruitment/info night;
- computer/laptop-only Repair Café;
- digital safety/phone help;
- repair demonstrations;
- planning meeting;
- apprentice/learner sessions.

### Why it may not suit the full model
The Library's existing Tech Help explicitly does **not** provide computer hardware repair. That does not automatically prohibit a separately approved community event, but a general Repair Café involving bikes, tools, soldering, messy furniture work or mains appliances would need specific permission and may be inappropriate for the room.

### Evidence
- Library facilities: https://www.mrl.nsw.gov.au/about-us/using-the-library/library-facilities
- Current fees: https://www.mrl.nsw.gov.au/about-us/using-the-library/fees-and-charges
- Tech Help context: https://www.mrl.nsw.gov.au/programs-and-events/programs-for-adults/tech-help

### Further investigation
- Ask Library Manager whether a community-hosted repair event is permissible.
- Confirm table capacity and power.
- Ask whether soldering is prohibited.
- Ask whether a computer/textile-only pilot would be acceptable.
- Determine whether the Library could promote rather than host a wider Repair Café.

## 5. Dubbo Community Men's Shed
**Address:** 171 Talbragar Street, Dubbo  
**Overall pilot fit:** **7.5/10 as technical/workshop partner; unproven as inclusive public venue**  
**Evidence:** VERIFIED CURRENT organisation, workshop culture; inclusive-event conditions UNVERIFIED.

### Why it fits
The Australian Men's Shed Association lists:
- wood and metal work;
- garage sale.

Existing local research also shows a community-project/restoration culture.

Physically, a Shed may solve problems that meeting rooms do not:
- tools;
- workbenches;
- workshop culture;
- practical repair knowledge;
- tolerance for sawdust/noise/parts.

### Why it is not ranked first
The proposed Dubbo Repair Café needs to be visibly open to:
- women;
- young adults;
- families;
- people with disability;
- culturally diverse residents;
- people who do not want to join a Men's Shed.

Current public evidence does not establish that the Shed would host an all-genders/all-community event or that computer/electronics repair is within its capability.

### Evidence
https://mensshed.org/sheds/dubbo-community-mens-shed-inc/

### Further investigation
Ask directly:
- Could a Repair Café event be open to everyone, regardless of gender/membership?
- Can non-members use the workshop under supervision?
- Which tools can public visitors use?
- Is insurance extended to an external event?
- Which members have repair/electronics/bike/furniture skills?
- Would the Shed host, co-host or provide volunteers elsewhere?

## 7. Dubbo Showground — Centenary Pavilion
**Location:** Dubbo Showground, corner Fitzroy and Wingewarra Streets  
**Overall pilot fit:** **5/10 for first pilot; strong future large-event option**  
**Evidence:** VERIFIED CURRENT

The Centenary Pavilion is a 1,063 m² fully enclosed exhibition-style space with concrete floor and large access doors.

That makes it physically excellent for:
- a large annual repair/reuse expo;
- many repair stations;
- bikes/furniture;
- community partner stalls.

But it is significantly larger than needed for a first 8–20-item Repair Café and is likely to create unnecessary setup/cost complexity.

### Evidence
- https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/Showground/our-venues
- https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/Showground/book-your-event

### Further investigation
Only pursue after demand is proven:
- current not-for-profit hire fee;
- insurance;
- tables/power;
- minimum booking/setup;
- heating/cooling;
- storage;
- whether Council would subsidise a circular-economy event.

# Partnership venues worth exploring even without verified public-hire evidence

These are **leads, not ranked hire options**:

- **TAFE NSW Dubbo** — potential vocational skills/student partnership, especially electronics/IT/mechanical/textiles, but public Repair Café venue availability has not been verified.
- **Charles Sturt University Dubbo** — potential student/community partnership; public workshop/event approval needs verification.
- **local schools** — possible later family/student repair days; child-safety, insurance and site approval create more complexity.
- **commercial repair businesses** — better as referral/volunteer/parts partners than as neutral public venue hosts.
- **church/community halls** — potentially inexpensive and inclusive, but each needs direct checks for insurance, workshop activity and availability.

## Further investigation
- Contact TAFE/CSU community engagement teams.
- Ask Council/Anél which venue partners already participate in sustainability/community events.
- Invite venue offers through the public website rather than attempting to identify every private hall in advance.

# Suggested venue inspection checklist

Before committing to any venue, physically inspect:

1. step-free entry;
2. accessible toilet;
3. parking/drop-off;
4. room capacity;
5. tables/chairs;
6. number/location of power outlets;
7. electrical circuit limits;
8. lighting;
9. ventilation;
10. sinks;
11. floor protection/cleanability;
12. safe bike/furniture work area;
13. children/family visibility;
14. first-aid access;
15. fire exits;
16. lithium-battery isolation process;
17. soldering/tool policy;
18. noise limits;
19. setup/pack-down time;
20. storage (if any);
21. public-liability requirement;
22. risk-management requirement;
23. after-hours/key/alarm procedure;
24. recurring monthly availability;
25. total real cost.
