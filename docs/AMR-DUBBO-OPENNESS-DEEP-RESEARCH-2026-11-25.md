# Getting AMR Dubbo to say yes

AMR is **Australian Metal Recycling**, a trading name of **Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd** (ABN 51 640 765 091). It's a small, privately owned NSW scrap metal business with **seven licensed yards**. Its Dubbo yard is the old **Matthews Metal Management (MMM) site at 34 Mountbatten Drive**. AMR's website says the business was founded by **John and Gordon Matthews, the original owners of MMM**, who bought the regional yards back after a spell under InfraBuild. So the decision sits with a family owner-operator, not a corporate sustainability team, and your AMR contact will almost certainly need to take your proposal to them. Nothing public stands in the way of a yes. AMR has no stated policy against reuse, shows no data-destruction contracts or recycler certifications, NSW has no e-waste landfill ban, and a whole computer is worth only a few dollars as scrap. Nothing public paves the way either. AMR talks only about metal recovery, has no record of any reuse partnership, and I found **no Australian case of a scrap yard formally setting computers aside for an outside refurbisher**, so you're asking them to do something new. My judgement is that a small, written, zero-cost trial has reasonable odds if you clear four things first: data liability, legal title, whether any of AMR's computers are tied to the national TV and computer recycling scheme (NTCRS) or a downstream contract, and lithium-ion batteries, which AMR doesn't accept. Your best move is a one-page proposal your AMR contact can hand straight to the owners, with every risk sitting on your side.

*How to read this:* **Confirmed** means a cited source says it. **Inference** means it's my reasoning from the sources, so check it before you rely on it. None of this is legal advice.

## Four rule sets decide what AMR can safely agree to

### The NTCRS rewards only recycling, but nothing puts AMR in it

**Confirmed:** The NTCRS has run since 2011. It gives households and small businesses free, industry-funded collection through co-regulatory arrangements ([National EPA](https://www.nationalepa.gov.au/node/282)). The 2021 Rules define "recycle" as recovering usable materials and require processing by an **AS/NZS 5377-certified** recycler. They set a **90% material recovery target**, never mention reuse, and say nothing about who owns collected products ([Rules 2021](https://legislation.gov.au/F2021L00624/asmade/2021-05-25/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)). The 2014 reporting methodology says reuse or refurbishment "does not count towards the target" ([methodology](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/material-recovery-measurement-reporting-methodology.pdf)). A 2025 evaluation recommends more oversight of "the downstream flow" ([APO](https://apo.org.au/node/331180)). The Australian Digital Inclusion Alliance (ADIA) says this recycling-only design means working devices often get dismantled, and wants reuse counted ([ADIA](https://www.digitalinclusion.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/A-Digital-Inclusion-Approach-to-Device-Donation-and-Reuse.pdf)). No source links AMR to the NTCRS or to any co-regulatory arrangement.

**Inference:** If some of AMR Dubbo's computers come in under an NTCRS arrangement, or go to a processor under contract, diverting them could breach chain-of-custody terms. That is the most likely hard "no". Computers AMR takes in as ordinary scrap carry far fewer strings. Ask about this first, and if you need to, limit the trial to stock from outside the scheme.

### NSW has no e-waste landfill ban, so your shed is the sticking point

**Confirmed:** A 2025 bill to ban e-waste from NSW landfill was voted down on 18 September 2025 and lapsed ([NSW Parliament](https://www.parliament.nsw.gov.au/bills/Pages/bill-details.aspx?pk=18735)). Under the POEO Act, waste storage, processing and resource recovery need an EPA licence above set thresholds ([NSW EPA](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/150105-guide-to-licensing-factsheet.pdf)). **Section 143 makes it an offence to transport waste to a place that can't lawfully be used as a waste facility** ([bill explanatory note](https://www.parliament.nsw.gov.au/bill/files/18735/First%20Print.pdf)). 2025 amendments may require a storage licence where lithium-ion waste batteries pass hazardous-waste thresholds ([NSW EPA](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/Working-together/For-local-government/environmental-legislation-amendment-act-2025)). NSW's April 2026 battery reform covers small and removable batteries under 5 kg ([NSW EPA](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/news/epamedia/260407-nsw-leads-the-country-in-battery-reform-to-fight-fires-and-pollution)).

**Inference:** A computer handed to a scrap yard is probably "waste" in AMR's hands. I haven't checked that against the Act's own definition. Ten to twenty units sit far below any licensing threshold, but s 143 turns on where the waste goes, not how much of it there is. Three moves lower the risk. Have AMR select units as reusable goods before processing, with title passing to you in writing. Keep stock small and return rejects quickly. And ask the EPA's Environment Line whether a small reuse pilot needs anything. You also need to confirm the premises used for this can lawfully take it. Council knocked back the Whylandra proposal in October, so a written EPA view would strengthen your hand with both AMR and Council.

### The privacy duty sits with the original owner, but the headline would land on AMR

**Confirmed:** APP 11.2 requires an entity covered by the Australian Privacy Principles to destroy or de-identify personal information once it no longer needs it ([OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information)). A PwC study bought a second-hand phone and tablet for $50 to test how much data leaks out through e-waste ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/australian-it-pros-mitigate-data-leaks-e-waste/)). **NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 was finalised in September 2025.** It supersedes Rev. 1 and puts the focus on an organisation's whole sanitisation program ([NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final)). An erasure vendor says Rev. 2 keeps Clear, Purge and Destroy and warns against relying on Clear for modern flash media; treat that as a secondary source ([BitRaser](https://www.bitraser.com/article/nist-800-88-r2-media-sanitization-guidelines.php)). The Smith Family wipes "every donated device" with Blancco ([The Smith Family](https://www.thesmithfamily.com.au/get-involved/partnerships/device-donation)).

**Inference:** Nobody knows whether AMR itself falls under the Privacy Act, because that depends on its size, which isn't public. Either way its exposure is reputational. You're right to cite Rev. 2. Because Rev. 2 is about programs, back the Blancco certificates with a short written procedure covering intake, erase, verify, certificate and destroy-on-fail, instead of leaning on the tool alone. Use Purge-level methods on SSDs.

### Electrical safety and scrap law govern what you hand out

**Confirmed:** NSW says anyone who "sell[s] or supply[s] secondhand electrical equipment" has legal safety obligations ([NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/safety-home/electrical-safety/buying-and-using-electrical-appliances)). Non-declared items still have to be safe ([NSW Fair Trading](https://www.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/1084419/Safe-electrical-goods.pdf)). NSW government pages name both a 2017 Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act and an "Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act 2024", so check which one is current ([NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/selling-goods-and-services/electrical-approval-schemes)).

**Inference:** Donating probably counts as supplying. Test and tag the machines, and make sure the chargers carry approval marks. Logging serial numbers and keeping the arrangement cash-free deals with any worry about stolen goods under NSW scrap metal law.

## No scrap yard has done this publicly, but every piece has a precedent

**Confirmed:** Australia's refurbish-and-donate sector runs mostly on planned corporate and government IT refreshes. WorkVentures' National Device Bank, for example, takes donations from companies and agencies ([WorkVentures](https://workventures.com.au/national-device-bank/)). The closest matches to your model are recyclers that sort for reuse in-house. **Beyond eWaste** at Charmhaven on the Central Coast offers free drop-off with "100% secure industry standard data destruction" and gives refurbished computers to women's shelters, Men's Sheds and others ([RNY](https://recyclingnearyou.com.au/business/30670)). **Substation33** in Logan started as an e-waste recycler and now processes over 200,000 kg a year ([Sustainable Brisbane](https://www.sustainablebrisbane.com.au/tackling-e-waste-with-your-local-community/)); in 2020 it rebuilt 700+ computers in four months, backed by Logan City Council ([YFS](https://www.yfs.org.au/?p=6646)). **Bendigo E-Waste**, Joe's business, calls itself "committed to repair or recovery" before it recovers materials ([RNY](https://recyclingnearyou.com.au/material/home/televisions/HumeVIC)).

Closer to home, **Parkes Shire Council** gives its retired PCs, tablets and laptops to the Parkes Men's Shed because IT dealers and auction houses lost interest. The article is undated (about 2018 going by its URL) and doesn't say how data was handled ([Parkes Champion-Post](https://www.parkeschampionpost.com.au/story/5596298/new-homes-for-used-technology/)). **Tenterfield Shire's May 2025 ICT disposal policy** wipes data with shredding software and sells "as is where is", accepting no responsibility ([Tenterfield](https://www.tenterfield.nsw.gov.au/content/uploads/2025/06/Disposal-of-ICT-Assets-Policy-May-2025.pdf)). Westpac runs periodic onsite inspections of WorkVentures, which supplies sanitisation certificates ([ADIA](https://www.digitalinclusion.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/A-Digital-Inclusion-Approach-to-Device-Donation-and-Reuse.pdf)). Wanneroo Men's Shed has put 1,000+ computers back into its community since 2010 and sends about 98% of dismantled material to recyclers ([Wanneroo Times](https://www.communitynews.com.au/wanneroo-times/news/shed-team-refurbishes-pcs)).

Policy is moving your way. The **National Device Reuse Principles**, agreed on 4 September 2026, call for managing "security, records and risk" and sending unsuitable devices to responsible recycling ([Dept of Finance](https://www.finance.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-08/National-Device-Reuse-Principles.pdf)). The Tech Reuse for Good Charter has big corporates signed on ([Minister Wells](https://minister.infrastructure.gov.au/wells/media-release/new-tech-reuse-charter-deliver-free-devices-families-and-children-need)).

**Confirmed (demand and specs):** **20.6% of Australians are digitally excluded or highly excluded.** Outside the capitals, 12.4% are mobile-only ([ADII 2025](https://digitalinclusionindex.org.au/the-2025-findings/)). Government refresh fleets run **50–70% reusable** ([ADIA](https://www.digitalinclusion.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/A-Digital-Inclusion-Approach-to-Device-Donation-and-Reuse.pdf)), but no data exists for scrap-yard intake. WorkVentures prefers 5th-gen i5 or newer ([WorkVentures](https://workventures.com.au/national-device-bank/)). Windows 10 support ended on 14 October 2025 ([iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com/News/113686/windows-10-is-dead-your-pc-doesnt-have-to-be)), and Windows 11 needs TPM 2.0 and Secure Boot ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/107157-charities-face-tough-choices-security-e-waste-windows.html)). Computerbank installs Linux Mint on every machine it refurbishes ([Computerbank](https://computerbank.org.au/about)). Dubbo Library runs free one-on-one tech help ([Macquarie Regional Library](https://www.mrl.nsw.gov.au/learn/tech-help)).

**Inference:** Scrap-yard machines will be older and more stripped than refresh fleets, so expect a yield well below 50–70%. Pitch the trial as "let's measure it together" rather than promising a number. Laptops are what recipients want most. Pairing devices with the library's tech help answers the "a device alone isn't enough" problem.

## Answer the objections before your AMR contact has to

The objections are inference: nothing public records AMR raising them. They're drawn from the barriers documented above.

| Likely objection | Your answer |
|---|---|
| "What if data leaks with our name on it?" | You wipe before anything else, with a Blancco certificate per device tied to its serial and a copy to AMR. Drives that fail erasure are physically destroyed. AMR can inspect your log and workspace any time, as Westpac does with WorkVentures. |
| "Can we even give this away?" | A signed collection docket passes title and risk to you, "as is where is", with no warranty from AMR (Tenterfield's wording). |
| "Our e-waste goes under a scheme or contract" | The trial uses only stock that's free of those strings, or you get the arrangement's written OK. |
| "Who sorts it?" | One simple rule, e.g. complete laptops and desktops that look under about eight years old. You supply a cage, collect on a fixed day, and do their site induction and wear their PPE. |
| "We lose metal" | Every reject comes back within a set number of days. The scrap value at stake is a few dollars a unit. |
| "Lithium batteries?" | You take responsibility for batteries through a separate lawful channel and never return them to AMR. |
| "Who's liable if a donated PC fails?" | You hold public liability cover, indemnify AMR, and test and tag before anything is donated. |
| "What's in it for us?" | A quarterly one-page report: units reused, kg returned, recipients, certificate list. You also give them a local good-news story to use as they wish. |

Then work through these steps in order:

| # | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ring (02) 6882 1033 to confirm hours, your AMR contact's role and who signs off. | The owners, not your AMR contact, decide. |
| 2 | Ask whether any computers come in under the NTCRS or go to a contracted processor, and what happens to laptops with batteries. | This settles the most likely hard no. |
| 3 | Sort out insurance, premises approval, an EPA Environment Line query, a battery channel and test-and-tag. | Your proposal should only promise what you can deliver. |
| 4 | Confirm with Joe how the Blancco certificates will be issued, and in whose name. | AMR needs certificates it can rely on. |
| 5 | Write one page to Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd t/a Australian Metal Recycling: 10–20 units, a fixed period, then a review, with no money changing hands. | It names the right entity, and your AMR contact can pass it straight to Gordon Matthews. |
| 6 | Line up two or three recipient organisations before the first pickup. | It proves demand and stops waste piling up in the shed. |
| 7 | Ask Joe for his intake criteria, wipe workflow and reuse rate, and whether you can quote them. | A working peer example persuades better than theory. |
| 8 | Keep fallbacks warm: Dubbo Scrap Metal (SM-0599), Orana Wool Scrap Metal (SM-0135) and Orreco (SM-0126), all on the [register](https://www.police.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/743500/Public_Register_8_September_2026.pdf). | Don't hang the project on one yard. |

## Conclusion

Your proposal isn't really a green pitch. It's a risk-transfer document. AMR's own language is about value and landfill, and its owners won't lose sleep over a few dollars of scrap. What they need is certainty that nothing leaving their gate can come back to bite them. Oddly, AMR's lack of certifications and scheme ties is good news for you. A small owner-run yard with no visible NTCRS or secure-disposal contracts is probably an easier partner than a certified e-waste recycler, whose paperwork is built to make sure everything ends up shredded.

The trial is also a measurement. Scrap-yard computers are structurally worse stock than refresh fleets, so AMR will most likely be a supplementary source rather than your main supply. If the yield is low, the stronger long-term stream is direct donations from local businesses and councils, the Parkes model. The new National Device Reuse Principles now push government agencies the same way. A clean, documented AMR trial is the track record that opens those doors.
