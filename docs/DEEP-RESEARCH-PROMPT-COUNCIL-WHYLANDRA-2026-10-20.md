# Master deep-research prompt — Dubbo Regional Council e-waste reuse approval (20 October 2026)

## How to use

Copy this prompt into a research-capable assistant with authorised access to the relevant correspondence, current public legislation, Council documents and GitHub repositories. Work from **today's actual law and adopted policies**, not stale secondary summaries. Prior research and recommendations are leads, not legal conclusions. Do not send emails, obtain quotes, move devices or represent Council approval without explicit operator authorisation.

### Mission / question

Act as an interdisciplinary NSW local-government procurement adviser, e-waste/ITAD compliance researcher, privacy and cybersecurity assessor, WHS/lithium safety specialist, risk adviser and contract reviewer. **Find the exact legal, regulatory, procurement, operational, contractual and evidence conditions that would have to be satisfied before Dubbo Regional Council could consider permitting a small, reuse-first, controlled pilot of selected laptops and desktop computers from Whylandra Waste and Recycling Centre.** Produce an approval-ready dossier, not merely an aspirational proposal.

Council has declined a separate collection arrangement at this stage, stating concerns over data sanitisation, privacy, chain of custody, insurance, liability, governance and reputation. It retains an existing e-waste arrangement. Council mentioned formal assessment, approvals and a documented agreement for any future change. These are decision categories, **not a fully specified checklist or commitment to approve**. The actual requirements must be obtained from authoritative material and, where unavailable, translated into precise written questions. Do not publish or quote Council email contents in public repos without the sender's consent; a minimal paraphrase of decision status is enough.

### Scope to be assessed separately

1. **Model A, independent off-site direct donors:** source devices by appointment from known businesses/residents, not through Council or its waste facility; refurbish and donate/sell after compliant assessment. Council collection permission is irrelevant but ordinary law still applies.
2. **Model B, incumbent recycling contractor partnership:** title transfer and diversion organised solely under its valid contractual authority, with Council consent if required; do not presume contractor discretion.
3. **Model C, Council-managed segregated pilot:** Council workers first select screened devices, operator collects only via authorised facility procedure under signed agreement. More Council risk and approvals; no assumption of feasibility.
4. **Model D, longer-term procurement:** public tender, EOI, RFQ or documented lawful exemption for future service, with neutral evaluation criteria and no preferential private benefit.
5. Compare **donation vs sale vs paid secure ITAD**, and **business operation vs Repair Café volunteer event**; do not transpose contracts, insurance, licensing or personal-data consents between them.

Constrain illustrative Phase 0 to laptops/desktops with intact safe components, prearranged tiny batches, no general drop-off, no public scavenging, no server racks, no loose/damaged batteries, no unattended charging, no shredding, no resale until release checks. Any illustrative volume is an assumption requiring agreement.

### Non-negotiable research methodology

- Give each consequential claim a **verifiable primary-source URL, document title, effective/adoption date, section/page or paragraph**, access date and exact applicability. When quoting, short excerpts only and lawful use.
- Evidence states: **LEGAL REQUIREMENT** (cite actual provision); **COUNCIL ADOPTED POLICY/PROCEDURE** (cite version); **CONTRACTUAL REQUIREMENT** (only when verified in an actual document); **GUIDANCE/BEST PRACTICE** (non-binding unless incorporated); **PROPOSED PROJECT CONTROL**; **UNKNOWN — AGENCY CONFIRMATION REQUIRED**; **DRAFT/NOT ADOPTED**.
- Never invent Council insurance amounts, delegated officers, permits, licence thresholds, dates, contractor identity, AS 5377 certification, NTCRS status, existing contract termination rights or third-party sign-offs.
- Work from NSW legislation in force at research date; examine recent amendments, SEPP/Dubbo LEP, POEO thresholds/definitions, Fair Trading classes/exemptions and Commonwealth law. Obtain a qualified local legal opinion for contentious cases.
- Trace any conflicts: Council business page references both $225k and $250k figures; OLG legal threshold and Council draft use $250k incl GST. Confirm the live position and how different advertising/internal thresholds may relate. **Do not treat the 2026 draft Procurement Policy as adopted** without confirmed minutes.
- Don't equate e-waste resource-recovery orders (often about land application) with generic permission to refurbish PCs. Do not assume all laptops are trackable hazardous waste; classify by condition/material and applicable rules.
- Treat missing answers as **open launch gates**, not pass-by-default. Report a confidence level, who can legally decide and whether a written ruling is needed.
- Be privacy preserving: no donor names, device serial numbers, detailed home layout, personal email, customer data, confidential Council correspondence or access credentials in public reports. Private evidence location only.

### Research work packages — investigate ALL 32

**01 — Council decision map:** Map DRC organisational responsibilities: Waste Coordinator, Resource Recovery & Efficiency, Corporate Governance/Risk, Procurement, Legal, IT/Privacy, WHS, facility manager, General Manager, delegated approval officer, elected Council, contractor. Confirm who is decision owner vs adviser and what records evidence a decision.

**02 — Property/title:** At what legal moment is a resident's device surrendered? Who owns it at the drop-off point and after contractor loading? Analyse chattel title, abandonment, site signage/T&Cs, policies and contract transfer. Does a resident's consent alone help after deposition?

**03 — Incumbent agreement:** Establish contractor name/legal entity, contract award/RFQ, expiry/renewal, Whylandra scope, exclusivity, diversion, subcontracting, reuse, title, hazard limits, disposal/certification, insurance and variations. Distinguish public proof from speculation. Seek redacted documents/GIPA if required; no bypass of confidentiality.

**04 — Lawful contracting mechanism:** Classify proposed trial (procurement, sale/disposal of goods, licence, subcontract, unsolicited proposal, partnership/grant, in-kind transfer). Evaluate section 55 Local Government Act 1993, Local Government (General) Regulation 2021 Part 7, OLG Procurement Guidelines and exceptions. Compare what an officer can approve and when Council resolution is required.

**05 — DRC procurement:** Compare 2022 published Procurement Policy and August 2026 draft (public consultation through 27 October). Retrieve current purchasing procedures, quotation requirements, total estimated contract value calculation, conflict/ethics, competitive neutrality, VendorPanel and local-supplier advantage. Flag stale/inconsistent webpages.

**06 — Governance/integrity:** Assess probity, gifts, supplier fairness, conflict of interest, public benefit/value for money, unauthorised selective access to reusable stock, anti-corruption and record keeping. Propose transparent EOI/pilot evaluation criteria rather than creating privileged access.

**07 — Council risk register:** Obtain risk appetite and matrix, current risk policy/ISO 31000 methodology, who can accept residual risk, who signs risk assessments, monitoring frequency, incident tolerance and independent audit expectations.

**08 — Contract terms:** Produce an annotated, solicitor-reviewable heads-of-agreement with parties, recitals, scope, pilot limits, site entry, price/value, title, transfer, custody, waste classification, subcontractors, equipment standards, liability, insurance, indemnity, privacy, IP, confidentiality, FOI/GIPA, audit, records, breach, complaints, incident reporting, recall, termination, exit and dispute resolution.

**09 — Council reputational exposure:** Branding/publicity, Council logo, photography, beneficiary selection/fairness, bad-device recalls, public complaints, downstream dumping, media enquiries and supplier insolvency. Give mitigations and stop triggers.

**10 — Privacy legislation:** NSW PPIP Act, HRIP Act if health data, NSW Privacy Management Plan, mandatory notification of data breaches (where applicable), privacy contacts, contractor vs controller responsibilities, personal-data minimisation and IT security.

**11 — Data-security program:** Build NIST SP 800-88 Rev.2 programme map, ASD Information Security Manual relevance, media risk classification, Clear/Purge/Destroy decision, device-native commands and their independent verification, failed sanitisation, drive-less machines, hidden soldered media, encryption/keys, locked/managed devices, sanitisation vendor validation, immutable evidence and exception approval.

**12 — Chain of custody:** Design complete barcode-to-physical tracking from authorised handover to recycler/donor with twin counts, sealed transfer, timed signatures, exception reconciliation, footage/privacy requirements, locked quarantine, role permissions, tamper evidence, and randomly sampled spot checks. Require an end-to-end worked example with synthetic records.

**13 — Waste definitions:** At each stage decide whether item remains waste vs lawful product; determine what determines end-of-waste/legitimate reuse. Analyse POEO Act, Waste Regulation, EPA resource recovery guidance and special streams, avoiding unfounded blanket exceptions.

**14 — EPA licensing and planning:** Environment protection licences for on-site resource recovery/waste storage/processing/transport at actual volumes; local area/scheduled activities; planning consent under Dubbo Regional LEP 2022, applicable SEPP, facility environment licence and any condition preventing on-site diversion; seek NSW EPA and DRC Planning written determinations.

**15 — Waste transport:** Classify safely embedded batteries versus damaged/loose cells, other trackable/hazardous waste, transport thresholds, road/DG packaging, vehicle insurance, tracking, destination acceptance and consequences of moving waste to an unauthorised receiving premises.

**16 — Site WHS:** Safe interaction with operating transfer-station traffic, manual handling, sharps/broken glass, biological contamination, trip hazards, access escort, tools, PPE, emergency procedures, weather and out-of-hours controls. Clarify SWMS versus task risk assessment where legally required.

**17 — Lithium and fire:** Detailed acceptance/red-flag list, storage/segregation, inspection, temperature, charging supervision, thermal runaway, emergency isolation and approved disposal. Consult FRNSW/SafeWork/NSW Fair Trading and insurer; do not casually store swollen batteries on a residential site.

**18 — Electrical safety:** Scope for simple DC/low-voltage repair versus licensable mains work, electrical testing competency, charger/lead safety, used electrical appliances, state supply standards, product recalls, AS/NZS standards where applicable. No blanket promise that test-and-tag alone certifies a product safe for sale.

**19 — Consumer/resale law:** Australian Consumer Law guarantees and exceptions, used-item disclosures, warranty representations, change-of-mind versus statutory rights, recalls, eBay/online selling, record retention, beneficiary transfers and donation liability.

**20 — NSW second-hand dealer regime:** Pawnbrokers and Second-hand Dealers Act/Regulation; meaning of prescribed electronic goods; donated vs bought vs sold vs destroyed items; recycling/charity exceptions; 14-day retention/alteration and mandatory record/ID rules; exactly which operating variants trigger licensing. Seek Fair Trading written position.

**21 — Business legality:** ABN/legal structure, GST, tax, consumer receipts, business naming, public liability/product liability/professional indemnity/cyber coverage as applicable, workers/volunteer cover, motor/transit cover, residential lease/landlord and home occupation; referral for accounting/legal review.

**22 — Insurance:** Obtain quotations/underwriting terms for all declared operations; create matrix of public liability, product/completed operations, professional negligence, property in care/custody/control, statutory liability, cyber/privacy, transit, stock, employers/volunteers and defective batteries. **Council sets any contractual limit; no invented amounts.** Identify deductibles, exclusions, duty of disclosure and certificate wording.

**23 — Third-party partners:** Can an appropriately accredited existing ITAD operator or incumbent recycler perform data sanitisation/secure custody and reduce Council's exposure? Verify actual legal identity, licences, insurance, certification scope, downstream auditing, cost and SLA.

**24 — AMR / recycler:** Confirm whether Australian Metal Recycling is relevant as an actual contracted counterparty versus a separate downstream facility; ascertain current processing route, battery handling, certificate availability, commercial residual acceptance, fees, AS 5377 or other accreditation claims, and the first downstream destination with written proof.

**25 — National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme:** Distinguish legitimate household e-waste recycling scheme obligations and eligibility from rights to divert whole computers for private resale. Confirm whether reuse affects recycler scheme credits/contracts.

**26 — Environmental impact:** Avoid generic carbon-saved per device claims; estimate baseline impacts using recognised methods, additional useful life, defect/repair yields, transport distances, energy use, reuse vs recycling, emissions assumptions and uncertainty.

**27 — Pilot design:** 3 escalating pilot choices: zero-Council private-donor proof of controls; incumbent subcontract; supervised Council demonstration if authorised. Budget each with limited duration, device cap, staff time, site access, schedule, transport, data safety, nonreusable outputs and stop/review points.

**28 — Performance and outcomes:** Monthly numbers accepted/rejected/sanitised/reused/donated/sold/recycled, defect rate, storage dwell time, audit discrepancy, near miss/breach, complaints/returns, net costs, diversion, actual recipient benefit, independent oversight.

**29 — Information access:** Search DRC public tenders/awarded contracts/contract register, Council minutes, policies, GIPA access; draft targeted informal/GIPA request for missing current contractor clauses and approval standards, with redaction options.

**30 — Comparison councils:** Find at least five real NSW council waste-site IT equipment reuse/diversion arrangements, preferably actual signed contracts, e.g. councils with tip shops or accredited social-enterprise partners. Show operating model, evidence, governance, failure/success, not generic Repair Café webpages. Explicitly mark non-Dubbo comparators.

**31 — Resubmission protocol:** List exact documents to send Council, sequencing, recipient, requested decision and whether an EOI is more appropriate than direct permission. Draft a polite brief clarifying Council's pathway; do not imply right to approval or challenge the refusal.

**32 — Verification/audit:** Assess repo's AssetFlow against promised claims through code, tests and safe synthetic datasets; identify missing chain-of-custody transitions, erasure validation, RLS, report export, product recalls, bad actors, file permissions and event audit immutability. Distinguish mock, implemented, tested, deployed and physically verified.

### Required deliverables — produce these files and an executive answer

1. **Executive conclusion:** answer 'What exact requirements exist, who controls them, and what remains unknowable without Council/contractor response?' Include three most feasible models and Council's ability to decline notwithstanding compliance.
2. **Legal applicability matrix** with 50+ rows: jurisdiction, obligation, authority+section, scope trigger, action, type (binding/adopted/draft/optional), decision authority, evidence, current status, risk if unresolved, source date.
3. **Current Council procurement and contract map** comparing adopted policy, draft, OLG requirements, advertising language and new contract thresholds with contradiction log.
4. **Data-security, privacy and custody threat model:** insider access, theft, SSD failures, device lock/ownership disputes, accidental public distribution, breach escalation, data retention.
5. **Operational SWMS/JSA-ready risk register** with risk ratings before/after controls, owner, verification method, frequency, insurer condition, Council approval and stop triggers.
6. **Insurance requirements matrix** with broker questions and exact Council-specific gaps, no assumed limits.
7. **Legal/approval flowchart** showing Council, existing contractor, regulator, landlord, insurer and second-hand dealer decisions, each with written evidence gates.
8. **Mock pilot SLA and heads of agreement** clearly labelled as draft for Council and lawyer negotiation; avoid representing it as legal advice.
9. **AssetFlow requirements backlog** linked to actual modules, schema, tests and 1–3 genuinely prioritised coding changes; no privacy-sensitive datasets in GitHub.
10. **Contractor and downstream diligence report** with actual evidence versus speculation; unanswered questions for provider.
11. **Costed 3-scenario pilot** with explicit assumptions and break-even/stop-loss, private-source-only pilot first.
12. **Document templates:** Council scoping email, informal records request, operator capability statement, device title/consent form, tamper-evident custody manifest, sanitisation certificate, exception log, incident report, recycling receipt, beneficiary transfer, pilot evaluation, closeout declaration.
13. **Source ledger:** URL, access date, page/section, direct short quote if allowed, interpretation, legal force, confidence, contradiction/staleness.
14. **Questions for officials:** numbered and routed to DRC Waste/Procurement/Legal/Privacy/Planning, NSW EPA, NSW Fair Trading, incumbent contractor, insurer, fire safety adviser.
15. **Remaining unknowns:** every unanswered issue with the exact document or written response needed to close it; do not invent closure dates.
16. **Council decision brief:** 2-page respectful, defensible submission seeking a pathway to assessment, not a declaration that all requirements have been met.
