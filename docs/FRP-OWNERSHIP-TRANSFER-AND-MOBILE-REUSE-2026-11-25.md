# Factory reset protection, ownership transfer and mobile reuse
**Research date:** 25 November 2026 (AEDT). **Status:** source-backed review; no device unlock or recovery demonstrated.

## Research prompt for future agents
Investigate how Android FRP affects legitimate repair, refurbishment and electronic waste. Use primary manufacturer, platform, legal, standards and upstream OS documentation. Compare independent consumer refurbishment with managed enterprise decommissioning. Study the security model and history of reported weaknesses at a non-operational level; do not collect exploit recipes, payloads, bypass utilities or instructions to defeat verification. Any future security testing must be explicitly authorised on isolated laboratory-owned devices with synthetic accounts, with a written scope and responsible disclosure plan. Do not test unknown-owner donations. Assess supported ownership-transfer mechanisms, independently verifiable sanitisation, sustainable OS support, market impact, Australian law, policy reform, and practical reuse yield. Record evidence, uncertainty, dates, limitations and concrete acceptance tests. Prefer existing upstream mobile OS projects rather than another distribution. Keep personal identifiers and donor data private. Do not claim successful activation or OS installation without physical verification.

## Findings
1. **Separate security controls.** FRP verifies authorised reuse after reset; screen lock protects access to user data; verified boot enforces trusted firmware; enterprise enrolment and financing locks may survive reinstall. A reset or stock firmware restoration is not evidence of ownership transfer, sanitisation or release. Android's [device protection documentation](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9459346) describes account verification. [AOSP device state](https://source.android.com/docs/security/features/verifiedboot/device-state) explains the verified boot trust model. Replacing Android is not an authorised remedy for unresolved ownership restrictions.
2. **Consumer prevention.** Google's [old-device reset guidance](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/17299872) describes removing the owner's accounts before authorised erasure. The donor should act privately and never supply their password to staff. Verify setup is clear of old account/management prompts before redistribution. [Samsung device protection](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10003349/) describes the same consumer protection and model-dependent credential choices.
3. **Enterprise precedent.** [Google enterprise FRP](https://support.google.com/work/android/answer/14549362), [Android Enterprise security](https://developer.android.com/work/dpc/security) and [enterprise feature list](https://developers.google.com/android/work/requirements) document managed recovery policies and administrator-controlled decommissioning. These apply to properly managed devices, not retrospectively to anonymous donations. This is a scalable pathway for future institutional batches.
4. **Huawei.** [Huawei Google verification guidance](https://consumer.huawei.com/en/community/details/topicId-7174/) directs customers to authorised support with purchase evidence when normal verification fails; [current lock-screen support](https://consumer.huawei.com/en/support/content/en-us15825204/) likewise says Google verification can follow reset. No publicly documented independent-refurbisher FRP release scheme based solely on intake paperwork was found in this review. Do not infer that alternate proof is accepted, or that absence of published policy proves technical impossibility.
5. **Industry impact.** [iFixit August 2024](https://www.ifixit.com/News/98249/activation-locks-are-trashing-millions-of-usable-phones-refurbishers-tell-the-fcc) and [Resource Recycling August 2024](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2024/08/01/refurbishers-urge-fcc-to-give-phone-locks-a-closer-look/) report a proposal for an OEM/refurbisher verification and release system. A US recycler reported over 66,000 reusable activation-locked iPhones scrapped in 2015–2019 and 25% of its iPhone intake locked in 2018. These are historical Apple-specific observations, not Android or Australian estimates. The [FCC proceeding](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-77A1_Rcd.pdf) principally concerns carrier unlocking; the advocacy asks for a wider scope, not proof that regulation already mandates FRP clearance.
6. **Australian alignment.** [Tech Reuse for Good Playbook](https://www.techreusecharter.org.au/playbook) identifies removing firmware, activation and management locks during preparation. [National Device Reuse Principles](https://beta.finance.gov.au/digital-and-data/data-and-digital-ministers-meeting/national-device-reuse-principles) support safe public-sector reuse. [ReUse Mobiles terms](https://www.reusemobiles.com.au/terms-and-conditions/) require account-free devices and rightful ownership. [SERI mobile reuse guidance](https://sustainableelectronics.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Guidance-for-the-Reuse-of-Mobile-Devices.pdf) addresses locked devices and component reuse; check current R2 requirements before making any certification claim.
7. **Legal caution.** [Productivity Commission Right to Repair report, 2021](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/repair/report/) recommended improvements to repair information and software support. Recommendations are not an automatic licence to circumvent access controls. [Criminal Code computer offences](https://www.cdpp.gov.au/crimes-we-prosecute/cybercrime) and [Copyright Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1968A00063/latest/text) can be relevant depending on authorisation, conduct and the measure involved. This is not legal advice; do not infer blanket legality or illegality of FRP research. Obtain legal review before independent security testing or distributing any lock-related tool.
8. **Mobile OS reuse.** Prefer [postmarketOS](https://postmarketos.org/), [LineageOS](https://lineageos.org/), [Ubuntu Touch](https://devices.ubuntu-touch.io/) and [Droidian](https://droidian.org/) on explicitly compatible, authorised devices with supported boot paths. Record working display, touch, charging, Wi-Fi, audio, suspend, security updates and cellular/emergency-call suitability. A processor match is insufficient. Do not duplicate JoshOS or extend the desktop ReuseOS builder merely to create a mobile brand.

## Current cases
- Huawei AMN-LX2: operator reported reset and both PHONE Locked and FRP Lock in fastboot. Likely Huawei Y5 2019 retail identity. No permitted activation or alternative OS path demonstrated. HiSuite restoration was proposed but not verified; it is not an FRP clearance process. HOLD or approved parts route.
- HTC Desire 530: PR [70](https://github.com/joshualparris/DubboEwaste/pull/70) added non-authoritative FRP hints. USB without authorised ADB is not proof of a lock.
- iPad 3: [legacy experiment](../tools/ipad-legacy-assistant/LEGACY-ARCHIVE.md) archived with unresolved Apple account binding. Apple data/activation protection is distinct from Android FRP.

## Five highest-priority follow-on actions (proposed)
1. Add explicit pre-erasure account/organisation release gates to existing AssetFlow intake; pilot with 20 units. Measure avoided lock cases, operator time and completed release. No default PASS.
2. Build an upstream mobile OS compatibility catalogue and integrate it into the existing [Universal Device Recovery Toolkit](../tools/ipad-legacy-assistant/README.md). Pilot one authorised, supported handset and document physical functionality. No flashing unknown-owner locked units.
3. Establish enterprise decommissioning checklist for donors, including proof of asset transfer, administrator release, sanitisation and clean setup validation. Pilot a batch with a willing authorised donor; do not assume existing donor agreement.
4. Measure actual lock-related losses: by exact model, lock category, condition, whole-device and component outcomes, technician time and costs. Publish only aggregated figures. Compare yield with pre-release intake controls.
5. Design a proposal for a manufacturer-authorised refurbisher release scheme: independent provenance, stolen-device checks via appropriately licensed services, privacy-preserving records, audit, fraud controls, appeal and accountable release. Preserve component reuse when whole-device recovery is not available.

## Open questions
Australian FRP prevalence; OEM acceptance of donor documentation; paid commercial stolen-device checks; cost-effectiveness of lock clearance vs parts; actual supported OS coverage of incoming stock; how a manufacturer release service could prevent theft laundering. No new device testing or repo software changes were performed in this research.
