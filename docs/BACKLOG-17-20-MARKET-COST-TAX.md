# 17. Dubbo competitor map

## Executive finding

Dubbo does **not** have an empty e-waste/refurbishment market.

The current local ecosystem already includes:

- retailers and repairers selling refurbished/second-hand devices;
- phone/electronics repairers;
- pawnbrokers buying/selling used electronics;
- Officeworks trade-in and free tech recycling;
- Council e-waste disposal;
- scrap/e-waste recyclers;
- general IT/MSP businesses.

The gap for Dubbo E-Waste is therefore not simply **"we take old electronics"**.

The strongest differentiated proposition remains:

> **reuse-first collection + documented chain of custody/data handling + refurbishment + asset reporting + resale/redeployment + transparent downstream recycling.**

That combined service is much less visible in Dubbo's public market.

#### Clarification — Officeworks is a collection/trade-in channel, not the Dubbo processor

Officeworks is relevant to the Dubbo competitor/substitute map because it gives residents another way to hand over unwanted technology.

It should **not** be described as an industrial e-waste recycler operating in the Dubbo store.

The accurate distinction is:

- **Tech Trade-in:** eligible devices may be traded for Officeworks value/gift-card value and can enter a reuse/resale pathway.
- **Bring it Back / recycling:** selected unwanted technology can be handed in and then sent onward through Officeworks' recycling partners.
- **Dubbo store:** this is the local consumer handover point, not the specialist dismantling/shredding plant.
- **National vs local acceptance:** do not assume every nationally advertised category is accepted at the Dubbo store without checking the store/program conditions.
- Officeworks stopped in-store battery collection in February 2024.

**Correct repo wording:**

> **Officeworks is an existing retail trade-in / technology collection channel and disposal substitute for some Dubbo households. It is not being described as the local downstream recycler.**

This matters strategically because Dubbo eWaste should not compete on "somewhere to get rid of an old laptop". It should compete on **reuse triage, documented data handling, refurbishment, asset reporting, local resale/redeployment and transparent final outcomes**.

## Competitor positioning conclusion

Do not position Dubbo E-Waste as:

> "Dubbo finally has somewhere to take old electronics."

That is demonstrably false.

A stronger positioning hypothesis is:

> **"We try to keep usable technology in use first. For businesses we can inventory assets, document data sanitisation, refurbish/redeploy viable equipment and provide an auditable route for the remainder."**

That still needs market validation.

# 18. Resale economics

## eBay Australia — major correction

The repository's old working assumption of roughly **13% eBay fees** is wrong for the intended small pilot if the account qualifies for eBay's current Australian free-selling model.

eBay's current Australian policy says an Australia-based seller:

- with **AU$25,000 or less in eBay sales over the prior 12 months**;
- who is **not on an eBay Pro plan**;

can sell on eBay.com.au with **no transaction/final-value fee**.

Other costs can still apply, including:

- optional listing upgrades;
- overseas delivery/international fees;
- postage labels;
- refunds;
- promoted listings if used;
- additional fees for poor seller performance in applicable circumstances.

Source:
- https://www.ebay.com.au/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees-managed-payments-sellers?id=4822

### Above the threshold / Pro plans

Current monthly Pro subscriptions, GST-inclusive:

- Pro Starter: **$0/month**
- Pro Basic: **$27.45/month**
- Pro Featured: **$82.45/month**
- Pro Anchor: **$604.95/month**

Pro sellers and sellers above AU$25,000 rolling-12-month eBay sales pay category-dependent transaction fees.

Source:
- https://www.ebay.com.au/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/ebay-pro-selling-fees?id=4809

### Implication for pilot modelling

For a tiny pilot, do **not** deduct 13% automatically.

Track the actual account state:

- rolling 12-month eBay sales;
- Pro plan status;
- optional promotion/listing fees;
- label/postage cost;
- refunds/returns.

When the project approaches AU$25,000 rolling eBay sales, rebuild the margin model from the then-current Pro fee table.

## Sold-price research

Public sold-listing searches are useful for **individual model pricing**, but they are not a defensible substitute for a proper average:

- configurations differ;
- condition differs;
- freight may be included/excluded;
- parts-only units distort results;
- public search samples can be incomplete;
- eBay Product Research/Terapeak-style analytics are account/tool dependent.

### Phase 0 pricing rule

For each accepted device, price using **same-model or genuinely comparable sold listings**, not asking prices.

Record:

- CPU/RAM/storage;
- battery condition;
- grade/defects;
- charger;
- warranty/returns promise;
- postage;
- date of sale.

The pilot tracker has been expanded in backlog item 31 so these economics can be measured rather than guessed.

## Platform return economics

Even when a seller selects "no returns", eBay buyer-protection/consumer-law circumstances can still generate returns for damaged, faulty or not-as-described goods.

Therefore every margin calculation should reserve for:

- outbound postage;
- return postage where seller-liable;
- packaging;
- occasional refund/write-off.

Source:
- https://www.ebay.com.au/help/buying/returns-refunds/return-item-refund?id=4041

# 20. Tax, accounting and structure

## Sole trader versus company

Australian Government guidance currently shows:

### Sole trader

- ABN: free;
- business-name fee if using a name other than personal name: $47/1 yr or $108/3 yrs;
- individual remains legally responsible for the business.

### Company

- separate legal entity;
- proprietary company registration: $636;
- annual review: $342;
- business-name fee may also apply.

Source:
- https://business.gov.au/planning/business-structures-and-types/business-structures/difference-between-a-sole-trader-and-a-company
- https://business.gov.au/registrations/work-out-your-business-registrations/categories

This does not by itself decide the right structure. Waste/product/data liability may justify professional advice before scaling.

## GST

GST registration becomes compulsory at **$75,000 GST turnover**.

Source:
- https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst

Below the threshold it can generally be voluntary, but voluntary registration creates GST/BAS obligations.

## Second-hand goods and GST — important future issue

Division 66 can provide special GST treatment where a GST-registered business acquires qualifying second-hand goods from a supplier whose sale was not taxable and acquires them for sale/exchange in the ordinary course of business.

ATO material explains that this can allow an input tax credit even though the unregistered supplier did not charge GST.

Records for relevant second-hand acquisitions include:

- supplier name/address;
- description and quantity;
- acquisition date;
- consideration paid.

Sources:
- https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=%22GSD%2FGSTD20132%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001%22
- https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf?DocId=ESO%2FESLI2025D11%2F00001&PiT=99991231235958&filename=law%2Fview%2Fpdf%2Fesg%2Fesli2025-d011.pdf
- https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=AID%2FAID20039%2F00001

### Free donations

Do not assume a GST credit exists merely because a donated device later gets resold.

Where acquisition consideration is **$0**, there is no purchase consideration from which to derive the normal Division 66 second-hand acquisition credit.

Get accounting advice before applying Division 66 to mixed free/donation/buyback/part-out streams.

### Parting out second-hand goods

ATO interpretative material specifically contemplates a second-hand dealer acquiring a lot, dividing it and reconditioning/selling parts, provided the Division 66 conditions and records are satisfied.

Source:
- https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=AID%2FAID20039%2F00001

This is relevant if Dubbo E-Waste becomes GST registered and routinely parts purchased second-hand assets.

## Home-based business deductions

ATO guidance distinguishes:

- **running expenses**, such as additional energy, internet, depreciation/repairs of business equipment;
- **occupancy expenses**, which depend on whether an area has the character of a place of business and can have other tax implications.

Sources:
- https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/deductions-you-can-claim/working-from-home-expenses
- https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-8551e46a-7b10-44c9-b5b4-7fc8bd0c32d5

Because the current property is rented, the homeowner-CGT consequence is not Josh's current issue, but occupancy/rent apportionment should still be accountant-led if claimed.

## Record retention

ATO's general rule is **5 years** for most business/tax records, with some circumstances requiring longer.

Sources:
- https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?LocID=%22TXR%2FTR967%2FNAT%2FATO%22
- https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-53cc7a8e-0668-4c9d-95d7-eb841eb09c04

## Instant asset write-off

ATO's current 2026–27 small-business guidance says the instant asset write-off threshold is **$20,000 per eligible asset** for eligible small businesses using simplified depreciation, with aggregated turnover below $10 million. Both new and second-hand eligible assets can qualify.

Source:
- https://community.ato.gov.au/s/article/a07Mo00002BaUJ6IAN/can-my-small-business-claim-the-20000-instant-asset-writeoff

Check the main ATO legislative guidance again before making a material purchase.

## Business-name / trademark / domain availability

Public search results are **not sufficient** to declare "Dubbo E-Waste" available.

Before registration:

1. run ASIC's current business-name availability check;
2. search IP Australia's Trade Mark Search/TM Checker for confusingly similar marks;
3. check the desired .com.au/.au domain with an accredited registrar;
4. check social handles.

Business-name registration does **not** create exclusive brand rights.

Source:
- https://business.gov.au/registrations/register-your-business-name

## Scrap/e-waste commodity value

Australian Metal Recycling's current public model distinguishes between materials it **buys** and streams it accepts as **free recycling**.

Its current public material says it pays for commodity metals such as copper, aluminium, brass, lead, stainless steel and other recoverable scrap, while e-waste is promoted primarily as a **free recycling** stream.

A current Dubbo AMR social/local listing published late August 2026 advertised indicative copper/brass rates such as roughly:

- bright & shiny copper: **$14/kg**;
- bright copper: **$13/kg**;
- burnt copper: **$12/kg**;
- clean brass: **$8/kg**.

Those numbers are **secondary, date-specific commodity quotes**, not guaranteed e-waste payouts.

### Margin rule

Do **not** assign a generic per-kilogram scrap value to incoming computers.

Instead record the real outcome:

- recycler charges $X;
- recycler takes it free;
- recycler pays $X for a separated commodity fraction.

The project's economics should remain driven by **reuse/refurbishment**, not speculative commodity prices.

Primary operator source:
- https://australianmetalrecycling.com.au/

## Remaining price research that is inherently live

The following cannot be usefully "solved once" online:

- SSD/RAM/screen/battery replacement prices;

- sold-market price for a particular refurb configuration;
- current commodity payout;

The correct implementation is already in the expanded pilot tracker: record the actual direct price/cost on each job rather than maintaining a static average.
