# Dubbo non-government schools: IT asset retirement and e-waste pathways

**Research checked:** 6 October 2026  
**Scope:** non-government schools physically operating in Dubbo, NSW, with a short regional note for Wellington.  
**Purpose:** identify realistic, lawful supply/partnership pathways for a small reuse-first Dubbo ITAD/refurbishment operation.  
**Evidence rule:** public and authorised sources only. Internal/confidential school material found in connected storage was deliberately excluded from this public report.

## Executive summary

1. **No current retired-IT contractor or last-batch destination was publicly established for any Dubbo non-government school or school system.** This is a public-evidence gap, not proof that the schools lack contracts.
2. Dubbo has **five independent school organisations/campuses relevant to this research**: Dubbo Christian School, Macquarie Anglican Grammar School, Central West Leadership Academy, Burrabadine Christian Community School and Skillset Senior College – Dubbo. Vista Lodge is a Macquarie boarding campus, not a separate procurement/disposal prospect.
3. Dubbo also has **five Catholic systemic schools**: St Johns College, St John's Catholic Primary, St Laurence's Catholic Primary, St Mary's Catholic Primary and St Pius X Catholic Primary. They should be treated first as **one Catholic Education Diocese of Bathurst (CEDB) ICT/procurement opportunity**, not five unrelated cold leads.
4. **Macquarie is a weaker student-device source than raw enrolment suggests.** Its public One-to-One Learning material says Years 5–12 bring devices that comply with purchase specifications. Student BYOD therefore should not be counted as school-owned retirement stock.
5. **Central West Leadership Academy is similar:** its current fee/about material says all students are required to **purchase a Chromebook**. Those student Chromebooks are not a credible school-owned ITAD pool unless the school separately owns loaners/admin/staff devices.
6. **Burrabadine is materially different.** Its current site advertises “Chromebooks for every student”, one-to-one Chromebook use and student access to laptops; its 2022 annual report recorded MacBook Airs being rolled out to teachers and Year 7. This is strong evidence of a school-managed fleet, although legal ownership and the current refresh/disposal route remain unverified.
7. **Skillset is the strongest historical disposal lead.** The college currently says it provides access to computers and other technology. Skillset Ltd’s 2018–19 annual report recorded commissioning the Dubbo campus network and **recycling more than 3 tonnes of e-waste**. That is organisation-level historical evidence, not proof that 3 tonnes came from the college or that the same recycler is still used.
8. **Dubbo Christian School has a substantial institutional fleet opportunity**, with almost 700 K–12 students, about 125 employees and a current 2027 ICT Systems Administrator vacancy confirming an internal ICT function. An older Viatek case study proves a managed-print/PaperCut relationship but does **not** prove general-device disposal or a current ITAD contract.
9. **NSW non-government-school not-for-profit rules change the pitch.** Current NESA guidance says school assets may be sold, with proceeds used for school operations, and disposal must be at least reasonable market value. Current rules also expressly permit qualifying depreciated/redundant equipment donations in specified circumstances. A private refurbisher should therefore pitch documented ITAD, valuation, settlement/revenue return and compliant downstream handling — not “give us your old computers”.
10. The best next step is **direct verification of the last retired batch**. Public research can identify ownership clues and decision pathways, but it cannot yet answer who collected the last laptops, what they paid, who sanitised them, or whether they were reused before recycling.

## 3. The biggest strategic correction: student devices are not automatically school assets

### 3.1 Macquarie Anglican Grammar School — BYOD removes a large apparent pool

Macquarie’s public One-to-One Learning document says:

- Years 5–12 participate in the program;
- students are expected to have devices every day;
- purchases must comply with the school’s recommended specifications;
- the school publishes current BYOD minimum specifications.

This strongly indicates that the large Years 5–12 student device population is **family-owned/BYOD**, not a school-owned refresh fleet.

**ITAD implication:** do not model Macquarie as “650 students ≈ hundreds of school laptops”. The viable school-owned pool is more likely:

- staff laptops/desktops;
- administration devices;
- specialist classroom/lab equipment;
- loaners/spares;
- servers/networking;
- displays/peripherals;
- boarding/admin devices.

Governance is also more complex than a standalone school. A 2024 Anglican Schools Corporation candidate brief states that the Corporation’s Group Office provides schools with shared services including **IT, financial planning, accountancy, governance and administration**, while the School Council operates under delegated Corporation authority.

**Best first question:** does asset ownership/disposal sit with the local school, the Corporation Group Office, or a vendor/lease provider?

Sources:
- https://www.mags.nsw.edu.au/about-us/term-dates-downloads/
- https://www.mags.nsw.edu.au/assets/Downloads/One-to-One-Learning.pdf
- https://www.tasc.nsw.edu.au/resources/PDFs/Employment/Principal%20-%20Macquarie%20Anglican%20Grammar%20School%20-%20Candidate%20Brief%20-%20January%202024.pdf

### 3.2 Central West Leadership Academy — family-purchased Chromebooks

The Academy’s current About page states plainly:

> All students are required to purchase a Chromebook.

The current home page also says students have their own Chromebooks and use online virtual classrooms.

**ITAD implication:** the one-to-one student Chromebook population should not be assumed to enter the school’s asset-disposal stream. The opportunity is limited to school-owned:

- staff/admin devices;
- loaners;
- infrastructure;
- classroom technology;
- any devices purchased for support/equity purposes.

A separate **family/community Chromebook recycling event** could someday be relevant to a Repair Café/e-waste community model, but that is different from institutional school ITAD.

Most importantly, Skillset Ltd’s 2018–19 annual report says its ICT team:

- **recycled over 3 tonnes of eWaste**.

### Important qualification

The report does **not** establish:

- that the entire 3 tonnes came from Skillset Senior College;
- which recycler received it;
- whether equipment was tested for reuse first;
- whether the same arrangement still exists in 2026.

Treat this as a high-value **historical lead**, not a current-provider fact.

### Opportunity assessment: HIGH

This is the best prospect for a direct “what did you do last time, and who does it now?” conversation because there is proven historical organisational e-waste activity.

Ask:
1. Who collected that historical e-waste?
2. Who handles current retired assets?
3. Does the College own the student computers?
4. Is asset retirement handled by Skillset Ltd, the College, or an external MSP?
5. Is reusable equipment remarketed before recycling?
6. What documentation is required?

## 8. NSW non-government-school asset rules materially affect the business model

### 8.1 Sale of school assets

Current NSW NESA Not For Profit Guidelines, D3.2, state that proprietors/schools may sell school assets, but:

- income from the sale must be used for the operation of the school; and
- disposal must be at least at **reasonable market value**.

NESA suggests records including:

- reasonable-market-value evidence;
- conflicts/related-party management;
- asset register;
- approval by an authorised person;
- method/date of disposal;
- amount recovered.

For DubboEwaste, this strongly favours:

- documented buyback;
- graded valuation;
- revenue-return/settlement;
- transparent auction/quote benchmark where appropriate;
- serialised disposition records.

Source:
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/not-for-profit-school-regulation/not-for-profit-guidelines/d3

### 8.2 Donation of depreciated/redundant equipment

NESA B5.7 says a school does not operate for profit merely because qualifying depreciated/redundant equipment is donated to another school, a charity or a local council, and it describes records expected around the donation. D3.2 uses broader shorthand referring to other schools, charities and not-for-profit entities.

The underlying 2025 amending regulation expressly refers to:
- another school;
- a charity; or
- a local council.

**Practical conclusion:** a normal private for-profit refurbisher should not build its school-sourcing pitch around “donate the assets to us”. Use a documented commercial ITAD/sale/settlement model, or structure any social-donation route through an eligible recipient and obtain proper advice on the arrangement.

Sources:
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/not-for-profit-school-regulation/not-for-profit-guidelines/b5
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/not-for-profit-school-regulation/not-for-profit-guidelines/d3
- https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/pdf/asmade/sl-2025-664

### 8.3 Privacy/data destruction

OAIC APP 11 guidance says reasonable destruction steps for electronic personal information depend on the hardware; in some cases hardware may be sanitised, while hardware that cannot be adequately sanitised may need another method of irretrievable destruction.

This supports an ITAD offer that records:

- device/serial;
- authority to transfer;
- data-bearing status;
- sanitisation method/tool;
- validation result;
- exception/failure;
- physical-destruction pathway where necessary;
- final destination.

Do not promise “certified destruction” unless the actual process and provider support that claim.

Source:
- https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information

See also the repo’s NIST SP 800-88 Rev.2 sanitisation work.

## 11. Recommended DubboEwaste offer to schools

The strongest pitch is not “e-waste collection”.

Offer an **auditable retirement service**:

1. pre-approved pickup;
2. chain-of-custody receipt;
3. serial/asset capture;
4. quarantine of data-bearing devices;
5. documented sanitisation and verification;
6. functional grading;
7. fair-market-value assessment;
8. agreed settlement/revenue return for reusable assets;
9. refurbishment/remarketing where authorised;
10. parts recovery/recycling for failures;
11. final per-asset disposition report;
12. recycler certificate/receipt for residuals where available.

For the earliest pilot, do not promise:
- high-volume school fleet capacity;
- formal certifications you do not hold;
- data-destruction services outside actual capability;
- charity status;
- guaranteed residual values.

A partnership with an established certified ITAD provider may be the most credible early school route, with DubboEwaste providing regional collection/triage/logistics.

## 12. Direct-verification interview — ask about the last batch

Opening:

> “I’m researching how regional schools manage retired IT and whether there’s room for a secure local reuse-first pathway. I’m not asking you to hand over equipment today — I’m trying to understand your current process.”

Questions:

1. Who legally owns the school’s laptops/desktops/Chromebooks when they are replaced?
2. Who has authority to approve disposal?
3. What happened to the **most recent retired batch**?
4. Roughly when was it, and what device types were involved?
5. Was equipment sold, returned to a supplier/lessor, donated, stored or recycled?
6. Who collected it?
7. Was the provider local or metro-based?
8. Who performed data sanitisation?
9. What wipe/destruction evidence or certificates were supplied?
10. Did the school receive a rebate/residual-value return?
11. Were usable devices tested for reuse before recycling?
12. Are Chromebooks/Apple/Windows devices released from Google Admin, Apple School Manager/MDM, Intune/Autopilot or other management before transfer?
13. Is there an existing contract, preferred supplier or procurement panel?
14. When is the next refresh likely?
15. Would a local provider offering serialised reporting, verified erasure, reuse-first grading and a documented settlement be useful?
16. What insurance/accreditation/security requirements would a new provider have to meet?

### Role to approach first

| Prospect | First role |
|---|---|
| Skillset | Operations Manager / organisational ICT or finance asset owner |
| CEDB | Manager ICT Services / Finance & Operations / procurement |
| DCS | Business/finance asset owner + ICT leadership |
| Burrabadine | Business Manager |
| Macquarie | local Business/ICT lead, then Anglican Schools Corporation Group Office if disposal is central |
| CWLA | Principal/operations/business office |

Use official switchboards/contact pages rather than scraping personal details.

## 13. What is proved, likely and still requires contact

### PROVED from public evidence

- Macquarie Years 5–12 operates a BYOD/one-to-one program requiring student devices.
- CWLA requires students to purchase Chromebooks.
- Burrabadine currently advertises one-to-one Chromebooks / Chromebooks for every student and access to laptops.
- Burrabadine historically rolled out MacBook Airs to teachers and Year 7.
- Skillset Senior College provides access to computers/technology.
- Skillset Ltd historically reported recycling more than 3 tonnes of e-waste while also commissioning the Dubbo college network.
- DCS currently has an internal ICT function and is large enough to have a material institutional IT estate.
- CEDB has a central ICT Services support structure and is a CEnet member.
- Dubbo has five Catholic systemic schools in the Bathurst Diocese.
- NSW non-government-school asset sales have not-for-profit governance/market-value/record-keeping requirements.

### LIKELY BUT NOT PROVED

- Burrabadine owns most/all student Chromebooks.
- CEDB controls or materially influences disposal decisions for all five Dubbo Catholic schools.
- DCS, Macquarie and Skillset generate recurring usable business-class retired computers.
- existing device suppliers/MSPs may already have take-back or ITAD arrangements.
- schools will prefer an accredited/certified partner for higher-risk data-bearing batches.

### REQUIRES DIRECT CONTACT

For **every** Dubbo non-government school/system:

- current disposal contractor;
- last retired batch;
- device volume;
- refresh cycle;
- lease vs ownership;
- sanitisation method;
- certificate/report requirements;
- resale/reuse percentage;
- recycling downstream;
- revenue/rebate terms;
- next refresh date;
- procurement onboarding requirements.

## Bottom line

Dubbo non-government schools are a **real ITAD market**, but they are not a simple source of free laptops.

The strongest near-term prospects are the organisations that demonstrably own or provide devices and have an institutional asset path: **Skillset, CEDB, DCS and Burrabadine**.

The most valuable offer is:

> **local collection + chain of custody + verified data sanitisation + reuse-first grading + documented fair-value settlement + responsible residual recycling**

That is much more compatible with school governance than “we will take your e-waste away”.
