# Lean launch checklist

Goal: validate Dubbo eWaste from an existing home shed with the least possible capital and regulatory risk.

## Before accepting equipment

- [ ] Define the exact legal entity that would operate Dubbo eWaste.
- [ ] Check availability/register the **Dubbo eWaste** business name if proceeding.
- [ ] Ask NSW Fair Trading in writing whether the **recycling and rubbish collection program exemption** covers the proposed collect → refurbish/resell → recycle model.
- [ ] If an existing IT business may operate the project, confirm its legal entity, licence status and whether the proposed activity/premises are actually covered.
- [ ] Ask Dubbo Regional Council planning staff whether the proposed home-shed use is permissible and whether consent is required.
- [ ] Keep operational floor area within the relevant planning limit unless Council advises another approval pathway.
- [ ] Obtain public/product liability insurance quotes.
- [ ] Identify a commercial downstream e-waste recycler and obtain its acceptance rules and pricing.
- [ ] Check whether NSW scrap-metal registration is required for the planned activities.

## Minimum operating controls

- [ ] Asset intake record for every device.
- [ ] Record who transferred the equipment and that ownership was transferred.
- [ ] Unique asset/intake ID.
- [ ] Separate data-bearing devices immediately.
- [ ] Documented erase/test process.
- [ ] Record erase result or physical destruction outcome.
- [ ] Triage categories: reuse / repair / parts / redeploy-donate / recycle.
- [ ] Separate batteries from ordinary stock where appropriate.
- [ ] Working smoke/heat detection in charging/storage area.
- [ ] Non-combustible charging surface.
- [ ] No unattended/overnight charging.
- [ ] Damaged/swollen/leaking batteries quarantined and never charged.
- [ ] No business waste sent through household CRC services as the business disposal pathway.
- [ ] Keep stock volumes small enough that the shed remains orderly and safe.

## Cheapest pilot

### Spend only on

- business registration actually required
- insurance
- battery/fire safety
- labels/tubs/basic storage
- essential replacement parts after a device has a clear resale path
- downstream recycling costs

### Defer

- warehouse
- dedicated van
- paid staff
- industrial processing equipment
- elaborate website
- large stock purchases
- custom software
- signage/shopfront

## Pilot offer

Initial wording to test:

> **Dubbo eWaste**  
> Unwanted computers and IT equipment collected in Dubbo.  
> Secure data handling. Reuse and refurbishment wherever practical. Responsible recycling for equipment that cannot be saved.

For businesses, test a stronger service:

> We collect redundant IT, inventory the assets, securely erase data, reuse or refurbish viable equipment, and send the remainder through an appropriate recycling stream.

## What to measure for the first 100 devices

- source
- category/device type
- collection cost/time
- reusable as received?
- repair required?
- repair parts cost
- labour time
- resale value
- days to sell
- parts value if dismantled
- downstream recycling cost/rebate
- data-erasure outcome
- battery condition
- final destination

After 100 devices, calculate:

- reuse rate
- repair success rate
- average gross resale value
- average parts cost
- average handling hours
- average recycling cost
- gross margin per collected device
- storage days per device
- proportion of incoming equipment that is a liability rather than an asset

## Go/no-go gates before scaling

Do not lease a workshop until there is evidence that:

- supply is consistent
- business pickups are recurring
- resale inventory actually moves
- downstream costs are understood
- battery/problem-waste handling is controlled
- the home shed is genuinely becoming the constraint rather than simply feeling crowded
- revenue can support rent, insurance and utilities

## Questions to resolve next

1. Fair Trading: does the recycling exemption cover this exact reuse/resale model?
2. Council: is the home-shed model permissible at the proposed scale?
3. Existing IT businesses: is there a compliant structure worth operating under?
4. Australian Metal Recycling (former Matthews Dubbo): confirm small-commercial e-waste categories, lithium-battery removal, TVs/CRTs, fees, pickup minimums and recycling receipts.
5. Regional benchmark: what are the actual reuse rate, margin and disposal costs in an established regional e-waste business?
