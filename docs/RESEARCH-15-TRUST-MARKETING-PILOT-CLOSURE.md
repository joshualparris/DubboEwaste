# Research 15: Trust, marketing and pilot closure

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)

**Gap covered:** `codexGAPS.md` sections 18–19: trustworthy public claims, local credibility, repeatable experiments and a clear stop/continue decision.

**Status:** evidence and experiment framework. It is not legal approval of any proposed wording or warranty policy.

## Executive decision

Trust should be built from inspectable records: honest condition descriptions, test evidence, clear business identity, visible remedy handling, safe-product controls, and narrow environmental claims. Marketing should follow the measured operation, not make promises that the operation has not yet proved.

The first pilot closes when the data supports one of three decisions:

- **repeat:** route and controls are working;
- **redesign:** demand exists but a bottleneck or risk must change;
- **stop:** the route is unsafe, uneconomic, unsupported or cannot produce defensible evidence.

## 1. Seller obligations and trust baseline

ACCC guidance states that businesses selling goods must meet consumer guarantees, including when goods are second-hand or sold online. Goods must be safe, match description and be fit for purpose where applicable; consumer guarantees cannot be removed by “no refunds,” “sold as-is” or similar wording. The remedy depends on the problem and circumstances.

ACCC Product Safety guidance says suppliers are responsible for product safety, including second-hand products, and encourages sourcing checks, testing and a compliance program. This supports an evidence-first listing process: identify the exact item, test what is claimed, disclose what was not tested, and retain the listing and test record.

## 2. Trust record for every listing

Before publication, the listing record should contain:

```text
asset_id
business identity/contact
make/model/serial or IMEI handling decision
condition grade and plain-language definition
included/excluded accessories
tested functions and test date
battery/charger/port/display observations
data sanitisation status
known faults and limitations
compatibility/lock status
price, postage and return/remedy channel
photographs linked to the asset
reviewer/approver
listing version and expiry date
```

Do not use “fully tested,” “excellent,” “refurbished,” “unlocked,” “works perfectly,” “securely wiped,” “eco-friendly” or “recycled” unless the record supports the exact term. Prefer specific statements such as “boots to operating system; Wi-Fi, keyboard and two USB ports tested on [date]; battery health not independently measured.”

## 3. Claims register

Maintain a claim-to-evidence table:

| Claim | Evidence required | Approval |
|---|---|---|
| tested | test steps, date, result | operator |
| repaired | fault, part, work record, retest | repair reviewer |
| data wiped | method, tool/result, asset ID | data reviewer |
| unlocked | model-specific check and account state | operator |
| warranty/return | written policy and remedy workflow | business owner |
| recycled | receiving document and stream/outcome | downstream owner |
| environmental benefit | defined boundary and calculation | evidence reviewer |
| community impact | partner handoff and consented outcome | partner owner |

ACCC guidance says advertising claims, images, descriptions, prices, testimonials and environmental statements must be accurate and based on reasonable grounds. Keep the evidence before publishing the claim.

## 4. Review and testimonial policy

- ask for honest feedback from actual buyers or partners;
- do not write reviews for customers or manufacture testimonials;
- do not offer a benefit only for positive reviews;
- retain negative feedback and the remedy outcome;
- do not publish a person’s name, image, hardship story or partner logo without permission;
- do not imply that one good outcome is representative of every item;
- disclose if a review relates to a donated, discounted or pilot device;
- review claims periodically because a route, product support status or downstream partner can change.

## 5. Customer-facing trust pack

Use a one-page pack linked from each listing or handoff:

1. who DubboEwaste is and how to contact it;
2. what “tested,” “refurbished,” “parts” and “untested” mean;
3. exact inclusions and known limitations;
4. data/privacy statement;
5. battery and charger safety information;
6. price, delivery and collection terms;
7. consumer-guarantee/remedy contact;
8. returns and fault triage process;
9. accessibility or compatibility caveat;
10. environmental claim boundary.

Do not hide a material limitation in a photograph or rely on a buyer to decode a vague grade.

## 6. Pilot design

Choose one route and one measurable question:

| Pilot | Question | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| front-door triage | can the operator reject unsafe/low-value offers quickly? | offers, accepted rate, minutes, refusal reasons |
| repair | does a defined category clear the repair cap profitably? | fault, time, parts, retest, outcome |
| resale | can a category sell with accurate grading and manageable remedies? | listings, views/messages, sale time, returns |
| donation | does a named partner have fit and support capacity? | need, match, handoff, 30/90-day use |
| downstream | can residuals leave with proof and known cost? | weight, receipt, fee, rejection, route status |

Do not run several unmeasured routes and call the result a pilot. Each route needs an owner, start/end date, cap, success rule, stop rule and evidence location.

## 7. Pilot scorecard

At the end of each cohort record:

- total offers and accepted items;
- front-door rejection rate and reason distribution;
- complete-ID and title-authority rate;
- data-sanitisation completion rate;
- safety incidents and near misses;
- owner hours per route;
- contribution per owner hour;
- days to clear;
- sale-through and return rate;
- repair success and repeat fault rate;
- downstream receipt rate and unresolved weight;
- customer/partner complaints and remedy time;
- storage-cap breaches;
- community use at 30/90 days where applicable.

## 8. Suggested decision thresholds

Set thresholds before seeing results. Example starting thresholds for a low-volume pilot:

- 100% of accepted items have an owner/source record;
- 100% of listed devices have a test record and limitation disclosure;
- 100% of controlled downstream loads have a receiving record;
- zero uncontrolled battery incidents;
- zero known data exposures;
- no repeated consumer issue caused by an omitted disclosure;
- stock is cleared within the published time limit;
- route contribution/hour is positive after all logged work;
- social-impact devices have an identified partner and support path.

These are pilot gates, not statutory safe harbours. If any safety, privacy or legal control fails, stop regardless of revenue.

## 9. Local trust experiments

Run one experiment at a time:

1. **transparent listing test:** compare a detailed test table with a short generic listing; measure questions, conversion and returns;
2. **condition-grade test:** publish grade definitions with photographs and measure whether misaligned expectations fall;
3. **local partner test:** ask one repairer, community organisation or small business to review the handoff and refusal process;
4. **receipt test:** issue an asset/route receipt for a donor and measure whether it improves repeat supply without creating unsupported tax claims;
5. **support test:** provide a setup sheet and a defined contact window, then measure support minutes and recurring problems.

Do not optimise only for conversion. A higher sale rate with more unsafe products, returns or unresolved support is a failed experiment.

## 10. Closure memo template

Every pilot ends with:

```text
pilot name and dates
question and hypothesis
scope/cap and exclusions
source and recipient/channel
items accepted/refused
route outcomes
owner hours and direct costs
contribution/hour
safety/privacy/consumer incidents
downstream evidence and unresolved weight
customer/partner feedback
what the data can and cannot show
decision: repeat / redesign / stop
changes required before next run
next review date and owner
```

Archive the closure memo with the source data. Do not edit the historical result to make a later decision look better.

## 11. Stop/escalation rules

Stop the pilot and escalate when:

- the business cannot prove a claim;
- the item is unsafe or subject to a recall/ban concern;
- data status is unknown after intake;
- a customer remedy is mishandled or repeatedly delayed;
- a partner’s logo, certificate or acceptance is being represented beyond its permission;
- a downstream route is unavailable or rejects material;
- environmental or community impact is being overstated;
- the route is negative after complete time and cost logging;
- the premises or storage cap is breached.

The correct response is to pause, preserve evidence, remedy or return where required, and revise the route—not to delete the listing or rename the problem.

## 12. Open questions

- What exact business name, ABN/contact and remedy process will be used publicly?
- Which products are too safety-sensitive for the first public listings?
- What condition vocabulary can buyers understand without a long explanation?
- Which claims need a second reviewer before publication?
- What return reserve and support hours can the project afford?
- Which local partner can independently review the pilot?
- What result would cause the project to stop even if revenue is positive?
