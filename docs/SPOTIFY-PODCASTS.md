# Spotify podcast listening list for Dubbo eWaste

**Curated:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)  
**Purpose:** a practical listening list tied directly to the Dubbo eWaste research: reuse-before-recycling, ITAD, secure data handling, refurbishment economics, repair/right-to-repair, social reuse, digital inclusion, circular business models and downstream material recovery.

This is not a generic sustainability list. Each item was selected because it maps to a specific question already researched in this repo.

## Recommended listening order

If time is limited, start with the first **8**. They cover the project's core model better than another broad e-waste explainer.

---

## 1. All Things Circular — EP 59: Refurbished Technology, ITAD, Circular Growth & AI Innovation with Martin Series of Foxway

**Spotify:** https://open.spotify.com/episode/27XCbC3CjrJ2Wy5nCoYjlq  
**Published:** 3 June 2026  
**Length:** ~35 min  
**Relevance:** **10/10**

### Why this is one of the most relevant episodes

This sits almost exactly on top of the business model this repo has converged on:

- IT asset disposition (ITAD);
- device lifecycle management;
- refurbishment;
- circular technology services;
- recovering value from old business IT.

Foxway operates at much larger scale than the proposed Dubbo pilot, but that is useful because it shows what a mature version of:

**collect → identify → sanitise → grade → refurbish → resell/redeploy → recycle failures**

looks like as an actual commercial sector.

### Listen for

- how ITAD differs from ordinary e-waste collection;
- where refurbishment creates more value than materials recycling;
- how corporate clients think about old devices;
- device grading and lifecycle value;
- what parts of the process scale well.

## 2. The Eco Sheroes Podcast — “Is Your Phone's Data Safe When You Recycle It? The Right Way to Do It”

**Spotify show:** https://open.spotify.com/show/0ZYeGIT5uL9WotjuovC3J1  
**Episode date:** 5 May 2026  
**Length:** ~49 min  
**Guest:** Mukul, founder of R3 Factory  
**Relevance:** **10/10**

### Why it is unusually relevant

The episode covers several of the exact Dubbo eWaste issues in one conversation:

- second-hand electronics grading;
- why **factory reset is not the same as proper data sanitisation**;
- repair versus reuse versus recycling;
- refurbished-device quality;
- reverse logistics;
- why useful electronics should be saved before destruction.

The episode's published chapters include:

- grading second-hand electronics;
- personal-data risk;
- data wiping vs formatting;
- buying refurbished devices;
- repair vs reuse vs recycle;
- reverse logistics.

That makes it one of the closest podcast matches to the proposed Phase 0 operating system.

### Listen for

- how a refurbisher explains data security to ordinary customers;
- grading language;
- intake criteria;
- the distinction between "recycling" and "reuse";
- what could be translated into a Dubbo intake/test checklist.

## 4. The Energy Forum — Episode 30: Beyond Recycling: The Global Systems Behind E-Waste

**Length:** ~41 min  
**Relevance:** **9.5/10**

This is one of the best recent high-level episodes because it explicitly argues that e-waste is **more than a recycling problem**.

It covers:

That maps directly to the repo's discovery that saying "it gets recycled" is nowhere near enough to understand what actually happens.

### Listen for

- the difference between reuse and material recovery;

# Important limits

- Podcast episodes are useful operating/industry insight, **not regulatory authority**. NSW planning, Fair Trading, EPA, WHS and consumer-law decisions must still use primary official sources already recorded elsewhere in this repo.
- Some Spotify search results expose the **show page rather than a unique episode URL**. Where that happened above, the exact episode title/date is supplied so it can be selected immediately in Spotify.
- International examples are included for operating-model insight only. They should not be used as evidence of what NSW law requires.
- Company-hosted podcasts may naturally present the company positively. Treat numerical performance claims as company claims unless independently verified.
