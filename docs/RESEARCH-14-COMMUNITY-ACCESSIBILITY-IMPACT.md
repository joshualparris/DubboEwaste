# Research 14: Community need, accessibility and impact

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)

**Gap covered:** `codexGAPS.md` section 17: proving whether a device creates useful community impact, rather than treating donation as the outcome.

**Status:** partner and measurement design. No recipient need, referral relationship or impact claim is confirmed until a named local organisation agrees to the pilot.

## Executive decision

Do not accept donation-targeted stock without both:

1. a named recipient pathway with an identified need; and
2. a support pathway for setup, connectivity, training, accessibility and eventual repair/recycling.

The first social-impact pilot should be small, consent-based and outcome-measured. A device handed over without connectivity, accessible setup or support can create a short-lived handoff rather than durable inclusion.

## 1. What the current research establishes

The 2025 Australian Digital Inclusion Index reports higher exclusion outside capital cities and identifies access, affordability and digital ability as separate dimensions. Its findings show that regional, First Nations, disability, public-housing and low-education groups can face different barriers. A device-only intervention therefore needs a local needs assessment rather than a generic “one laptop helps everyone” assumption.

Good360’s current digital-access work similarly describes the need for devices, internet access and training, and its National Device Bank model combines refurbishment with charity/school distribution. This is a useful model to study, not evidence that a DubboEwaste recipient or partnership already exists.

The Australian Government Digital Inclusion Standard treats inclusion as equitable access and use, with attention to people who may be excluded by the design or delivery of a digital service. For a refurbisher, the practical translation is to make the device, instructions, support channel and handoff accessible.

AIHW’s Australia’s Disability Strategy outcomes work identifies information and communication access as relevant to participation in education, employment, services and community life. It supports measuring continued use and accessibility, not just the number of devices distributed.

## 2. Recipient discovery, not recipient assumptions

Build a recipient register with one record per organisation:

```text
recipient_id
legal/trading name
named contact and role
local service area
population served
need statement and evidence
device types/specifications needed
accessibility requirements
connectivity/training/support capacity
referral and consent process
data/privacy expectations
acceptance/repair/return terms
funding or co-contribution
pilot status
last verified date
```

Classify each record as `lead`, `interviewed`, `need-confirmed`, `pilot-agreed`, `active` or `closed`. A charity directory, school logo or public grant announcement is a lead, not proof of current capacity or demand.

## 3. Interview questions

Ask the organisation, not the individual in crisis, wherever possible:

- Who currently lacks a device or reliable access?
- What work, study, health, family or service task is blocked?
- Is a laptop, desktop, tablet or phone the appropriate form factor?
- What operating-system, software, keyboard, screen or accessibility requirements exist?
- Is fixed internet, mobile data, hotspot or offline use required?
- Who performs setup and teaches the recipient?
- What happens if the device fails, is lost or is no longer needed?
- Can the organisation accept refurbished devices with a written condition/disclosure?
- What consent, safeguarding, privacy and data-minimisation process applies?
- What would success look like after 30, 90 and 180 days?

Do not collect unnecessary personal stories or identify recipients in public case studies. Record the need at the service level unless the recipient has freely consented to a specific use.

## 4. Device-to-need matching

Use a matching sheet:

| Need | Minimum device requirement | Support requirement | Failure fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| study/admin | browser, office/PDF, webcam, reasonable battery | setup and basic training | loaner or repair partner |
| job search | reliable browser, camera/audio, secure account setup | privacy and scam awareness | support appointment |
| accessibility | recipient-specific input/output and OS support | specialist advice | do not substitute a generic device |
| communication | phone/tablet or laptop depending on task | SIM/data or Wi-Fi plan | referral for connectivity |
| creative/technical | RAM/storage/ports/software compatibility | configuration and updates | sell/route elsewhere if mismatch |

The lowest-cost device is not automatically the best social-impact device. Match fit, support burden, repairability and total cost of ownership.

## 5. Support bundle

Where safe and authorised, a device handoff should include:

- plain-language setup sheet;
- charging and battery-safety guidance;
- accessibility settings review;
- update and account-ownership explanation;
- scam/privacy basics;
- local support contact;
- warranty/repair boundary;
- return or recycling route at end of use;
- connectivity referral where the project has a legitimate partner.

Do not create accounts for recipients using the operator’s email, retain passwords, or leave personal data on a test image. Confirm account ownership at handoff.

## 6. Accessibility checklist

Ask what the person needs rather than diagnosing them. Potential checks include:

- screen size, contrast, scaling and text-to-speech;
- keyboard, mouse, switch or touch needs;
- webcam/microphone and hearing support;
- ports for assistive devices;
- one-handed or low-force use;
- battery runtime and charging location;
- language and plain-language instructions;
- transport, weight and physical setup;
- support person or carer involvement with consent.

Do not promise accessibility from a model number alone. Test the actual configuration and document limitations honestly.

## 7. Pilot outcomes

Measure at three levels:

### Delivery

- device ID and condition;
- recipient organisation and purpose;
- setup/support time;
- cost of device, data and accessories;
- accessibility adjustments;
- handoff consent and date.

### Use

At 30 and 90 days, ask the partner whether the device is still in use, for the intended task, and whether support was needed. Do not demand private browsing history or content.

### Durable outcome

With appropriate consent, record a service-level result such as study participation, job application access, communication continuity, service access or reduced support barrier. Use qualitative feedback and countable measures, but avoid claiming causation from a tiny uncontrolled pilot.

## 8. Safeguarding and privacy boundary

- use an organisation as the primary handoff where possible;
- do not publish names, addresses, photos or stories without explicit informed consent;
- minimise personal data in the register;
- keep recipient records separate from public marketing;
- define who can access support notes;
- record device return/destruction obligations;
- avoid remote access after handoff unless explicitly authorised and technically necessary;
- have a complaint and withdrawal process;
- refer complex welfare, disability, housing, family-safety or legal needs to qualified services.

DubboEwaste is a device/refurbishment project, not a substitute for a social worker, disability assessor, financial counsellor or crisis service.

## 9. Community pilot design

Run one 5–10 device pilot with a partner that has both a documented need and support capacity:

1. agree the cohort and device specification;
2. agree title, data, condition, support and return terms in writing;
3. match and prepare devices;
4. record setup/accessibility work without over-collecting personal data;
5. hand off through the organisation;
6. check at 30 and 90 days;
7. record failures, repairs, unused devices and unmet needs;
8. decide whether to repeat, redesign or stop.

Reject a donation lot if the partner cannot identify a recipient pathway or if the support burden is beyond the pilot’s capacity.

## 10. Impact claims

Allowed after evidence:

> “During the pilot, [number] devices were sanitised, matched to [service-level need], handed to [partner] on [date], and [number] were reported in use at the 30-day check.”

Avoid:

- “closed the digital divide”;
- “changed lives” without consent and evidence;
- “guaranteed access” when connectivity is not provided;
- “accessible” without recipient-specific testing;
- “zero waste” when residuals remain;
- publishing individual hardship stories as marketing by default.

## 11. Open questions

- Which Dubbo or Orana organisation has a current, documented device need and technical support capacity?
- Can one partner provide connectivity or a referral without making an unsupported promise?
- What accessibility support is available locally for device matching and setup?
- Which measures can be collected ethically at 30 and 90 days?
- Who pays for accessories, data, transport, setup and repairs?
- What happens to devices that are unsuitable, unused or returned?
- Is the partner’s insurance, safeguarding and privacy process compatible with the pilot?
