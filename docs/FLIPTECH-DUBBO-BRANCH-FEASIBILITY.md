# Fliptech Australia deep dive — could a Dubbo branch work?

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)  
**Question:** What exactly is Fliptech Australia, how does its reuse/ITAD model work, and is there a credible case for a Fliptech presence in Dubbo?

## Executive verdict

### Short answer

**Yes — a Fliptech operation in Dubbo is operationally plausible, and Dubbo is unusually well aligned with the type of regional expansion Fliptech has talked about.**

However:

> **No public evidence was found that Fliptech has announced a Dubbo branch, offers franchises, licenses its brand to local operators, or currently operates any branch outside its Brookvale base.**

The public evidence supports a more realistic first step than a conventional "branch":

1. **regional collection / ITAD hub**
2. **Fliptech-authorised local operating partner**
3. **secure satellite depot with scheduled transport to Brookvale**
4. only later, if volume proves sufficient, a **full secure processing branch**

This is because Fliptech's business is not primarily a walk-in e-waste shop. Its current positioning is:

**corporate IT decommissioning + secure collection + data sanitisation + asset value recovery + reuse/donation + recycling + reporting.**

Dubbo has a credible market for that service, but the missing information is commercial:
- how much regional volume Fliptech already handles;
- its minimum profitable pickup size;
- asset resale margins;
- transport costs;
- whether Fliptech wants owned branches, partners or simply nationwide collection contracts.

A direct discussion with Fliptech is required before treating a Dubbo branch as an actual opportunity.

# 3. What Fliptech actually sells

Fliptech should be understood as an **IT asset disposition / decommissioning company**, not merely an e-waste recycler.

Current public material shows several service layers.

## 3.1 IT decommissioning

Fliptech markets secure and accountable handling of end-of-life IT equipment for organisations.

Current LinkedIn material says the company also supports organisations through:
- IT upgrades;
- asset decommissioning;
- installation;
- planning;
- workspace handover.

That means the business can get involved **at the refresh event**, before old devices become anonymous waste.

Source:
https://au.linkedin.com/company/fliptech-australia

---

## 3.2 Hardware buybacks and value recovery

A current Fliptech sales job advertisement says sales staff pitch tailored solutions including:

- **hardware buybacks**
- **subsidised erasure**
- **paid services**

This is one of the clearest public clues to the business economics.

The model is therefore not simply:
> charge a disposal fee.

It can also be:
> recover asset value from the equipment and use that value to offset service/data-erasure costs.

Source:
current Fliptech Australia sales-role listings indexed by Indeed.

This is very close to the commercial logic being considered for Dubbo eWaste.

---

## 3.3 E-waste bins

Fliptech supplies e-waste bins to buildings.

Its published process is:

**bin delivered**
→ client fills it
→ Fliptech collects/replaces it
→ material goes to Fliptech's facility
→ e-waste is sorted
→ data-bearing assets are securely erased
→ remaining materials enter recycling.

Source:
https://www.fliptech.com.au/bins

This is a recurring service model rather than one-off public drop-off.

# 12. The distance problem creates a reason for a regional node

Dubbo is around a **five-hour drive from Sydney**.

Dubbo Regional Council and Invest Regional NSW both describe the city as approximately five hours north-west of Sydney.

Brookvale is on Sydney's Northern Beaches.

### Inference

For small individual collections, sending a Brookvale vehicle to Dubbo and back is likely inefficient.

A local regional node could:
- aggregate multiple clients;
- reduce empty/low-volume long-haul trips;
- provide faster regional pickups;
- hold equipment securely until consolidated transport;
- potentially perform local sanitisation/triage if Fliptech approved the facility and processes.

This is **business-model inference**, not a published Fliptech plan.

# 15. Four possible Fliptech-in-Dubbo models

## Model A — Sydney collection route

Fliptech remains entirely Brookvale-based.

A salesperson develops Dubbo/Orana accounts and collections occur:
- when volume reaches a threshold;
- on scheduled regional runs;
- through freight/logistics partners.

### Advantages
- almost no local fixed overhead;
- keeps all processing at current secure site;
- easiest to test demand.

### Weaknesses
- Sydney-distance collection cost;
- slower response;
- unwiped assets have to travel long distance;
- low-volume clients may be uneconomic.

### Evidence
This is consistent with Fliptech having nationwide clients while publicly showing only one main location.

---

## Model B — Dubbo collection / consolidation hub

A small secure regional facility:
- receives approved business assets;
- records serials;
- provides chain-of-custody intake;
- checks battery/safety condition;
- holds assets securely;
- consolidates loads;
- transports batches to Brookvale.

### Advantages
- much lower technical overhead than duplicating full processing;
- improves regional collection economics;
- allows same-day local pickups;
- can service a larger Central West/Orana catchment.

### Weaknesses
- still transports data-bearing devices before sanitisation unless on-site wipe is added;
- requires planning, insurance, security and battery controls;
- needs enough throughput to justify premises/staff.

### Evidence assessment
**Plausible. No evidence Fliptech currently offers this model.**

---

## Model C — Dubbo authorised operating partner

A local IT/refurb operator works under a formal Fliptech agreement.

Potential local functions:
- sales/business development;
- collection;
- chain of custody;
- asset inventory;
- approved sanitisation;
- testing/grading;
- simple refurbishment;
- regional resale/social deployment;
- bulk shipment of final material into Fliptech's downstream system.

### Advantages
- local knowledge and relationships;
- low capital for Fliptech;
- uses an existing regional operator;
- fits Fliptech's national-growth history.

### Weaknesses
- major brand/security risk for Fliptech;
- requires strict process control;
- contracts/insurance/liability;
- no public Fliptech partner/franchise model exists today.

### Evidence assessment
This is the most interesting **Dubbo eWaste ↔ Fliptech** concept, but it is entirely unconfirmed.

---

## Model D — full Fliptech Dubbo branch

A genuine second secure facility performing:
- collections;
- erasure;
- certificates;
- testing;
- grading;
- refurbishment;
- resale preparation;
- donation;
- downstream recycling consolidation.

### Advantages
- strongest regional service;
- data can be sanitised locally;
- supports fast corporate turnaround;
- potential hub for western NSW;
- aligns with NSW Government's stated e-waste investment opportunity for Dubbo.

### Weaknesses
- largest capital/overhead;
- would need volume well beyond a hobby/small pilot;
- duplicate security/process infrastructure;
- staff training;
- planning and environmental/waste classification;
- downstream freight remains necessary for some fractions;
- current public company scale may make a full second facility a meaningful strategic commitment.

### Evidence assessment
**Possible, but there is not enough public financial/volume evidence to say it would currently be commercially justified.**

Actual territory, routing and commercial viability would have to be decided by Fliptech.

# 18. Why Fliptech may NOT want a Dubbo branch yet

This side is equally important.

1. **No public franchise/branch program exists.**
2. **Only Brookvale is publicly listed as a Fliptech location.**
3. Current growth hiring is centred on **Brookvale/Sydney sales**, not regional facility management.
4. Fliptech may already serve regional accounts efficiently through logistics without needing real estate.
5. Dubbo's population alone does not establish sufficient enterprise-ITAD volume.
6. High-value corporate refreshes are irregular and lumpy.
7. Government/school assets can be locked into existing procurement contracts.
8. A secure ITAD site costs more than a basic e-waste depot.
9. Battery/fire/storage/planning requirements add overhead.
10. A regional hub could cannibalise little if most jobs are currently consolidated centrally; there may be no economic case until regional account volume grows.

# 20. A practical low-risk Dubbo test

Before any branch, Fliptech could run a regional proof-of-demand program.

## Stage 1 — account discovery
Target 15–30 organisations across:
- business;
- health;
- professional services;
- manufacturing;
- agriculture;
- utilities/REZ contractors;
- MSP/client fleets.

Ask:
- devices retired per year;
- current disposal method;
- data-security requirements;
- current cost;
- dissatisfaction with current service;
- interest in buyback/reuse reporting.

## Stage 2 — scheduled regional collection day
Fliptech:
- books multiple clients;
- uses its secure collection workflow;
- consolidates the route;
- processes assets in Brookvale.

Measure:
- kg/assets per route;
- resale yield;
- erasure workload;
- transport cost;
- customer acquisition cost;
- repeat demand.

## Stage 3 — temporary/local secure aggregation
Only if route demand is recurring:
- establish a compliant secure local aggregation point;
- keep processing central initially.

## Stage 4 — on-site sanitisation / refurbishment
Only after volume proves it can justify:
- trained staff;
- certified process;
- security;
- equipment;
- premises.

## Stage 5 — full branch
Only once it is demonstrably cheaper/better than repeatedly hauling assets to Sydney.

- assets recycled;
- recovery value;
- revenue from erasure/paid services;
- logistics cost;
- turnaround;
- repeat clients.

### Go/no-go question
> Is a permanent local hub cheaper and commercially stronger than servicing the same clients from Brookvale?

# 26. Final conclusion

There is enough evidence to justify **approaching Fliptech about Dubbo**.

There is **not** enough evidence to claim Fliptech should immediately open a full second processing facility.

The most credible proposition is:

> **Dubbo as a Central West/Orana regional collection + ITAD + reuse pilot, designed to prove the volume before a permanent Fliptech hub.**

This aligns with:
- Fliptech's historical national-expansion ambition;
- its current enterprise sales strategy;
- its reuse/data-security positioning;
- its rural Royal Far West partnership;
- NSW Government's explicit promotion of Dubbo as an e-waste investment location;
- the local absence of a clearly advertised enterprise-grade secure ITAD/reuse service matching Fliptech's full offer.

The remaining question is not:
> "Could it physically work?"

It is:
> **"Does the regional asset volume and account value justify Fliptech dedicating people, premises and security infrastructure to Dubbo?"**

Only Fliptech's internal commercial data and a Dubbo pilot can answer that.
