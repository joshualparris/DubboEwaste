# Repair Café Dubbo: zero-personal-spend launch, grants and operating answers
**Researched:** 9 October 2026. **Status:** research and proposed actions, not insurance approval, grant eligibility determination, venue booking or market permission.

## Recommendation
Do **not** personally finance a $1,500–$3,000 standalone setup. First seek a not-for-profit host/auspice, donated equipment, and a free community room. Apply for a small local Council grant only once entity, eligibility, launch timing and venue/scope are agreed. Operate textiles, simple non-powered mechanical objects and IT advice initially; avoid mains/electrical appliance and lithium battery repair until specifically authorised and insured.

## Answers to all 10 outstanding startup questions

### 1. Who legally operates Repair Café Dubbo?
**Not decided.** Recommend a signed auspicing/hosting agreement with an existing incorporated NFP. Specify organiser, assets ownership, insurance named insured, bank/donations, decisions, privacy controller, incident reporting, financial approvals, supervision, dissolution and grant accountability. Avoid assuming a GitHub repo or informal volunteer group is a legal organisation.

### 2. Will a community organisation host and insure it?
**Unknown until written agreement and insurer confirmation.** Best leads CCS, and Library as venue but not automatically legal auspice. Ask host to forward a written description of tools/items and intended venue to insurer/broker; get confirmation of public liability, volunteer personal accident, product/completed operations and third-party property risks, and exclusions.

### 3. Will the Farmers Market permit active repairs?
**Unconfirmed.** Current public application is for producer stalls with committee discretion and $10m certificate requirement. Seek special community-guest/demo approval *before* formal application, specify whether no-power mending vs technical repairs, site/foot-traffic and market food separation, weather, 8am set-up and departure, electrical use, fee/waiver and levy.

### 4. Which repairs and safety equipment?
**Pilot scope proposal**, requiring risk review: hand sewing/mending, minor mechanical non-powered toys, loose fasteners, non-electrical IT guidance. No mains-device opening/testing, power supplies, damaged lithium batteries, battery repacks, soldering, grinders or other hazardous operations. Provide first-aid kit, clear tool/public boundary, stable tables, adequate lighting and accessible egress, incident log and responsible safety lead. For bikes, avoid brakes/steering safety-critical fixes unless suitable verified competence and insurer approval. Host and insurer decide allowed scope.

### 5. Who brings equipment and transports it?
**Undetermined.** Inventory volunteer-owned loan items rather than assume ownership. Borrow 2 tables, chairs, toolkits, tubs, sewing kit and first aid. One car-load indoor pilot, with no gazebo. Log owner, condition and responsible person; transport by agreement, no permanent storage before host signs.

### 6. Volunteer minimum
Suggested **minimum 4 people** for a modest 2-fixer session: lead/safety/coordinator (may also do welcome only when feasible), intake/queue, textile fixer, general small-item fixer; add setup/floater when available. Each attendee opts in by session; unfilled safety/welcome/skill slot prevents publication. Session: 2–3 public hours plus setup/packdown. Four is a **planning recommendation**, not a legal minimum or confirmed roster.

### 7. Spare parts, donations and refunds
Owner keeps item, brings any reasonably anticipated parts and accompanies repair. Initially no paid parts stock, no sale of refurbished goods, no promises of successful fixes. Donations are optional and not a precondition of service, handled by host receipts/cash policy; no refunds if there was no fee, but there still must be an incident/complaint/compensation escalation procedure agreed with organiser/insurer. Document consent, repair limits and privacy. Do not use a disclaimer as a substitute for safety or liability cover.

### 8. Where to store kit?
**Unconfirmed.** Avoid permanent purchases/storage initially. Small labelled transport tubs stay with equipment custodian by agreement; secure dry storage for donated assets if host offers it. Do not leave lithium batteries or unsafe items in volunteer homes.

### 9. What if a repair fails or causes damage?
Make clear repair attempts may not succeed; record intake, condition/photos with consent, agreed work scope, owner presence, outcome and incidents; stop work if hazardous, isolate unsafe items without taking custody unnecessarily, escalate accidents/property damage to event lead and insurer promptly, preserve incident evidence and follow insurer directions. Participant agreement must not claim to extinguish statutory rights.

### 10. Can local businesses donate equipment/sponsor?
**Possible, not confirmed.** Bunnings has published community donation process. Ask local IT/repair/bike/sewing suppliers for *specific* donated consumables or tools, and invite volunteers to bring equipment. Keep cash sponsorship and commercial ITAD activities separately governed; no automatic conversion of public Repair Café items into E-waste commercial inventory.

## Indicative cash needs: avoid imaginary precision
**Scenario A – partner-supported indoor pilot:** $0–$300 direct purchase of expendables/signage/first aid if donated or borrowed elsewhere, **ONLY IF** written host insurance is confirmed for repair activities and venue use is permitted. May cost much more if not covered. $0 is a target, not a guaranteed actual cost.
**Scenario B – micro-funded indoor pilot:** ask Council for up to $500 if eligible, e.g. first aid $80, supplies $120, non-powered hand-tool gaps $150, signage/printing $50, contingency $100 = **$500 proposal**, not quotes. Check grant dates/eligibility and reimbursement requirements before relying on it.
**Scenario C – independent market stall:** unknown, potentially substantially higher than indoor: insurance premium not quoted, minimum $10m certificate, market fee/levy, marquee, weights, tables and heat safety. **Do not budget an unverified fixed insurance amount.**
**Scenario D – financed expanded repair range:** defer to later stages and quote actual insurers/competence/testing requirements.

## A concrete launch path
1. Hold an **enquiry-only/volunteer recruitment** session first if venue/host permit; don't call it a public repair session.
2. Send host request to **Connecting Community Services** asking about auspice, volunteers, liability and room.
3. Phone **Dubbo Library** about free room, category-specific repair permission, dates and accessibility; no booking claimed.
4. Ask Council grants officer if an unincorporated group can apply through an auspice, and whether $500 micro grant can fund portable kits and insurance, plus 2027 project start dates.
5. Ask **Dubbo Farmers Market** if a free guest community stall, or nearby cross-promotion, is possible without standard produce-stall classification. No assumption of a waiver.
6. Compile donated-items roster and test repair categories only with insured competent volunteers.
7. Draft event risk assessment, item intake/consent, condition photos optional, adverse event process, donation ledger and a clear exclusions sign.
8. Run a **single low-risk 2–3h session**, review outcomes and volunteer load, then consider three-session pilot and funding.

## Further verification before public launch
- Read the current Council Applicant Handbook in full, especially auspicement, permitted costs and minimum grant start dates.
- Obtain real insurance quotes and written coverage for venue and repair types.
- Obtain specific written venue/market activity permission and dates.
- Get volunteer expressions of interest; independently assess competence for specialist repairs.
- Confirm transport, equipment ownership, donations financial processes, child safety, accessible setup and extreme heat/weather contingencies.
- Do not announce market partnership, confirmed venue, a successfully funded grant, or insured electrical repair before evidence.
