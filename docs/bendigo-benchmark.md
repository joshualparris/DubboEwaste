# Bendigo E-Waste benchmark

Updated: 2 October 2026

This note compares the publicly documented history and operating model of Bendigo E-Waste with the proposed low-cost Dubbo E-Waste concept.

## What the public record shows

### Before the formal business

Bendigo E-Waste's own About page says Joe had spent nearly 20 years in the technology industry before founding the business. He says he began by processing his own electronic waste and then started doing the same for friends and family.

This suggests the activity developed from hands-on reuse/repair/scrap experience before becoming a formal public-facing business.

Source:
- https://www.bendigoewaste.com.au/about-1

### 2023 — early business stage

Bendigo E-Waste's website says Joe founded Bendigo E-Waste in **early 2023**.

A stale third-party business-directory listing records Bendigo E-Waste at **2 Kinrap Street, Kangaroo Flat**. That address is a residential house and was sold in August 2023. This is suggestive of a possible early home-based phase, but it is not strong enough evidence to state as fact that the business began from that house.

Sources:

## Current operating model

Bendigo E-Waste has developed well beyond simple scrap collection.

### Intake

- free 24/7 warehouse drop-off
- residential and commercial quantities
- broad electronics acceptance
- complete, broken and raw e-waste
- whitegoods accepted in some circumstances
- loose batteries/media charged by weight

### Collections

Current advertised price:

- **from $150 per collection within 10 km of Bendigo CBD**
- further travel may cost more

This is important: **drop-off is free, but convenience/transport is a paid service**.

Source:
- https://www.bendigoewaste.com.au/book-a-collection

### Commercial clients

The business offers:

- regular or ad-hoc collections
- workplace e-waste bins
- collection when bins are full
- box, trailer and pallet quantities
- forklift/pallet drop-off
- data-destruction options

Source:
- https://www.bendigoewaste.com.au/general-clean

### Reuse before scrap

Joe explicitly asks people not to dismantle devices before bringing them in. The published hierarchy is:

1. restoration / recovery
2. reuse as complete equipment
3. donor devices / repair parts
4. offer useful devices and parts to repairers
5. commodity/scrap recovery as a last resort

Source:
- https://www.bendigoewaste.com.au/services-7

### Resale and niche buyers

The business does not rely solely on ordinary refurbished-computer sales.

It also:

- sells second-hand/vintage electronics
- holds clearance sales
- maintains a database of collectors
- offers collectors wholesale pricing
- works with local scrappers and invites them to bring unusual/valuable items before destroying them
- supplies repairers in Australia and overseas when whole-device restoration is not possible

Sources:
- https://www.bendigoewaste.com.au/local-scrappers
- https://www.bendigoewaste.com.au/general-1
- https://www.bendigoewaste.com.au/services-7

### Data services

Bendigo E-Waste offers paid data-destruction/data-erasure services and advertises documented photographic evidence and certificates for commercial collections.

Source:
- https://www.bendigoewaste.com.au/book-a-collection

### Repair business

Bendigo TV Repairs is operated from the same broader business and advertises authorised service-centre work for several television brands plus other electronics repairs.

This creates another destination for devices that are valuable enough to repair rather than scrap.

Source:
- https://www.bendigoewaste.com.au/services-7-1

## Relationship with City of Greater Bendigo

### What is confirmed

The City of Greater Bendigo operates its **own free public e-waste network**, currently including:

- Eaglehawk Recovery/Recycling Centre
- Heathcote Recycling Centre
- Strathfieldsaye Recycling Centre
- Goornong Recycling Centre
- smaller collection points

The City's current e-waste page **does not list Bendigo E-Waste as a Council e-waste facility or contractor**.

Sources:
- https://www.bendigo.vic.gov.au/residents/general-waste-recycling-and-organics/waste-disposal-guide/e-waste
- https://www.bendigo.vic.gov.au/residents/general-waste-recycling-and-organics/eaglehawk-landfill

### No public evidence found of a Council contract

Searches of City of Greater Bendigo pages, Council material and Bendigo E-Waste's public material did **not identify a Council procurement contract, exclusive tip agreement or Council-supplied e-waste stream involving Bendigo E-Waste**.

That does not prove no informal/commercial relationship exists, only that one has not been verified publicly.

### Huntly community drop-off

A Huntly Community Hub social post advertises a local e-waste drop-off point and directs people to Joe/Bendigo E-Waste. This shows at least one community collection relationship, but it is not evidence of a City of Greater Bendigo contract.

## Do not confuse Bendigo E-Waste with Eaglehawk Recycle Shop

The **Eaglehawk Recycle Shop** was a separate not-for-profit social enterprise at the Council landfill.

Its documented history is very different:

- began in **1994**
- founders were given access to recover/scavenge reusable material from the landfill
- Council provided the site under a peppercorn-rent arrangement
- Council also provided financial support
- the operation earned revenue by reselling diverted goods
- from **1 November 2025**, the City of Greater Bendigo took direct control of the site

There is no public evidence found that Joe/Bendigo E-Waste held the Eaglehawk shop's landfill salvage rights or operated that facility.

Sources:
- https://www.eaglehawkrecycleshop.com/about/
- https://www.bendigo.vic.gov.au/about-us/news-and-media/statement-regarding-eaglehawk-recycle-shop
- https://www.bendigo.vic.gov.au/about-us/news-and-media/eaglehawk-recycle-centre-transition-city-management
- https://www.bendigo.vic.gov.au/about-us/news-and-media/city-operate-new-eaglehawk-recycling-centre-november-1

## The important lesson for Dubbo

Bendigo E-Waste appears to have grown **alongside a free Council e-waste system rather than by replacing it**.

The model that appears transferable is:

**technical experience → own/friends' equipment → selective collection → free drop-off → reuse/repair first → resale/collector network → paid collections → commercial bins/data services → warehouse expansion**

That is materially different from:

**get a Council tip contract first → receive all Council e-waste → sell the best items**

There is currently no public evidence that the second path explains Bendigo E-Waste's growth.

## Bendigo vs proposed Dubbo pilot

| Bendigo E-Waste today | Cheapest Dubbo pilot |
|---|---|
| dedicated industrial warehouse | existing residential shed |
| 24/7 drop-off | Tue/Sat controlled intake |
| broad e-waste acceptance | high-quality/selective electronics |
| collects complete/broken devices | accept only devices with clear reuse/repair/parts value |
| collection from $150 | start with selective pickup only where economically useful |
| commercial bins and pallets | defer until demand exists |
| data-destruction service | documented secure erasure from day one |
| TV repair operation | possible future repair service |
| collectors/scrappers database | build local buyer/repairer/collector contacts early |
| substantial volume | deliberately low stock volume |
| GST-registered family-trust entity | cheapest compliant legal structure still to determine |
| warehouse suited to public drop-off | residential planning classification remains a gating issue |

## Questions worth asking Joe directly

Public records cannot answer these:

1. Where was the first Bendigo E-Waste operation physically run in 2023?
2. How much money did he spend before receiving the first outside e-waste?
3. What percentage of incoming devices are resold whole, parted out and scrapped?
4. Where does his non-reusable e-waste actually go?
5. Does he pay downstream recyclers, get paid for commodities, or both depending on stream?
6. Did Council ever provide material, referrals, bins, grants or contracts?
7. Has he ever recovered/sourced items through Eaglehawk landfill/recycle facilities?
8. What permits, EPA registrations or planning approvals did he need at each stage?
9. When did he move from home-scale activity to a warehouse, and what forced that move?
10. What items does he now regret accepting for free?
11. Which electronics consistently make money?
12. Which categories become storage/disposal liabilities?
13. What insurance does he carry?
14. How does he handle lithium battery fire risk?
15. How does he document transfer of ownership and data destruction?
16. What would he do differently if starting again with a shed and almost no capital?

These answers would be more valuable to the Dubbo model than copying Bendigo E-Waste's current mature operation.
