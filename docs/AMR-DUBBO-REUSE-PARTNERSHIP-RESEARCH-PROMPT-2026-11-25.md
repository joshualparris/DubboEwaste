## Copyable deep research prompt

Act as an Australian ITAD operations investigator, NSW waste-law/procurement researcher, data-sanitisation assurance engineer, circular-economy economist and partnership designer. Your goal is to determine whether a **small, written-authority, reuse-first AMR Dubbo laptop/desktop pilot** can operate lawfully, securely, economically and without harming AMR's existing customers or recycling contracts. Focus on unresolved AMR-specific questions, not a generic e-waste industry report.

1. Read the linked existing corpus and relevant repo trees, current issues, tests and latest changes first. Construct a prior-coverage matrix: question, canonical file, existing answer, source date, evidence quality, remaining delta. No new duplicate document for answered questions; update or link the original.
2. Review authorised private stakeholder notes and correspondence only if access is permitted. Separate first-hand conversation reports (not written approval), public primary sources, inference, historical leads and unknowns. Keep personal contacts, email excerpts, private terms, device identifiers and unpublished negotiations private. No outreach, purchases, scraping private systems, collection, use of credentials or claims of partnership without separate permission.
3. Search official AMR/ABR/ASIC material, NSW EPA and legislation, council GIPA/contracts/tenders/minutes, NetWaste, DCCEEW scheme/co-regulatory annual reports, certification directories, processor sites, Blancco documentation, government reuse guidance and comparable operators. Check latest dates and actual source text; record URL, title, exact section/page, date, applicability and access date. Inspect PDF pages for key tables. No invented contracts, quotes, processor links, certifications or regulatory thresholds. Stop repeating exhausted historical searches unless new evidence or search avenues exist.
