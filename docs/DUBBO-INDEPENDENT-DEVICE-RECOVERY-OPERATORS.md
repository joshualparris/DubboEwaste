# Dubbo independent device recovery: named operators and unresolved backyard leads

Research checked **2 October 2026**. The question was who the independent people reportedly recovering devices from their backyards in Dubbo are, and what they actually do. See also the [national comparison](NATIONAL-ITAD-OPERATORS-DUBBO-COVERAGE.md), [local disposal prospects](DUBBO-DEVICE-DISPOSAL-PROSPECTS.md) and [lead tracker](dubbo-independent-device-recovery-leads.csv).

## Answer supported by public evidence

**The exact people behind the reported backyard operations have not been identified reliably.** Public research did identify named local repair/business operators and advertised recycling/refurbishment routes. Those findings are useful, but none establishes that a named person is one of the backyard collectors described. There is no basis to say such informal operators do not exist.

**CBM Computers is the strongest confirmed local end-of-life collection lead.** Its owners are publicly named, and its own website invites customers to bring unwanted technology to its premises for recycling. **Orana Business Solutions and Tech Savvy advertise refurbishment or refurbished resale. Brendan Riley advertises repair and upgrades.** These are different activities, and the evidence should preserve that distinction.

No operators were contacted. Private residences and personal contact details are not collected here; the tracker uses public business contact pages. Presence on a website means an advertised offer as retrieved, not that availability was confirmed by a call or a test delivery.

## What each local route does — and what remains to establish

### CBM Computers: recycling drop-off

The business identifies Nasser and Farzaneh Sedghi as its owners/managers. Its environmental-responsibilities page invites customer drop-offs and describes end-of-life items being dismantled and reused as secondary raw materials. The public business location is 111 Talbragar Street. [L01–L02; EVD-LOCAL-001–002]

This supports an **advertised recycling collection point**. It does not show whether dismantling occurs in Dubbo, identify the receiving recycler, specify accepted batteries/TVs or charges, or establish that discarded machines are refurbished for resale. Those questions remain open. The page's support for local charities is not evidence that unwanted computers are donated to those charities.

Useful next question: what items can be dropped off, what happens to reusable machines versus scrap, and who receives the residual material?

### Brendan Riley: repair and upgrades

Custom Computer Creations explicitly names Brendan Riley as its contact. It advertises custom PC/laptop builds, repairs, upgrades, virus servicing, phone repairs and recovery of customer data. The website describes keeping staffing small and currently lists a business-park shop by appointment. [L03; EVD-LOCAL-003]

This establishes a small local repair operator. **Data recovery retrieves information; it does not establish data destruction.** No public evidence reviewed confirms retired-fleet collection, bulk purchasing, parts harvesting from donated machines, sanitisation certificates or an e-waste downstream partner. A historical directory address differs from the current business website; it is not used to infer a home operation.

Useful next question: does the business buy or accept unwanted machines, resell refurbished stock, or only repair customer-owned devices?

### Matt Hoyle / Orana Business Solutions: refurbishment

The official refurbishment page describes checking preloved devices, repairing defects and upgrading hardware/software, with availability dependent on stock. The official contact page names Matt, and a public professional profile supplies the surname and managing-director role. A 2023 Cobar Weekly report independently associates Matt Hoyle with the business. [L04–L07; EVD-LOCAL-004–005]

This is a genuine advertised reuse offer. The page does not identify its stock source or establish collection of other people's retired computers. Its broader printer/business-equipment activity means **“devices” should not automatically be read as laptops or phones**. Resale volume, customer data handling and final disposal remain unknown.

Useful next question: what categories are refurbished, where do trade-ins come from, and is there an opportunity for locally sourced surplus?

### Tech Savvy: repaired devices and refurbished retail

Tech Savvy advertises phone/tablet/laptop repair and second-hand/refurbished-device sales, with a 12-month warranty on those devices. Its published location is a shopping-centre shop. No reliable owner identity was established in the reviewed primary pages. [L08; EVD-LOCAL-006]

Selling refurbs does not establish that it refurbishes all stock itself or collects Dubbo's discarded devices. Buying/trade-in criteria, stock origin, erasure process, actual device categories and scrap handling are unresolved. The public business name is useful; supplying a guessed personal name would be misleading.

### Dubbo Computer Repairs / tech exe: promising but historical

An older Best in Australia article, indexed as roughly six years old, describes collecting old laptops, gadgets and computers. Its exact publication date and the underlying business website could not be confirmed by direct retrieval; the article is a **secondary historical lead**, not a current operating offer. [L09; EVD-LOCAL-007]

The ABN history extracted on 2 October 2026 for **30 552 723 640** records entity **D.M COLLISON & T.J IRELAND**, with:

- DUBBO COMPUTER REPAIRS business-name period **11 March 2022–11 April 2024**;
- tech exe business-name period **6 December 2021–15 January 2026**;
- ABN cancelled from **30 June 2026**, after an earlier cancellation and subsequent reactivation. [L10; EVD-LOCAL-008]

These dates do not prove either person has stopped all repair work or establish their current trading entity. They also do not establish that this later partnership ran the earlier article's collection activity. A community recommendation gives only a first name; that is insufficient to expand registry initials into a full identity. Old directory listings and undated closure snippets should not be treated as live service confirmations.

This lead should remain historical/unresolved until an original current advertisement, public business identity or direct confirmation connects a person, Dubbo and device collection.

### Gravity IT: company reuse, with a Dubbo office

The group's own account describes collecting client hardware during upgrades, repurposing usable computers for a community centre/children's home in the **Philippines**, and disposing of equipment it cannot repurpose. Its resources name founder Glenn Chapman and its website lists a Dubbo office. [L11–L12; EVD-LOCAL-009–010]

This is an adjacent model to investigate, but the story does not trace Dubbo equipment, identify the final recycler or establish a free public drop-off. Safe Haven should not be mislabelled a Dubbo charity.

## Search coverage and rejected matches

Research covered repairers, refurbished retailers, old-device collection advertisements, regional business directories, business-name history, scrap-metal leads and indexed public Facebook/Instagram results. Searches combined Dubbo with computer/e-waste recycling, old laptops, free pickup, shed/backyard, and candidate business names. Primary pages were preferred over directories. Social pages that could not be opened were not treated as authenticated advertisements.

Important exclusions:

- Scrap dealers and national companies with Dubbo landing pages are not evidence of locally owned backyard device businesses. A historical scrap-dealer registration alone does not prove computer refurbishment or secure data handling.
- Social snippets combined a Dubbo retailer's post with an unrelated pickup offer. Author, locality and service could not be tied together. No personal identity was inferred from that result.
- A community member asking where to dispose of computers is not the recycler. Question authors were excluded as operator identities.
- Repair businesses without an unwanted-device offer remain repair leads. Refurbished sales do not, by themselves, establish local collection or in-house refurbishment.

**Outstanding identity gap:** a name, nickname, business/page name, photograph of an advertisement or original public post from the user's informant would allow the backyard claim to be tested directly. The research result is not a completed census of informal sellers; some operate through word of mouth or non-indexed listings.
