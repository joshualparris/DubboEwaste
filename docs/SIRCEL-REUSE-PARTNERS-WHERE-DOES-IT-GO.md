# Sircel reuse partners: where do the reused devices actually go?

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)

This note follows the question raised by Sircel's FY2023-24 ESG report:

> Sircel says it supplied reuse partners with working laptops, desktop computers and hard drives, but its visibility ended once those assets entered the partner's operational control.

The aim here is to trace what happens **after that handoff**.

## Bottom line

Public evidence does **not** reveal Sircel's complete reuse-partner list or the final destination of every one of the 2,350 assets it reported as reused in FY2023-24.

But the downstream picture is now much clearer.

There are at least **four reuse pathways**:

1. **Charity / digital-inclusion distribution**
2. **Commercial resale through an approved broker**
3. **Redeployment back into client/commercial use**
4. **Failure after handoff, leading to responsible recycling**

The strongest named downstream pathway found is **Good360 Australia's digital-inclusion ecosystem**. Good360's 2024 Annual Report and 2025 Digital Access Report both list **Sircel among product donors helping bridge the digital divide**.

That does **not** prove that all 2,350 Sircel reuse assets went through Good360, nor does Good360 identify the serials or number of devices supplied by Sircel.

It does prove that Sircel has participated in a real Australian device-donation/distribution network that sends refurbished technology into charities, schools and ultimately to people who otherwise may not have a computer.

---

## 1. What Sircel itself says happens after triage

Sircel's current ITAD page says reuse-qualified assets are:

- donated to a charity; or
- resold to an approved broker.

Its FAQ also says passing devices are cleaned and returned to **commercial use**, often through **charity and community organisations**.

Sircel's FY2023-24 ESG report adds more detail:

- 2,350 assets were processed through the reuse pathway;
- data was erased with Blancco;
- suitable assets were redirected to **consumer-facing organisations**;
- Sircel supplied partners with working:
  - laptops;
  - desktop computers;
  - hard drives;
- Sircel explicitly said its visibility ended once the devices came under the partner's operational control.

So the handoff point is real and acknowledged.

What Sircel does **not** publish:

- a full list of reuse partners;
- number of devices going to each partner;
- the approved brokers' names;
- the end-user identity of reused devices;
- whether each device was donated, resold, redeployed or later recycled;
- how long the second life lasted.

---

## 2. Named downstream route: Sircel -> Good360 Australia

This is the clearest public link found.

Good360 Australia's **2024 Annual Report**, in its digital-divide section, thanks organisations helping supply devices and explicitly includes:

- Sircel;
- Compnow;
- Google;
- HP;
- Optus;
- SA Power Networks;
- Schneider Electric;
- other technology donors.

Good360's **January 2025 Digital Access Report** is even clearer. Under a section describing product donors helping vulnerable communities access digital essentials, **Sircel is again named**.

### What this proves

It proves that Sircel has been a **product donor into Good360's digital-device ecosystem**.

### What it does not prove

It does not prove:

- that Good360 received all 2,350 Sircel reuse assets;
- what quantity Sircel supplied;
- exact make/model/age;
- whether the devices were already fully refurbished by Sircel;
- whether Good360 performed a second wipe/test;
- whether Sircel's current 2026 reuse stream still follows the same distribution mix.

That distinction is important.

---

## 3. What Good360 does with donated devices

Good360's current Digital Divide program publishes a clear chain.

### Step 1 - Reclaim

Corporate and government donors provide used:

- laptops;
- phones;
- tablets.

Good360 says its target devices are generally:

- in good working condition;
- ideally no more than about 3-5 years old.

For donor fleets, homogenous batches are preferred because they are cheaper and easier to refurbish.

### Step 2 - Refurbish

Good360 says accredited refurbishment partners:

- securely wipe data;
- test devices;
- prepare/refurbish them;
- reject and responsibly recycle devices that are not fit for purpose.

Its current FAQ says the program can provide:

- data destruction;
- hygiene cleaning;
- test and tag;
- delivery;
- matching to a recipient.

Good360 also says certified data-destruction evidence can be supplied.

### A named refurbishment partner: Compnow

Good360's Compnow case study explains the practical refurbishment process in much more detail.

Compnow:

1. collects end-of-lease corporate fleets;
2. securely wipes all IP/data;
3. assesses damage and cosmetic wear;
4. tests hardware including:
   - battery condition;
   - speakers;
   - camera;
5. performs authorised repairs, including battery replacement where needed;
6. reinstalls the operating system;
7. sources missing accessories such as power adapters;
8. boxes and labels each laptop;
9. delivers the finished device to Good360 for distribution.

This is a useful model for what "refurbished for charity distribution" actually means.

It is **not proof that Compnow refurbishes Sircel's particular devices**, because Sircel may already have completed wiping/testing before donation.

---

## 4. Good360 then matches devices to charities and disadvantaged schools

Good360 does **not normally hand laptops directly to random individuals**.

The device goes through a local organisation.

Its current program uses a vetted national network of thousands of:

- registered charities;
- disadvantaged schools;
- community organisations.

Good360 says NFP recipients must generally be ACNC-registered, and schools in its digital divide program must meet its disadvantage/ICSEA criteria.

The charity or school then distributes the device to a person in its own community.

This is a key design principle:

**central donor/refurbisher -> trusted local organisation -> final user**

rather than:

**central donor -> unknown individual**

The local organisation already understands:

- who is genuinely in need;
- what support they need;
- whether a laptop is appropriate;
- whether data/connectivity/training is also required.

## 5. What happens at the final-user end?

Good360's current impact stories show several real outcomes.

### Education

The Chai Charitable Foundation distributed laptops to upper-secondary students taking VCE subjects who did not own a suitable personal device.

The laptops were then used for:

- assignments;
- research;
- organisation;
- school work at home.

### Employment and independence

Uniting Vic.Tas distributed laptops to people facing digital exclusion through employment and community-care programs.

Good360 records recipients using devices for:

- job search;
- training;
- accessing health information;
- communicating with family;
- online services;
- education.

### Social housing

Housing Choices Australia distributed laptop/data packages to more than 40 residents in a Melbourne housing program.

Reported uses included:

- study;
- job applications;
- online classes;
- communication;
- managing life/admin tasks;
- reducing social isolation.

### Domestic/family violence support

Good360 reports community partners keeping phones/SIMs and laptop libraries available for people who have fled domestic violence.

The device can become a safety and independence tool:

- contacting support;
- managing finances;
- applying for housing;
- study;
- communicating without an abusive partner controlling the device/account.

### Disaster recovery

Good360's 2024 report says 100 refurbished laptops were distributed into Northern Rivers flood-affected communities through the Consortium of Neighbourhood Centres.

Optus added prepaid SIM/dongle connectivity.

So the actual endpoint of a reused device can be:

**student's desk, job seeker's home, social-housing resident, family-violence service, flood-recovery household, community laptop library or NFP program.**

## 6. Connectivity and digital skills can follow the hardware

A laptop alone does not necessarily solve digital exclusion.

Good360 therefore also works on:

- mobile data;
- SIMs/dongles;
- digital training/support.

Optus has supplied connectivity under its Donate Your Data and related programs.

This becomes even more explicit in the **National Device Bank** model.

## 7. National Device Bank: the newer Australian reuse infrastructure

The National Device Bank (NDB) was formally launched in 2025 by:

- **WorkVentures**
- **Good360 Australia**
- **Good Things Australia**

It creates a more formal national pipeline:

**corporate/government donor -> secure refurbishment -> community network -> final recipient + skills/support**

### WorkVentures' role

WorkVentures leads technical refurbishment and secure device lifecycle work.

Its current published process includes:

1. inventory and condition assessment;
2. secure collection;
3. data sanitisation;
4. certificate issuance;
5. refurbishment;
6. reuse, resale or donation;
7. logistics to community;
8. support and reporting.

WorkVentures says it uses **Blancco** and sanitisation standards such as **NIST SP 800-88 or equivalent**.

Devices are:

- assessed;
- repaired where economical;
- imaged/configured;
- prepared for a new user.

Its NDB material says it may apply a Windows image with base software before delivery.

### Recipient layer

The NDB says refurbished devices are distributed through the community networks of:

- WorkVentures;
- Good360;
- Good Things Australia.

Eligible recipients are reached through community organisations.

The NDB terms state devices supplied to recipients are **not to be resold or used for commercial gain**.

### Ongoing support

The NDB is more than a laptop handout.

Its model includes:

- digital coaching;
- literacy support;
- connectivity assistance;
- technical support.

WorkVentures historically supplies support/warranty with refurbished equipment; exact terms vary by program.

### Important Sircel caveat

There is strong evidence that Sircel is/was a Good360 product donor.

There is **not yet public evidence proving that Sircel is a formal National Device Bank donor/ITAD feeder in 2026**.

So the NDB is best treated as:

- a documented continuation/evolution of the same Australian digital-inclusion ecosystem;
- a very plausible destination model for reuse-quality devices;
- **not a confirmed route for every current Sircel asset**.

## 8. Commercial route: Sircel -> approved broker -> second-hand market

Sircel also says qualifying assets can be **resold to an approved broker**.

This branch is much less transparent.

### Confirmed

Sircel:

- assigns/guarantees fair-market-value recovery for qualifying end-user devices;
- offers an agreed-value buy-back model;
- says approved brokers are used for resale;
- is R2v3-certified at Villawood and describes rigorous downstream-partner approval.

### Not publicly disclosed

No public source found in this research identifies:

- the approved broker companies;
- broker purchase prices;
- whether brokers resell in Australia only;
- whether brokers sell wholesale or direct to consumers;
- what warranty is supplied;
- whether brokers export reusable devices;
- how long Sircel tracks a reused device after broker sale.

The strongest Sircel statement remains that its own outcome visibility ends after a reused device enters the partner's operational control.

### Likely commercial flow - labelled as inference

The normal ITAD broker path would be:

**Sircel wipes/tests device -> broker acquires it -> broker grades/possibly refurbishes again -> wholesale or retail resale -> business/consumer buys the second-hand device**

But the **broker names and exact Sircel flow are not publicly verifiable**, so this should remain an inference rather than a repo claim of fact.

---

## 9. Client redeployment is another endpoint

Sircel's current FAQ says cleaned/tested devices may be **re-used by clients** and returned to commercial use.

That creates a third pathway:

**organisation refreshes fleet -> Sircel sanitises/tests -> asset still useful -> redeployed to another employee/site/use**

This is arguably the cleanest form of reuse:

- no third-party resale;
- no charity logistics;
- no need to find another owner;
- useful life is extended inside the original organisation.

For a Dubbo business client, this could mean:

- older laptop no longer suitable for engineering/CAD;
- wipe/reimage;
- redeploy to reception, training room, volunteer, casual staff or kiosk use.

---

## 10. What happens if a reused device later fails?

This is precisely where Sircel says its direct visibility becomes limited.

Once a charity, community organisation, broker or end user controls the device, Sircel generally no longer knows:

- how many years it lasts;
- whether it gets repaired;
- whether it changes owner;
- whether it eventually returns to a recycler;
- whether it is improperly discarded.

This is a real circular-economy measurement problem.

Good reuse systems try to reduce that risk through:

- quality thresholds;
- refurbishment;
- warranties/support;
- recipient agreements;
- local repair networks;
- end-of-life take-back/recycling.

The National Device Bank model is stronger here because it combines device distribution with community organisations and support infrastructure.

## 11. Why this matters for Dubbo E-Waste

There is an opportunity to do something unusually transparent at small scale.

Instead of stopping reporting at:

> **"reused"**

Dubbo E-Waste could record:

- device serial;
- source;
- wipe certificate;
- repair history;
- destination;
- sale/donation date;
- recipient category;
- warranty period;
- if returned;
- eventual recycling outcome if it comes back.

For privacy, a public/client report need not identify an individual recipient.

It could say:

- **Laptop 001 -> sold locally -> still in service**
- **Laptop 002 -> donated via youth service**
- **Laptop 003 -> reused for school/home study**
- **Laptop 004 -> parts harvested -> remainder recycled**
- **Laptop 005 -> failed erase -> drive destroyed -> chassis recycled**

That would provide stronger chain-of-outcome evidence than Sircel's own FY24 ESG report currently provides after partner handoff.

## 12. A model Dubbo social-reuse channel

A highly practical regional structure could be:

### Commercial stream
Good current equipment:
- securely wiped;
- repaired;
- sold on eBay/local market;
- value returned to Dubbo E-Waste/client.

### Social stream
Good equipment that has low commercial value but high practical usefulness:
- securely wiped;
- repaired;
- configured;
- donated through approved local NFP/school partners.

### Parts stream
Not worth selling whole but contains useful:
- SSD;
- RAM;
- charger;
- screen;
- keyboard;
- GPU;
- CPU;
- Wi-Fi card.

### Recycle stream
Only genuinely dead/end-of-life remainder.

This would preserve both:

- financial value;
- social value.

It is essentially a small regional version of the Sircel + Good360/WorkVentures ecosystem.

## 13. The most interesting potential Dubbo partner model

Rather than donating directly to individuals, build a **recipient network**.

Possible categories to investigate locally:

- homelessness services;
- domestic/family violence services;
- youth organisations;
- disability services;
- First Nations organisations;
- disadvantaged schools;
- neighbourhood/community centres;
- employment services;
- refugee/migrant support;
- disaster/recovery groups;
- churches with formal welfare programs.

Do **not** assume any specific organisation wants devices. Each would need to be approached and checked for:

- demand;
- eligibility;
- support capacity;
- privacy;
- safe recipient selection;
- ability to handle warranty/support;
- whether devices can be given permanently or loaned.

A trusted local partner should be the organisation that decides **who receives the device**, not Dubbo E-Waste guessing who is most deserving.

## 14. The crucial unanswered questions for Sircel

The remaining questions are now very specific.

Ask Sircel:

1. Can you provide the names of your current **reuse partners**?
2. Is **Good360 Australia** currently one of them?
3. How many of the 2,350 FY24 reused assets went to:
   - charities;
   - community organisations;
   - brokers;
   - client redeployment?
4. What quantity went to Good360?
5. Which approved brokers do you use?
6. Are those brokers required to keep the equipment in Australia?
7. Are reused devices sold wholesale or retail?
8. What warranty/support is supplied?
9. Does Sircel receive sale reports back from brokers?
10. Does Sircel ever get serial-level final-destination evidence?
11. Why does ESG reporting stop once a partner has operational control?
12. Under R2v3, what downstream due-diligence obligations continue for reuse partners?
13. Are partners contractually required to return devices to responsible recycling at final end-of-life?
14. What percentage of reused devices are still known to be in service after 12/24/36 months?
15. Does Sircel now feed any equipment into the **National Device Bank**?
16. Could a Dubbo regional refurbisher become a Sircel reuse partner?
17. Could Dubbo E-Waste receive devices from Parkes/Sircel that pass reuse triage but are uneconomic for Sircel to process centrally?
18. Could Dubbo E-Waste return failed devices to Sircel for material recycling?
19. Would Sircel accept serial-level outcome reporting from a local reuse partner?
20. Would it support a Dubbo pilot that keeps devices in regional NSW?

## Final finding

The phrase **"distributed to reuse partners"** is not the end of the story.

At least one real, evidenced Sircel-linked pathway continues into:

**Sircel / product donation -> Good360 ecosystem -> secure refurbishment/matching -> registered charity or disadvantaged school -> individual end user -> education, work, safety, connectivity or community use.**

But the evidence stops short of proving that this pathway accounts for all, or even most, of Sircel's 2,350 reported FY24 reused assets.

The commercial-broker branch remains opaque.

For Dubbo E-Waste, that opacity is an opportunity: build **serial-level outcome tracking through the second life**, not just to the point of handoff.
