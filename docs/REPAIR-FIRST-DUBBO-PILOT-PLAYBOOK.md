# Repair First Dubbo — internal pilot playbook

**Updated:** 8 October 2026  
**Audience:** Circular Economy Dubbo staff/volunteers, potential Council/community partners and Dubbo eWaste planning.  
**Status:** proposed pilot design based on current evidence. It is not an operating subsidy or announced Council program.

> **Behaviour target:** Before you replace it, get one repair check.

## 1. What actually changes repair behaviour

The evidence points to five levers working together:

1. **Price** — the repair needs to look financially sensible beside replacement.
2. **Convenience** — one place to find the right repairer, book, diagnose and decide.
3. **Confidence** — clear quote, expected turnaround, warranty and honest “not worth repairing” advice.
4. **Capability** — repairers, skills, parts, manuals and safe referral routes must exist.
5. **Normality** — repair has to become the obvious first check rather than a niche hobby.

A 2026 review of broken-electronics repair found pricing in 48% of the literature, social norms in 42%, product design in 36%, repair knowledge/capability in 33% and values in 31%. It also found repair cost is often *assumed* to be too high before the owner obtains an objective diagnosis.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667378925000616

Australian research using 2,717 households describes repair and maintenance as real “consumption work” requiring time, skill, space and household capacity. A repair system that asks residents to research parts, compare many providers and chase reimbursements recreates the barrier.

Source:  
https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/repair-and-maintenance-in-australian-households-what-drives-this-/

## 2. The repair journey Dubbo should create

### Step 0 — check consumer rights first

Before public money is used, ask:

- Is the item still under a manufacturer/retailer warranty?
- Could Australian Consumer Law require a free repair, replacement or refund?
- Is there a recall?

If the consumer already has a right to a free remedy, direct them there rather than subsidising a private repair.

Current NSW consumer-rights guidance:  
https://www.nsw.gov.au/legal-and-justice/consumer-rights-and-protection/repairs-replacements-and-refunds/rights-to-a-repair

The ACCC reported in April 2026 that consumer-guarantee contacts rose 20% in 2025, with electronics and whitegoods the largest sector for reports. This makes a rights check a meaningful part of “Repair First”.

Source:  
https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/rising-contacts-raise-concerns-about-electronics-and-whitegoods-sector-compliance-with-consumer-guarantee-rights

### Step 1 — Repair Check

Give the resident a fast first answer:

- probable fault or diagnostic result;
- repairable / uncertain / not sensible;
- expected repair price or range;
- expected turnaround;
- comparable replacement price;
- likely useful life after repair;
- commercial repairer / Repair Café / parts / replace-and-recycle next step.

The Repair Check is the most important intervention because many replacement decisions occur before anyone obtains a repair quote.

### Step 2 — remove the price friction

If repair is sensible and not covered by ACL/warranty, offer a small **instant** Repair Bonus through a participating repairer.

### Step 3 — remove downtime where it matters

For high-dependency items such as laptops and phones:
- small loan-device pool;
- common spare chargers/adapters;
- scheduled pickup/drop-off;
- same-day/rapid routes where participating repairers already offer them.

Repair-convenience research identifies time, travel, guarantee and temporary replacement options as meaningful parts of willingness to pay.

Source:  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921344923002987

### Step 4 — make a positive repair experience visible

With consent, show:
- “new equivalent: $650”
- “repair: $180”
- “Repair First discount: $54”
- “resident paid: $126”
- “kept their laptop”

Use local money/time stories first; environmental benefits second.

## 3. Recommended 12-week Dubbo pilot

This is a **test design**, not a prediction.

### Weeks 1–2 — build the network

Interview/onboard repairers by category:
- computers/laptops;
- phones/tablets;
- TV/electronics;
- appliances/whitegoods;
- bikes;
- textiles;
- selected tools/small mechanical goods.

For each repairer record:
- categories/brands;
- diagnostic fee;
- indicative labour rate;
- turnaround;
- call-out/travel fee;
- warranty;
- parts constraints;
- spare weekly capacity;
- items they do **not** want referred.

Current local listings demonstrate that Dubbo has visible repair capacity across computer/phone, TV/electronics, appliance and bicycle categories. These businesses are **candidate contacts, not current project partners**.

### Weeks 3–5 — diagnosis-first baseline

Offer Repair Check / Repair Café / referral **without a cash repair rebate**.

Measure:
- how many people seek a check;
- quote acceptance;
- common faults;
- common reasons people still replace;
- repairer capacity;
- typical repair/replacement price ratios.

This gives a local baseline before money changes behaviour.

### Weeks 6–12 — small instant Repair Bonus

Pilot hypothesis:

- **diagnosis/quote support:** up to **$30**;
- **repair support:** **30% of eligible repair cost, capped at $75**;
- benefit applied at the transaction where practical rather than asking the resident to wait for reimbursement;
- one supported repair per household during the pilot.

The $30 diagnosis support deliberately mirrors a feature in Austria’s current 2026 program. The 30%/$75 repair level is **not claimed as the proven optimum**; it is a deliberately smaller Dubbo pilot level to measure price sensitivity before committing to a 50% scheme.

Austria’s current Device Rescue Bonus pays 50% up to €130 per device and up to €30 for a cost estimate. In early 2026 it quickly developed thousands of participating repair businesses and high voucher redemption.

Sources:
- https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/umwelt_und_klima/energie_und_ressourcen_sparen/geraete-retter-praemie
- https://www.bmluk.gv.at/service/presse/klima-umwelt/2026/geraeteretterpraemie-zeigt-wirkung-hohe-einloesequote-bestaetigt-erfolg.html

### Economic guardrail

For the pilot, consider support only where repair is reasonably competitive with replacement.

A proposed operating rule to test:

> **pre-subsidy repair quote normally <= 50% of a credible comparable replacement price, unless there is a strong durability, sentimental, accessibility or specialist reason.**

This is a pilot guardrail, not a universal research threshold.

Consumer research commonly finds willingness to pay is sensitive to repair price and that many consumers think in terms of repair cost relative to replacement. International studies often locate perceived acceptable repair cost around 15–20% of new price, although this varies by product and service convenience.

Source:  
https://www.nim.org/en/studies/detail/repair-or-buy-new

The purpose of the subsidy is to move a repair that is technically sensible closer to the customer’s acceptable range, not to subsidise obviously uneconomic repairs.

## 4. Why the discount should be immediate

A 2026 choice experiment with 972 participants found the funding level was important, but process design and payment/application friction were nearly as influential. Some consumers are strongly “process-oriented” and prefer simple, low-effort, immediate benefits.

Source:  
https://doi.org/10.55845/joce-2026-41360

Dubbo should avoid:

> Pay $200, upload receipts, wait weeks, then receive a rebate.

Prefer:

> Repair is $200. Eligible Repair First discount is $60. Resident pays $140. Repairer/program settles the remainder.

The exact reimbursement mechanics need to match whichever organisation legally funds/administers the pilot.

## 5. Prevent subsidy waste

A September 2026 study of Austria’s repair subsidy found:
- some users experience no behaviour change;
- some are nudged into considering repair;
- some actually repair more;
- **windfall effects** occur where already-committed repairers receive money for behaviour that would have happened anyway;
- awareness and scheme design are prerequisites for success.

Source:  
https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/consumer-perceptions-of-financial-incentives-for-repair-informati/

Before revealing/confirming the bonus, ask:

> **Without this program, what would you most likely do?**
> - pay for this repair anyway
> - buy a replacement
> - throw/recycle the item
> - keep it broken
> - try to fix it myself
> - not sure

This lets Dubbo measure **additionality**, rather than reporting every voucher as a behavioural success.

## 6. Measure the thing that matters: did a repair displace replacement?

Minimum record for each participant:

### At intake
- item category;
- approximate age;
- fault;
- warranty/ACL status checked;
- intended action without program;
- comparable replacement price;
- previous repair attempt?;
- how they heard about the program.

### Repair Check
- repairable? yes/no/uncertain;
- quote;
- diagnosis fee;
- predicted turnaround;
- parts availability;
- commercial/community referral.

### Outcome
- repaired?;
- repair cost;
- bonus amount;
- resident out-of-pocket;
- repairer;
- turnaround;
- reason if declined;
- item recycled/reused/stored/replaced.

### 30-day follow-up
- repair still working?;
- satisfaction;
- did they avoid buying a replacement?;
- would they repair first next time?;
- would they recommend the repairer/program?

### 6-month follow-up where practical
- item still in service?;
- further faults?;
- replacement eventually purchased?;
- second repair attempted?

Core metrics:
- Repair Check → completed repair conversion;
- baseline vs bonus-phase repair conversion;
- **incremental repairs** attributed to the program;
- cost per additional repair;
- cost per reported replacement avoided;
- local repair spend generated;
- median turnaround;
- referral completion;
- repairer capacity/utilisation;
- repeat participation;
- participant trust/satisfaction.

Do not claim carbon savings from a generic “item repaired” multiplier unless a defensible product-specific method is used.

## 7. Categories: do not treat all repair the same

### Strong early pilot categories

**Computers/laptops**
- high replacement value;
- local repair capability;
- possible loaners;
- parts/donor ecosystem with Dubbo eWaste;
- clear software/hardware triage.

**Phones/tablets**
- high dependence/downtime cost;
- common battery/screen repairs;
- local repair capability;
- account/data and battery safety controls needed.

**Bikes**
- mature repair culture;
- clear workshop services;
- high community/health benefit;
- suitable Repair Café maintenance workshops.

**Textiles**
- low safety risk;
- good volunteer/skill-building category;
- repair cost/value logic differs from electronics.

### Second-stage categories

**Whitegoods**
- large waste benefit;
- strong NSW repair-policy interest;
- but higher call-out/logistics/safety complexity.

**TV/electronics**
- local specialists exist;
- parts/panel economics can make some repairs unattractive.

### Exclusions / special routes

Do not use community repair volunteers for:
- unsafe mains work beyond competence/insurance;
- swollen/damaged lithium batteries;
- gas/refrigeration work;
- recalled unsafe goods;
- safety-critical repairs without appropriate qualifications.

## 8. Repairer-network standard

The scheme should **support local repair businesses**, not replace them.

Candidate participating commercial repairers should agree to:
- ABN/business identity recorded;
- appropriate insurance/licensing for their trade;
- clear diagnostic/quote fee;
- no work above an agreed amount without customer approval;
- expected turnaround;
- repair workmanship/parts warranty stated;
- ACL-compliant repair notice where required;
- data-handling warning for devices retaining user data;
- refurbished-part notice where applicable;
- simple program outcome reporting;
- honest “not economically sensible” assessments.

Australian Consumer Law requires repair notices in relevant circumstances for data-retaining goods and where refurbished goods/parts may be used.

Sources:
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/legal-and-justice/consumer-rights-and-protection/repairs-replacements-and-refunds/rights-to-a-repair
- https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B01420/2026-05-20/2026-05-20/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html

## 9. Where Dubbo should put the prompt

Repair incentives work best **at the replacement/disposal decision**, not only in sustainability advertising.

Potential touchpoints to propose:

### Council / waste journey
- MyDRC Waste / bulky-rubbish booking: “Could this be repaired? Get one Repair Check first.”
- Whylandra/CRC signage or QR: repair/reuse before recycling where lawful and operationally appropriate.
- Council A–Z recycling pages.
- Waste education events.
- Circularity Cooperative website/materials.

Council’s Waste Strategy explicitly tells residents to repair and mend rather than replace, and includes a Circularity Cooperative, Circular Economy Roadmap, business collaboration and a circular-economy project role.

Sources:
- https://yoursay.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/2024-waste-strategy
- https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/ArticleDocuments/242/Waste_Strategy_2025_Adopted.pdf.aspx

### Community journey
- Macquarie Regional Library: information/referral only unless service boundaries change;
- Repair Café events;
- Men’s Sheds as possible skill/venue contacts if they agree;
- neighbourhood/community organisations;
- schools/young people for maintenance/repair culture education.

### Commercial journey
- participating repairer counters/websites;
- participating second-hand/refurb sellers;
- workplaces: lunchtime “bring your broken tech/bike/small item” diagnosis days.

Do not describe any named organisation as a partner until it has agreed.

## 10. Repair Café’s role

Repair Café should be:

- a **front door** for repair;
- simple diagnosis;
- maintenance;
- low-risk fixes;
- skill sharing;
- volunteer recruitment;
- commercial repair referrals;
- local repair data collection.

It should not be:
- a free substitute for specialist commercial repairs;
- an anonymous drop-off queue;
- a place for unsafe battery/mains work without controls.

Repair Café International’s 2024 RepairMonitor logged more than 37,000 attempts with 62% successfully repaired.

Source:  
https://www.repaircafe.org/en/repairmonitor-factsheet-provides-insight-into-repair-data-for-2024/

Sunshine Repair Cafe in Victoria reports 6.2 tonnes diverted, 239 people reached and 277 volunteer hours, and uses appointments to match volunteers to repair categories. Its organisers emphasise repeated visibility and learning-to-fix habits.

Source:  
https://www.sustainability.vic.gov.au/news/news-articles/fix-dont-toss-repair-cafes-teach-community-to-repair-re-use-refurbish

## 11. Link Repair First to Dubbo eWaste

Dubbo eWaste can strengthen repair without becoming a free repair shop.

Useful roles:
- electronics Repair Check;
- donor parts from genuinely end-of-life equipment;
- spare chargers/adapters/RAM/storage where lawful and safe;
- loan laptops;
- referral to commercial repairers;
- secure data handling;
- refurbish/reuse pathway when the original owner no longer wants the device;
- responsible recycling when repair/reuse is finished.

Potential future “donor part bonus” can borrow from France’s current approach, which gives extra support where circular/reused parts are used in eligible repairs.

Sources:
- https://www.economie.gouv.fr/particuliers/mes-droits-conso/bien-consommer/bonus-reparation-comment-ca-marche
- https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/politiques-publiques/bonus-reparation-quest-ce-que-cest

Start without an extra parts bonus; measure donor-part availability first.

## 13. Indicative micro-pilot budget

A **hypothetical** $10,000 pilot could be designed approximately as:

- $2,000 — diagnosis/Repair Check support;
- $4,500 — repair bonuses;
- $1,000 — volunteer/repairer consumables and common parts;
- $500 — loan-device preparation/chargers;
- $1,000 — promotion/signage/booking materials;
- $1,000 — insurance/venue/admin/data collection contingency.

This is an example for program design, **not a quote**.

A lower-cost pilot can start with:
- no repair cash subsidy;
- donated venue;
- Repair Check;
- directory/referral;
- Repair Café;
- outcome measurement.

Then use local conversion/quote data to decide whether a bonus is actually needed and at what level.

## 14. Messaging

### Main public line

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

### Supporting lines

> **Fix it if it makes sense. Replace it if it doesn’t.**

> **Know the repair price before you spend on a replacement.**

> **Keep the thing you already own working for longer.**

### Avoid leading with

- guilt;
- tonnes of abstract waste;
- “save the planet” as the only reason;
- claims every object should be repaired.

Environmental benefits are real, but cost, convenience and trust are the decision levers.

## 15. What success looks like after 12 weeks

Do not judge success by:
- social-media impressions;
- voucher claims alone;
- number of items presented.

Judge it by:

1. more people obtaining a repair assessment before replacement;
2. higher completed-repair conversion after the incentive is introduced;
3. documented additional repairs that likely would not have occurred otherwise;
4. acceptable cost per additional repair/replacement avoided;
5. residents reporting higher confidence in repair;
6. local repair businesses gaining work without unsustainable admin;
7. repairer capacity remaining healthy;
8. unsafe/uneconomic items being correctly refused/referred;
9. real local evidence strong enough to design the next phase.

## 16. Best next actions

1. **Interview 8–12 Dubbo repair businesses** across electronics, appliances, bikes and textiles.
2. Ask each for diagnosis fee, common job value, turnaround, warranty, capacity and biggest reasons customers decline repairs.
3. Build the internal Repair Dubbo directory.
4. Run **three measured Repair Café / Repair Check sessions** before subsidising repairs.
5. Collect baseline quote-acceptance and replacement-intention data.
6. Approach Council’s Resource Recovery & Efficiency / circular-economy work with the local evidence.
7. Ask SSROC for the Phase 1 repair-incentive report/pilot architecture and lessons from Phase 2.
8. Design a small instant Repair Bonus around the observed Dubbo price gaps.
9. Pilot for 6–8 weeks.
10. Publish the result honestly, including what did **not** work.

## Evidence hierarchy

**Strongest for Dubbo design**
- Australian Productivity Commission / ACCC / NSW consumer law;
- 2025–26 Australian repair research;
- SSROC/NSW EPA repair-incentive project;
- current Dubbo Waste Strategy and funding program;
- current local repair-capacity mapping.

**Transferable comparator evidence**
- Austria repair subsidy;
- France Repair Bonus;
- Repair Café outcomes;
- other Australian council/community repair programs.

**Still needs Dubbo field evidence**
- residents’ actual quote acceptance;
- preferred subsidy level;
- repairer spare capacity;
- local diagnosis pricing;
- average downtime;
- parts lead times;
- willingness of local businesses to participate;
- true incremental repair rate.
