## 34. Repair Café role (INFERENCE grounded in the above)
Where it fits: the **front end** of the system — a place for owners to get a repairability assessment *before* disposal, learn skills, and create a volunteer repairer network. It does not replace Whylandra reuse, a refurb enterprise, or commercial repairers.

Design points drawn from comparators:
- Council partnership (venue, insurance, promotion) is typical; Council-run exemplars (Waverley) show Tag & Test must be on site for electricals.
- Inclusive access: Dubbo's Men's Sheds are men-oriented, so an open-to-all event fills a different niche; recruit Shed members as volunteers (partnership, not competition).
- Triage categories to start: textiles, small electricals, bikes, simple furniture, knives, books, toys; computers/phones as a "tech clinic" (data privacy rules).
- Commit to "unrepaired items go home" (Adelaide model) to avoid creating a dump.
- Keep a repairs log (success rate, causes of failure, parts needed) — this is the evidence stream Council needs for Strategy KPIs.
- Insurance/risk, WHS, electrical safety, volunteer screening, venue are the main Council hurdles (Risk, Property, Library, Events).
