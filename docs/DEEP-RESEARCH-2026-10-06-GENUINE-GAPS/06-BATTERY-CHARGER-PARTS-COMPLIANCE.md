# 06 — Australian battery, charger and replacement-parts compliance

**Questions answered:** 19–21  
**Research date:** 6 October 2026

## 19. Replacement batteries

The current Electrical Equipment Safety System (EESS) guidance states that **extra-low-voltage (ELV) battery-powered equipment is not currently EESS in-scope**.

That does **not** mean “unregulated” or “safe by default”.

EESS says not-in-scope electrical equipment still must be electrically safe and suppliers retain safety responsibility.

Sources:
- https://www.eess.gov.au/equipment/not-in-scope/
- https://www.eess.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/General-Safety-tips-for-Lithium-ion-battery-powered-equipment-v1.0.pdf

ACCC lithium-ion safety guidance recommends:

- replacement batteries from the original or a reputable supplier;
- correct compatibility;
- correct charger;
- stopping use for swelling, overheating, damage or other abnormal behaviour.

Source:
https://www.productsafety.gov.au/product-safety-laws/safety-standards-bans/product-safety-laboratory-testing/lithium-ion-batteries

### Operational conclusion

For laptop/phone batteries:

**Preferred**
1. genuine/OEM through authorised Australian channel;
2. reputable Australian replacement supplier with traceable model/brand, warranty and safety evidence.

**Avoid**
- anonymous marketplace battery;
- no manufacturer/importer identity;
- missing model/rating markings;
- suspiciously low price;
- no warranty/returns;
- no transport/safety documentation on request;
- cells/battery modified/repacked without an appropriate controlled process.

Do not perform cell-level battery rebuilding in Phase 0.

## 20. Chargers and power supplies — much stronger regulatory position

EESS explicitly gives a mobile-phone charger as an example of in-scope **Level 3 “Power supply or charger”** equipment.

For in-scope equipment, downstream suppliers such as retailers must:

- source it from a **Registered Responsible Supplier**;
- ensure Level 2/3 equipment is registered;
- ensure appropriate RCM marking.

Sources:
- https://www.eess.gov.au/equipment/not-in-scope/
- https://www.eess.gov.au/responsible-supplier/other-suppliers/
- https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/eess-registration-database/

The EESS public database can be searched without an account.

### Critical Dubbo ITAD distinction

**Buying a compliant charger from an Australian registered supply chain and reselling it with a laptop** is very different from:

**directly importing cheap generic chargers from overseas.**

A local manufacturer/importer can become the “Responsible Supplier” with registration, certification/evidence and labelling obligations.

For Phase 0:

> **Do not direct-import generic mains chargers for resale.**

Buy from a reputable Australian supplier and preserve invoice/supplier/model evidence.

### Used OEM chargers

Second-hand electrical goods still need to be safe. The existing repo's electrical-safety work should continue to govern physical inspection/testing questions.

RCM presence by itself is not proof that a damaged used charger remains safe.

## 21. Supplier evidence checklist

For any replacement battery, charger or safety-relevant electronic part record:

### Supplier identity
- Australian legal/trading name;
- ABN where applicable;
- address/contact;
- invoice.

### Part identity
- manufacturer/brand;
- exact part number;
- compatibility;
- voltage/current/wattage;
- battery Wh where relevant;
- serial/batch if present.

### Charger / in-scope electrical
- RCM present;
- responsible supplier identifiable;
- EESS public database check for Level 2/3 equipment;
- certificate/registration reference when applicable.

### Battery
- OEM or replacement brand;
- credible Australian supply chain;
- warranty;
- compatibility;
- no swelling/damage;
- manufacturer safety information;
- evidence supplied if a business customer asks.

### Recall
- Product Safety Australia / OEM recall check before installation/resale.

### Record outcome
- source;
- installed-in asset ID;
- install date;
- test result;
- old part final route.

## Strongest sourcing rule

For a low-volume refurbisher, saving $25 on a no-name battery/charger can create:

- fire/electrical risk;
- warranty/ACL exposure;
- returns;
- reputation damage;
- inability to evidence supply chain.

Cheap safety-critical parts are a false economy.
