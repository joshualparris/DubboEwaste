# Repair First Dubbo — evidence-based implementation plan

**Research date:** 8 October 2026  
**Question:** How do you actually incentivise people in Dubbo to repair their stuff instead of automatically buying new?  
**Scope:** electronics, appliances, clothing, bikes and household items; behaviour change; local repair capacity; local government; funding; repair cafés; repair rebates; trust; convenience; parts and skills.

---

# Executive conclusion

The evidence is consistent:

> **People do not switch to repair mainly because they are told it is environmentally better. They switch when repair becomes cheaper, easier, faster, safer and more trustworthy than replacement.**

For Dubbo, the strongest intervention is not one campaign. It is a **repair ecosystem** with six reinforcing pieces:

1. **Repair Check** — free or very cheap first diagnosis.
2. **Repair Bonus** — an immediate discount on eligible repairs.
3. **Repair Dubbo directory** — one trusted place to find the right local repairer.
4. **Repair Café / Fix-It sessions** — free/community help for simple repairs and skill-building.
5. **Repair guarantees + clear quotes** — reduce fear that money spent on repair will be wasted.
6. **Parts, loaners and referrals** — remove practical reasons people give up.

The single best behavioural goal is:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

That is more realistic than trying to turn every resident into a DIY repairer.

# 1. What the research says actually stops people repairing

Repair decisions are influenced by three broad things:

## A. Can it technically be repaired?

Barriers include:
- sealed/glued product design;
- unavailable spare parts;
- lack of repair manuals;
- software/firmware locks;
- proprietary tools;
- unsupported devices.

A 2023 systematic review of repair barriers found technical repairability matters, but the next category — **convenience** — is often even more decisive for consumers.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2023.139705

## B. Is repair convenient and affordable?

The strongest recurring barriers are:

- diagnosis cost;
- repair price;
- waiting time;
- travel;
- difficulty finding the right repairer;
- no spare part locally;
- uncertainty about final price;
- losing access to a phone/laptop/appliance while it is away.

Repair-service convenience research specifically identifies speed, flexibility, search effort and access to temporary replacement devices as important.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2023.137763

## C. Does the person *want* to repair?

Even when an item is technically repairable and a service exists, replacement can still win because of:

- novelty;
- new-product promotions;
- distrust of repair;
- belief the repaired product will fail again;
- low attachment to cheap goods;
- not thinking of repair at all;
- a cultural default that “broken = replace”.

Research on public repair behaviour finds repair intention is influenced by values and attitudes, but also by **skills, repair access and the economic benefit of repair**.

Source:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1111/jiec.13451

# 2. Australian evidence: people say they value repair, but behaviour lags

CHOICE's submission to Australia's Productivity Commission found:

- **85%** of Australians considered long product life important;
- **73%** said repairability was important;
- only **39%** regularly chose longer-lasting products;
- only **46%** chose easily repairable products all or most of the time.

That is a classic attitude–behaviour gap.

Source:
https://www.choice.com.au/consumer-advocacy/submissions-reports-complaints/2021/submission-to-productivity-commission-issues-paper-on-right-to-repair

The Productivity Commission concluded there are significant and unnecessary repair barriers in Australia and that no single policy solves them.

Source:
https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/repair/report/

A 2026 Australian academic study on repair incentives identified:

- affordability barriers;
- lack of repair venues;
- repair-skill shortages;
- spare-parts constraints;
- repair-information constraints;
- strong justification for incentives, but a warning that incentives need repair capacity behind them.

Source:
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14486563.2026.2667758

# 3. Why a repair bonus is promising

## Austria

Austria's national Repair Bonus became extremely popular.

The Austrian program's 2025 annual reporting says:

- **452,425 repairs** were approved in 2025 alone;
- €46.7 million in repair bonuses were approved;
- demand was so high that funding was exhausted and the program paused in May 2025;
- a successor program launched in January 2026.

The Austrian Government also reported around **1.7 million repair vouchers** redeemed since the national program began in 2022.

Sources:
- https://www.umweltfoerderung.at/fileadmin/user_upload/public_consulting/KPC_GeschaeftsberichtDE_2025_Webversion.pdf
- https://www.bundeskanzleramt.gv.at/eu-aufbauplan/aktuelles/reparaturbonus-pausiert-foerderaktion-seit-26-mai-2025-ausser-kraft.html

## France

France's Bonus Réparation gives an **instant discount** through accredited repairers.

The explicit purpose is to make repair financially more attractive than replacement and lengthen product life.

Source:
https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/politiques-publiques/bonus-reparation-quest-ce-que-cest

## What subsidy research says

A 2024 economic study found repair subsidies can:
- increase repair demand;
- improve repair-business profitability;

but the subsidy design determines how effective it is.

Source:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652624025514

A 2026 study of repair-voucher systems found:
- demand increases;
- awareness and willingness to repair increase;
- but vouchers alone do not fix shortages of repairers, spare parts or repairable products.

Source:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652626003975

# 4. The design of the subsidy matters almost as much as the amount

A 2026 repair-subsidy choice experiment with 972 participants found:

- funding level matters;
- **administrative friction matters almost as much**;
- consumers strongly prefer immediate benefits;
- retrospective reimbursement is disliked;
- people prefer simple, low-effort procedures.

The study recommends:
- low-friction application;
- immediate invoice reduction;
- meaningful support;
- clear communication.

Source:
https://www.circulareconomyjournal.org/ojs/JoCE/article/view/360

## Dubbo rule

Do **not** create this:

> Pay the repairer $180 now, fill in a form, upload documents, wait six weeks and maybe receive $90.

Create this:

> Repair is $180. Your Repair First Dubbo discount is $90. You pay $90 today. The repairer claims the rest.

That removes:
- liquidity pressure;
- uncertainty;
- paperwork;
- delay.

# 5. Repair bonuses are not enough by themselves

A subsidy can fail if:

- nobody knows who repairs the item;
- local repairers are booked for weeks;
- the part cannot be sourced;
- diagnosis costs money before the owner knows whether repair is worthwhile;
- the owner needs the product immediately;
- repairers are perceived as risky;
- the owner does not trust the outcome.

The strongest evidence therefore supports **repair infrastructure plus a subsidy**, not subsidy-only policy.

For Dubbo, this means the voucher should launch only after:
- local repair capability is mapped;
- participating businesses agree on basic standards;
- booking/referral exists;
- outcome data can be collected.

# 6. The biggest behavioural opportunity: diagnosis before replacement

Many repair decisions are lost before a repairer ever sees the item.

People do not know whether:

- the fault is trivial;
- a battery is all it needs;
- software is the problem;
- a cable or power supply is faulty;
- a filter is blocked;
- a switch has failed;
- a $20 part would restore it.

## Proposed program: Repair Check

Offer a **free or subsidised first diagnosis** through:
- participating commercial repairers;
- Repair Café sessions;
- Dubbo eWaste electronics triage;
- workplace pop-ups.

The person receives:

**Repairable / likely repair cost / replacement equivalent / expected extra life / recommended next step**

The key prompt is:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

This is likely to be cheaper to fund than subsidising every completed repair while still changing the decision point.

# 8. Build a Repair Dubbo directory

One trusted page should answer:

- What item is broken?
- Who repairs it in Dubbo?
- What brands/categories do they accept?
- Diagnosis fee?
- Typical turnaround?
- Appointment or walk-in?
- Home call-out?
- Repair warranty?
- Can they source parts?
- Do they repair or only replace modules?
- If repair is uneconomic, what is the next reuse/recycling pathway?

## Categories

Start with:
- computers/laptops;
- mobile phones/tablets;
- TVs/electronics;
- whitegoods;
- small appliances;
- bicycles;
- clothing/textiles;
- furniture;
- garden tools/mowers;
- sewing machines.

## Why this matters

Search effort is a repair cost.

If a person has to:
- Google;
- call six shops;
- explain the problem repeatedly;
- drive around;
- wait for callbacks;

buying a replacement becomes psychologically easier.

A directory reduces that hidden cost.

# 9. Make trust part of the incentive

People compare:

**new product + warranty + certainty**

against:

**repair + uncertainty**

So “repair” must look professional and low-risk.

Participating commercial repairers in a Repair First scheme should ideally provide:

- written quote or price range;
- no work above an agreed amount without approval;
- clear labour/parts breakdown;
- realistic turnaround;
- clear warranty on repair workmanship/parts;
- no-surprise-price policy;
- privacy/data-handling statement where relevant;
- honest “not worth repairing” advice.

## Important cultural point

A repair program becomes credible when it sometimes says:

> **Replace this one. Repair does not make sense.**

The goal is not to keep every object alive forever.

It is to stop premature replacement where repair is sensible.

# 12. Repair Café should complement commercial repairers, not undercut them

A badly designed Repair Café can annoy local businesses if it gives away work that would otherwise support skilled local repairers.

The division should be:

## Community Repair Café

Best for:
- diagnosis;
- simple fixes;
- maintenance;
- teaching;
- sewing/mending;
- minor bike adjustments;
- low-value devices;
- basic laptop/software support;
- “is this worth repairing?” assessment.

## Commercial repairers

Best for:
- specialist phone repair;
- whitegoods;
- mains electrical;
- board-level electronics;
- specialist TV repair;
- significant bike work;
- safety-critical work;
- warranty-backed commercial repairs.

## Referral loop

When the volunteer cannot or should not perform the job:

> refer to a participating local repair business.

This converts the Repair Café into a **customer acquisition channel for local repairers**, not a competitor.

# 16. Repair First Dubbo pilot design

## Phase 0 — map capacity before spending money

Interview local repair businesses.

Ask:
- categories accepted;
- brands;
- diagnosis fee;
- minimum labour;
- typical turnaround;
- common jobs;
- common “not worth it” jobs;
- spare-parts problems;
- capacity per week;
- whether they would accept vouchers;
- warranty;
- safety/compliance requirements.

Then map:
- community repair volunteers;
- Men's Sheds;
- sewing skills;
- bike skills;
- electronics/computer volunteers.

## Phase 1 — Repair Check

Run the simplest behaviour-change intervention first:

> **Free Repair Check: before replacing it, get one diagnosis.**

Possible model:
- resident books online/phone;
- approved repairer/Repair Café gives diagnosis;
- resident gets repair quote and replacement comparison;
- project records decision.

## Phase 2 — Repair Bonus

Test an **instant point-of-sale** contribution.

Recommended values for testing, not final policy:

### Portable electronics / small appliances
- up to 50% of eligible labour/repair cost;
- pilot cap around **$50–$75**.

### Phones / laptops / TVs
- up to 50%;
- pilot cap around **$75–$100**.

### Larger appliances
- potentially **$100–$150**, but only after local repair pricing is understood.

Do not finalise these amounts until Dubbo repairers provide real job data.

## Phase 3 — Repair Café

Monthly:
- pre-booked repair items;
- limited walk-ins;
- one item per person initially;
- volunteer + commercial referral model.

## Phase 4 — workplace/school/community pop-ups

Bring diagnosis to:
- large employers;
- community centres;
- events;
- Wellington;
- selected villages.

# 17. Who should receive the Repair Bonus?

A repair should qualify only when:

- item is owned/legitimately held by applicant;
- outside ordinary free warranty/consumer-guarantee remedy where applicable;
- participating repairer judges repair technically sensible and safe;
- repair provides meaningful additional use;
- replacement cost substantially exceeds customer repair contribution;
- product is not recalled/unsafe;
- repair does not create unreasonable safety risk.

## Do not subsidise

- cosmetic-only work where the program is aimed at waste avoidance;
- mandatory recall repairs that should be handled by manufacturer;
- warranty/ACL work that should already be free;
- unsafe devices;
- clearly uneconomic repairs;
- duplicate/fraudulent claims.

---

# 18. Avoid a simplistic “50% of new price” rule

Repair decisions depend on:

- product age;
- expected remaining life;
- efficiency;
- software support;
- replacement price;
- emotional/sentimental value;
- availability of parts;
- repair warranty;
- safety.

Research on small electrical products has found willingness to repair can be very low when repair costs approach replacement costs; some literature estimates willingness around 20% of replacement value for small EEE.

Source:
https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/5/2993

That does **not** mean Dubbo should hard-code 20%.

Instead the program should capture:

**repair price / equivalent replacement price / expected extra life**

and use its own pilot data to find the real Dubbo threshold.

---

# 19. Immediate price comparison should be part of every quote

A repair quote is psychologically stronger when it says:

> **Repair: $120**  
> **Equivalent replacement: ~$650**  
> **Likely additional useful life: 2+ years**  
> **Repair First discount: $60**  
> **You pay: $60**

This makes the decision concrete.

Do not lead with:
- kilograms;
- carbon;
- landfill.

Lead with:
- **money saved**;
- **time saved**;
- **years of use regained**.

Then add the environmental impact.

# 20. Use local social proof

A repair culture is built when people repeatedly see repair succeeding.

Weekly/fortnightly stories could show:

> “10-year-old laptop. Slow, not dead. SSD + clean-up. Back in use.”

> “Phone battery replaced. No need for a $1,200 upgrade.”

> “Bike needed a service and brake parts, not a new bike.”

> “Jacket zip replaced instead of buying another jacket.”

Track:
- repair cost;
- replacement estimate;
- dollars avoided;
- age;
- extra life;
- category;
- local repairer.

The campaign should make the behaviour feel:

**normal, practical and financially smart.**

Not:
**fringe, cheap or preachy.**

# 21. Purchase-stage incentives matter too

Repair culture starts when people buy.

CHOICE found Australians want durability/repair information but often do not act on it because information is weak.

Create a local:

> **Repairable Choice checklist**

Ask before buying:

- How long will software/security updates last?
- Is the battery replaceable?
- Are replacement parts sold?
- Is there a local repairer?
- Is a service manual available?
- Is it glued or screwed together?
- Does the brand support independent repair?
- What is the typical replacement battery/screen/pump/motor cost?

SSROC has advocated a national **Repair Star Rating** similar to energy/water labels.

Source:
https://ssroc.nsw.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/20241016-SSROC-Submission-Productivity-Commission-CE-Enquiry.pdf

# 28. Repair before the tip

An especially strong behaviour-change moment is **before disposal**.

At Council e-waste/hard-waste channels, messaging could ask:

> **Could this be repaired first?**

Possible options:
- QR code to Repair Dubbo directory;
- Repair Check booking;
- “working / repairable / end-of-life?” decision guide.

Important:
- this must happen **before** an item is legally surrendered into a recycling contract/stream;
- do not encourage scavenging from waste facilities.

This is a front-door diversion strategy, not tip scavenging.

- repair experiences;
- willingness to repair;
- reasons for replacement.

## Repair Check data

Record:
- item category;
- age;
- fault;
- diagnosis;
- repair quote;
- estimated replacement price;
- owner intention before diagnosis;
- decision after diagnosis.

## Completed repair data

Record:
- repairer;
- repair cost;
- subsidy;
- parts;
- turnaround;
- warranty;
- outcome;
- estimated extra useful life.

## Failure data

Record why repair did not occur:
- too expensive;
- no parts;
- no local skill;
- too slow;
- unsafe;
- owner chose new;
- unsupported software;
- unrepairable design.

## Behaviour change

At 3–6 months:
- would owner repair again?
- did repaired item remain in use?
- did owner repair another item?
- would owner recommend repair?

---

# 30. The most important KPI is not “number of vouchers”

A scheme can waste money subsidising people who would have repaired anyway.

The best KPI is closer to:

> **additional repairs caused by the intervention**

Ask every participant:

> “Before receiving the Repair Check / Bonus, what were you most likely to do?”

Options:
- repair anyway;
- buy new;
- buy second-hand;
- keep broken;
- dispose/recycle;
- unsure.

Then estimate **additionality**.

This addresses one of the Productivity Commission's concerns about subsidies paying for repairs that would have happened anyway.

---

# 31. Use a control/comparison where possible

For a stronger evaluation:

- launch in Dubbo first, not Wellington;
- or use different voucher amounts/categories;
- or stagger rollout.

Then compare:
- repair uptake;
- conversion from diagnosis to repair;
- cost per additional repair.

A simple staged rollout would make any future grant/business case much stronger.

---

# 32. Repair First Dubbo: recommended package

## Public promise

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

## Service stack

### 1. Repair Check
Free/subsidised initial diagnosis.

### 2. Repair Bonus
Immediate point-of-sale discount through participating repairers.

### 3. Repair Dubbo
Trusted directory + booking/referral.

### 4. Community Repair Café
Monthly practical repair + learning.

### 5. Donor Parts
Dubbo eWaste helps supply reusable components.

### 6. Loan Pool
Phones/laptops where practical.

### 7. Refurbished alternative
If original is uneconomic, offer/referral to refurbished before new.

### 8. Repair stories
Local price/outcome examples every week.

### 9. Repairable purchasing guide
Help residents buy products that can be kept longer.

### 10. Data dashboard
Publish outcomes and structural barriers.

---

# 33. What Dubbo eWaste should do in this system

Dubbo eWaste should **not** try to become every type of repair business.

Its strongest role is the **triage and circular-routing layer**:

**broken item**
→ diagnose
→ simple repair / DIY help
→ specialist local repairer
→ refurbish/upgrade
→ donor parts
→ refurbished replacement
→ recycle only when genuinely end-of-life

Specific strengths for an electronics-focused Dubbo eWaste operation:

- laptop/desktop triage;
- SSD/RAM upgrades;
- operating-system reuse;
- Linux clinics;
- device data handling;
- donor electronics parts;
- refurbished replacement devices;
- e-waste downstream referral.

This complements:
- appliance repairers;
- phone shops;
- bike shops;
- textile repairers;
- Men's Sheds.

# 35. What not to do

## Do not rely on guilt

“Save the planet” is weaker than:
- “save $500”;
- “same day”;
- “warranty included”;
- “local repairer”.

## Do not create paperwork-heavy rebates

Retrospective reimbursement adds friction.

## Do not create demand without repair capacity

A bad first experience teaches people that repair is impossible.

## Do not subsidise every repair

Target additionality.

## Do not use volunteers for work that needs licensed/skilled commercial repair

Safety matters.

## Do not create a free service that destroys the commercial repair market

Repair Café should feed local repair businesses.

## Do not make the waste facility the repair centre

Repair needs to be available before disposal.

---

# 36. Recommended first policy experiment

If Dubbo can only test one intervention:

> **Repair Check + instant Repair Bonus**

Design:

- resident receives free/subsidised diagnosis;
- written quote;
- repair-vs-replacement comparison;
- if eligible, instant discount at participating repairer;
- all outcomes measured.

Add the Repair Café as the social/community layer.

Why this is stronger than Repair Café alone:

- works for people who do not want DIY;
- supports existing businesses;
- handles specialist work;
- changes the economics;
- builds reliable data.

Why this is stronger than voucher alone:

- gets people to consider repair;
- reduces uncertainty;
- creates trusted referral;
- diagnoses structural barriers.

---

# 37. Bottom line

To change Dubbo from:

> **broken → buy new**

to:

> **broken → check → repair if sensible → reuse/refurbish → replace only when necessary**

you need to redesign the decision environment.

The winning formula is:

> **lower the price + lower the hassle + lower the risk + make repair visible + make trusted repair easy to find + make sure parts and skills exist.**

The best local campaign line is:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

And the strategic role for Dubbo eWaste is bigger than recycling:

> **become part of the local infrastructure that prevents useful products becoming waste in the first place.**
