# Research 02 — skills, courses, videos, and practice path

**Status:** first deep research tranche; recommendations require local availability and safety checks
**Research date:** 2 October 2026
**Scope:** identify the cheapest credible way to learn front-door triage, computer refurbishment, basic electronics repair, data/lock handling, and resale judgement without pretending that a certificate alone creates competence.

This report follows [`RESEARCH-01-FRONT-DOOR-TRIAGE.md`](RESEARCH-01-FRONT-DOOR-TRIAGE.md). It answers the learning portion of `codexGAPS.md` and separates formal training, free self-study, creator media, supervised practice, and skills that should be referred to specialists.

## Executive finding

The right learning plan is a staged combination:

1. **WHS, batteries, privacy, ownership, and consumer safety first.**
2. **Routine computer assembly, module replacement, OS installation, and documentation.**
3. **Structured diagnostics and category-specific testing.**
4. **Disassembly and parts harvesting on safe practice devices.**
5. **Only later: component-level electronics, microsoldering, liquid damage, or data recovery.**

The cheapest sensible first capability is not a full electronics trade qualification or an expensive enterprise ITAD certification. It is a documented practical competency set: recognise hazards, refuse bad intake, identify a device, test it without destroying data or hardware, clear locks only with legitimate authority, sanitise appropriately, repair bounded faults, disclose defects, and record a repeatable result.

Formal training is valuable where it supplies supervised practical assessment. It is not proof that the operator can safely triage every phone, battery, TV, or board. Conversely, YouTube and free guides are useful for pattern recognition and technique but are not Australian legal, safety, or resale authorisation.

## 1. Skills map for the proposed operation

### Tier 0 — must be competent before accepting public intake

- identify swollen, leaking, hot, punctured, crushed, wet, fire-affected, or unknown batteries;
- isolate and refuse hazardous items without charging or opening them;
- identify exposed mains hazards, damaged chargers, CRTs, toner, mould, pests, and contamination;
- record ownership/authority and refuse suspicious or disputed equipment;
- recognise Apple Activation Lock, Google/Android protection, Microsoft Autopilot/MDM, BIOS passwords, carrier locks, and encryption states;
- maintain `UNWIPED — RESTRICTED` custody;
- use no customer password or recovery code;
- explain refusal and data-risk wording clearly;
- keep private identifiers out of public listings and repositories;
- complete a chain-of-custody and incident record.

### Tier 1 — routine computer refurbishment

- identify CPU, RAM, storage, display, ports, power, battery, cooling, and firmware;
- assemble/disassemble a desktop or business laptop without lost hardware or ESD damage;
- replace known-fault modules safely;
- install a supported operating system and drivers;
- run bounded memory, storage, thermal, display, network, input, audio, and sleep/wake tests;
- interpret SMART/NVMe and battery reports without overstating what they prove;
- distinguish a charger, battery, storage, RAM, display, connector, and motherboard fault;
- document test method, version, result, and exceptions;
- calculate repair labour and parts cost against a route ceiling.

### Tier 2 — phones, tablets, consoles, and network devices

- perform manufacturer-supported reset and account-removal workflows;
- verify clean setup without retaining personal data;
- test cameras, microphones, radios, charging, touch, buttons, ports, biometrics, and carrier/network state;
- identify parts compatibility and battery risk;
- sanitise routers, NAS devices, printers, cameras, and removable media;
- identify cloud, subscription, tenant, and accessory dependencies;
- write category-specific listing and warranty disclosures.

### Tier 3 — specialist or partner-only work

- component-level fault finding on live circuits;
- microsoldering, BGA, board repair, and liquid-damage recovery;
- data recovery from failed or sensitive media;
- CRT/high-voltage work;
- damaged-lithium-battery handling;
- mains electrical repair or modification;
- large-screen panel repair and freight-intensive stock;
- enterprise tenant/MDM remediation without the owner’s administrator.

These should not be included in Phase 0 merely because an online video makes the procedure look repeatable.

## 2. Formal Australian training routes

### 2.1 UEE20520 Certificate II in Computer Assembly and Repair

The current national register describes [UEE20520](https://training.gov.au/Training/Details/UEE20520/qualdetails?pageSize=50&tableUnits-page=1) as covering component selection, computer assembly, and routine hardware repair generally performed by replacing known faulty components under prescribed routines. Its core units include:

- `UEECD0007` — apply WHS regulations, codes, and practices;
- `UEECD0016` — document and apply WHS risk controls;
- `UEECD0019` — fabricate, assemble, and dismantle components;
- `UEECS0003` — assemble, set up, and test computing devices;
- `UEERE0001` — apply environmentally sustainable procedures;
- a competency-development unit.

Relevant electives include operating systems, networking, documentation, receiving/storing equipment, sourcing parts, module replacement, and basic electronic apparatus repair.

**Why it fits:** it maps more closely to routine refurbishing than a generic software course.

**What it does not settle:** whether an RTO is available in Dubbo, exact delivery mode, practical equipment, total cost, recognition of prior learning, licensing boundaries, or competence with phones and lithium batteries.

The register’s current RTO listing must be checked directly before enrolment. Do not assume that a qualification shown nationally is offered locally or online in a form suitable for an adult part-time operator.

### 2.2 ICTSS00048 Hardware Technician Skill Set

The [Hardware Technician Skill Set](https://training.gov.au/TrainingComponentFiles/ICT/ICTSS00048_R4.pdf) is designed for basic technician skills for hardware installation and configuration and includes operating-system installation/optimisation. It may be a narrower path than a full qualification.

**Research question:** whether the current skill-set units cover enough hands-on diagnosis and documentation for this business, or whether they are too focused on installation/configuration.

### 2.3 TAFE NSW electronics pathways

TAFE NSW currently lists:

- [Certificate II in Electronics UEE21920](https://www.tafensw.edu.au/course-areas/electrotechnology/courses/electrotechnology--UEE21920-01), including basic circuits, functional testing, and basic component replacement;
- [Certificate III in Electronics and Communications UEE30920](https://www.tafensw.edu.au/course-areas/electrotechnology/courses/engineering--UEE30920-01), including practical repair and maintenance at component/sub-assembly level;
- an [electronics course area](https://www.tafensw.edu.au/course-areas/electrotechnology/electronics) with multiple certificates and a diploma.

The course pages currently show no study/location option for some listed courses. That is a live availability question, not evidence that the course cannot be delivered. Ask TAFE NSW directly about Dubbo, nearby campuses, block release, online theory with practical intensives, apprenticeship/adult pathways, and recognition of prior learning.

**Likely fit:** Certificate II/selected units for fundamentals if practical access is available.

**Likely overkill for Phase 0:** full Diploma or advanced industrial electronics before the pilot proves enough board-level volume.

### 2.4 ICT Certificate III options

TAFE NSW lists [Certificate III in Information Technology ICT30120](https://www.tafensw.edu.au/course-areas/information-and-communication-technology/courses/ABOR--ICT30120-01) units such as `ICTSAS309 Maintain and repair ICT equipment and software` and `ICTSAS311 Maintain computer hardware`.

This is worth comparing with UEE20520 because it may be more accessible for help-desk, operating-system, and client-support skills, while UEE20520 is more directly hardware/electrotechnology oriented.

### 2.5 Competency-unit shortlist to ask RTOs about

Ask for unit-level delivery rather than accepting a vague “computer repair course” description. The highest-value units to investigate are:

- WHS and electrotechnology risk control;
- assemble, set up, and test computing devices;
- maintain and repair ICT equipment/software;
- maintain computer hardware;
- install/configure client operating systems;
- receive/store equipment;
- maintain documentation;
- source/purchase parts;
- repair basic computer faults by module replacement;
- repair basic electronic apparatus faults by component replacement;
- customer service and instruction.

For each RTO, request unit codes, practical assessment conditions, equipment list, trainer qualifications, attendance requirements, total price, duration, RPL process, and the exact campus or online arrangement.

## 3. Free and low-cost self-study

### 3.1 Microsoft hardware troubleshooting

[Explore physical hardware troubleshooting](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/modules/explore-physical-hardware-troubleshooting/) is a beginner Microsoft Learn module covering hardware problems, USB/wireless issues, built-in diagnostic information, and a troubleshooting approach. It is useful for vocabulary and process, but it is Windows-focused and not a complete refurbishment course.

Use it to create exercises:

- identify a device in Device Manager;
- gather hardware information;
- separate USB, wireless, driver, and physical-fault hypotheses;
- document a reproducible troubleshooting path.

### 3.2 Windows compatibility and health

Use Microsoft’s current [Windows 11 system requirements](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/compatibility/windows-11-system-requirements) and [PC Health Check guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/compatibility/how-to-use-the-pc-health-check-app) to teach eligibility checks. The current requirements include a compatible processor, 4 GB RAM, 64 GB storage, UEFI/Secure Boot capability, TPM 2.0, compatible graphics, and a suitable display.

**Practice output:** a redacted compatibility report that clearly separates:

- hardware eligibility;
- current condition;
- battery health;
- licence/activation state;
- support/lifecycle expectation;
- buyer-use case.

Never list “Windows 11 compatible” as if it means “good condition”.

### 3.3 iFixit

[iFixit’s repair-guide instructions](https://www.ifixit.com/Info/Repair_Guide) are unusually useful for teaching disciplined repair documentation: warnings, tools, difficulty, time, photographs, steps, connector identification, screw lengths, and prerequisites. [iFixit EDU](https://edu.ifixit.com/) offers structured technical-writing/repair projects used by educational institutions.

Use iFixit for:

- model-specific disassembly research;
- identifying common failure points;
- estimating tool and time requirements;
- practising clear instructions;
- learning when a repair has prerequisites or hidden risk.

Do not assume every community guide is current, complete, or safe for a customer device. Check manufacturer service information and battery warnings.

### 3.4 CompTIA A+ as a knowledge map

[CompTIA A+](https://www.comptia.org/certifications/a) is a paid certification route, not a free course. Its exam objectives can still be used as a checklist for hardware, operating systems, networking, troubleshooting, security, and support knowledge.

**Recommendation:** use the current objectives as a gap map before paying for study material. Buy or enrol only if the objectives, assessment, and employer/partner recognition justify the cost. A pass does not demonstrate safe battery handling, Australian consumer-law compliance, or profitable resale judgement.

### 3.5 Free/open-source diagnostic practice

Use a clean, offline, versioned toolkit and keep outputs with the private asset record:

- [smartmontools](https://www.smartmontools.org/) for SMART information;
- [nvme-cli](https://github.com/linux-nvme/nvme-cli) for NVMe identity/health/error information;
- [Memtest86+](https://memtest.org/) for memory testing;
- [stress-ng](https://github.com/ColinIanKing/stress-ng) for carefully bounded load tests;
- [lm-sensors](https://github.com/lm-sensors/lm-sensors) for Linux sensor observations;
- [fwts](https://github.com/fwts/fwts) for firmware/ACPI checks on supported systems;
- a trusted Linux live environment for hardware inventory and offline inspection.

Training exercise: run each tool on known-good equipment, deliberately introduce a safe software-level fault, record output, and write what the tool cannot prove. Do not run destructive commands or stress tests on suspect batteries, overheating devices, or data-bearing systems before the privacy/sanitisation plan is settled.

## 4. Practical media syllabus

### 4.1 Australian repair and restoration

[Hugh Jeffreys](https://www.hughjeffreys.com/) documents repairs/restorations of phones, tablets, computers, and other electronics and is useful for seeing diagnosis, disassembly, parts, and repair communication. Use one video per category and record the model, symptoms, tools, hidden risks, time, and outcome.

### 4.2 Scrap and material sorting

[eWaste Ben](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=04FVYFX-dW4) is useful for seeing scrap sorting, board/cable separation, and commodity thinking. It should teach the difference between material recovery and reuse—not encourage accepting random dead electronics, home metal processing, or unsafe battery work.

### 4.3 Used PC parts and pricing behaviour

[Tech YES City](https://www.youtube.com/@TechYESCity) can help study used PC-part sourcing, testing, bargaining, and buyer expectations. Record the country, date, exact model, and whether a figure is an asking price, a purchase price, or a completed sale. Do not transfer overseas market behaviour directly to Dubbo.

### 4.4 Board-level repair

[NorthridgeFix](https://northridgefix.com/videos/) and [Rossmann Group](https://rossmanngroup.com/louis-rossmann) are useful for observing diagnostic reasoning, board-level fault patterns, and the difference between a repairable board and a replacement/recycling decision.

They are **not** a Phase 0 authorisation. Board work needs separate tooling, ESD/fume controls, competence, insurance, customer communication, and a rework warranty decision.

### 4.5 FlipTech founder/business context

The [Life on Planet A episode with Austin Turpin](https://podcasts.apple.com/au/podcast/82-e-waste-revolution-with-austin-turpin/id1508865350?i=1000705662337) is useful for startup assumptions, regulation, and mission. FlipTech’s public [bin process](https://www.fliptech.com.au/bins) and [data-erasure article](https://www.fliptech.com.au/articles/8e4e2ca7-22e2-4d68-bf22-d0420c297db4) are useful first-party benchmarks for collection, sorting, erasure, and reporting language.

They do not disclose every floor threshold, test tool, margin, repair rate, or internal software decision. Treat those as interview questions, not facts.

## 5. Eight-week practical curriculum

This is a suggested part-time sequence. It is not a claim that eight weeks creates a qualified electronics technician.

| Week | Focus | Evidence of learning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | batteries, WHS, ownership, privacy, locks, refusal language | correctly refuse a mixed box and complete a safe record |
| 2 | device anatomy, labels, serials, chargers, ESD, storage | identify and label ten safe practice devices |
| 3 | Windows/Linux inventory, boot, firmware, storage, memory | produce five redacted diagnostic reports |
| 4 | displays, keyboards, ports, audio, wireless, camera, thermals | complete five repeatable bench tests with timings |
| 5 | sanitisation, account removal, Autopilot/MDM escalation, certificates | process simulated lock/data cases without passwords |
| 6 | disassembly, module replacement, parts labelling, reassembly | repair two safe donor devices and pass post-repair tests |
| 7 | phone/tablet/console/network-device route decisions | complete category sheets and identify partner-only cases |
| 8 | pricing, listings, consumer guarantees, warranty, returns, pilot review | create a truthful listing, cost model, and go/no-go decision |

Every week should include a “what I must refuse” exercise. A training plan that only rewards successful repairs creates unsafe intake bias.

## 6. Practical assessment rubric

Use a simple observed rubric on safe practice devices:

| Competency | Pass evidence | Automatic fail |
|---|---|---|
| Hazard screen | identifies battery/contamination/electrical hazards before power-on | charges or opens a suspect battery |
| Ownership | records transfer basis and flags uncertainty | accepts disputed/suspicious item on verbal promise |
| Data/locks | isolates data-bearing item and follows authorised process | asks for/records customer password or lists locked device |
| Identification | records model/serial privately and labels device | mixes identifiers or exposes them publicly |
| Diagnostics | follows written sequence and records actual output | invents a pass or omits failed tests |
| Repair boundary | stops at time/parts/safety ceiling | continues unsafe or uneconomic work without approval |
| Disclosure | lists defects, parts, battery, and test limits honestly | uses “certified”, “secure”, or “fully tested” without evidence |
| Final route | places item in one labelled route and preserves evidence | leaves unknown stock in a mixed pile |

## 7. Course and provider questions

Before paying or enrolling, ask each provider:

1. Is the course nationally recognised, a skill set, a short course, or informal content?
2. Which exact units and current release are taught?
3. Is practical assessment performed on real devices, simulated equipment, or theory only?
4. Are laptops, phones, batteries, soldering, ESD, data privacy, and account locks included?
5. What tools/PPE/equipment are supplied and what must the student buy?
6. What are the total fees, materials, travel, attendance, and duration?
7. Is delivery available in Dubbo, nearby, online, block release, or by RPL?
8. Can the provider show assessment conditions and trainer credentials?
9. What is the pathway after completion, and who recognises it?
10. What work is outside the training and must be referred to a licensed or specialist person?

Do not accept a certificate title as an answer to the actual operating question. Ask the provider to map the course to the proposed triage and refurb tasks.

## 8. Lowest-cost sequence recommended for this project

### Before spending money

- complete the free Microsoft Learn hardware module;
- read the relevant current training.gov.au unit outlines;
- study iFixit guides for safe, common laptop repairs;
- watch one Australian repair/restoration example per category;
- build the triage and test forms;
- practise with known safe devices and no customer data;
- ask TAFE/RTOs for course/RPL/practical details;
- ask an insurer and WHS adviser what training/equipment the exact activity requires.

### First paid decision

Choose between:

- selected nationally recognised hardware/electronics units with practical assessment;
- a local repair apprenticeship/mentor or supervised workshop;
- a formal full qualification;
- no formal course yet, but a documented closed pilot with specialist referral.

The choice should depend on cost, time, local availability, practical depth, and the intended category scope—not on the prestige of the certificate.

### Defer

Defer microsoldering, high-voltage/CRT work, advanced data recovery, battery repair, and large-screen panel repair until a partner, insurer, and measured volume justify them.

## 9. Open questions remaining

1. Which TAFE NSW or other RTO option is actually available to an adult in Dubbo or nearby?
2. Can UEE20520 be completed part-time or through RPL with enough practical assessment?
3. Are the ICTSAS309/311 units accessible through a local or online provider?
4. Which course includes real phone/tablet diagnostics and account-lock handling rather than only PC repair?
5. What practical training does a local repairer recommend for battery safety and ESD?
6. What does the insurer require as evidence of competence?
7. Which open-source diagnostic outputs are reliable enough to attach to a buyer-facing report?
8. Which videos show failed repairs, stop decisions, and return/warranty economics rather than only successful restorations?
9. Can FlipTech, WorkVentures, Sircel, Reconnect, or PonyUp offer a non-confidential job-shadow, interview, or partner-training path?
10. What training is necessary for another person to run the front-door screen without the founder present?

## Recommended next actions

1. Contact TAFE NSW Dubbo and two other RTOs with the ten provider questions above.
2. Build a private learning log with source URL, date, category, learning objective, practical exercise, and pass/fail result.
3. Complete the eight-week free/low-cost curriculum on safe practice devices.
4. Ask one local repairer to review the triage card and observe a mock intake.
5. Do not purchase advanced repair equipment until the first pilot documents enough volume and a positive repair/referral comparison.
6. Update the public repository only with redacted outcomes and source-backed course/availability claims.
