# AMR (Australian Metal Recycling): sustainability, circular economy and community stance, and how open it might be to a Dubbo computer reuse pilot

Research date: 25 November 2026. Items older than 2024, or undated, are flagged inline.

## Who "AMR Dubbo" actually is (identity check that the other questions depend on)

### Takeaway
"AMR Dubbo" is confirmed as Australian Metal Recycling's yard at 34 Mountbatten Drive, Dubbo. The legal entity is Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd, and its main business location is Dubbo's postcode. The yard used to be a Matthews Metal Management (MMM) site. The Matthews family founded MMM, then sold it to InfraBuild/Liberty OneSteel around 2019, and the family bought the regional yards back around 2025 under the AMR name. So AMR is a recently re-formed, privately owned, regional NSW family-founded business, not a corporate or listed recycler.

## What does AMR say publicly about sustainability, the circular economy, and reuse vs recycling? Any reports or certifications?

### Takeaway
AMR's public sustainability language is generic and centred on recycling: keep metal out of landfill and recover it for new steel, copper and aluminium. There is no sustainability report, ESG statement or certification (ISO 14001/9001, R2, e-Stewards, AS/NZS 5377) on its website. Reuse, refurbishment and data destruction are not mentioned anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- Homepage, "Commitment to Environment": "Our core mission is to reduce the amount of waste sent to landfill". AMR says it is "committed to supporting sustainable practices that benefit our communities and industries across Australia". — [AMR homepage](https://www.australianmetalrecycling.com.au/)
- About page: "Continuously committed to sustainability, AMR regularly reviews its operational practices and policies". It says AMR aims to reduce waste to landfill through metal and electrical waste recycling. — [AMR About](https://www.australianmetalrecycling.com.au/about/)
- About page, value framing: "We don't just accept scrap, we help businesses turn excess metal into an ongoing value stream." — [AMR About](https://www.australianmetalrecycling.com.au/about/)
- E-waste service page:
  - The only end-of-life statement is that "the metal inside will be recovered and recycled instead of going to landfill".
  - Reuse, refurbishment and data security/destruction are not mentioned.
  - NTCRS/TechCollect is not mentioned.
  - It excludes "Lithium and lithium-ion batteries". — [AMR e-waste recycling](https://www.australianmetalrecycling.com.au/services/e-waste-recycling/)
- The homepage defines e-waste as "discarded electrical and electronic equipment containing metal parts", including computers, monitors, printers and fax machines. Lithium-ion items are excluded. The only credential shown is scrap metal licence "SM: 0759"; no ISO or AS/NZS 5377 certification is listed. — [AMR homepage](https://www.australianmetalrecycling.com.au/)
- The Dubbo page accepts "Electronic waste (excluding lithium-ion batteries)". It says nothing on data destruction or reuse. — [AMR Dubbo](https://australianmetalrecycling.com.au/locations/dubbo/)
- The National Recycling Week 2026 advertising feature frames AMR's role as metal going back into new steel, copper and aluminium "instead of sitting in landfill". It makes no reuse claims. — [Newcastle Herald, 7 Nov 2026](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/9384032/turn-unwanted-metal-into-value-with-australian-metal-recycling/)

### Inferences
- AMR doesn't publicly separate reuse from recycling, so a reuse pilot wouldn't contradict any stated policy. It would be a new add-on, not an extension of an existing programme.
- With no certifications and no data-destruction offering, AMR is probably not selling "secure IT asset disposal" to corporate clients. That lowers the chance of a hard contractual "everything must be shredded" obligation on computers dropped at the Dubbo yard. It also means AMR has no established chain-of-custody process to plug a reuse partner into.
- The "reduce waste to landfill" and "sustainable practices that benefit our communities" lines give the pitch a natural hook. A reuse pilot can be framed as the top of the same waste hierarchy.

### Gaps
- No sustainability report, ESG disclosure or environmental policy document was found. AMR is a private company and none would be expected.
- It's unclear whether AMR is an NTCRS/TechCollect collection partner or where its e-waste goes downstream. Neither is named publicly.

## Business model: does pulling working computers out conflict with AMR's revenue? What do industry sources say about scrap recyclers' e-waste economics?

### Takeaway
AMR makes money on metal value: non-ferrous weigh-and-pay plus volume steel, which is accepted free. Whole computers are low-value scrap per kilogram, roughly in the same range as steel and far below copper or brass. Laptops also carry lithium-ion batteries, which AMR explicitly won't accept. Diverting a small number of working computers is unlikely to hurt AMR's revenue materially. It might even remove a slightly awkward item. That's inference from proxy prices; AMR's actual e-waste economics aren't public.

### Cited Findings
- How AMR makes money:
  - At Dubbo, scrap steel (tin, wire, roofing, hot water systems, white goods) is a free drop-off, and non-ferrous metals are weighed and paid on site. — [AMR Dubbo](https://australianmetalrecycling.com.au/locations/dubbo/)
  - The AMR "Tradie Bin" model: AMR collects, weighs and pays for the metal. — [Newcastle Herald, 7 Nov 2026](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/9384032/turn-unwanted-metal-into-value-with-australian-metal-recycling/)
  - "Values of scrap metal products will vary depending on material type." The e-waste page doesn't state a price for general e-waste. — [AMR e-waste recycling](https://www.australianmetalrecycling.com.au/services/e-waste-recycling/)
- US scrap-yard benchmarks (proxy only; no Australian per-item computer scrap price found):
  - PC towers (complete) about US$0.17–0.19/lb (Jul 2026).
  - Incomplete laptops US$0.33/lb, partial laptops with a good screen US$0.75/lb (Jun 2026).
  - Whole computer tower US$0.12/lb (3 Jun 2026). — [ScrapMonster electronics scrap prices](https://www.scrapmonster.com/scrap-yard/price/electronics-scrap/4); [ScrapMonster whole computer tower](https://www.scrapmonster.com/scrap-yard/price/whole-computer-tower/300)
- Australian metal price context (Melbourne, June 2026, indicative): copper A$7–12.50/kg, aluminium A$0.90–2.80/kg, steel (HMS) about A$0.30–0.45/kg, brass A$4.20–7.20/kg. — [Manhari Recycling](https://manharirecycling.com.au/?p=12949)
- How metal recyclers handle e-waste (Victoria): about 75% of the ~150,000 t of e-waste processed went to metal recyclers and 25% to e-waste reprocessors. Metal recyclers "shred e-waste and focus on ferrous and non-ferrous metal recovery resulting in higher residual losses". — [Sustainability Victoria, Victorian e-waste material flow analysis](https://sustainability.vic.gov.au/research-data-and-insights/research/research-reports/victorian-e-waste-material-flow-analysis)
- Competing free channels for computers:
  - The NTCRS (since 2011) makes manufacturers and importers share the cost of recycling TVs and computers. — [NSW EPA TV & computer recycling guide](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/television-and-computer-recycling-guide-160449.pdf)
  - Australia has a free national drop-off scheme for computers, laptops, monitors and similar items. — [RecyclingNearYou computers](https://www.recyclingnearyou.com.au/computers)
  - Dubbo Regional Council offers "free e-waste recycling" at its Recycling and Waste Depots. — [Dubbo Regional Council A–Z Recycling](https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/Households-Residents/Rubbish-Recycling-and-Sustainability/Domestic-Waste-Services/a-z-recycling)
- Lithium-ion batteries are a known hazard in scrap:
  - AMR excludes lithium and lithium-ion batteries. — [AMR e-waste recycling](https://www.australianmetalrecycling.com.au/services/e-waste-recycling/)
  - In a 2023 Sims Metal yard fire case (Auckland, decided 2025), the judge noted "there is currently no available technology capable of reliably identifying or detecting them within scrap material". — [Auckland Council, 5 May 2025](https://ourauckland.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/news/2025/05/ac-v-simsmetal/)
  - Dubbo Regional Council reported a garbage truck fire from an embedded battery device in August 2025. — [Dubbo Regional Council, Aug 2025](https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/news-and-media/news-and-resources/council-news/2025/embedded-battery-device-causes-garbage-truck-fire)
- Older precedent: a directory listing for MMM Dubbo (undated, InfraBuild era or earlier) noted "No TVs, CRT Monitors or Printers accepted". — [BusinessRecycling listing, MMM Dubbo](https://businessrecycling.com.au/business/15852)

### Inferences
- Rough order of magnitude: about US$0.18/lb is about US$0.40/kg, roughly A$0.60/kg at about 0.65 AUD/USD. An 8–10 kg desktop tower is therefore worth only a few dollars as scrap. That's comparable to steel per kg and an order of magnitude below copper. This uses US proxy prices and an assumed exchange rate, so treat it as indicative only.
- So 10–20 machines a month would be worth tens of dollars in scrap value to AMR. That's commercially trivial, which supports the pitch that diverting them doesn't hurt AMR's revenue. The "unusable gear returned to AMR" term actually protects AMR's metal stream.
- Laptops with embedded lithium-ion batteries look like a nuisance or fire-risk item for a yard that won't accept lithium batteries. Diverting laptops to someone who removes the batteries safely could be pitched as a small operational benefit to AMR.
- Computers have many free disposal channels in Dubbo (Council depots, NTCRS). The computers that reach AMR are probably incidental: mixed in with business clean-outs and farm or office clean-ups. Volumes of reusable machines may be modest.

### Gaps
- No Australian, AMR-specific or Dubbo-specific price for whole computers or laptops was found. AMR doesn't publish e-waste pricing.
- It's unknown whether AMR pays, accepts free, or charges for computers at the Dubbo yard, or where its e-waste goes downstream (own shredding vs a third-party processor).

## Negative signals: complaints, disputes, regulatory issues, or a strict "everything gets recycled" / security-driven no-reuse policy

### Takeaway
No negative regulatory, legal or media signals specific to AMR or Scrap Metal Services were found. No explicit no-reuse or security policy exists publicly either. The main friction points are structural: no data-destruction or chain-of-custody process, a recycling-only e-waste message, and NSW scrap-metal regulation, which affects how any "payment" or exchange is structured.

### Cited Findings
- No EPA prosecutions, fines or media complaints involving AMR, Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd or the Dubbo Mountbatten Drive yard appeared in searches. Results returned other recyclers, e.g. NSW EPA's $30,000 fine on Sunrise Metal Recycling (July 2026), which is unrelated. — [NSW EPA via Medianet, July 2026](https://newshub.medianet.com.au/2026/07/unlawful-waste-facility-hit-with-30000-fine/162622/)
- AMR notes that under the NSW Scrap Metal Industry Act 2016, "scrap metal recyclers are prohibited from offering cash payments for scrap metal", with an exception for Cash for Cans. Payment is electronic or by cheque. — [AMR homepage](https://www.australianmetalrecycling.com.au/); [AMR Dubbo](https://australianmetalrecycling.com.au/locations/dubbo/)
- AMR's e-waste messaging covers only metal recovery and recycling. No data-security service or statement exists. — [AMR e-waste recycling](https://www.australianmetalrecycling.com.au/services/e-waste-recycling/)

### Inferences
- Probable objections:
  - Liability if a device with someone's data leaves the yard intact. AMR offers no data-destruction service, so it has no process for this today. Josh's offer of certified secure erasure, documented per device, directly addresses it.
  - Ownership and title: whether items dropped at a scrap yard become AMR's property, and whether AMR can give them away.
  - Licensing and record-keeping: the yard operates under a scrap metal dealer licence, so any arrangement should be documented, probably as a no-cash, written arrangement.
  - Staff time and yard space for a "set-aside" bin.
- No strict corporate "everything must be shredded" policy shows up publicly. Such policies are typical of certified ITAD/e-waste processors and corporate recyclers, not small owner-run scrap yards. This is inference, not confirmed.
- Ownership changed recently (InfraBuild to the Matthews family's new entity, ~2025). Policies may still be forming, which could make the owners more open to a new idea, or more cautious. Unknown.

### Gaps
- Online customer reviews for AMR Dubbo weren't systematically checked.
- The NSW Scrap Metal Industry Act's record-keeping rules for non-metal items, or items given away, weren't researched. A short legal or compliance check may be worth doing before formalising.
- It's unknown whether AMR has commercial e-waste contracts (e.g. with councils or businesses) that promise certified destruction of incoming IT gear. That could limit which items can be set aside.
