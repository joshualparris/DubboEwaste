# AMR Dubbo downstream forensic trace — who actually receives the e-waste?

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)  
**Question:** After Australian Metal Recycling (AMR) accepts TVs, computers/laptops, monitors, smartphones/tablets and printers/scanners at 34 Mountbatten Drive, Dubbo, **which company/facility receives that e-waste next?**

## Executive verdict

After searching AMR/Scrap Metal Services material, NSW/Federal government sources, NTCRS co-regulator records, current TechCollect partner information, historic Matthews Metal Management contracts, regional council records, recycler websites and exact-name searches across the current co-regulator domains:

> **No public source found names the current downstream company or facility that receives AMR Dubbo's e-waste.**

That means there is currently **no defensible public evidence** for a statement such as:

- "AMR Dubbo sends it to Sircel Parkes"
- "AMR Dubbo sends it to ACE Recycling"
- "AMR Dubbo sends it to Sims"
- "AMR Dubbo sends it through TechCollect"
- "AMR Dubbo sends it to Rutherford first"

Each of those has some surrounding evidence or historical plausibility, but none has the missing direct link.

What the research *does* achieve is narrow the field substantially and show exactly what would prove the chain.

---

## 1. Highest-confidence current chain

### Confirmed
**AMR Dubbo**
- 34 Mountbatten Drive, Dubbo NSW 2830
- operated by Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd / Australian Metal Recycling
- registered NSW scrap-metal yard under SM-0759
- accepts e-waste including TVs, computers/laptops, monitors, smart phones/tablets and printers/scanners
- AMR says e-waste/electrical waste is accepted free
- lithium-ion material/batteries are excluded in AMR's published material rules

### Not publicly disclosed
The next named node is missing:

**AMR Dubbo → [UNDISCLOSED FIRST DOWNSTREAM FACILITY]**

From there, a specialist recycler would normally dismantle/shred/sort and send commodity fractions to later processors. But the first downstream recipient from Dubbo remains unverified.

## 2. Could the next stop simply be another AMR yard?

### Rutherford is a real AMR processing/consolidation hub — but not proven for Dubbo e-waste

AMR's 2025 National Recycling Week article calls **Rutherford, 71 Kyle Street**, a "major operational hub" for the Hunter/Newcastle/Maitland/Central Coast region. It says metal dealers consolidate material there for **processing and onward sale**.

The same article separately describes the regional yards at:
- Tamworth
- Dubbo
- Kempsey
- Maclean

and says they provide the same door-trade service.

It also lists e-waste among the materials AMR handles across the network.

### What this proves
- AMR has an internal network with consolidation/processing capability.
- Rutherford is explicitly a major hub.
- AMR moves recovered material onward for sale/processing.

### What it does **not** prove
- that Dubbo e-waste is trucked to Rutherford;
- that Rutherford dismantles/shreds e-waste;
- that Rutherford is the first stop after Dubbo;
- that e-waste follows the same route as ferrous/non-ferrous scrap.

**Status: plausible internal-node hypothesis, not evidence of the Dubbo e-waste route.**

## 3. The "Australian Metal Refiners" clue

Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd (ABN 51 640 765 091) currently has several registered business names:

- Australian Metal Recycling
- **Australian Metal Refiners**
- Metal Refiners
- Wilson Metal Collections
- Wilson Metal Recyclers
- Wilson Metal Recycling

These names were registered in March/April 2025.

The same legal entity now has AMR yards at Wyong and Woy Woy, which had historically operated under the **Australian Metal Refiners** name before the current Scrap Metal Services registration took over those locations.

### Important limitation
A business name containing "Refiners" is **not evidence that the company operates a specialist e-waste refinery**.

No current AMR source found identifies:
- a PCB precious-metal refining line;
- a CRT processing line;
- a dedicated e-waste shredding line;
- a lithium-ion treatment line;
- a specific AMR "refinery" receiving Dubbo e-waste.

So this corporate-name clue does **not** close the chain.

## 4. Historical Matthews link to the NTCRS

This is the strongest historical clue.

A 2015 Great Lakes/MidWaste council report says Matthews Metal Management had provided free e-waste collection/recycling and explicitly says the free service was possible because Matthews was **operating under the Australian Government's National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme (NTCRS)**.

The report says Matthews later terminated that free arrangement toward the end of 2014 after scheme funding/collection-volume problems.

A separate historical Recycling Near You listing for Matthews' Parkes site stated:
- only e-waste covered by the NTCRS was accepted;
- users should refer to **TechCollect's** accepted-products list.

### What this establishes
- the Matthews business definitely participated in NTCRS-funded e-waste collection historically;
- at least one Matthews listing explicitly pointed to TechCollect's product scope.

### What it does not establish
- that current AMR/Scrap Metal Services is currently a TechCollect partner;
- that AMR Dubbo's current free service is NTCRS funded;
- that current AMR material follows the old Matthews route.

The ownership/legal structure and operating network have changed since the historical arrangement.

## 5. If historical Matthews was using TechCollect, who were the NSW recyclers?

ANZRP's 2014–15 annual report shows TechCollect's NSW recycling network included:

- **Sims**
- **TES-AMM**

The 2015–16 ANZRP report again lists:
- Sims Recycling Solutions — NSW
- TES-AMM Australia — NSW/QLD/VIC

### Can we say Matthews e-waste went to Sims or TES-AMM?
**No.**

The historical council record identifies Matthews as the collection/recycling contractor, and TechCollect identifies its NSW recycler pool, but no source found maps a particular Matthews load or collection site to one specific recycler.

Therefore the strongest honest historical statement is:

> Historical Matthews NTCRS material could have entered a TechCollect NSW recycler network that included Sims and TES-AMM, but the exact recycler for Matthews loads has not been established.

## 6. Current TechCollect / ANZRP route

The current DCCEEW list (updated 20 May 2026) identifies five approved NTCRS co-regulatory arrangements:

1. Activ Group Solutions / Ecoactiv
2. Australia and New Zealand Recycling Platform / TechCollect
3. Ecocycle
4. Ecycle Solutions
5. Sustainable Product Stewards

For TechCollect specifically, ANZRP's 2024–25 audited annual report names the NSW recycling partners as:

- **ACE Recycling Group**
- **Sircel**

ANZRP says every TechCollect delivery receives a unique ID and is tracked:
**collection site → logistics provider → recycler**.

It also says first-stage recycling occurs in Australia and involves dismantling, shredding and sorting into glass, metals, plastics, batteries, PCBs, toner/ink and other fractions.

### Current TechCollect collection-partner check
TechCollect's current NSW collection-partner page lists sites including Albury, Blaxland, Burwood, Canada Bay, Hornsby, Inverell, Katoomba, Kimbriki, Lismore, North Sydney and Shoalhaven.

**AMR, Matthews and Dubbo are not listed on that current NSW collection-partner page.**

Exact-name searches across TechCollect/ANZRP and the other current NTCRS co-regulator domains did not produce a current public AMR/Scrap Metal Services relationship.

### Conclusion
ACE and Sircel are **real current NSW TechCollect recyclers**, but there is no public evidence found that AMR Dubbo is currently feeding TechCollect.

## 7. Sircel Parkes — the geographically strongest candidate, but still unproven

Sircel operates a current e-waste facility at:

**55 Brolgan Road, Parkes NSW**

This matters because:
- Parkes is a regional NSW processing location comparatively close to Dubbo;
- Sircel publicly says it receives regional e-waste at Parkes;
- Sircel documented receiving its first Parkes e-waste delivery from **Narromine Shire Council**;
- Sircel says its expansion into regional NSW was supported by **NetWaste**;
- Parkes Shire Council records e-waste from Parkes Landfill being collected by Sircel;
- Sircel is one of ANZRP/TechCollect's current NSW recycling partners.

Sircel says its facilities assess, repurpose or recycle e-waste and that it prioritises reuse before recycling.

### Why this looks attractive as a hypothesis
Geographically and operationally, **Sircel Parkes is exactly the kind of facility that could receive Central West e-waste**.

### Why it still cannot be named as AMR Dubbo's processor
No AMR, Sircel, council, tender, TechCollect or government source found says:

> "AMR Dubbo sends its e-waste to Sircel Parkes."

Exact searches for combinations of Australian Metal Recycling / Scrap Metal Services / Gordon Matthews with Sircel produced no documentary link.

**Status: strong regional candidate, zero direct AMR link found.**

## 8. ACE Recycling Group — another real current NSW recycler, but no AMR link

ACE Recycling Group is the other current NSW recycler named in ANZRP's 2024–25 annual report.

Current ACE NSW facilities include:
- **St Marys** — 7 & 8 Kommer Place, NSW 2760
- **Heatherbrae/Newcastle** — 24 Heather Street, NSW 2324

ACE says it:
- dismantles and sorts e-waste;
- separates PCBs, metals, plastics and glass;
- processes e-waste under AS/NZS 5377;
- provides chain-of-custody/data-destruction documentation.

A Penrith planning document for ACE's St Marys operation describes receiving whole e-waste units (TVs, computers etc.), dismantling them into plastics, circuit boards, glass, batteries and metal, then sending those fractions to downstream recyclers.

### Why ACE is plausible
It is a genuine current NSW first-stage processor in the TechCollect network, and Heatherbrae is in the same broad Hunter transport corridor as AMR's Rutherford operations.

### Why it cannot be named
No source found connects AMR/Scrap Metal Services to ACE.

**Status: credible industry candidate, no direct AMR evidence.**

## 9. Sims — strong regional history, but not proof of AMR's current route

NetWaste's current e-waste information says **Sims Metal Management collects e-waste from the majority of NetWaste councils**.

Older NetWaste strategy material described regional e-waste being shredded at **Sims St Marys** and also discussed recovery work with Ecycle Solutions.

This gives Sims an established Central West/NetWaste footprint.

### However
AMR Dubbo's own free e-waste service is a separate commercial service unless proven otherwise. No source found links current AMR Dubbo loads to Sims.

**Status: current regional downstream player; no direct AMR link.**

## 10. Dubbo Council's route is not automatically AMR's route

Dubbo Regional Council's adopted 2025–35 Waste Strategy recorded:
- Matthews Metals — e-waste collection
- commencement: 30 June 2023
- expiry: 30 June 2025

The strategy predates/overlaps the corporate transition to the current AMR/Scrap Metal Services operation.

No post-30-June-2025 public contract has been found that identifies Dubbo Council's current e-waste collector/processer.

Therefore:
- the **Council → downstream** route cannot safely be used as proof of the **AMR retail-yard → downstream** route;
- even if Council now uses Sircel, Sims or another provider, that does not automatically mean AMR does.

## 11. One important finding about reuse

Formal recycling networks make a distinction between **reuse** and **material recycling**.

Sircel publicly says its ITAD operation prioritises reuse/repurposing where viable.

TechCollect's public guidance focuses on recycling collected NTCRS products through approved recyclers; first-stage recycling is dismantling/shredding/sorting.

AMR's current public material is overwhelmingly framed around:
- scrap/electrical waste;
- recoverable metals;
- weighing;
- processing;
- material returning to manufacturing.

No AMR public source found describes:
- powering devices on;
- testing whether they work;
- refurbishing laptops/phones/TVs;
- listing working electronics for resale;
- diverting reusable devices to charities/refurbishers.

This does **not** prove AMR never reuses anything internally. It means there is no published reuse-triage process found.

That makes the pre-recycling reuse layer proposed for Dubbo eWaste materially different from AMR's published service.

## 14. What would count as proof

Any one of these could close the gap:

1. **AMR weighbridge / dispatch docket** showing the destination of an e-waste load.
2. **AMR certificate of recycling** naming the processing facility.
3. A **downstream vendor invoice/account statement**.
4. An **AMR transport manifest / consignment note**.
5. A written AMR response naming its downstream recycler.
6. An **AS/NZS 5377 certificate** or subcontractor record tied to AMR's e-waste service.
7. A current **NTCRS co-regulator contract/partner record** naming Scrap Metal Services/AMR.
8. A council tender/contract schedule that names both AMR and the downstream processor.

Until one of those exists, the downstream name remains unverified.

## 15. Exact questions to ask AMR

The most useful wording is deliberately difficult to answer with generic marketing:

> **"What legal entity takes ownership or physical custody of the e-waste after it leaves 34 Mountbatten Drive, Dubbo?"**

Then:

> "What is the street address of the first facility it is transported to?"

> "For a pallet/cage of computers and TVs, is the first destination one of your own yards or an external recycler?"

> "If it is another AMR yard, which yard, and what processing happens there before it goes to a specialist recycler?"

> "Who is the contracted e-waste processor — company name and ABN?"

> "Can you give me a sample certificate of recycling, weight docket or chain-of-custody report with commercial details redacted?"

> "Is the service funded under an NTCRS co-regulatory arrangement? If yes, which one?"

> "Is the first-stage recycler AS/NZS 5377 certified, and what is the certificate/scope?"

If the person at the Dubbo yard does not know, ask for **the operations/compliance manager responsible for e-waste downstream vendors**, rather than accepting "it all gets recycled".
