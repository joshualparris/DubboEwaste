# Sircel ITAD / reuse-first model — deep dive for Dubbo E-Waste

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)

This note focuses on the most strategically relevant part of Sircel's model for Dubbo E-Waste: **triage first, reuse second, recycling only for genuinely end-of-life equipment**.

The key insight is that Sircel does not treat every old computer as "waste". It inserts a decision point before destruction:

> **Is this asset still useful enough to preserve as a complete device?**

If yes, it enters an IT asset disposition / reuse pathway.  
If no, it moves into material recycling.

That distinction is exactly where a small Dubbo operation could create value.

---

## 1. Sircel's hierarchy: reuse is deliberately before recycling

Sircel's current ITAD page says:

- "The first principle of recycling at Sircel is reuse."
- data-bearing assets are securely erased in preparation for reuse/redeployment;
- assets that cannot be recovered are mechanically recycled;
- reusable end-user assets include laptops, desktop PCs, unlocked mobile devices and tablets;
- suitable assets may be donated to charity or re-sold to an approved broker;
- Sircel says it guarantees fair market value on repurposed end-user computing devices.

Its current process page makes the triage gate explicit:

1. collection and logistics;
2. receival and tracking;
3. **triage**;
4. size reduction and separation;
5. recovered-commodity reuse;
6. metals recovery.

At triage, Sircel removes batteries, large plastics, steel and wood, but also specifically identifies devices that are **not actually end-of-life**. Those are diverted away from the destructive process and into ITAD.

This is not just marketing language. NSW EPA's waste hierarchy independently supports the same ordering: after avoidance/reduction, **reuse is preferred to recycling**, because reuse avoids the energy and resource costs of processing material back into commodities.

### Why this matters

A functioning laptop is worth more economically and environmentally as a functioning laptop than as:

- 1-2 kg of mixed metal/plastic/glass;
- a small PCB fraction;
- a battery;
- a commodity-value hard drive/SSD.

The whole-device value disappears the instant the machine enters a shredder.

For Dubbo E-Waste, **triage is therefore the highest-value operation in the entire business**.

---

## 2. What Sircel actually does at triage

Sircel's current process description says every lot is:

- received at a facility;
- weighed at a weighbridge or pallet scale;
- allocated a **unique tracking number**;
- triaged before mechanical processing.

At triage Sircel identifies:

### A. Reusable / recoverable IT assets
These are routed to its ITAD team.

Examples explicitly named in Sircel material include:

- laptops;
- desktop PCs;
- unlocked phones;
- tablets;
- working hard drives;
- other data-bearing computing equipment.

### B. End-of-life devices
These are routed to mechanical processing after batteries and incompatible materials are removed.

### C. Hazardous / separately handled items
Batteries are not sent through the main mechanical line. Sircel says batteries are triaged and sent to accredited/certified downstream partners.

### D. Other material that needs separation
Sircel specifically names:

- large plastics;
- steel;
- wood;
- batteries.

This shows triage is not a quick "works / doesn't work" check. It serves **three purposes at once**:

1. **reuse identification**;
2. **process safety**;
3. **material-routing efficiency**.

## 3. Sircel's reuse pathway in detail

### Step 1 — secure collection / chain of custody

Sircel says it provides:

- secure collection and transport;
- chain-of-custody services;
- secure cages/bins for collection;
- unique load tracking;
- audit reporting.

Its council collection page says Sircel can triage e-waste **onsite at council events/recovery centres**, so unsuitable or incompatible material can be separated before transport.

For Dubbo E-Waste this suggests an important future model:

**do triage before valuable devices disappear into a council e-waste cage.**

A council/school/business collection event could have separate streams:

- **reuse candidate**
- **parts candidate**
- **material recycling**
- **battery/hazardous stream**

rather than throwing everything together.

## 4. Data security is the gate that makes reuse possible

For business and government equipment, reuse is impossible unless the former owner's data risk is solved.

Sircel's solution is **Blancco Drive Eraser**.

Its current ITAD page says clients can select erasure standards including:

- NIST Clear / Purge;
- DoD 5220.22-M / ECE;
- BSI-GS/GSE;
- IEEE 2883-2022.

It says each erasure is verified and generates a digitally signed data-erasure certificate.

A separate current Sircel booking page is slightly more prescriptive and says **all data-bearing assets are erased to IEEE 2883-2022**. These two pages are not perfectly aligned, so the safest interpretation is:

- Sircel clearly uses Blancco;
- Sircel clearly issues audit/certificate evidence;
- it supports IEEE 2883-2022 and other recognised standards;
- the exact standard can depend on the engagement/client workflow.

### Independent evidence for Blancco

This is not merely Sircel asserting that its software is secure.

Blancco Drive Eraser 7.3.1 received **Common Criteria EAL2 certification under Australia's AISEP program** in June 2023. The certificate was signed by the Head of the Australian Cyber Security Centre and remains relevant to that evaluated product/version.

Blancco also currently reports ADISA product-assurance certification for:

- NIST SP 800-88;
- IEEE 2883-2022;
- HDD;
- SSD;
- NVMe media.

Blancco's current product material says Drive Eraser supports Clear/Purge methods and digitally signed erasure certificates.

### Why the certificate matters

The certificate turns:

> "I wiped it"

into something closer to:

> "Device serial X / drive serial Y was sanitised using method Z, verification passed, at time/date T."

That is materially more useful to:

- schools;
- councils;
- MSPs;
- medical practices;
- legal firms;
- businesses;
- government.

For Dubbo E-Waste, this is potentially one of the highest-value services you could eventually offer.

## 5. Sircel does not just wipe devices — it hardware-tests them

Sircel's FAQ states that each computing device:

1. has its data sanitised;
2. receives a data-erasure certificate;
3. undergoes a **full hardware test using Blancco Drive Eraser's built-in test module**;
4. if it passes Sircel's criteria, is cleaned and reused.

Sircel does **not publicly disclose its complete minimum hardware specification or grading matrix**, so we should not invent one.

However, its published model establishes the sequence clearly:

**data-safe → hardware-tested → condition/viability assessment → reuse or destruction**

That is a much better model than:

**"it powers on, therefore sell it."**

## 6. What happens to assets that pass

Sircel says suitable assets can go to:

### Charity / community reuse
Its ITAD page says passing devices may be **donated to charity**.

Its FAQ says reused equipment often goes back into commercial use through **charity and community organisations**.

### Approved resale brokers
Sircel says suitable assets may be sold to an **approved broker**.

### Client redeployment
Its pages also refer to reuse or redeployment, meaning a client can potentially reuse its own sanitised assets internally.

### Buy-back / value recovery
Sircel advertises:

- **fair market value (FMV)** for repurposed end-user devices;
- an **Agreed Value Buy-Back Scheme** for devices meeting minimum specifications.

It does not publicly publish the minimum specifications or buy-back rate card.

That is a meaningful business-model clue. The supplier of the old IT equipment can receive a financial return instead of paying only for disposal.

For Dubbo E-Waste this could eventually become:

- outright purchase;
- free collection;
- revenue share;
- consignment;
- donation;
- trade-in credit;
- zero-cost secure disposal for low-value assets.

## 7. Hard evidence that Sircel really does reuse devices

Sircel's FY2023-24 ESG Impact Report is the strongest quantitative evidence found.

For July 2023 to June 2024 Sircel reported:

- **1,877 tonnes** diverted from landfill;
- **2,350 items reused**;
- **202 partners**;
- **1,081,422 kg** of processed metals output;
- **119,239 kg** plastic diversion.

The report specifically says a significant quantity of the laptops and desktop computers Sircel receives are still working.

Its stated sequence was:

**receive working laptop/desktop → data erase → distribute for reuse through partners.**

The report says Sircel formalised its ITAD reuse-diversion process during FY2023-24, including:

- secure data erasure;
- refurbishment;
- reuse or recycling.

It says the 2,350 assets were processed with Blancco erasure before suitable units were redirected through consumer-facing organisations.

### Important transparency limitation

The same ESG report says its visibility ends once reused assets enter the operational control of its reuse partners.

That is useful honesty. Sircel knows:

- what it receives;
- what it wipes;
- what it sends to reuse partners;

but it does not necessarily track the complete second life of every device after transfer.

Dubbo E-Waste could actually improve on this at small scale by maintaining serial-level records through resale/donation.

## 8. What happens when an asset fails reuse triage

Sircel's ITAD model has a hard fallback:

> if it cannot be repurposed, it is securely destroyed and materially recycled.

Sircel says these devices are mechanically reduced to fractions smaller than 1 mm.

Its main recycling line:

1. removes batteries;
2. feeds whole end-of-life devices into mechanical size reduction;
3. separates commodities at multiple stages;
4. reduces material down to approximately 1 mm fractions;
5. uses further metallurgical recovery for metals;
6. directs recovered commodities into manufacturing/reuse markets.

This has an important **data-security side effect**: once a storage-bearing device has been reduced to sub-millimetre fractions, practical data recovery from the original device is impossible.

But for Dubbo E-Waste, mechanical destruction should be the **last** option, not the first.

## 9. The commercial logic behind Sircel's model

Sircel is effectively optimising every incoming asset for its **highest remaining value**.

A simplified hierarchy is:

### Level 1 — whole-device reuse
Highest-value outcome.

Examples:

- laptop;
- desktop;
- tablet;
- unlocked phone.

Revenue/value comes from:

- resale;
- redeployment;
- buy-back;
- charity/social value.

### Level 2 — component reuse
If the whole device is not viable, useful parts may still carry more value than raw material.

Examples:

- SSD;
- RAM;
- CPU;
- charger;
- display;
- keyboard;
- GPU;
- working HDD;
- Wi-Fi module.

Sircel's public material emphasises whole-device ITAD more than parts harvesting, so the component-level opportunity may actually be **more relevant to Dubbo E-Waste than to Sircel's mass-processing model**.

### Level 3 — material recovery
When device/component reuse no longer makes sense:

- steel;
- aluminium;
- copper;
- PCBs;
- plastics;
- precious/base metal fractions.

### Level 4 — unavoidable treatment/disposal
Only genuine residuals.

This is exactly consistent with the NSW waste hierarchy.

## 10. Sircel's reuse model is much closer to the proposed Dubbo business than ordinary scrap recycling

The strongest conceptual comparison is not:

**Dubbo E-Waste vs AMR**

It is:

**small-scale local ITAD/refurbisher + regional material recycler**

Sircel combines both functions in one group:

- collection;
- triage;
- ITAD;
- data sanitisation;
- asset recovery;
- resale/donation;
- mechanical recycling;
- metals recovery.

Dubbo E-Waste does not need to reproduce the last two expensive industrial stages.

It could specialise in the first five and use a downstream partner for the remainder.

### Proposed Dubbo architecture

**Businesses / schools / households**
↓
**Dubbo E-Waste intake**
↓
**Ownership + serial capture**
↓
**Triage**
↓
Four streams:

**A. Reuse**
- wipe
- test
- clean
- refurbish
- sell/donate/redeploy

**B. Parts**
- wipe storage
- harvest/test components
- sell/use parts

**C. Recycling**
- strip data media if needed
- separate batteries
- send end-of-life material to verified recycler

**D. Reject / hazardous**
- swollen batteries
- unsafe CRTs
- contaminated material
- unsupported categories
- immediately route to approved specialist

This keeps capital expenditure low while preserving most of the economic opportunity.

## 11. A practical Dubbo triage system modelled on Sircel

Sircel does not publish its exact grading rules. The following is therefore a **proposed Dubbo operating model informed by Sircel's process**, not a claim about Sircel's internal SOP.

### Gate 0 — ownership / legal intake

Before touching the device:

- supplier identity;
- signed authority/ownership transfer;
- business/school asset disposal authority where required;
- serial number;
- manufacturer/model;
- source;
- received date;
- locked/managed status declared;
- physical condition photographs.

**Do not wipe or alter a device until the second-hand dealer-law position and any 14-day hold obligation are resolved.**

### Gate 1 — safety triage

Immediately separate:

- swollen/punctured/hot batteries;
- leaking cells;
- CRT damage;
- liquid-contaminated equipment;
- burnt devices;
- exposed mains wiring;
- obviously hazardous material.

No charging questionable lithium batteries.

### Gate 2 — security / ownership locks

Check:

- BIOS/UEFI passwords;
- BitLocker state;
- Windows Autopilot / Intune;
- Apple Activation Lock;
- Google FRP;
- MDM;
- Computrace/Absolute where relevant;
- domain/enterprise enrollment.

A technically good laptop with an unresolved ownership lock may have **zero resale value**.

### Gate 3 — first power / hardware viability

Record:

- POST/boot;
- CPU;
- RAM;
- storage presence/health;
- display;
- keyboard/trackpad;
- ports;
- Wi-Fi/Bluetooth;
- battery condition;
- charger;
- thermals;
- physical hinges/chassis;
- cameras/audio where relevant.

### Gate 4 — market-value test

Ask:

> What would this sell for fully tested and cleaned?

Then subtract:

- missing charger;
- SSD/RAM needed;
- battery;
- parts;
- platform fees;
- postage/packaging;
- labour/time;
- warranty/returns risk.

If positive enough: **reuse candidate**.

If not, ask whether the **parts value** beats the commodity-recycling value.

### Gate 5 — data sanitisation

For equipment chosen for reuse:

- use a defined standard;
- verify the erase;
- retain serial-level evidence;
- generate a certificate/report;
- if erasure cannot be proven, physically destroy or retain the storage rather than selling it with unknown data.

The long-term professional target should look more like Sircel/Blancco's auditable approach than a generic Windows reset.

### Gate 6 — refurbishment

Only spend money where the resale margin supports it.

Likely high-value jobs:

- SSD install;
- RAM upgrade;
- battery replacement when economical;
- charger matching;
- OS install;
- cleaning;
- thermal maintenance;
- minor cosmetic repair.

### Gate 7 — final disposition

Each serial should end as one of:

- sold;
- donated;
- redeployed;
- used for parts;
- recycled;
- hazardous downstream;
- returned to supplier.

This turns "e-waste collection" into a traceable asset-management service.

- unique lot tracking;

> **We make old IT safe, useful and accountable.**

Not:

> **We take old computers.**

## 13. The council opportunity is bigger than simple collection

Sircel says it works with local councils through:

- public drop-off events;
- cages/bins at council recovery centres;
- collaboratively designed systems;
- onsite triage.

It says that since 2019 it has worked with more than 19 councils and collected nearly 1,000 tonnes through household events.

Its wider site currently reports work with **80 councils to date**, although that number includes more than only e-waste collection events.

### Best Dubbo council pilot concept

Instead of asking:

> "Can Dubbo E-Waste take the council's e-waste?"

ask:

> "Can we pilot a reuse-first triage stream before reusable computers enter the bulk recycling stream?"

A pilot could be:

1. separate cage labelled **Potential Reuse — laptops/desktops/tablets only**;
2. no damaged/swollen batteries;
3. Council staff do not need to assess value;
4. Dubbo E-Waste collects on an agreed schedule;
5. serials and outcomes logged;
6. secure wipe certificate for data-bearing assets;
7. report back:
   - units received;
   - units reused;
   - units donated;
   - units parted;
   - kg sent to recycling;
   - avoided recycling/destruction count;
   - resale/revenue-share where applicable.

This directly aligns with NSW EPA's reuse-before-recycling hierarchy.

## 14. Sircel's current corporate situation matters

There is a major due-diligence caveat.

Sircel Limited and related companies entered **receivership and voluntary administration on 31 October 2025**.

Sircel stated on 17 November 2025 that:

- operations continued across all sites;
- staff were retained;
- licences/accreditations remained in place;
- regulators had been notified.

A June 2026 insolvency report said receivers were still pursuing sale/recapitalisation options after initial bids were deemed unviable. The administrators had recommended winding up if an adjournment was not granted, while further proposals were being explored.

As of this research:

- Sircel's website is active;
- Parkes remains listed as an operating location;
- Parkes Shire's 2025-26 reporting continued to reference Sircel e-waste/shared-agreement work.

### Strategic conclusion

**Copy the model, not the balance sheet.**

The reuse-first / secure-ITAD workflow is highly relevant.

But Dubbo E-Waste should **not depend on Sircel as its sole downstream partner** without confirming current commercial and insolvency status.

The safer design is:

- Dubbo E-Waste owns its triage/reuse/data process;
- maintain more than one verified downstream recycling option;
- Sircel/Parkes can be investigated as one potential partner;
- AMR/NTCRS/MobileMuster/other certified processors remain alternatives by category.

## 16. The most useful Sircel metrics for Dubbo E-Waste to copy

Track **items**, not just kilograms.

Recommended pilot metrics:

### Intake
- number of devices;
- source/client;
- device class;
- total weight;
- estimated resale value.

### Reuse
- number reused;
- reuse rate by count;
- resale revenue;
- donation count/value;
- average turnaround time;
- repair spend;
- labour time.

### Data
- number of data-bearing devices;
- erasure passed;
- erasure failed;
- drives physically destroyed;
- certificates issued.

### Parts
- devices parted;
- parts harvested;
- parts sold/reused;
- parts value recovered.

### Recycling
- units sent to material recycling;
- kg recycled;
- downstream company;
- receipt/certificate number.

### Rejection
- locked;
- hazardous;
- uneconomic;
- physically destroyed;
- unsupported item type.

### Financial
- revenue per received device;
- gross margin per reused device;
- average cost per failed device;
- disposal/downstream cost;
- client revenue share.

Sircel's ESG report shows why this matters: **weight alone hides reuse value**. A 2 kg laptop reused is strategically more interesting than 2 kg of metal recovered.

## 19. Recommended Dubbo E-Waste positioning after this research

The emerging model is clearer now.

### Dubbo E-Waste should not primarily be:
- a tip;
- a scrap yard;
- a place that accumulates random electronics;
- a cheap imitation of a large industrial recycler.

### It could be:
**a regional reuse-first IT asset recovery service.**

Core promise:

1. **Identify what still has a useful life.**
2. **Protect the former owner's data.**
3. **Recover the maximum practical value.**
4. **Keep complete devices in use where possible.**
5. **Recycle only what is genuinely end-of-life.**
6. **Provide evidence of what happened to every significant asset.**

That is much closer to Sircel's ITAD model than to ordinary household e-waste collection.

And it fits a tiny shed pilot far better because **triage, testing, wiping, cleaning and resale are low-capital activities** compared with mechanical shredding, metals separation and refining.

## Bottom line

The most valuable thing Sircel has built is not its shredder.

It is the **decision system before the shredder**.

That is the part Dubbo E-Waste can realistically copy:

**receive → identify → secure data → test → value → refurbish → reuse → parts → recycle last.**
