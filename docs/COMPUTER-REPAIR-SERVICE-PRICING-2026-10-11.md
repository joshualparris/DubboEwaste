# How much should I charge for a computer repair?
**Practical Australian service-pricing field lesson | 11 October 2026 | DubboEwaste / Parris Tech Services context**

> **Scope:** commercial repair and IT support quotations, not resale pricing. All dollar amounts below are **illustrative training figures, not verified Dubbo quotes or a Parris Tech Services public price list**. Source links were researched in October 2026; recheck rates and legal requirements before using the guide with customers. Repair Café volunteers do not charge a surprise commercial repair fee during a community session. A referral to a paid repairer is a separate, transparent decision.

## Outcomes
After this course, construct a costed repair quote, distinguish cost-plus **markup** from target gross **margin**, choose a suitable diagnosis/hourly/fixed-fee model, explain pricing in plain language, secure informed approval before extra work, and decide when not to repair.

## 1. What is being sold?
Separate: diagnostic/assessment; labour to repair; ordered parts and consumables; backup/data migration; testing, documentation, follow-up; travel/onsite work; disposal/return; and urgency. Tell the customer which tasks are and are not included. A three-hour disk clone may need only a fraction of three hours of active technician involvement: do not automatically invoice every unattended minute as hands-on labour. But bench occupancy, monitoring, failure risk and equipment are real costs if included transparently in the agreed price.

**Billable hours are not available hours:** marketing, administration, purchasing, insurance, cancellations, travel and warranty rework reduce the hours a solo technician can actually bill. If a hypothetical business spends $600 a month on expenses, wants $2,400 as labour remuneration, and expects only 40 customer-billable hours, the rate must recover at least ($600 + $2,400) / 40 = **$75 per billable hour** before extra profit, tax obligations and unusual job costs. Count parts separately; do not charge the same overhead twice.

**Do not double count:** You may use (A) a selling labour rate which already includes wage, overhead and target profit, then add parts and clearly priced extras, or (B) cost-based quotation (true labour cost + parts + overhead + reserve) and then apply a target margin. Do not use a fully loaded selling labour rate AND re-add its included overhead and profit.

## 2. Markup is not margin
* **Markup:** (selling price − cost) / cost. If cost is $160 and markup is 20%, price is **$192**.
* **Gross margin:** (selling price − cost) / selling price. If cost is $160 and required margin is 20%, price is **$200** because $160 / 0.80 = $200.
* A **25% margin** on $180 fully allocated cost means **$180 / 0.75 = $240** ex GST.
* This is a simplified job-level gross-margin target, not net profit after all tax and owner drawings. Use internally consistent ex-GST amounts.

### Worked fictional SSD quote (all costs ex GST)
| Cost component | AUD | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| Technician labour cost, not a customer selling rate | $70 | Costed time, including legitimate work and testing |
| Delivered SSD or other part | $80 | Landed cost, not arbitrary online RRP |
| Allocated business overhead | $20 | Unrecovered overhead not already in labour cost |
| Expected warranty/rework provision | $10 | Evidence-based estimate; do not represent as a legally limited warranty |
| **Total cost** | **$180** | |
| Target job gross margin | **25%** | Business choice, not an industry standard |
| **Quote excluding GST** | **$240** | $180 / (1 − 0.25) |
| **If business is GST-registered: consumer price including 10% GST** | **$264** | $240 × 1.10 |
| **If not GST-registered** | **$240** | No GST is added; use a regular invoice |

The job also needs disclosure of risks around existing files and consent to data-handling; a clone is **not** automatically a verified backup. The owner may prefer replacement or safe return if the upgrade is poor value.

## 3. Choose a defensible pricing model
* **Assessment / diagnostic fee:** say whether it applies even when no repair is completed, and whether it is credited to approved work. No surprises.
* **Flat fee:** predictable for well-bounded tasks (e.g. laptop SSD install plus agreed OS transfer and test), with explicit exclusions for bad drives, inaccessible screws, BitLocker/account recovery and complications.
* **Hourly labour:** better for unknown faults. Give a range, an authorisation cap and the point at which you will seek approval.
* **On-site and remote:** separate travel/minimum attendance from time at the bench. State when billing begins.
* **Parts:** landed cost, quality, supply delay, suitability, manufacturer status and warranty-risk provision. Any parts markup is a business choice; do not double count it in a later whole-job margin calculation.
* **Friends/family/concessions:** decide the normal commercial value first, then explicitly show the voluntary discount, donation or fair exchange. Record used-part condition and permission to keep parts; avoid coercion or implying swaps were agreed without consent.

### Three hypothetical quote scenarios
1. **SSD upgrade for an older laptop:** 45–90 min active labour, possible long unattended clone, a $20 used SSD, plus compatibility, data-verification and unexpected-drive-failure risks. Quote the *defined outcome*, not 'four hours of copying' with no explanation. Used SSD must be tested, data-safe, honestly described and suitable.
2. **Complex no-power laptop:** charge an agreed diagnostic cap and give stop-work/referral options for board faults, risks and cost ceiling. Do not promise repair before evidence exists.
3. **Friend's laptop and donated working ProBook:** cost the work, SSD, paste, other laptop's value and any parts exchanged separately. Discount/gift is a conscious choice after disclosure; clarify who owns donor RAM/SSD and that receiving a device is not automatic payment.

## 4. Research the market without copying blindly
Find 3–5 current comparable repairers in your **actual service area**. Record date, published *customer* price, GST status, shop/on-site/remote, whether parts are included, turnaround, data handling and any diagnostic minimum. Use published prices only as benchmarks; do not infer every local business charges the same. If no published Dubbo rate is confirmed, mark the benchmark **not verified** rather than present a made-up 'Dubbo average'. Benchmark again quarterly and after larger changes in expenses. Older draft business plans may contain guesses (e.g. $30–$100+/h); they are historical drafts, not verified 2026 local rates.

## 5. Consumer safeguards are part of a good quote
**Repair notice:** Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) guidance says a business must give consumers a **written repair notice before accepting a product for repair** if it can store user-generated data; a specific additional warning is required when refurbished parts may be used. The notice needs to be clearly distinguishable and identify the Australian Consumer Law. Read the current ACCC wording before issuing customer paperwork. An assessment-only intake is distinguished in its guidance. Ask about backup, BitLocker/account locks, storage handling, password handling, data access boundaries and customer approval. Keep a testable handover record.

**Approval and changes:** record initial diagnosis scope, quoted total or labour cap, part condition, exclusions, uncertain outcomes and the client's affirmative approval. When a repair exceeds scope/cap, stop and obtain revised agreement instead of retroactively invoicing surprise extras. Do not advertise unconditional 'no refunds' or waive non-excludable Australian Consumer Law guarantees.

**GST:** do not add GST unless the supplier is registered; compulsory registration usually applies when business GST turnover reaches $75,000, subject to official rules. A GST-registered business issues tax invoices; an unregistered business uses ordinary invoices and must not label them tax invoices. A business should confirm its own registration status, not infer it from having an ABN.

**Referral:** explain when the device's expected remaining life, replacement cost, unresolved ownership, data/safety risk or technician competency makes the repair unjustified. A Repair Café learning outcome is not a licence to offer regulated mains repairs.

## 6. Customer-facing quote template
> Job: [make/model, asset serial recorded privately]. Diagnosis [fee and conditions]. Proposed work [plain language and included validation]. Labour [fixed fee or hourly rate, estimate, approval cap]. Parts [type/new/used/refurbished, price and approval]. Backup/data notice [separately provided in writing as required, owner action and risk]. Exclusions [data recovery, broken chassis, software licence, unexpected damage, etc.]. Price [GST-inclusive consumer price where applicable; GST-registration status]. Turnaround estimate [non-guaranteed if supply/diagnostics uncertain]. Approval [customer records clear consent and contact for authorising variations].

## 7. Active-recall and adaptive challenge
**Don't just read and tick.** Without notes, explain why markup differs from margin; calculate $160 cost at 20% margin; and identify the documented approval threshold for an unforeseen component.

**Practitioner scenario:** a fictional job costs $80 in parts + $55 in internal labour cost + $25 in overhead and rework. What is the target ex-GST price for a *20% job margin*? Show $160/(1−0.20) = **$200**; a 20% markup would instead be $192. State which costs were included and whether the business is GST-registered.

**Expert scenario:** you quoted $210 maximum including the original part; while opening the machine, you discover an additional $95 replacement is needed. Describe the stop-work decision, new quote, risks to customer data, explicit approval record, and repair-vs-replace alternative. Do not secretly install the part or hope to 'sort the invoice later'.

**Mastery test:** produce two defensible quotes (a straightforward SSD swap and an uncertain boot fault), with direct/indirect costs, gross margin, parts provenance, data notice, GST, maximum authorisation and alternative repair/referral. Compare with 3 current **actual** local quotes before adopting a real public price list. Peer review the reasoning, not just the numerical answer.
