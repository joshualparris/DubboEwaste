# Australian precedents for recycler / refurbisher / community partnerships to reuse computers (research notes, 25 Nov 2026)

_Scope: Australia, with regional NSW first. Sources are flagged as [OLD] where they date from before 2023. "Confirmed" means a source states it. Anything under Inferences is my reasoning, not something a source says._

## 1. Australian computer reuse and refurbishment models: who they are and how they source equipment (including any recycler setting items aside)

### Takeaway
Australia has a well-established refurbish-and-donate sector. Nationally that means WorkVentures, the National Device Bank, Good360, Computerbank and Substation33. Regionally it means SORT Recycling, Men's Sheds and Beyond eWaste. Almost all of it is supplied by **corporate, government, university and council IT refreshes, or by public drop-off**, not by scrap or e-waste recyclers. I found **no documented Australian case of a scrap metal or e-waste recycler formally setting computers aside for an outside community refurbisher.** The nearest precedents are recyclers or processors that run reuse in-house (Substation33, SORT, City of Sydney's processor, Total Green Recycling) and a regional NSW council giving its retired IT to a Men's Shed (Parkes).

### Inferences
- The supply chain behind almost every successful Australian program is a **planned IT refresh**: same-model fleets, known history, often under 5 years old, with the donor organisation owning the data risk and signing off. Scrap-yard intake is mixed, of unknown origin and older. That makes Josh's proposed source unusual, and it explains why he would need tight intake rules.
- Josh's pitch looks most like SORT, Beyond eWaste and Substation33, where recycling and refurbishing happen under one roof. The difference is that he is proposing an outside volunteer partner. I found no public template for that split.
- The Parkes Shire precedent is a useful regional NSW story because it is only about 1.5 hours from Dubbo. A council there was happy to hand retired IT to a volunteer group once the resale market dried up. Data handling in that case is undocumented.

### Gaps
- No documented case found of a **scrap metal** recycler (Sims, Australian Metal Recycling, Matthews etc.) setting aside computers for an outside refurbisher. Absence of public evidence does not mean it never happens; informal local arrangements may exist without publicity.
- No Dubbo or Orana-region computer refurbisher or Men's Shed computer program was found online.
- Infoxchange's current device program details (e.g. "Digital Access" or Tech for Good device offers) were not retrieved.
- Current status of SORT Recycling sites is not confirmed.
- The "National Device Bank" figures on goodthingsfoundation.org (about 10,000 recipients, 40+ donors, only donations of 50+ devices accepted) appear to be the **UK** program, not Australia's. Don't use them as Australian data. — [Good Things Foundation (UK)](https://goodthingsfoundation.org/our-services/national-device-bank)

## 2. Council and recycler "tip shop" / reuse-before-recycling models in regional NSW and Victoria, and how they handled data security and liability

### Takeaway
Regional NSW councils commonly run tip shops or reuse sheds for general goods. Explicit **computer** reuse at tips is rare, mainly because of data and scheme obligations. Where councils do reuse IT, it is their **own** retired fleet. They handle it through an ICT disposal policy: council wipes the data, then sells or donates "as is where is" with no liability accepted. Victoria's e-waste landfill ban (July 2019) pushed e-waste into recycling streams, but I found no Victorian council tip shop doing computer reuse with documented data controls.

### Cited Findings
- Kyogle Council (Northern Rivers NSW) runs a Reuse Shed / Tip Shop at its landfill, taking whitegoods, household appliances and building materials. Computers are not explicitly listed. — [Kyogle Council](https://www.kyogle.nsw.gov.au/waste-and-recycling-information/reuse-repair-and-recycling/)
- **Tenterfield Shire Council (regional NSW) Disposal of ICT Assets Policy (May 2025)**: before disposal, all data is backed up and "data shredding software" is used to securely remove data. Items are sold "on an 'as is where is' basis with Council accepting no responsibility for any faults or error of description". Sales are reported to Council with item, purchaser and price. This is a working regional NSW template for liability and data handling. — [Tenterfield Shire Council policy](https://www.tenterfield.nsw.gov.au/content/uploads/2025/06/Disposal-of-ICT-Assets-Policy-May-2025.pdf)
- Parkes Shire Council passes its replaced devices to the Men's Shed for resale (see Q1). Data handling not documented. — [Parkes Champion-Post] [OLD](https://www.parkeschampionpost.com.au/story/5596298/new-homes-for-used-technology/)
- City of Sydney tells residents: "The recycling process doesn't include data wiping". It also says items its processor refurbishes "will have their data wiped before they are resold". So residents carry first responsibility, and the processor wipes again if it reuses an item. — [City of Sydney](https://www.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/waste-recycling-services/recycle-e-waste)
- Willoughby Council (NSW) notes that some e-waste items "are reused rather than recycled so please ensure personal data is erased from your device". — [Willoughby Council](https://willoughby.nsw.gov.au/Residents/Waste-and-recycling/FAQs-and-other-resources/A-Z-of-recycling/Computers)
- The Hills Shire Council (NSW) Repair Fair lets The Reconnect Project collect phones, tablets and **laptops under 10 years old** (in any condition) and erase, repair and refurbish them. Desktops, monitors and printers are not accepted. — [The Hills Shire Council](https://www.thehills.nsw.gov.au/Upcoming-Events-Activities/Repair-Fair)
- Victoria banned all e-waste from landfill from 1 July 2019. — [Murrindindi Shire](https://murrindindi.vic.gov.au/Services/Bins-Rubbish-and-Recycling/E-waste); [Greater Bendigo via National Tribune] [OLD](https://www.nationaltribune.com.au/greater-bendigo-gears-up-for-e-waste-ban/)
- City of Greater Bendigo took over the Eaglehawk Recycle Shop from 1 November 2025, renaming it Eaglehawk Recycling Centre and running it like the City's other recycling centres. — [City of Greater Bendigo](https://www.bendigo.vic.gov.au/about-us/news-and-media/city-operate-new-eaglehawk-recycling-centre-november-1)
- NSW EPA publishes "Television and Computer Recycling in NSW – A guide for councils, social enterprises, e-waste recyclers and collection partners". It includes "what to look for when negotiating an agreement with e-waste service providers". Not fetched in full; likely 2016-era. — [NSW EPA guide PDF] [OLD](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/television-and-computer-recycling-guide-160449.pdf)
- Dubbo's Whylandra Waste and Recycling Centre is about 15 km west of Dubbo on Cooba Road. The council page found no reuse or tip-shop arrangement for computers. — [Dubbo Regional Council](https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/Households---Residents/Rubbish--Recycling-and-Sustainability/whylandra-waste-recycling-centre)
- DTA's Sustainable Device Management Guide (May 2025) tells government agencies to make sure "the device is cleansed and no residual data remains before donation or disposal". It also says to remove security controls and encryption tools, run a supply-chain risk assessment, and put ethical disposal requirements into contracts. — [DTA / Australian Government Architecture](https://architecture.digital.gov.au/node/1162); [DTA guide PDF](https://architecture.digital.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/sustainable_device_management_guide.pdf)

### Inferences
- Councils that reuse IT generally own the devices and wipe them themselves. Public drop-off e-waste is a different risk class, because the council can't vouch for the data on it. That probably explains the Whylandra decline: the risk of reusing other people's computers was a step beyond a normal council reuse shed.
- The "as is where is, no responsibility accepted" wording (Tenterfield) and "residents must wipe their own data" (City of Sydney) are two common risk-transfer devices Josh can mirror in a recycler agreement.

### Gaps
- No regional NSW or Victorian **council tip shop** found that resells computers from public drop-off with a documented data-wipe process.
- The NSW EPA negotiating guide was not read in full. It may cover contract clauses for reuse.
- No council-recycler contract clause on "reuse permitted / not permitted" was found publicly.

## 4. Typical structure of these arrangements: MOU vs contract, title transfer, chain of custody, erasure certificates, insurance, residuals, diversion reporting, trial periods

### Takeaway
Public detail on recycler-to-refurbisher agreements is thin. The published models (WorkVentures, The Smith Family, National Device Reuse Principles, DTA guide, council ICT policies) share a recognisable set of elements: **per-item tracking from intake, certified erasure (often Blancco), a clear point where ownership and risk transfer ("as is where is"), donor reporting (devices reused, kg diverted, recipients), recycling of rejects through a responsible channel, and audit or inspection rights.** None of the sources showed an actual MOU or contract text. Insurance requirements were not documented anywhere I looked.

### Cited Findings
- WorkVentures CircularIT process: (1) inventory and assessment (catalogue assets, assess condition and residual value) → data sanitisation and certification → refurbishment or recycling. All storage media are sanitised "per industry standards". — [WorkVentures CircularIT](https://workventures.com.au/circularit/)
- WorkVentures provides "Auditing to provide a detailed report before and after sanitisation" and "Certification for your compliance records". — [WorkVentures Secure IT Lifecycle](https://workventures.com.au/?p=3503); [WorkVentures e-waste](https://workventures.com.au/e-waste/)
- National Device Bank donor workflow: the donor selects the sanitisation provider, advises the percentage or volume to donate, collection is free to the donor, and the donor receives reporting on e-waste avoided, social benefit, destination and a case study. — [WorkVentures](https://workventures.com.au/national-device-bank/)
- Westpac runs periodic **onsite inspections** of WorkVentures, and WorkVentures supplies data sanitisation certification and regular security reporting. This is an audit-rights precedent. — [ADIA paper, Mar 2024](https://www.digitalinclusion.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/A-Digital-Inclusion-Approach-to-Device-Donation-and-Reuse.pdf)
- The Smith Family gives donors a report covering devices donated, refurbished devices delivered, estimated e-waste diverted and carbon avoided. It uses Blancco. — [The Smith Family](https://www.thesmithfamily.com.au/get-involved/partnerships/device-donation)
- National Device Reuse Principles (Sept 2026), seven principles: prioritise digital inclusion; consider reuse early; align with existing frameworks; **manage security, records and risk** ("appropriate oversight of devices throughout decommissioning, transfer and disposal"); **work through appropriate partners** (arrangements "fair, transparent"); **support quality second-life pathways** (unsuitable devices to "responsible recycling"); and review and improve over time. No specific erasure standard, title-transfer or liability terms are given. — [National Device Reuse Principles PDF](https://www.finance.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-08/National-Device-Reuse-Principles.pdf)
- Tenterfield Shire Council: "as is where is", council accepts no responsibility, sale details recorded and reported. — [Tenterfield policy](https://www.tenterfield.nsw.gov.au/content/uploads/2025/06/Disposal-of-ICT-Assets-Policy-May-2025.pdf)
- DTA guide: put ethical and sustainable disposal requirements into contracts and run a supply-chain risk assessment covering security standards. — [DTA](https://architecture.digital.gov.au/node/1162)
- NTCRS context: co-regulatory arrangements may only contract recyclers certified to **AS 5377** (the Australian Standard for collection, storage, transport and treatment of e-waste). The material recovery target is 90%. — [National EPA – Information for recyclers](https://www.nationalepa.gov.au/node/268)
- Volume thresholds are common. Computers for Young and Old only collects rurally for 100+ items. — [Recycling Near You](https://recyclingnearyou.com.au/business/35030)
- Fujitsu's guide to choosing an e-waste processor warns of "brand damage if asset tagged or branded equipment is detected at an illegal eWaste dump". Downstream accountability is a reputational issue for whoever handed the gear on. — [Fujitsu, 2020] [OLD](https://www.fujitsu.com/au/imagesgig5/eWaste-8questions_to_ask_your_processor_2020.pdf)

### Inferences
These are suggested pitch elements, drawn from the patterns above rather than any single source:
- **Instrument:** for a 10–20 unit trial, a short letter of agreement or MOU is consistent with the informal council→Men's Shed and charity models. A contract is more typical of corporate ITAD (WorkVentures). Start light and formalise after the trial.
- **Title and risk transfer:** state the point where ownership passes (e.g. signed collection docket) and that items are taken "as is where is". AMR then carries no product liability on donated items, and Josh carries data responsibility from that point.
- **Chain of custody:** a per-item log (serial, make/model, date collected, Blancco certificate ID, outcome: donated / recycled / returned). This mirrors WorkVentures' scan-on-arrival practice. Josh's planned AssetFlow tool fits this need.
- **Residuals:** everything that fails testing goes back to AMR (as Josh proposes). That keeps AMR's metal value and its scheme or contract volumes as intact as possible. It is also the "responsible recycling" requirement in Principle 6.
- **Reporting:** a quarterly one-page report covering units collected, units reused, kg returned to AMR, kg diverted, recipient organisations, and certificate list. This matches what corporate donors receive from WorkVentures and The Smith Family.
- **Audit rights:** offer AMR the right to inspect the workspace or certificates (the Westpac precedent).
- **Trial:** a fixed number of units and a fixed period, then a review. This matches Principle 7's "review and improve".

### Gaps
- No public example of an MOU or contract between an e-waste or scrap recycler and an outside community refurbisher.
- No public information on insurance requirements (public liability, professional indemnity, cyber) asked of volunteer refurbishers in Australia. Josh should check what cover a volunteer group or sole trader would need. The Repair Café and Men's Shed networks may have group insurance schemes (not verified).
- I did not confirm whether the 2021 NTCRS Rules changed the "reuse does not count" position (see Q5).

## 5. Why recyclers decline, and what has helped overcome it

### Takeaway
The sources directly support three barriers: **data-security fear, scheme design that rewards recycling not reuse, and liability and logistics costs.** Recycler-specific reasons (staff time to sort, theft or provenance concerns, lost metal value, WHS) are plausible but **not documented in Australian sources I found**; treat them as inference. What has helped elsewhere: certified erasure with per-device certificates, donor reporting, returning residuals, starting small, and wider policy momentum (Tech Reuse Charter, National Device Reuse Principles, ADIA calls to count reuse in the NTCRS).

### Cited Findings
- **Scheme design**: under the NTCRS material-recovery methodology, "any re-use, refurbishment and/or re-marketing of televisions and computers is not considered material recovery and does not count towards the target". — [Dept of Agriculture, NTCRS material recovery methodology] [OLD, c. 2014](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/material-recovery-measurement-reporting-methodology.docx)
- The ADIA paper reports a Right to Repair finding: because the NTCRS was designed as a recycling-only scheme, functional devices are often dismantled rather than reused. ADIA recommends including reuse in NTCRS targets, as the Productivity Commission recommended in 2021. — [ADIA paper, Mar 2024](https://www.digitalinclusion.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/A-Digital-Inclusion-Approach-to-Device-Donation-and-Reuse.pdf)
- A proposed product stewardship scheme for small electrical and electronic equipment (SEEE) would require the administrator to facilitate reuse and repair collection on request. — [ADIA paper](https://www.digitalinclusion.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/A-Digital-Inclusion-Approach-to-Device-Donation-and-Reuse.pdf)
- NTCRS co-regulatory arrangements must only contract recyclers certified to AS 5377 and meet a 90% material recovery target. — [National EPA](https://www.nationalepa.gov.au/node/268)

### Inferences (not directly sourced)
- **Scheme or contract obligations**: if AMR Dubbo collects computers under an NTCRS arrangement, or sends e-waste on to a downstream processor under contract, diverting units for reuse may (a) reduce weight it can claim or invoice and (b) breach "all material to recycler" terms. - **Corporate approval**: if AMR is part of a multi-site group, the local manager (your AMR contact) may need head-office sign-off on data and liability. A one-page risk summary aimed at head office could help.
- **Data liability**: AMR's main fear is likely a newspaper story about data found on a computer that came out of its yard. Signed per-device Blancco certificates, wipe-before-anything-else, and physical destruction of drives that fail erasure answer this directly.
- **Staff time**: offer clear, simple set-aside criteria (e.g. laptops only, under about 8 years old, complete with screen) and a dedicated cage or bin Josh empties on a fixed schedule. Nothing extra for AMR staff to do.
- **Theft or provenance**: because NSW regulates scrap dealing, AMR may worry that reused items could be stolen goods. Recording serials and keeping a log reduces this risk.
- **Metal value**: a laptop's scrap value is small. Returning residuals (batteries, boards, cases) back to AMR keeps most of it.

## 6. Realistic yield, worthwhile ages and specs (Windows 11 / Linux), and demand from community organisations

### Takeaway
No Australian source gives a reusable share for **scrap-yard or e-waste-stream** computers. The only figure found is 50–70% reusable for **planned government refresh fleets**, which are far better stock. Scrap intake yield is likely to be much lower (inference). The specs that refurbishers and charities want are roughly Intel 5th–6th gen i5 or newer, 8 GB RAM, SSD and webcam, and laptops over desktops. Windows 10's end of support (14 Oct 2025) means pre-8th-gen machines need Linux (Computerbank's approach) or should be recycled. Demand is real and larger outside capitals: about 1 in 5 Australians are digitally excluded, and mobile-only use is higher in regional areas.

### Inferences
- Scrap-yard computers come in unsorted, often stripped or damaged (missing drives or RAM, broken screens), and skew older. A realistic **guess** for a first trial: perhaps 2–6 of 10–20 units donatable as whole machines, plus parts harvesting. This is an estimate with no source behind it; the trial itself is the way to measure it. Josh should frame the trial as "let's find out the yield together" rather than promise a number.
- Practical cut-off: Intel 8th gen or newer (or AMD Ryzen 2000+) with TPM 2.0 → Windows 11. Intel 4th–7th gen with SSD and 8 GB → Linux Mint (Computerbank model) for recipients who can cope with it. Older than that → recycle.
- Laptops are what charities want most. Desktops are harder to place (The Reconnect Project won't take them) unless a specific organisation asks for them.
- Recipients and partners in Dubbo could include public housing tenants, older people, First Nations organisations, job seekers and students (ADII's highest-exclusion groups). Pairing devices with Dubbo Library tech help addresses the "device plus skills" point.
