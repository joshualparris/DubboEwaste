# Manufacturer-authorised FRP recovery — Dubbo ITAD
Date: 27 November 2026 (AEDT). Status: research and enquiry only; no accreditation obtained.

## Evidence and current position
- Huawei Australia consumer support stated in a chat supplied by the project operator that Roadhound Electronics can process Huawei FRP removal after proof of purchase and a completed Unlocking Agreement, with data erased. This is reported support guidance, not an executed service or formal agreement.
- A subsequent Roadhound conversation reportedly directed the operator to seek authorisation directly from manufacturers rather than through Roadhound. The claim that Roadhound is the sole Australian authorised provider needs written confirmation of brand, service, geography and time scope.
- Roadhound service contact supplied by Huawei: service@roadhound.com.au, 02 4949 3030, 14 Pendlebury Road, Cardiff NSW. Confirm postal postcode and instructions before dispatch (2285/2286 discrepancy).
- Roadhound is a potential commercial and recycling partner; verify current brand relationships, MobileMuster participation, bulk pricing and postal service independently. No cost for Roadhound's accreditation has been established.
- No public fixed-price Australian FRP-only accreditation programme was identified in preliminary research. Manufacturer fees, eligibility, training, tooling, annual charges and per-device fees are unknown, not zero.
- General repair certification, enterprise service-provider accreditation, MDM enrolment and Apple's free Independent Repair Provider programme do not themselves confer account-lock removal authority.
- Goal: reuse-first recovery for lawfully acquired, documented donated and decommissioned devices in regional NSW, with manufacturer-approved erasure and ownership verification. Do not assume existing authorisation, accept unverified provenance or access former owners' data.

## Deep research prompt
Act as an evidence-first Australian ITAD, circular-economy and manufacturer-service partnership researcher. Find the cheapest feasible official route for a small regional operator in Dubbo NSW to gain manufacturer-approved FRP recovery authority (not merely ordinary repair certification). Prioritise Huawei, then Samsung, Motorola/Lenovo, HMD/Nokia, TCL/Alcatel, OPPO, realme, vivo, Xiaomi, Google Pixel and other brands relevant to Australian discarded phones. Separately examine Apple's Activation Lock policies; do not conflate them with Android FRP.

1. Identify the Australian legal entity, consumer after-sales/service network, partnership team, current authorised providers and exact official application route for each manufacturer. Determine whether there is a limited FRP/ITAD accreditation, subcontractor, regional referral, supervised pilot or full service-centre-only pathway. Verify the Roadhound exclusivity claim and distinguish each brand, device family and service scope.
2. Find actual published or written-quote prices in AUD: application, training/exams, official software access, renewals, per-device fees, audit, insurance, premises, equipment, minimum stock/volume, freight, GST and any required deposits. Mark unknown prices explicitly; do not infer prices from unrelated certification or unverified resellers. Identify whom to ask for a written quotation.
3. Establish eligibility: sole technician versus company, ABN, experience, premises/security requirements, personnel checks, Australian consumer-device scope, supported legacy models, geographic restrictions, manufacturer training, renewal and termination terms.
4. Verify lawful ownership evidence accepted for donated, business-decommissioned, council or recycler-sourced devices: original receipt, asset register, signed transfer, donor consent, chain of custody, and any alternative manufacturer-approved proof. Distinguish disposal authority from authority to remove an account lock. Address lost/stolen devices, carrier blocklists and rejection/quarantine procedures. Do not propose bypass techniques, unauthorised tools or credentials.
5. Compare authorised mail-in/bulk processing through Roadhound, manufacturer recovery, donor-assisted recovery, and OEM-approved regional pilot alternatives. Research MobileMuster, reuse grants and manufacturer-sponsored circular-economy programmes; distinguish recycling from reuse and any funding eligibility.
6. Assess relevant Australian privacy, consumer, e-waste and data-sanitisation requirements using regulator and standards-body sources; specify recordkeeping, data minimisation, retention, verification of erasure, and escalation. Distinguish legal obligations from contractual requirements and voluntary standards.
7. Model total cost and cost per successfully recovered phone at 10, 50, 100, 250 and 1,000 devices/year, factoring in eligibility/rejection, shipping, labour, and recovery value. Label assumptions, show sensitivity and break-even against outsourced processing. Rank only evidence-supported pathways by cost, feasibility, time and reuse yield.
8. Deliver brand-by-brand evidence table, official contacts and application links, exact quotation questions, source register with access dates and short supporting excerpts, unresolved questions, and a 30/60/90-day pilot plan with stop/go gates. Draft manufacturer enquiry and ownership evidence checklist; do not send messages or apply without approval. Separate verified public facts, reported stakeholder statements, estimates and unknowns. Prefer primary Australian sources, compare global examples only as leads, and record contradictions. Do not invent answers when commercial terms are confidential.
