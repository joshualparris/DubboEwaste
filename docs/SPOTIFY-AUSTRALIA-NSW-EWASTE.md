# Start here — strongest Australia / NSW matches

## 1. UNSW Business School — *IT Upgrade: The future of recycling office e-waste*

**Podcast:** The Business Of — UNSW Business School  
**Spotify show:** https://open.spotify.com/show/6kUw4yMSMNMJQQm4StZBhi  
**Exact episode:** **IT Upgrade – The future of recycling office e-waste**  
**UNSW episode page:** https://www.unsw.edu.au/business/our-schools/agsm/about-us/the-business-of-podcast/the-business-of-recycling

### Why this is the closest match to Dubbo E-Waste

This is not a generic sustainability episode. It is about **Renew IT**, an Australian ITAD company headquartered at **Lane Cove West, NSW**, and founder James Lancaster.

Lancaster describes Renew IT's original model:

**corporate laptop refresh → collect old laptops → sanitise data → issue wipe certificates → on-sell viable devices.**

That is extremely close to the commercial model being considered for Dubbo.

The episode also covers the problem that appeared later: equipment such as office printers had **no secondary resale value** and began accumulating. That is exactly why the Dubbo pilot needs:

- selective intake;
- a known downstream path before accepting something;
- shelf limits;
- real resale-value testing.

Lancaster later partnered with **UNSW Sydney** to turn difficult e-waste plastics into new material via a MICROfactorie.

### Most relevant points for this repo

- refurb/resale was built into the original business;
- secure data handling created business trust;
- corporate refreshes supplied consistent equipment;
- some assets have strong secondary value while others become liabilities;
- downstream traceability matters;
- Lancaster explicitly recommends that businesses work with an accredited ITAD and ask what happens downstream.

### NSW connection

Renew IT's head office and major facility are at **91 Mars Road, Lane Cove West NSW**.

Sources:
- https://renew-it.com/about-us/
- https://renew-it.com/contact-us/
- https://www.unsw.edu.au/business/our-schools/agsm/about-us/the-business-of-podcast/the-business-of-recycling

**Priority: #1. Listen to this first.**

**Spotify:**  
https://open.spotify.com/episode/6WyTlDuqgcYpBieTiEsdB2

### Why it is highly relevant

Again features **James Lancaster / Renew IT**, so despite the host show not being NSW-specific, the business being discussed is an Australian-owned ITAD company headquartered in Sydney.

The episode focuses more heavily on:

- e-waste;
- circular technology;
- difficult e-waste plastics;
- reuse/remanufacturing;
- Renew IT's MICROfactorie work.

### Dubbo connection

This is useful for understanding where Dubbo E-Waste could stop:

> intake → data → test → repair → resale → parts

and where a specialist downstream company such as Renew IT can take over:

> low-value material → separation/remanufacturing/material recovery.

It reinforces that Dubbo does **not** need its own shredder or plastics processing plant.

### NSW connection

Renew IT and its UNSW-linked MICROfactorie are based in **Lane Cove, Sydney**.

https://open.spotify.com/show/3LiamrXYr7oHazXJliLqKW

LiteHaus is an Australian digital-inclusion organisation.

The episode describes a path from **a single laptop donated from a garage in 2017** to a program that has refurbished more than **23,000 devices** and reached hundreds of thousands of students across Australia and the Pacific.

### Dubbo connection

### Especially interesting

The starting story is small — which makes it relevant to the Joe/Elise pattern:

**one device / tiny operation → prove usefulness → grow behind demand and partnerships.**

# The one I would play first

**UNSW Business School — IT Upgrade: The future of recycling office e-waste.**

It is strikingly close to the model in this repository.

James Lancaster says Renew IT originally went into companies during laptop refreshes, took the old devices, **sanitised the data, gave the customer data-wipe certificates and on-sold the devices**.

Then the company discovered the same trap the Dubbo research keeps warning about: low-value equipment that nobody wants starts accumulating.

That makes it useful for both halves of the project:

1. **how to make money from good retired IT**, and
2. **why you need a downstream route before accepting the bad stuff.**

Renew IT is also genuinely local enough to matter: its head office is in **Lane Cove West, NSW**, and it partnered with **UNSW Sydney** on its e-waste plastics MICROfactorie.
