# Podcasts featuring Australian ITAD / e-waste companies

**Curated:** 2 October 2026  
**Scope:** podcast/radio appearances featuring founders, CEOs or senior people from the Australian ITAD/e-waste companies compared in this repository.

This list is different from the broader e-waste podcast guides. Every item below is tied directly to one of the companies/operators we researched.

Priority is given to:

- Spotify where a direct link is available;
- Australian / NSW operators;
- episodes explaining how the business actually started, makes money, handles reuse/resale, secures data, or scales.

# 2. Renew IT — James Lancaster

## All Things Circular — EP 49: *E-Waste, Microfactories & Circular Plastics Innovation with James Lancaster of Renew IT*

**Direct Spotify episode:**  
https://open.spotify.com/episode/6WyTlDuqgcYpBieTiEsdB2

**Published:** 18 March 2026  
**Length:** about 20 minutes

### Why it is one of the most relevant episodes in the entire repo

Renew IT is a NSW-based operator whose original business model closely resembles the eventual Dubbo commercial model:

**corporate refresh → collect retired laptops → sanitise data → provide wipe certificates → resell viable devices → process the remainder.**

The episode then goes further into Renew IT's e-waste plastics/MICROfactorie work.

Useful for understanding:

- the boundary between ITAD/refurbishment and industrial material processing;
- why Dubbo can outsource the industrial end;
- how a reuse company can grow into more sophisticated downstream recovery.

---

## UNSW Business School — *IT Upgrade: The future of recycling office e-waste*

**Spotify show:**  
https://open.spotify.com/show/6kUw4yMSMNMJQQm4StZBhi

**Exact episode:**  
**IT Upgrade – The future of recycling office e-waste**

**Official episode/transcript:**  
https://www.unsw.edu.au/business/our-schools/agsm/about-us/the-business-of-podcast/the-business-of-recycling

**Published:** 21 May 2025  
**Length:** about 27 minutes

### Why this is probably the single best Renew IT episode

James Lancaster explains Renew IT's original purpose very plainly.

The early model was:

1. go into a corporation during a laptop refresh;
2. remove the old laptops;
3. sanitise the data;
4. provide data-wipe certificates;
5. **on-sell the devices**.

He then explains the problem created by low-value assets such as multifunction printers that accumulated because there was no secondary market.

That directly validates two of Dubbo's most important rules:

> **Reuse/resell what has value.**

and

> **Do not accept low-value streams without a known downstream exit.**

# 3. The Reconnect Project — Annette Brodie

## Career Stories — *Annette Brodie: Closing the Digital Divide, Social Enterprise and Backing Yourself*

**Listen / official episode:**  
https://stories.the-career-library.com/episodes/annette-brodie

**Alternative official page:**  
https://www.the-career-library.com/career-stories-episodes/annette-brodie

**Apple:**  
https://podcasts.apple.com/au/podcast/annette-brodie-closing-the-digital-divide-social/id1873863711?i=1000768617292

**Published:** 20 May 2026  
**Length:** about 49 minutes

### Why this is exceptionally relevant

The Reconnect Project may be the closest social-enterprise comparison to a small NSW reuse-first operation.

Annette discusses:

- collecting donated phones, tablets and laptops;
- secure refurbishment;
- distributing devices through 130+ social-service agencies;
- employment pathways for neurodivergent young adults;
- designing the organisation with **income-generating business units**, rather than relying solely on grants;
- building the organisation from scratch.

The episode also covers the personal financial risk involved in securing the first shopfront.

### Dubbo lesson

This is valuable for thinking about a future model with both:

- **commercial services/revenue**, and
- **social reuse**.

That is much closer to the repo's commercial-reuse + social-reuse concept than a pure charity giveaway model.

# 6. Total Green Recycling — Michael Coghill

**Direct Spotify episode:**  
https://open.spotify.com/episode/5NZws8pogFpNxIbTv8tmCa

**Published:** 3 May 2021  
**Length:** about 1 hour

### Why it matters

Michael Coghill is one of the founding directors of Total Green Recycling.

The podcast is mainly a leadership conversation, but his company history is particularly relevant.

Total Green has publicly described beginning with Michael and James Coghill:

> **working from their dad's garage and collecting e-waste from verge collections.**

That makes Total Green another Australian example of:

**tiny/home-scale beginning → serious e-waste operation.**

Even though Total Green eventually became a large WA recycler, the early story reinforces that physical scale does not need to come first.

# Most useful insight from this podcast search

The strongest episodes are not the industrial-recycling ones.

For the current Dubbo project, the most useful audio is where founders talk about the **business transition**:

### Austin Turpin / FlipTech
tests assumptions around:
- sales;
- hiring;
- warehouse space;
- ISO;
- delegation.

### James Lancaster / Renew IT
explains:
- corporate refresh supply;
- data wipe certificates;
- resale;
- the problem of low-value leftovers.

### Annette Brodie / Reconnect
explains:
- donated-device refurbishment;
- earned revenue;
- social distribution;
- starting/building a physical operation.

### Total Green
provides another:
- garage-start;
- e-waste entrepreneur;
- scale-up precedent.

Taken together, those four are probably the closest podcast equivalent to the **Joe Parker / Elise Parker startup-history research** already in the repo.
