# PonyUp for Good → SecondBite → Dubbo

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)

## Bottom line

There is a real institutional chain between PonyUp for Good, SecondBite and Dubbo, but the connection is **financial/social-impact**, not a proven device-logistics chain.

The defensible chain is:

**retired corporate technology → PonyUp reuse/resale → PonyUp profit → SecondBite nationally**

and separately:

**SecondBite / Coles food-rescue network → Connecting Community Services in Dubbo → local food relief**

No public evidence was found showing that:
- a Dubbo computer has been processed by PonyUp;
- PonyUp's SecondBite contribution is earmarked for Dubbo;
- SecondBite receives refurbished electronics from PonyUp;
- Connecting Community Services receives computers from PonyUp or SecondBite.

Those distinctions matter.

---

## 1. What PonyUp actually does

PonyUp for Good is a reuse-first corporate IT asset-disposition/social-enterprise model.

Its published process is:
1. collect donated/decommissioned corporate technology;
2. securely erase data and remove branding;
3. refurbish/remarket viable devices;
4. send non-reusable equipment to accredited recycling;
5. donate **50% of PonyUp's profits** to SecondBite;
6. report reuse/recycling/community-impact outcomes to clients.

PonyUp says it collects corporate technology across Australia.

Its published impact material currently reports:
- **600,000+ kg** of technology diverted from landfill;
- **32.5%** of collected equipment reused rather than recycled;
- **1,000,000 meals** generated through the SecondBite relationship.

These are PonyUp-published impact figures.

Sources:
- https://www.ponyupforgood.com/
- https://www.ponyupforgood.com/faq
- https://www.ponyupforgood.com/1-million-meals
- https://www.ponyupforgood.com/telstraenterprise

---

## 2. What flows to SecondBite

The important correction is:

> **The technology does not flow to SecondBite. Money does.**

PonyUp's reuse/resale process creates residual commercial value. PonyUp says 50% of its profits are donated to SecondBite.

SecondBite then operates its own food-rescue network.

Therefore it is not accurate to describe SecondBite as a technology recipient, electronics recycler, refurbishment partner or downstream e-waste processor.

---

## 3. The specific Dubbo SecondBite connection

**Connecting Community Services / Dubbo Neighbourhood Centre Ltd** is a current SecondBite partner in Dubbo.

Its current public page says:
- it receives weekly food donations through the **SecondBite program and Coles**;
- food is distributed free to people in need;
- the organisation operates from **31–33 Church Street, Dubbo**.

Source:
- https://ccsd.org.au/community/food-for-those-in-need

This is a concrete local SecondBite relationship.

### Important limit

No public evidence found traces a particular PonyUp donation, client collection, dollar or meal to Connecting Community Services.

The connection is therefore:

**PonyUp → SecondBite nationally**

plus

**SecondBite → Connecting Community Services Dubbo**

not a proved transaction-level PonyUp → Dubbo chain.

---

## 4. Why Connecting Community Services is particularly relevant to device reuse

Connecting Community Services already operates a **digital-access service** in Dubbo.

Its current public information says it provides:
- free access to a computer and internet;
- basic computer-skills help;
- access for tasks such as résumés, services and online information.

Source:
- https://ccsd.org.au/community/computer-internet-access

It also provides broader services for:
- families;
- young people;
- seniors;
- migrants;
- people needing community information/referrals.

Its migrant-support service has outreach to:
- Wellington;
- Gilgandra;
- Mudgee;
- Narromine.

Sources:
- https://ccsd.org.au/
- https://ccsd.org.au/family/migrant-support

### What was not found

No current public evidence was found that Connecting Community Services:
- receives refurbished laptops from PonyUp;
- receives devices from SecondBite;
- runs a formal device giveaway/refurbished-computer program;
- is already a TechCollect/WorkVentures/PonyUp electronics-reuse partner.

That makes it a **potential local social-reuse partner**, not a confirmed one.

Separate repo research has found:

- Dubbo Support Center appears organisationally eligible for the NSW Device Bank based on published rules, although no Dubbo rollout was found;
- Digital Nebula – Dubbo has received Good Things/Be Connected support;

See:
- `GOOD360-COMPNOW-NATIONAL-DEVICE-BANK-DUBBO.md`

## 6. Is there a PonyUp e-waste operation in Dubbo?

No named Dubbo PonyUp project, client, collection point, office or refurbishment facility was found publicly.

PonyUp does say it collects retired corporate technology across Australia and has national enterprise relationships, including Telstra Enterprise.

Therefore:
- **service coverage may include Dubbo**;
- **existing Dubbo activity is not proven**.

Do not describe PonyUp as currently operating a Dubbo electronics program without direct confirmation.

---

## 7. What this means for Dubbo eWaste

The relevant lesson from PonyUp is the model, not a current local branch:

**local business refresh**
→ **serialised intake**
→ **secure erase**
→ **test / grade**
→ **reuse / refurbish / remarket**
→ **recycle failures**
→ **document impact**

A Dubbo-specific social layer could then be added through a local community organisation.

Possible local recipient/support partners to investigate include:
- Connecting Community Services;
- Dubbo Support Center;
- other ACNC/community organisations identified by Good Things/Device Bank research.

No partnership should be claimed until the organisation agrees.

## 10. Final finding

The strongest current Dubbo-specific PonyUp/SecondBite connection is:

**PonyUp creates profit from reuse of corporate IT → 50% of profits support SecondBite → Connecting Community Services is a current SecondBite partner in Dubbo.**

That does **not** mean PonyUp technology is currently being refurbished or distributed in Dubbo.

The most interesting opportunity is to connect the already-present community-support network with a local, secure electronics-reuse supply chain.
