## Executive recommendation

**Approach Dubbo Farmers Market about a three-session market-linked pilot** before choosing a fixed monthly calendar. Prefer either (A) a small, explicitly authorised low-risk repair-and-demonstration stall at the market or (B) a nearby sheltered repair room paired with a market information/recruitment point. Market days are a community reach opportunity, not inherently a clash to avoid. The existing third-Saturday 10am–1pm proposal should remain editable until volunteer, market, insurance and venue feasibility are established.

Distinguish documented external operating practice from recommendations inferred for Dubbo. In particular, published examples **do not verify** their insurance wording, risk controls, stall fees or behind-the-scenes scheduling software.

### Operating practices confirmed in other settings

- **Repair with the owner, not drop-off:** Transition Australia describes intake desk, owner watching/learning, matching a repairer, signed acknowledgement, free volunteer labour and optional donation. [Transition Australia](https://transitionaustralia.net/community-repair-cafes-hubs/)
- **Visitor guidance and walk-in flexibility:** Hunter Multicultural Communities' 2026 pop-up used free tickets primarily for estimated numbers, accepted walk-ins and imposed a final item arrival time ahead of closing. [HMC event](https://events.humanitix.com/pop-up-repair-cafe-hmc)
- **Role separation:** Gawler Environment Centre describes reception volunteers greeting/registering visitors, managing time slots and recording outcomes; café helpers serving refreshments; and repairers fixing items. [Gawler](https://www.gawlerenvironmentcentre.org.au/volunteer/)
- **Three-hour, occasional volunteer commitments:** St Kilda Repair Cafe expressly says volunteers need not attend every month and that setup/takedown are additional. [St Kilda](https://www.stkildarepaircafe.org.au/get-involved/)
- **Travelling events:** Repair Café International documents Repair Café Silicon Valley's use of shifting venue partners to attract visitors and repairers. [Repair Café International, May 2026](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/pop-up-repair-cafes-in-unusual-venues-from-museums-to-universities/)

## Proposed Dubbo pilot: three sessions

All aspects below are **proposals**, not evidence that any organiser agreed.

1. **Contact Dubbo Farmers Market** about community-stall eligibility, location/food separation, demonstration vs active repairs, dates, hours, stall fee, electricity, signage, rain/heat, insurance certificates, site emergency procedures, waste, and public liability / product liability implications. Request written approval for the precise activities.
2. **Identify a host/legal entity** responsible for the event, public liability and volunteer cover; seek insurer/broker advice specifically describing the repair activities, tools, portable electrical goods and market site. The market's general requirements are not proof that repair work is insured.
3. **Pilot format A (within market):** one staffed enquiry/intake/demo desk plus one or two low-risk repair stations, **only if approved**. Prefer hand mending, simple toy repair and pre-screened basic bicycle adjustments where space and safety allow. No automatic promise to accept mains-powered devices or damaged lithium batteries; no soldering/open flames/power tools without explicit assessed controls.
4. **Pilot format B (adjacent):** a small market sign-up and demonstration presence directing people to a separately approved sheltered repair space nearby (similar in principle to Woodend). This is especially useful for quieter repair work, electricity, weather protection and food-market separation.
5. **Public offering:** free, owner stays with item, diagnosis is not guaranteed repair. Optional donation only if approved and handled with financial controls. Clear last-intake time (e.g. 30–45 min before close), numbered queue, one portable item per visitor initially, and referral for unsuitable/high-risk items.
6. **Staffing target to validate:** event coordinator/safety lead, welcome/intake volunteer, at least two suitably skilled repairers (dependent on categories), floating helper/relief; no assumption all roles can be fulfilled by the same person.
7. **Capacity target:** start around 8–12 repair enquiries/items across three hours **only as a planning estimate**, then adjust for actual time/repair type. Do not advertise a guaranteed number.
8. **Schedule:** test market opening hours and volunteer setup/packdown separately; market 8am–12pm is not interchangeable with the earlier Repair Café 10am–1pm model. Confirm organiser's operating windows.
9. **Success measures:** visitors engaged; accepted/refused items and reasons; safe completed/partially completed/diagnosed/referred counts; volunteer attendance and workload; host and fixer feedback; volunteers recruited; interest in future repair dates; costs and incidents/near misses. Record no unnecessary public personal data.
10. **Pilot decision:** after three sessions decide whether to repeat in-market, move nearby with shared promotion, or run a standalone monthly café. Treat a market information stall as valid phase zero even when full repairs cannot yet be approved.

## How to implement in existing app

Use the existing Repair Café programme, **not a new app, new login or public E-waste intake**.

- Add venue classification on Repair Café events: `market_stall`, `market_adjacent`, `standalone`.
- Market coordinator permission and host/market correspondence go in the private **Venues & offers** workflow; add proof of stallholder agreement, exact permitted activity classes and expiration, stall dimensions, power, shared emergency plan.
- Allow a parent `market_event` reference / external URL / named partner on the event, but **do not imply the host committed** from a public link.
- In volunteer scheduling, separate `setup`, `welcome`, `repair`, `packdown` slots and skill categories. Add conditional shade/heat management and an optional role for market outreach; availability is never assignment.
- Visitor intake, repair outcomes and waiting queue belong to the Repair Café session, not E-waste inventory or Library-of-Things loans.
- Public event panel should advertise only an approved/published session and say **at the market**, **near the market**, or **standalone** accurately. Confirmed venue and verified opening hours required; include a last-accepted-item time and repair exclusions.
- Metrics are per session so organisers can compare model A vs B vs standalone.
- Link relevant safety, volunteer induction, visitor intake and outreach modules from the existing Learning Hub; do not use the LMS as an event roster.
- Ensure mobile layout, scoped row-level permissions and coordinator-only host contact details. No auto-publication or unapproved volunteer assignments.

## Contacts/questions for evidence-gathering

**Dubbo Farmers Market:** confirm whether a volunteer-run free repair station is eligible as a community/guest stall and whether a recurring or occasional trial is possible. Ask about stall charges and waivers, 3m×3m gazebo constraints, power, safety, set-up time, third-Saturday space and weather contingencies. Do not treat an enquiry as a booking.

**Devonport Repair Café:** How many repairers attend? Is attendance a stall or separately sheltered area? Which items are accepted? What are insurer and market requirements? How are advance texts, queues and spare parts managed?

**Woodend Repair Café/Neighbourhood House:** Why locate in the undercroft rather than market proper? Does the market cross-promote? Which entity insures repair activities, and who schedules volunteer shifts?

**Hawker Men's Shed:** Who approves activity scopes, screens repair items and coordinates 25+ market stalls alongside fixers? Does market income fund repair operations?

## Decision register

- **Proposed:** Explore farmers-market participation rather than assuming third Saturday is a clash.

- **To choose after enquiry:** on-market vs adjacent vs standalone; frequency; public times; visitor booking/queue vs walk-ins.
