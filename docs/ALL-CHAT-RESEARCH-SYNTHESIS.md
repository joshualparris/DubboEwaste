# All-chat research synthesis — Dubbo eWaste

**Compiled:** 2 October 2026  
**Purpose:** capture the full Dubbo eWaste story built across multiple ChatGPT conversations and repo research so future work does not have to reconstruct the project from scratch.

This is a synthesis and index, not a replacement for the detailed source files.

> **Project thesis:** Dubbo eWaste should begin as a **reuse-first electronics collection, refurbishment and resale service**, not as an industrial recycling plant or general dumping point.

> **Core principle:** **Reuse first. Recycle second.**

The public repository deliberately does **not** contain the residential street address used in private feasibility discussions.

---

# 1. What problem Dubbo eWaste is trying to solve

Dubbo already has multiple ways to **dispose of** e-waste:

- Council facilities
- Officeworks/retailer take-back
- scrap/recycling businesses
- NTCRS collection channels
- MobileMuster for phones
- regional NetWaste arrangements.

The opportunity is therefore not:

> another place to dump electronics.

The opportunity is:

> **intercept useful devices before material recycling, securely handle the data, test/repair/refurbish them, put viable equipment back into use, recover parts where appropriate, and send only genuine end-of-life material downstream.**

This is closer to a small regional **ITAD / refurbishment / reuse business** than a traditional waste processor.

# 2. Current Dubbo household e-waste pathway

## Council

Dubbo Regional Council accepts household e-waste at Whylandra and other facilities under its current waste services.

Historical public documents show:

**Council facility → Matthews Metal Management in Dubbo → downstream recycling**

Council's 2025–2035 Waste Strategy listed:

- Matthews Metals
- Collection of E-Waste
- commencement 30 June 2023
- expiry 30 June 2025.

The exact post-30-June-2025 Council contractor remains unresolved.

## Current Mountbatten Drive operator

The former Matthews Dubbo yard is now operated publicly as:

**Australian Metal Recycling (AMR)**  
legal entity: Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd.

Current NSW Police scrap-metal registration and AMR's own site identify the Dubbo operation.

AMR says Dubbo is:

> Formerly Matthews Metal Management.

The corporate transition occurred between July 2025 and February 2026 based on NSW Police public-register evidence.

## What AMR takes

AMR publicly advertises free electrical/e-waste recycling and lists broad categories such as:

- TVs
- computers/laptops
- monitors
- phones/tablets
- printers/scanners.

But its lithium wording creates a major practical ambiguity for modern phones/laptops/tablets with embedded batteries.

The exact rule for:
- internal laptop batteries
- phones/tablets
- damaged TVs
- CRTs
- business volumes
- certificates

still needs direct confirmation.

## Exact downstream destination

The single biggest unresolved Dubbo physical-flow question remains:

> **Where does AMR Dubbo's e-waste physically go next?**

No public source currently names the first specialist processor.

Do not assume:
- Sircel
- Sims
- TechCollect
- ACE
- another recycler

without primary evidence.

# 3. Officeworks — why it matters

Officeworks is relevant because it is already a **consumer-facing alternative e-waste intake route**.

Its Bring it Back program currently accepts many technology products including:

- computers/laptops
- phones/smartwatches
- monitors
- printers/scanners
- keyboards/mice
- motherboards/PCBs
- storage media
- cables/chargers
- selected other tech.

Officeworks also operates a **trade-in** path for viable devices.

Its own circularity model is therefore:

**good device → trade-in/reuse**  
**non-viable device → recycling**

That is relevant competition/validation for Dubbo eWaste.

However Dubbo eWaste can differentiate through:

- pickup
- local refurbishment
- transparent testing
- local resale
- asset reporting
- business clear-outs
- data sanitisation
- repair/parts recovery
- traceability.

Officeworks does **not** currently accept loose batteries through Bring it Back.

Source:  
https://www.officeworks.com.au/information/about-us/recycling

# 6. TechCollect / ANZRP — a critical distinction

TechCollect is important but should not be confused with a local refurbishment operation.

Research found that equipment already surrendered into the TechCollect recycling stream is intended for **recycling**, not second-hand resale.

TechCollect tells owners of still-usable equipment to seek reuse/donation **before** surrendering it.

ANZRP nevertheless supports reuse upstream through partners such as:

- PonyUp
- Reconnect Project
- Laptop Initiative.

This gives a clean two-stage model:

**reuse decision first → recycling stream second**

That aligns strongly with the proposed Dubbo eWaste model.

[TECHCOLLECT-REUSE-DEEP-DIVE.md](TECHCOLLECT-REUSE-DEEP-DIVE.md)

# 7. Good360 / Compnow / WorkVentures / Device Banks

Multiple chats explored what happens after electronics enter social-reuse programs.

## Compnow

Compnow provides fleet/technology lifecycle services and refurbishment capability.

The relevant lesson is:
- enterprise-grade equipment can be processed systematically
- data is handled
- hardware is assessed
- viable devices can be returned to use rather than destroyed.

## Good360

Good360 is primarily a distribution platform/network for donated goods through eligible charities and community organisations.

It is not simply “an e-waste recycler”.

## National Device Bank

The National Device Bank ecosystem has involved organisations including:

- WorkVentures
- Good360
- Good Things Australia.

Its significance is that surplus technology can be:

**collected → securely wiped → refurbished → redistributed through community organisations**

rather than only sold or recycled.

## NSW Device Bank

NSW launched a separate Device Bank pilot in 2026.

The tender disclosed approximately:

- 2,800 laptops already available initially
- about 2,500 further laptops expected during 12 months
- mostly HP x360 1030 G3/G4, with some G7/G8
- many around five years old or older.

WorkVentures is the delivery partner.

The Device Bank is an especially important policy precedent:

> NSW Government itself now treats secure refurbishment/reuse as a legitimate destination for old government laptops.

## Dubbo connections

Research has identified Good Things/community digital-inclusion organisations in Dubbo, including local grant/program activity.

What remains unverified is a current published device-flow saying:

**Device Bank → named Dubbo recipient → devices distributed locally**

# 9. NSW public schools — separate from Council e-waste

One of the most important recent findings is that NSW public-school laptops do **not** simply enter Dubbo's Council household e-waste route.

The Department has a **dedicated eWaste contract**.

Current school workflow:

1. school gets disposal approval
2. inventories equipment
3. books through the Department eWaste application / EDConnect
4. contracted vendor builds a cost-efficient collection schedule
5. vendor collects
6. vendor sanitises all collected hard drives
7. vendor provides a sanitisation certificate
8. vendor provides a **Settlement Report**.

The operational guide says school-side hard-drive wiping/destruction is **no longer required** under that collection process.

The current vendor is **not named publicly** in the sources found.

The downstream outcome after settlement is also not public.

Therefore it remains unknown whether a Dubbo public-school laptop is:

- refurbished
- resold
- donated
- put into parts recovery
- recycled
- physically destroyed.

Whole-of-government Contract 9826 permits:
- reuse by donation/sale
- supplier supply-chain recovery
- third-party recycling

but that does not prove the dedicated Education contract uses each option.

See:
[NSW-EDUCATION-EWASTE-DUBBO-LAPTOPS.md](NSW-EDUCATION-EWASTE-DUBBO-LAPTOPS.md)

# 13. Phase 0 operating model agreed in the research

Proposed pilot:

- residential shed/workspace
- **Tuesday + Saturday**
- pre-approval required
- selective electronics only
- no unattended dumping
- no public battery program
- no CRTs
- TVs by prior approval only
- online resale
- small storage cap
- clear data chain
- reuse first
- residual recycling only after downstream route is known.

Priority intake:
- business-class laptops
- quality PCs/mini PCs
- unlocked phones/tablets
- monitors
- consoles
- selected useful electronics
- good accessories/components.

Reject initially:
- general rubbish
- random mixed low-value loads
- loose lithium batteries
- swollen/damaged/wet/fire-affected battery devices
- CRTs
- activation-locked devices
- unmanaged unknown institutional assets
- items with no plausible exit path.

# 14. Data handling model

Every data-bearing device should have:

1. intake ID
2. ownership/authority record
3. serial/IMEI
4. restricted UNWIPED state
5. media-appropriate sanitisation
6. verification
7. sanitisation record/certificate
8. release into repair/resale/parts workflow only after sanitisation.

Current repo work incorporates:
- NIST SP 800-88 Rev.2
- ASD/ACSC guidance
- HDD versus SSD/NVMe differences
- phone/tablet account/lock issues.

Avoid overclaiming:

> **documented secure data erasure**

is appropriate for the pilot.

Do not claim:

> certified data destruction

unless the actual process/accreditation supports that wording.

[BACKLOG-21-25-COMPLIANCE-DATA-SAFETY.md](BACKLOG-21-25-COMPLIANCE-DATA-SAFETY.md)

# 15. Windows / device viability findings

## Windows

Windows 10 normal support ended in October 2025.

For ordinary resale in late 2026:
- Windows 11-compatible PCs are a strong default target
- older devices require a clear Linux/vintage/parts/special-purpose use case.

## Phones

Post-3G Australian network compatibility matters.

A phone powering on is not enough to prove it remains usable for normal Australian calling/emergency requirements.

## Apple / Android locks

Do not build a business around bypassing:
- Apple Activation Lock
- Google FRP
- MDM/Autopilot
- firmware passwords.

Require legitimate removal/transfer.

# 16. Planning and land-use findings

Current public property evidence strongly points to an **R2 Low Density Residential** planning context for the proposed home operation, but the authoritative property-specific confirmation should still come from:

- NSW Spatial Viewer
- or a Council s10.7 certificate.

If R2 is confirmed:
- home business/home industry are listed consent uses
- goods repair/reuse and waste/resource facility classifications are not obvious listed principal uses.

A qualifying home business may still be exempt development under the Codes SEPP if every condition is met.

Key Phase 0 design choices help keep the activity low-impact:

- <30 m² business area
- resident operator
- minimal traffic
- no outside stock
- no general drop-off
- no walk-in shop
- online sales
- no nuisance.

The exact Council classification remains a **launch gate**.

# 17. NSW waste-law finding

A major misconception was corrected during research:

> Something can still legally be “waste” even if the intention is to reuse, refurbish, sell or recycle it.

Therefore:

- “donated”
- “free”
- “I will repair it”
- “it has resale value”

does not automatically remove waste-law considerations.

EPA licensing thresholds are far above a tiny pilot, but:

> being below an EPA licence threshold does **not** automatically make the land use lawful.

Planning classification is still the bigger Phase 0 issue.

# 18. Second-hand dealer law

NSW Fair Trading currently says a licence is generally required for prescribed second-hand electronic goods.

Current new-licence fee:

- 1 year: **$692**
- 3 years: **$1,574**
- 5 years: **$2,456**.

But Fair Trading also says no licence is needed to:

> engage in recycling and rubbish collection programs.

The exact unresolved question is whether:

**free collection → triage → refurbishment/resale → recycle remainder**

qualifies for that carve-out.

If a licence applies, there are significant process implications:
- supplier identification
- ownership statement
- electronic records
- Police reporting
- premises notification
- holding period
- no alteration during the holding period.

Do **not** pay the $692 until the exact model is clarified.

# 19. Scrap-metal law

NSW scrap-metal law does **not** make every metal-containing laptop “scrap”.

The object must be:

> sold or obtained as scrap for recycling/reprocessing of the metal.

That supports positioning Phase 0 around:

- electronics reuse
- repair
- refurbishment
- donor parts

rather than buying/collecting devices **as metal scrap**.

If regular commodity-metal sales become a business stream, revisit scrap registration.

Current registration fee researched:

**$260 / 3 years**

if required.

# 20. Battery strategy

Battery risk became one of the strongest reasons to keep Phase 0 selective.

Initial rules:

- no public battery collection
- no loose lithium battery intake
- reject visibly swollen/damaged/wet/fire-affected battery devices
- no unattended charging
- identify specialist damaged-battery downstream before opening.

B-cycle is not simply “get a free public bin”.

Becoming a drop point involves:
- accredited collector
- risk assessment
- safety plan
- suitable collection container/process.

Badly damaged/swollen batteries are not ordinary drop-point material.

# 22. Current startup cost philosophy

The project shifted from “maybe $20k” to:

> **spend almost nothing until the regulatory and downstream gates are resolved.**

Potential verified/known government costs:

- ABN: $0
- business name: current low-cost registration
- second-hand dealer licence: $692/yr **if required**
- scrap-metal registration: $260/3 yrs **if required**.

Big unknowns:
- insurance
- any Council approval
- premises remediation
- downstream exceptions
- safety/testing equipment.

Warehouse:
- **do not lease initially**.

Vehicle:
- **do not buy a dedicated truck initially**.

Stock:
- initial supply should primarily come from accepted/donated equipment, not speculative bulk purchases.

# 26. The current strategic picture

The research now points toward three potential Dubbo eWaste stages.

## Stage 1 — home pilot

Selective:
- households
- SMEs
- quality devices
- tiny inventory
- reuse/resale
- documented erasure.

Goal:
- prove economics.

## Stage 2 — local ITAD service

Offer businesses:
- collection
- asset list
- chain of custody
- data erasure
- reuse/refurbishment
- disposal certificate.

Goal:
- recurring commercial supply.

## Stage 3 — institutional / regional reuse

Potential relationships:
- councils
- NetWaste
- existing recyclers
- NSW Government suppliers
- schools under approved procurement
- social-device programs
- Device Bank/community organisations.

Goal:
- divert viable regional devices **before** shredding/material recovery.

The project does **not** need to become an industrial shredding plant for this to be valuable.

# Bottom line

Across all the research, the strongest version of Dubbo eWaste is now:

> **a small, selective, reuse-first regional electronics service that captures value before the traditional recycling stream, treats data handling professionally, measures every device, and only scales once the legal classification and real unit economics are proven.**

The largest strategic opportunity is not “more e-waste disposal”.

It is:

> **finding the good laptops, phones, tablets and PCs that are currently disappearing into large institutional/recycling systems, and creating a compliant local pathway that keeps the viable ones in use.**
