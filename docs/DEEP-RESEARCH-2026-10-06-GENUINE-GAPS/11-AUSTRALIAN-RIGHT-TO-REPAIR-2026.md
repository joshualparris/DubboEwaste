# 11 — Australian right to repair for electronics, October 2026

**Question answered:** 31  
**Research date:** 6 October 2026

## Current position

Australia does **not** currently have a broad consumer-electronics repair-information scheme equivalent to the motor-vehicle right-to-repair law.

Treasury describes the Motor Vehicle Information Sharing Scheme as **Australia's first right-to-repair law**. It commenced in 2022 and was reviewed in 2025–26.

Sources:
- https://treasury.gov.au/review/review-motor-vehicle-information-sharing-scheme
- https://treasury.gov.au/publication/p2026-740225

In 2026 Treasury consulted on improvements to that **motor vehicle** scheme. That consultation is not a general electronics right-to-repair regime.

## What happened to the broader Productivity Commission inquiry?

The Productivity Commission's 2021 Right to Repair report found significant/unnecessary barriers in some repair markets and recommended a package including:

- stronger consumer-guarantee enforcement;
- a software-update guarantee;
- repairability/durability information;
- copyright/IP changes supporting legitimate repair;
- consideration of repair access across product classes;
- better product stewardship/reuse outcomes.

Source:
https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/repair/report/

As of this research, no current Commonwealth source was found showing those recommendations have become a universal mandatory electronics parts/tools/manuals regime.

## Rights that already exist under ACL

Current ACCC guidance says manufacturers/importers must provide **repair facilities and spare parts for a reasonable time after purchase**, unless the consumer was told at purchase that they would not be available after a specified time.

Source:
https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/consumer-rights-and-guarantees

This is important, but it is not equivalent to a US/EU-style right requiring every OEM to sell every diagnostic tool, board schematic, calibration key or replacement component to independent repairers.

## Repair notices remain directly relevant

A business accepting a user-data-storing product for repair must warn that repair may result in data loss.

If goods may be repaired using refurbished parts or replaced with refurbished goods, specified repair-notice wording applies.

Source:
https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-notices

The existing repo already documents the notice; the new finding here is its place in the broader right-to-repair landscape.

## What this means for Dubbo ITAD / Repair Café

### You can

- use publicly available manufacturer service manuals;
- use legitimate replacement parts;
- perform owner-authorised repairs within competence/safety scope;
- teach modular repair;
- use manufacturer self-service programs where offered;
- make warranty/ACL-compliant disclosures.

### You cannot assume

- OEM must provide motherboard schematics;
- OEM must sell proprietary calibration tools;
- locked/MDM devices can legally/technically be bypassed;
- every part-pairing restriction has a statutory workaround;
- overseas right-to-repair rules apply in Australia.

## Strategic opportunity

The lack of a broad statutory electronics scheme makes these assets more valuable:

1. exact service manual library;
2. FRU/part-number lookup;
3. repairability score by device family;
4. supplier/provenance register;
5. known paired/calibrated components;
6. repair time/part availability history.

AssetFlow can gradually produce a **regional repairability knowledge base** from real jobs.

Suggested fields:

- official manual available;
- part lookup available;
- battery replaceability;
- storage replaceability;
- RAM replaceability;
- display availability;
- paired/calibrated component flags;
- OEM/aftermarket parts source;
- average parts lead time;
- average repair minutes;
- support/security end date.

## Watch list

Review every 6 months:

- Treasury right-to-repair policy;
- ACCC consumer-guarantee changes;
- Productivity Commission follow-up;
- Commonwealth copyright repair exceptions;
- product-stewardship reform;
- EU repairability/ecodesign rules that influence devices sold globally;
- OEM self-service repair expansion.

## Bottom line

In October 2026, Australia's strongest general electronics repair rights remain **consumer guarantees and ordinary lawful repair**, not a comprehensive statutory independent-repair access regime.

For Dubbo, this makes practical access to documentation, parts and legitimate ownership/lock release a competitive capability rather than something regulation guarantees.
