# Regional e-waste pathways around Dubbo

**Checked 2 October 2026.**

This note maps what currently happens to household e-waste in the towns and council areas around Dubbo, with a focus on practical implications for a reuse-first Dubbo e-waste/refurb operation.

Confidence key:

- **[Found]** current official council, NetWaste or operator source.
- **[Historical]** official source, but the contract/process may have changed.
- **[Secondary]** useful current third-party summary that cites council material, but should be confirmed directly.
- **[Unknown]** not established from current public sources.

## Executive summary

The surrounding region already has a surprisingly broad **free or low-cost council e-waste disposal network**. The dominant model is:

1. resident sorts e-waste from general rubbish;
2. resident drops it at a council waste facility, transfer station or periodic bulky-waste service;
3. the council accumulates it separately;
4. a regional or council-specific recycler collects it;
5. equipment is dismantled/recycled, with some reuse where the contractor or facility has a reuse pathway.

The strongest current regional source is **NetWaste**, the 25-council Central and Western NSW waste group. NetWaste says **Sims Metal Management collects e-waste from the majority of NetWaste councils**, while several councils have individual arrangements. Its current e-waste map lists Dubbo, Wellington, Narromine, Parkes, Forbes/Daroobalgie, Warren/Ewenmar, Mudgee, Gulgong and Kandos among collection points.

That means a Dubbo business should **not compete primarily on “we take e-waste”**. Council already does that for many households. The gap is earlier in the hierarchy: **identify reusable equipment before it becomes bulk e-waste, securely wipe it, refurbish it, resell/donate it, and send only the genuine end-of-life remainder to downstream recyclers.**

There is one major limitation in the public data: it is easy to establish where residents can drop e-waste, but much harder to establish the **exact current downstream processor for each council and the exact final material destinations**. NetWaste's current page establishes Sims as the majority collector, but not every council-specific route.

## What happens after council drop-off?

### 1. NetWaste is the main regional coordination layer

NetWaste is a voluntary regional waste group covering 25 Central and Western NSW councils, including Dubbo Regional, Narromine, Gilgandra, Bogan, Coonamble, Warren, Parkes, Forbes and Mid-Western.

Its current e-waste page states:

> Sims Metal Management collect eWaste from the majority of NetWaste Councils; several councils have individual arrangements.

That is the best current public description of the regional e-waste collection model.

**Do not overstate it:** this does **not** prove that every town above currently sends its e-waste to Sims. Some have separate arrangements, and several council pages do not name their contractor.

### 2. Sims' stated model is reuse + end-of-life recycling

Sims E-Recycling says it provides both **reuse and recycling** of redundant computers, TVs, phones and other electronics, including IT asset disposition. This supports a regional flow where at least some usable assets may be reused and the remainder dismantled/recycled.

The public Sims material does not identify the final destination of each material fraction collected from each Central West council.

### 3. Historical St Marys route exists, but should not be treated as current

NetWaste's 2023–2027 strategy recorded an earlier ancillary e-waste option under the regional scrap-metal contract and said e-waste was **shredded at the St Marys depot**, with a further recovery pilot involving E-cycle Solutions. That contract information had expiry dates around 2023 and is therefore **historical evidence only**.

The current NetWaste page still names Sims as the majority collector, but it does not say that the old St Marys process remains unchanged.

## What this means for Dubbo E-Waste

### The actual regional gap is reuse-before-recycling

Most councils already solve the **disposal** problem:

- residents can often drop e-waste free;
- councils segregate it;
- regional contractors remove it;
- material is recycled rather than put straight into landfill.

What is much less visible is a consistent **pre-disposal reuse triage** system for:

- working business laptops;
- repairable laptops/desktops;
- phones and tablets that can be unlocked/wiped;
- working monitors;
- modern flat-screen TVs;
- RAM, SSDs, chargers and other valuable components.

Mudgee's Tip Shop is the clearest nearby example where Council explicitly directs e-waste into a reuse-shop environment. Most other public pathways are framed primarily as waste/recycling services.

### Do not build the business around household disposal fees

Because many councils already offer free household e-waste drop-off, charging households just to take ordinary e-waste is unlikely to be a strong proposition.

A stronger pitch is:

- **free or convenient pickup of good-quality equipment**;
- secure, documented data wiping;
- asset register / serial-number reporting for businesses and schools;
- refurbish and resale where viable;
- donation/redeployment options;
- revenue share or buy-back for higher-value equipment where appropriate;
- certified downstream recycling for the non-reusable remainder.

### Council partnerships could be valuable, but deposited waste is not free stock

A council facility may already have contractor, ownership, WHS and site-access rules around deposited material. The business should **not assume it can collect or scavenge devices after residents drop them at a waste facility**.

The better partnership models to ask councils about are:

1. a signed **reuse-first diversion arrangement before material enters the e-waste cage**;
2. a scheduled collection day where staff direct suitable items to a separate reuse stream;
3. business/school referrals to Dubbo E-Waste before assets are taken to the tip;
4. a trial where Dubbo E-Waste reports how many devices were reused versus sent to recycling.

### Priority councils to contact

For a low-cost pilot, the strongest first calls are:

1. **Dubbo Regional Council** – largest local stream; Whylandra + Wellington + rural sites.
2. **Narromine Shire Council** – confirmed free domestic e-waste and close regional fit.
3. **Gilgandra Shire Council** – current public e-waste pathway is unusually unclear, which may reveal a service gap.
4. **Coonamble Shire Council** – confirmed free sorted e-waste, but downstream route is not transparent.
5. **Warren Shire Council** – current NetWaste e-waste point at Ewenmar.
6. **Bogan Shire Council** – verify Nyngan's current e-waste rules and contractor because public sources are inconsistent.
7. **Parkes Shire Council** – mature collection system; useful comparison and possible reuse diversion partnership.
8. **Mid-Western Regional Council** – ask how its Mudgee Tip Shop triages e-waste for reuse and what happens to unsold electronics.

## Questions to ask every surrounding council

Use the same short question set so answers can be compared:

1. Do you currently accept **laptops, desktops, monitors, phones, tablets, printers and TVs**?
2. Is it free for residents? What about **business/commercial loads**?
3. Which locations accept e-waste permanently?
4. Who is the **current downstream contractor**?
5. Is any equipment checked for **reuse/refurbishment before recycling**?
6. Can council legally divert suitable devices to an approved local refurbisher before they enter the recycling contract?
7. Who owns the equipment after it is dropped off?
8. Are there existing exclusivity clauses in the recycler contract?
9. Are lithium-battery devices accepted intact?
10. Can Council provide annual e-waste tonnage or item counts?
11. Would Council consider a small **reuse-first pilot** with serial-number tracking and diversion reporting?

## Deep downstream update — 2 October 2026

Further research found several **named physical routes**, improving on the earlier "contractor unknown" picture.

- **Parkes -> Sircel:** Parkes Shire's 2024-25 progress report says e-waste from Parkes Landfill was collected by Sircel on a circular arrangement. The 2025-26 progress report records continued investigation/delivery of an e-waste shared agreement with Sircel. Sircel operates at **55 Brolgan Road, Parkes**.
- **What Sircel does:** loads are weighed/tracked, triaged for reuse, batteries and incompatible material separated, reusable IT assets routed through ITAD/data sanitisation, and end-of-life equipment mechanically reduced/separated down to <1 mm fractions for commodity recovery. Steel, aluminium, copper and other fractions return to manufacturing/recovery partners.
- **Coonamble -> Matthews Dubbo:** Coonamble's February 2025 Council paper says Matthews Metals Dubbo agreed to work with Coonamble Waste for collection/disposal of e-waste and batteries. The 2026 continuation of this exact arrangement is not publicly proven.
- **NetWaste majority -> Sims:** NetWaste's current page still says Sims Metal Management collects e-waste from the majority of member councils. Its older 2023-27 strategy explicitly described the then e-waste option as being **shredded at Sims' St Marys depot**, with an E-cycle Solutions recovery trial. That St Marys detail is historical, not confirmed as the 2026 route.
- **Dubbo remains unresolved:** DRC's historical Matthews contract ended 30 June 2025. The current public Council pages do not identify the 2026 processor, so it is not safe to assume Whylandra material goes to AMR, Sims or Sircel without confirmation.
- **Separate scrap-metal change:** the late-2025 NetWaste scrap-metal tender F4459 was awarded to Access Recycling by participating councils. This does not prove Access took over e-waste; NetWaste still names Sims on its current e-waste page.

For the detailed device-to-material flow, data handling, NTCRS rules and real recovery numbers, see [WHAT-HAPPENS-TO-EWASTE.md](WHAT-HAPPENS-TO-EWASTE.md).
