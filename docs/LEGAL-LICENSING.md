# Legal and licensing detail

## 1. Second-hand dealer licence (NSW Fair Trading)  [VERIFIED, page updated 1 Sep 2026]

**Law:** Pawnbrokers and Second-hand Dealers Act 1996; Regulation 2026 (replaced the 2021 Regulation; clause numbers may differ from older sources).

**Who needs one:** anyone running a business that buys, sells or exchanges prescribed second-hand goods. Prescribed goods include electronic goods, jewellery/precious metals, musical instruments, sporting goods, towable watercraft, portable powered tools, car accessories.

**Not needing one:** items not designed to be portable; video games/computer games; motorised mobility aids; whitegoods (microwaves, fridges, washing machines); anyone operating under another authority (e.g. motor dealers); fundraising appeals; auctioneers; and anyone who will **"engage in recycling and rubbish collection programs"**.
*Note: the Fair Trading page lists video games as exempt; whether game consoles are covered should be confirmed.*

**Fees (2026-27, VERIFIED):**
| Term | New licence | Renewal | Restore |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 year | $692 | $500 ($494 online) | $562 |
| 3 years | $1,574 | $1,382 ($1,376 online) | $1,444 |
| 5 years | $2,456 | $2,264 ($2,258 online) | $2,326 |
Processing fee ($251 new) is non-refundable. Fees may rise with CPI each 1 July.

**Eligibility:** 18+; fit and proper person (financial and police checks); not disqualified. For a corporation: the corporation and **each director** must be fit and proper. Assessment aims for 30 days; **you can't trade prescribed goods while the application is pending.**

**Applying:** Application form via Service NSW centre or businesslicensing@customerservice.nsw.gov.au. Questions: **13 32 20**.

**Obligations once licensed:**
- Notify Fair Trading of all NSW premises used for business and for storing goods.
- Display licence name/number/category; "information may be furnished to police" sign; "right to claim goods" sign where the public has access.
- Check supplier identity and get a signed **ownership statement** with a detailed description.
- Keep electronic records (approved software) for 3 years; send records of prescribed goods to NSW Police within 3 days.
- Record buyer name/address for sales of $100 or more; record every sale by close of business that day.
- **Hold regulated goods for 14 days; goods cannot be altered until the hold ends**, and must stay on notified premises. (Matters a lot for a refurb business; ask Fair Trading how wiping/repair fits.)
- Display contract/stock numbers on goods.
- Police can require you to hold suspected stolen goods for 56 days (extendable).
- Record phone IMEI and WLAN MAC addresses (esp. for pawn records; see Fair Trading MAC guide).
- 12 or more demerit points in 3 years can lead to a show-cause notice for revocation.

**Exemptions:** An applicant can apply to Fair Trading for an exemption from specific provisions: licensing.exemptions@customerservice.nsw.gov.au (form on the Fair Trading page).

**Recycling-program carve-out:** The earlier 2021 Regulation said the Act doesn't apply to acts connected with conducting a recycling or rubbish collection program, selling goods collected in one, or contracting to give someone ownership of goods collected in one. Whether a private refurb operation is a "program" is not defined in anything I found. **Get Fair Trading's answer in writing.**

## 2. Licence-sharing with an employer or related business
- A licence is held by an individual, partnership or corporation, not a trading name. For "Dubbo eWaste" to be covered, it must be operated **by** the licensed entity, with the shed notified as premises.
- Unverified: whether Avance, or any related business, holds a licence.
- Risks to discuss with that business: its licence/demerit exposure, insurance, record-keeping duties across a second site, and conflict of interest.

## 3. Council planning
- Home occupation / home business / home industry are defined in the NSW Standard Instrument. A home business can't involve **retail sale or display of goods not produced at the dwelling, other than online**, nor interfere with amenity (noise, waste products, traffic generation), exceed signage and floor-area limits, or employ beyond the allowed number of non-residents.
- Public open days with walk-in drop-offs and sales likely exceed this. A development application for another land use may be required.
- Zoning of the property: **unconfirmed.** Nearby Boundary Road land includes R5, R2 and R1 with active planning proposals.
- Section 143 of the POEO Act (sending waste to a place that can't lawfully take it) also depends on the site being lawful under planning.

## 4. EPA / waste law
- Storage thresholds (outside the regulated Sydney/Newcastle/Wollongong area): 2,500 t on site or 12,000 t/yr. Shed scale is far below.
- Hazardous waste storage: licence triggered above 5 tonnes (cl 42(3)(a), amended 2023). Unknown whether damaged lithium batteries count; keep them minimal anyway.
- NSW e-Waste Bill 2025 (would have licensed >100 t/yr of e-waste): **defeated 18 Sep 2025, not law.**
- Not confirmed: thresholds for waste *processing/recovery* (dismantling). Ask EPA (131 555).

- **MobileMuster** for phones; **B-cycle** and council CRC for batteries.
