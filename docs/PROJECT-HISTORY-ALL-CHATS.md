**PRIOR-CHAT / ARCHIVED PROJECT CONTEXT**

A December 2024 Bendigo outreach request sought **five low-cost or donated laptops/desktops** for reuse rather than disposal.

# 1. Origin of the reuse idea — Bendigo, 2025

## September 2025: the project existed before "Dubbo E-Waste"

**USER-REPORTED / PRIOR-CHAT**

In September 2025, Josh was already exploring a model built around:

- recovering discarded computers/electronics;
- testing and repairing what still had value;
- refurbishing/reselling usable devices;
- recovering parts;
- using material recycling only for genuine end-of-life equipment;
- potentially offering collection and data-destruction services;
- approaching councils, businesses and schools as supply sources;
- starting lean rather than attempting to build a processing plant.

The initial context was **Bendigo**, not Dubbo.

This matters because the current project did not begin as an abstract "waste business". It grew from hands-on experience with old computers and reuse.

## 4 September 2025: 10 laptops from Bendigo E-Waste

**USER-REPORTED**

Josh reported receiving **10 donated laptops from Bendigo E-Waste**.

At intake:

- no chargers were supplied;
- exact working condition was unknown;
- the laptops looked substantially intact;
- there was little obvious external damage.

This is a useful real-world proof point:

> electronics entering an e-waste/recycling context can still include intact equipment worth testing before destruction.

It is not enough to estimate a reusable percentage for the wider waste stream, but it directly informed Josh's later reuse-first thinking.

## DadLAN as practical reuse experience

**USER-REPORTED / PROJECT CONTEXT**

DadLAN later became a larger hands-on environment for:

- old and inexpensive PCs;
- SSD/RAM upgrades;
- Windows installs/imaging;
- hardware comparison;
- networking;
- compatibility testing;
- gaming;
- keeping ageing machines useful.

An earlier Bendigo/DadLAN discussion recorded that some apparently "dead" or unwanted computers can turn out to have recoverable problems such as forgotten passwords, minor BIOS/configuration issues, missing storage/chargers, or straightforward component faults.

# 3. Move from Bendigo concept to Dubbo

By October 2026 the project had changed from:

> "Can discarded computers be recovered and resold?"

to:

> "Can a small Dubbo reuse/refurbishment service intercept worthwhile electronics before they become material recycling?"

The proposed Phase 0 model became deliberately narrow.

## Core Phase 0 operating decisions

**CURRENT REPO / PRIOR-CHAT CONSENSUS**

- operate on **Tuesdays and Saturdays**;
- use **pre-approved intake**, not uncontrolled drop-off;
- keep the model **reuse/refurbishment first**;
- online sale first rather than a home retail showroom;
- no unattended dumping;
- no "leave it at the gate";
- no outdoor stockpile;
- no public battery collection;
- reject damaged/swollen/wet/fire-affected lithium devices;
- TVs only by prior approval;
- no CRTs in Phase 0;
- every accepted item gets an asset/intake ID;
- every data-bearing item begins as **UNWIPED — RESTRICTED**;
- ownership/authority to transfer should be recorded;
- shelf full = pause intake;
- only accept items with a known reuse/parts/recycling exit;
- pilot only **20–30 devices** before scaling.

# 4. Lease/home-site findings discussed in chat

**CURRENT REPO / PRIOR-CHAT**

The residential tenancy agreement review found:

- no broad clause saying "no business";
- no generic "residential use only" or "no commercial activity" prohibition;
- standard nuisance/illegal-purpose/neighbour-peace/damage clauses;
- an important garage/car-space special condition saying that space is for **parking a motor vehicle**, not storage of personal goods/belongings;
- a separate storage-area clause that disclaims fitness/security/watertightness rather than creating a general business ban;
- the rear structure has appeared in property material as a **garage/shed**, making its exact lease classification relevant;
- existing condition evidence raised moisture/mould concerns.

### Operating implication

The legal/planning question and the lease/premises question are separate.

Even if a small home business is planning-lawful, storing electronics in a structure treated as the garage may conflict with the lease wording.

This is why the repo uses a hard gate:

> the intended workspace must be legally permitted, dry, secure and suitable before it holds valuable or unwiped electronics.

# 8. Downstream research: why reuse has to happen before formal recycling

## Core conclusion across chats

The project repeatedly converged on the same point:

> once usable electronics enter a conventional recycling stream, reuse opportunities can disappear.

### TechCollect

TechCollect says equipment surrendered into its normal recycling stream is recycled in Australia rather than resold second-hand. It explicitly encourages owners to pass working equipment to family/friends/charities **before** using the recycling stream.

See:
- [TECHCOLLECT-REUSE-DEEP-DIVE.md](TECHCOLLECT-REUSE-DEEP-DIVE.md)

### AMR Dubbo

No public evidence found that AMR tests functionality, securely wipes data for ITAD clients, refurbishes, or resells viable whole devices.

The strongest current interpretation is a receiving/sorting/consolidation/material-recycling role.

Therefore the proposed Dubbo model is to triage before downstream recycling, securely sanitise data, refurbish/redeploy/resell viable whole devices, use donor parts where sensible, and send only failures/uneconomic remainder into recycling.

See:
- [MATTHEWS-INFRABUILD-AMR.md](MATTHEWS-INFRABUILD-AMR.md)

# 9. Sircel research: reuse before destruction at industrial scale

The Sircel work became important because it demonstrated a mature Australian model that explicitly puts reuse before destruction.

Repo research covers IT asset disposition, testing/triage, Blancco sanitisation, certificates, buy-back/asset recovery, reuse/repurposing, and parts/material processing for failures.

The lesson for a small Dubbo operation is not to copy Sircel's industrial footprint. It is to copy the **decision hierarchy**:

> **whole-device reuse -> repair/redeployment -> parts -> material recovery**

# 11. Other reuse-first models researched

The repo compares three useful models:

## PonyUp for Good

Commercial/corporate ITAD with asset reporting, secure erasure, refurbishment/remarketing of viable assets, and recycling through ANZRP for failures.

Useful lesson: **recent business fleets are generally a better reuse source than random mixed household waste**.

## The Reconnect Project

Broader/rougher intake with repair, donor parts, secure erasure, affordable resale/social distribution, and recycling for the remainder.

Useful lesson: **repair skill and donor-parts capability can increase reuse beyond working-on-arrival devices**.

## The Laptop Initiative

Very selective, fast-flow charitable redistribution.

Useful lesson: **strict acceptance criteria and minimal warehousing can be a strength, not a weakness**.

# 12. Regional council research: disposal already exists

The surrounding-town research changed the strategic framing.

Dubbo E-Waste should **not** act as though Western NSW lacks e-waste disposal.

Current public pathways already exist through councils/NetWaste/other arrangements in Dubbo/Whylandra, Wellington, Narromine, Parkes, Forbes/Daroobalgie, Warren/Ewenmar, Mudgee/Gulgong/Kandos, Coonamble-area facilities, and other regional sites with varying certainty.

## Strongest reuse precedent

Mid-Western Regional Council explicitly routes e-waste through the **Tip Shop at Mudgee Waste Facility** as one current option.

That is the clearest nearby public-sector example found where an e-waste stream intersects with reuse/resale infrastructure.

## Strategic conclusion

The problem Dubbo E-Waste should solve is not "there is nowhere to recycle old electronics."

It is:

> **too many potentially useful devices may enter a recycling stream before somebody makes a disciplined reuse/repair/data/value decision.**

# 13. Supply-side conclusions across chats

## Best likely supply type

Prior research increasingly favoured **business/organisation fleet turnover** over random public dumping because fleet equipment is more likely to have consistent models, known ownership, known replacement cycle, chargers/accessories, clearer chain of custody, batch quantities, higher average age/value quality, and a reason to value documented data handling.

This is a hypothesis to test, not yet a proven Dubbo supply contract.

## Public schools

There was discussion about schools as possible supply sources, but historical chat retrieval did **not** provide reliable evidence sufficient to state an EDConnect/public-school disposal pipeline as fact here.

Treat school fleet access as unresolved unless supported by the dedicated current research in the backlog/supply files or direct organisational confirmation.

## Employment conflict

The project repeatedly identified a possible conflict-of-interest issue if supply, licensing or customer work overlaps with Josh's IT employment.

No assumption should be made that an employer's clients, licences, tools, e-waste, or supplier relationships can be used by Dubbo E-Waste without explicit written permission and a proper legal/entity arrangement.

# 15. Important corrections made during the conversations

This project changed quickly. Older chat statements should not automatically be repeated.

## eBay fees

**SUPERSEDED:** roughly 13% assumed for the small pilot.

**CURRENT:** eligible Australian sellers with no Pro plan and no more than AU$25,000 eBay sales over the prior 12 months currently pay no eBay transaction/final-value fee, though other costs may apply.

## Windows 10

**SUPERSEDED:** Windows 11 compatibility or Linux/parts only.

**CURRENT:** Windows 11 remains preferred, but consumer Windows 10 ESU can extend security support through **12 October 2027** under current Microsoft terms.

## Bendigo intake

**SUPERSEDED:** "Joe only takes good/sellable items."

**CURRENT:** mature Bendigo E-Waste accepts a broad range of complete/broken/raw e-waste. What matters for Dubbo is Joe's **reuse-first hierarchy** and the fact Dubbo does not yet have his downstream network.

## AMR categories

**SUPERSEDED:** old Matthews third-party listing saying no TVs/printers.

**CURRENT:** current AMR company policy explicitly lists TVs, computers/laptops, monitors, phones/tablets and printers/scanners, while lithium rules and Dubbo-specific commercial details remain unresolved.

## Battery-bin assumption

**SUPERSEDED:** "buy a metal battery bin" as a generic solution.

**CURRENT:** reject loose/damaged lithium in Phase 0; use risk-assessed controls and official fire/WHS guidance.

## Insurance estimate

**SUPERSEDED:** generic $800–$1,500/year estimate.

**CURRENT:** quote required for the actual home electronics/refurbishment/product/data/lithium risk.

## Data erasure

**SUPERSEDED risk:** vague "NIST 800-88 wipe".

**CURRENT:** NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 + current ASD/ACSC guidance, media-appropriate method, recorded tool/version and verification.

## Consumer warranty wording

**SUPERSEDED risk:** copying short "DOA/as-is/no warranty" terms from another operator.

**CURRENT:** business sales remain subject to ACL; known defects can be disclosed but statutory guarantees cannot simply be removed.

# 17. Core strategic model after all discussions

The project has moved away from the idea of being primarily a "recycling centre".

The clearest current model is:

> **small regional IT asset recovery and refurbishment service**

Flow:

1. source selected electronics before they become anonymous waste;
2. record ownership/authority;
3. triage for safety and value;
4. secure unwiped data;
5. sanitise and verify;
6. test/repair;
7. resell/redeploy/donate viable whole devices;
8. harvest donor parts where sensible;
9. send only genuine failures/end-of-life material to an authorised downstream route;
10. measure every item.

Potential revenue layers later include refurbished equipment sales, repair labour, collection/convenience, data sanitisation, asset reporting, parts, business fleet retirement services, and eventual documented recycling certificates through downstream partners.

Commodity scrap is **not** the core business case.
