## The answer in brief

1. **The current exemption has new wording.** The 2026 Regulation (2026 No 442, published 21 August 2026) replaced the 2021 one. Its clause 33, "Exemption for recycling programs", says the Act does not apply to acts or omissions relating to **(a) buying or selling goods collected in a recycling program or rubbish collection program**, or **(b) contracting to give someone ownership of such goods** [S01, E02].
2. **Selling refurbished goods that were collected in a program is inside the words.** Nothing in the clause excludes goods that were repaired or refurbished before sale.
3. **But "recycling program" is not defined anywhere we could find**, in the Act, the Regulation text seen or Fair Trading guidance [E04]. No registration, approval, minimum volume, charity status, non-profit rule or downstream-recycler rule appears. Those would be invented requirements.
4. **Whether a refurbish-and-sell operation counts is genuinely open.** The 2021 change was meant to cover "all recycling programs" including community schemes [S08, E09]. Against that, NSW waste law treats *reuse* and *recycling* as different things [S17, E23], and the Act exists to stop stolen goods moving through dealers [E10]. Phones and laptops are exactly that risk.
5. **No court decision and no 2026 explanatory paper were found** [E28]. Only Fair Trading's written view (or a court) can settle it in practice.
6. **Two routes need no exemption at all**: giving devices away instead of selling them, and repairing customer-owned devices. The Act only bites on a *business of buying or selling* [S09, E11].
7. **Buying stock changes the picture.** Devices bought from the public or traders aren't "goods collected in a recycling program", so they fall outside clause 33. The exceptions are goods bought from a government body or from a program itself [E16].
8. **The licence is $692 for one year (2026–27)** [E19]. The harder cost is the operating rules: ID checks, police reporting, and a 14-day hold with no alteration, which collides with wiping and refurbishing.
9. **Two discretionary routes exist.** The Act's s 4A lets Fair Trading exempt a person from the Act or specific provisions by written notice [E32]. The 2026 Regulation can waive, reduce or postpone fees, which in 2021 meant financial hardship or special circumstances [E33]. Neither is an entitlement, and no published exemption has removed the licence itself [E21].

**Recommended sequence:** ask Fair Trading the questions in the [draft enquiry](../templates/fair-trading-recycling-exemption-enquiry-DRAFT-2026-11-28.md) in writing. Until there's an answer, donate-only (or pausing sales) is the lowest-risk way to keep working. Keep any bought stock completely separate.

## 2. The current law

**The Act (version current from 20 August 2024; still current when accessed 10 December 2025)** [S12, E31]

- **s 7:** "A person must not carry on a business of buying or selling second-hand goods except in accordance with a licence held by the person." Maximum penalty 100 penalty units [S09, E11].
- **s 3:** second-hand goods means goods of a prescribed class that have been used, or are represented as previously bought but unused [S10].
- **s 38:** in proceedings, a person who sold second-hand goods on more than 6 days in any 12 months is presumed to be carrying on a business of buying or selling them. The presumption can be rebutted [S11, E12].
- **s 4(1):** no licence is needed for an activity authorised by a licence or authority under another Act [E15].
- **s 4A (inserted 2020):** the Secretary may, by written notice, exempt a person from the operation of the Act or specified provisions. Exemptions can have conditions and an expiry, and must be published [S28, E32].
- **s 4(2):** the Act does not apply to dealing in second-hand goods in the course of a fundraising appeal authorised under the Charitable Fundraising Act 1991, or to the business of an auctioneer [S10, E14].
- The Act also provides for forfeiture of goods dealt with by unlicensed persons [S13].

**The Regulation 2026 (2026 No 442)** [S01]. Read through search-index copies of the official page. Direct fetches failed, so the clause numbers other than cl 5, 7, 33 and 35 are unconfirmed.

- **cl 5(1)(b):** electronic goods are prescribed [E13].
- **cl 33, Exemption for recycling programs:** the wording quoted above [E02].
- **Government purchases:** the Act does not apply "to the extent" a person buys second-hand goods from a government sector agency, a statutory public authority or a State owned corporation [E16].
- **cl 35:** the Act does not apply to a person dealing only in factory seconds [E16].
- **Ancillary-business carve-out:** kept, for businesses not otherwise needing a licence. In 2021 it covered uncollected goods, trade-ins and rental goods [E17].
- **Penalty notice:** $1,100 (individual) under the 2021 Schedule. The 2026 amount wasn't read [E18].
- **cl 32, Powers to waive, reduce, postpone or refund fees:** the heading was seen but not the wording. The 2021 version applied for financial hardship or special circumstances [E33]. The processing fee is discounted online and isn't refunded if an application is refused or withdrawn [E34].

**Pending:** a Fair Trading and Building Legislation Amendment Bill 2026 proposes amendments to the Act (Schedule 14, licence cancellation). Its status wasn't checked [S25, E30].

## 3. How the exemption changed

| Instrument | Who was covered | What was covered |
|---|---|---|
| 1997 Reg cl 6A (from 24 Jul 1998) | Councils and their employees only | Conducting a recycling program; selling goods collected in one; contracting to give ownership [S07] |
| 2003, 2008, 2015 Regs | "Approved person": a council, or a council contractor who won a tender | Same three activities [S04, S06, S27] |
| 2015 Reg cl 6(3) (by July 2019) | Anyone, if done in the course of (or ancillary to) a business that doesn't need a licence | Same three, treated as not carrying on a dealer business [S05] |
| 2021 Reg cl 6(1) | **Anyone**: "approved person" removed | Conducting a recycling **or rubbish collection** program; selling collected goods; contracting [S03] |
| **2026 Reg cl 33** | Anyone (no person limit seen) | **Buying or selling** collected goods; contracting. No separate "conducting" limb seen [S01] |

The 2020 Regulatory Impact Statement explains the 2021 change. The exemption was expanded "to include all recycling programs as well as rubbish collection programs", with examples of programs run by the Australian Government and community recycling schemes. It sets no criteria [S08, E09].

Dropping the "conducting" limb in 2026 probably changes little for Dubbo eWaste. Collecting goods for free isn't buying or selling anyway. Adding "buying" means a recycler that buys goods from a program is covered too. No 2026 explanatory paper was found to confirm the intent [E28].

## 4. What "recycling program" requires: the actual legal criteria

| Question | What the law says | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Formal registration or approval? | None in the text seen. The "approved person" concept was removed in 2021 | No requirement found |
| Can an individual or sole trader run one? | Since 2021 nothing limits who. Before that, councils and their contractors only | Not excluded |
| Can it be commercial or profitable? | Not addressed | Silent |
| From home? | Not addressed by this Act. Planning rules apply separately | Silent |
| Minimum volume? | None | No requirement found |
| Must be a charity, council or association? | Not since 2021 | No requirement found |
| Does refurbishment and reuse count as recycling? | Undefined. See section 5 | **Open** |
| Online sales? | Not addressed. The s 38 presumption applies to any selling | Silent |
| Proceeds to pay costs or the operator? | Not addressed | Silent |
| Written policies, public collection, reporting? | None required by this law | Best practice only |
| Purchased equipment? | Bought goods aren't "goods collected in" a program, unless bought *from* a program | **Keep out** |
| Approved downstream recycler? | Not required by this law. POEO Act s 143 (lawful destination for waste) applies separately | Separate law |

"Program" in ordinary English suggests an organised, ongoing scheme with a stated purpose, rather than ad hoc acquisition. That's an inference, not a legal test [E24].

## 5. Does refurbish-and-sell fit? Arguments on both sides

**For a wide reading**
- The text covers *selling goods collected in* a program, with no condition about whether they were altered [E02].
- The 2021 change was deliberately broad: "all recycling programs", including community schemes [E09].
- Fair Trading's guidance simply lists recycling and rubbish collection programs as not needing a licence [S14].
- Industry usage calls device reuse "recycling". WorkVentures, for example, describes its laptop reuse work as an "e-waste recycling program" [S24].

**For a narrow reading**
- NSW waste law lists reuse, reprocessing and recycling as separate parts of resource recovery [S17, S18, E23]. A court could read "recycling" in its narrower sense.
- The Act's purpose is to limit stolen goods moving through dealers [E10]. A business reselling phones and laptops sits close to that risk, whatever it calls itself.
- Before 2021 the exemption only covered council programs. A regulator may still picture council-style schemes.
- If sales are regular, s 38 presumes a business, and "it's a program" becomes a defence to argue, not a status you already have.

**Assessment:** it's a real question with no settled answer. Treat clause 33 as *possibly* covering free-collected devices that are refurbished and sold. It doesn't cover bought stock. Get Fair Trading's view in writing before relying on it [E24, E28].

## 7. Other exemptions checked

- **Charitable fundraising (Act s 4(2)(a)).** Dealing in the course of a fundraising appeal *authorised under* the Charitable Fundraising Act 1991 is outside the Act [E14]. An ACNC charity is eligible for an authority, ABLIS lists no fee, and processing is up to 28 days [S19, S21, E25]. Participants need the authority holder's written authority [S20, E26]. Small volunteer-only fundraisers are exempt from holding an authority, but it's unclear whether their appeals count as "authorised" for s 4(2)(a) [E27].
- **Six-day rule.** The market-stall guidance says a licence is needed for selling prescribed goods on more than 6 days in 12 months [S15]. The Act's general version is the s 38 presumption [E12]. No other frequency, revenue or profit threshold was found, including for online sales.
- **Personal property.** Selling your own belongings isn't carrying on a business, but frequent selling raises the presumption.
- **Not designed to be portable, video games, whitegoods, mobility aids, large watercraft.** Excluded per Fair Trading's guidance [S14]. Most Dubbo eWaste stock is portable electronics, so this rarely helps.
- **Government-sourced equipment.** The Act doesn't apply "to the extent" a person buys from government bodies [E16]. Whether reselling those goods is also covered wasn't confirmed.
- **Trade-ins, uncollected goods, rental goods.** Only for a business that doesn't otherwise need a licence [E17].
- **Auctioneers.** Covers the auctioneer's business, not a buyer at auction [E14].
- **Another authority (s 4(1)).** Someone else's licence doesn't cover you unless you work inside their licensed business at notified premises. See [LEGAL-LICENSING §2](LEGAL-LICENSING.md).
- **Individually granted exemptions.** See section 8.

## 8. What the granted exemptions show

Fair Trading's published list (as of 27 February 2025) has 15 entries. Every one is a **licensee with a licence number**, and each exemption lifts *specific* duties: ID evidence (s 15), records (s 16) and the 14-day retention (s 21) [S16, E21]. Technology examples:

- TES-AMM (2PS04726): exempt from furnishing records to police (s 16(5A)), with a condition to notify if it starts buying from individuals.
- Asurion (2PS16175): exempt from s 16(5A) and the s 21 retention for IMEI/serial-tracked phones, laptops, tablets and wearables.
- Alegre (2PS11940): exempt from ID, records and retention, on condition each device is tracked by serial or IMEI.
- Assurant, Kingfisher Mobile, Likewise, Industry Trading: program-specific exemptions [E22].

**What this means:** s 4A allows Fair Trading to exempt a person from the Act entirely or from specific provisions [E32]. But every published exemption so far is a narrow one for a licensee, and none removes the licence itself. A narrow request with tracking conditions is likelier to succeed than a blanket one. The most useful precedent is for *after* licensing. A licensee can seek relief from the 14-day no-alteration hold for serial-tracked devices, which would let wiping and refurbishing start sooner. See the [exemption pack](../templates/fair-trading-exemption-application-pack-2026-11-28.md). Application cost and processing time weren't published on the pages read.

**Annual cost comparison.** Models 1–4 have no licence fee. Their costs are lost or redirected income, plus your time on records. Model 5 adds $491–$692 a year plus compliance time. The approved records-software cost wasn't found. No grant or auspicing route that covers the licence was found. The fee-waiver power (cl 32) is discretionary and needs evidence of hardship or special circumstances. Ask before paying [E33].

**Partnerships:** a partner's licence only covers *its* business at *its* notified premises. Working under a local computer business's or a recycler's licence would mean doing the work as part of their business, on terms they agree to. It can't be assumed.

## 11. Separating activities without disguising trade

These are legitimate, because each describes what actually happens:
- a donate-only stream (no sales) alongside a repair service;
- a charity's appeal selling devices it owns, with you as an authorised volunteer;
- collected-goods sales under cl 33 (if Fair Trading agrees), with **no** bought stock in the same operation.

Not legitimate:
- calling purchases "donations";
- asking for a "donation" in return for a device;
- moving stock between a "program" and a private sideline to dodge the hold or records;
- splitting one operation across names you control when nothing really differs.

The licence obligation is assessed on what a person actually does, across everything they do.

## 13. If the reading is wrong

- After applying for a licence, you can't trade while it's being assessed [E20].
