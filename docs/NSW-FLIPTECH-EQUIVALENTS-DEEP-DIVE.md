# NSW FlipTech equivalents — deep comparative research

**Researched:** 2 October 2026  
**Scope:** FlipTech Australia, Renew IT, ITC Asset Management, The Reconnect Project, WorkVentures and LinkBytes.  
**Question:** which NSW organisations are genuinely comparable to FlipTech, how did they start, how do they make the model work now, and which pieces are realistic to copy in Dubbo?

This file focuses on **business model and operating design**, not simply on companies that happen to accept e-waste.

---

# Executive conclusion

There is no single NSW company that exactly matches the proposed Dubbo E-Waste concept.

Instead, the six organisations sit on a spectrum:

| Organisation | Best description | Most relevant lesson for Dubbo |
|---|---|---|
| **FlipTech Australia** | young private e-waste/ITAD company with reuse, secure data handling, recycling and social donation | strongest "started tiny and grew" benchmark |
| **The Reconnect Project** | repair/reuse social enterprise + charity + public repair shop | strongest small-scale repair/reuse-first operating model |
| **ITC Asset Management** | commercial ITAD, buyback, data destruction, chain of custody and recycling | strongest blueprint for future business/government service |
| **Renew IT** | large-scale corporate ITAD + buyback + refurbishment/resale + downstream innovation | clearest mature commercial end-state |
| **WorkVentures** | long-running social enterprise combining ITAD, refurbishment, resale and digital inclusion | strongest social/device-bank hybrid |
| **LinkBytes** | wholesale refurbished electronics + ITAD/buyback/remarketing | useful resale/wholesale exit model; weaker public historical evidence |

The most useful Dubbo synthesis is therefore:

> **Start like early FlipTech / Reconnect; build process like ITC; scale toward Renew IT only if demand exists; use WorkVentures-style social pathways where appropriate; develop LinkBytes-style wholesale remarketing as an exit for batches.**

Public records show:

- previous entity name: **FLIPTECH RECYCLING PTY LTD**;
- ABN **54 653 401 024**;
- ABN active from **3 September 2021**;
- GST registered from **12 January 2023**;
- business name **FLIPTECH RECYCLING** from 6 September 2021;

Sources:

## Founder and origin story

FlipTech's founder is **Austin Turpin**.

Independent reporting says Austin started the idea at about **15 years old** after seeing a large amount of discarded electronics at a local tip.

The business reportedly began from the **family garage**.

Later reporting described the then-20-year-old founder as having grown FlipTech into an operation with employees and nationwide clients.

Sources:
- https://www.dailytelegraph.com.au/newslocal/manly-daily/fliptech-warriewood-austin-turpin-blossoms-from-teen-ewaste-entrepreneur-to-computer-recycling-boss/news-story/d4647eb51bfe76aa68bef295c85e29e7

### Important chronology nuance

LinkedIn says FlipTech was **founded in 2019**, while the current company ABN begins in September 2021.

That is consistent with a startup period before the present company structure, but the public evidence does not establish the exact legal arrangement used during 2019–2021.

## What FlipTech does now

FlipTech publicly positions itself as:

- secure e-waste / IT decommissioning;
- collection;
- sorting;
- secure data erasure;
- refurbishment / repurposing;
- material recycling;
- social donation.

Its Buy NSW profile specifically describes services for:

- corporate;
- residential;
- social-enterprise clients.

Source:
- https://buy.nsw.gov.au/supplier/profile/123199

## Collection model

FlipTech offers e-waste bins to buildings/businesses.

Published process:

1. FlipTech delivers a bin.
2. Customer fills it.
3. FlipTech collects/replaces it.
4. Material is sorted at FlipTech's facility.
5. Data-bearing assets are securely erased.
6. Material is routed onward.

Source:
- https://www.fliptech.com.au/bins

Austin has also publicly advertised **free recycling and data erasure for redundant electronics Sydney-wide**.

Source:
- https://www.linkedin.com/posts/austin-turpin-1608a91a1_got-e-waste-fliptech-provides-free-recycling-activity-7046635900344152064-8S2F

Do not assume that means every category / every postcode / every volume is always free in 2026; quote conditions can change.

## Data security

FlipTech publishes a detailed secure-custody approach including:

- unmarked locked collection vehicles;
- monitored transport;
- secure facility;
- 24/7 surveillance;
- restricted authorised access;
- data-erasure software;
- stated alignment to NIST 800-88 and DoD standards;
- Certificate of Destruction.

Source:
- https://www.fliptech.com.au/articles/8e4e2ca7-22e2-4d68-bf22-d0420c297db4

### Evidence caution

FlipTech uses wording such as "certified data erasure" and "Global Standards Framework".

This research did **not** independently verify a specific current ISO certification scope or AS/NZS 5377 certification from an external accreditation register.

Do not turn marketing wording into a certification claim without the certificate/scope.

## Reuse/refurbishment

FlipTech explicitly says it focuses on:

- responsible disposal;
- recycling;
- **refurbishing IT equipment**;
- ensuring devices are either repurposed or recycled.

Source:
- https://www.fliptech.com.au/articles/53b6be97-e467-4c86-9dc7-7bc1d9dff119

Independent reporting says usable equipment/components are refurbished/resold while true end-of-life equipment is recycled.

Source:
- Daily Telegraph reporting above.

## Social reuse

FlipTech has a documented partnership with **Royal Far West**.

Its own published explanation says much of the suitable IT collected through that relationship can be **donated back into communities**, particularly supporting rural/remote Australian children.

Source:
- https://www.fliptech.com.au/articles/3b6db187-5654-4e59-9515-e3202851afce

### Why this matters to Dubbo

Royal Far West is specifically concerned with rural and remote children.

That makes FlipTech's social-reuse model unusually relevant to a regional NSW project.

## Revenue model — best public reconstruction

Likely revenue streams include:

- collection / IT decommissioning services;
- secure data-erasure services;
- resale of reusable/refurbished devices/components;
- commodity/recycling recovery;
- ongoing bin/service arrangements.

The exact revenue split is not public.

## Why FlipTech is relevant

FlipTech is the strongest example in this comparison of:

> **young founder → garage → collect unwanted electronics → learn the stream → professionalise data/security → business/government clients → warehouse/facility → reuse + recycling + social donation**

That is much closer to the desired Dubbo growth path than starting as a mature industrial recycler.

## What Dubbo could copy now

- start small;
- prioritise usable business IT;
- offer convenient pickup;
- treat data handling as core value;
- keep reuse ahead of recycling;
- build a social redistribution path;
- document what happens to assets.

## What Dubbo should not copy yet

- unattended building bins;
- broad indiscriminate intake;
- national collection promises;
- security/certification language not actually held;
- warehouse overhead before supply is proven.

- around **35% annual revenue growth** at the time of the profile.

Renew IT acquired **Verser Technology Lifecycle Services** in 2021.

Renew IT says the acquisition expanded national capability and strengthened government-facing lifecycle services.

Source:
- https://renew-it.com/renews/

## Core commercial model

Renew IT's current model is highly relevant to Dubbo:

1. businesses refresh fleets;
2. Renew IT offers **buyback** for redundant IT;
3. data is sanitised;
4. viable hardware is **refurbished for resale**;
5. older/non-saleable hardware goes to downstream material recovery.

Source:
- https://renew-it.com/

That is effectively the mature version of the business model being designed in this repo.

## Data sanitisation

Renew IT says it sanitises data to **NIST 800-88**.

The public site uses this as part of the buyback/refurbishment service.

Source:
- https://renew-it.com/

For Dubbo, the key lesson is commercial:

> businesses may accept refurbishment/resale when the vendor can prove the old data is gone.

## Refurbishment / resale

Renew IT explicitly says it:

- buys redundant IT;
- sanitises it;
- **refurbishes hardware for resale**;
- returns value to the client.

This makes resale a central ITAD outcome, not a secondary hobby.

## Downstream: UNSW MICROfactorie

Renew IT partnered with the **UNSW SMaRT Centre**.

At its Lane Cove warehouse, a commercially operated plastics-filament MICROfactorie turns discarded hard plastics from e-waste into 3D-printer feedstock.

Source:
- https://renew-it.com/renews/
- https://www.smart.unsw.edu.au/news-events/news/commercial-success-story-smarts-plastics-microfactorie-tech

### Why this matters

It demonstrates a mature separation of roles:

- high-value whole-device reuse occurs first;
- specialist material innovation handles the difficult residual fraction.

Dubbo does **not** need to replicate this processing technology.

Dubbo needs a trustworthy partner for this stage.

## Customer base

Renew IT says it works with:

- SMEs;
- corporations;
- schools;
- universities;
- manufacturers;
- government.

## Revenue model

Public evidence supports revenue/value streams including:

- lifecycle services;
- ITAD;
- fleet deployment/recovery;
- buyback;
- asset remarketing/resale;
- data sanitisation;
- logistics;
- downstream recovery services;
- professional technology services.

Exact revenue by stream is not public.

## What Dubbo could copy now

- focus on business fleet refreshes;
- pay attention to residual asset value;
- data sanitisation before resale;
- resale whole devices where viable;
- transparent residual recycling route.

## What belongs much later

- multi-state facilities;
- 200+ staff;
- dedicated material-processing technology;
- national enterprise tenders;
- acquisition-led growth.

## Market focus

This is a **business-focused** operation, not a household drop-off model.

ITC explicitly says it is built for commercial IT assets, including:

- universities;
- councils;

## Why ITC is probably the best process blueprint for future Dubbo B2B work

ITC describes a tightly controlled process:

1. assets logged at collection;
2. secure chain of custody;
3. ITC employees transport data-bearing equipment;
4. devices sanitised or physically destroyed;
5. assets triaged for reuse/remarketing;
6. residuals recycled;
7. client gets certificates + disposition reporting.

Source:
- https://www.itcassetmanagement.com.au/services/it-asset-disposal-sydney/
- https://www.itcassetmanagement.com.au/locations/

This is almost exactly the workflow the Dubbo repo has independently evolved toward.

## Certifications

Buy NSW and ITC's site currently list:

- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** — information security;
- **ISO 14001:2015** — environmental management;
- **ISO 9001:2015** — quality;
- **ISO 45001:2018** — WHS.

Source:
- https://buy.nsw.gov.au/supplier/profile/89282
- https://www.itcassetmanagement.com.au/about-us/

Its recycling workflow is described as in line with **AS/NZS 5377**.

## Data destruction

ITC offers:

- software sanitisation;
- physical shredding;
- degaussing where appropriate;
- on-site/witnessed destruction;
- serialised Certificates of Destruction;
- device-level records.

Source:
- https://www.itcassetmanagement.com.au/services/data-destruction-services-sydney/

## Logistics / chain of custody

One particularly useful differentiator is that ITC says:

- data-bearing collection is handled by its own employees;
- secure transport is documented;
- asset records begin on-site;
- data destruction occurs before recycling;
- regional/interstate collections use the same process controls.

Source:
- https://www.itcassetmanagement.com.au/locations/

### Dubbo implication

A regional operation can compete on **trust/process**, not industrial scale.

Even if downstream processing happens elsewhere, the local operator can own:

- intake;
- custody;
- serial records;
- sanitisation;
- triage;
- final disposition evidence.

## Buyback / value recovery

ITC buys:

- desktops;
- laptops;
- servers;
- network hardware;
- storage arrays;
- monitors;
- mobile devices.

Working assets can be refurbished/remarketed, while the rest is recycled.

Source:
- https://www.itcassetmanagement.com.au/services/it-asset-buyback-sydney/

ITC calls its rebate/value-recovery approach a **Buy-Residual Flywheel** in its Buy NSW profile.

## Collection pricing

ITC says qualifying Sydney commercial volumes can receive **free collection**.

Smaller jobs can attract a minimum service charge.

Source:
- https://www.itcassetmanagement.com.au/services/data-destruction-services-sydney/

This is important because "free collection" can be funded by:

- residual device value;
- job size;
- paid services;
- commercial scale.

It is not evidence that every small batch is economic to collect for free.

## What Dubbo could copy first

- business-only scheduled intake;
- manifest / serial recording;
- secure transport;
- strict data-state control;
- buyback/residual value assessment;
- certificates;
- full asset disposition reports;
- own-client-facing process even if downstream is outsourced.

## What comes later

- multiple ISO certifications;
- on-site shredding;
- national logistics;
- enterprise/government procurement;
- large secure facility.

# 4. The Reconnect Project

## Current identity

The Reconnect Project is a NSW charity/social enterprise based in **Penshurst**.

Legal entity:

- **The Reconnect Project Limited**
- ABN **50 634 550 388**
- active from **1 July 2019**
- registered charity from July 2019
- GST registered from December 2024.

Source:
- https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View/50634550388
- https://thereconnectproject.com.au/

Founder and CEO: **Annette Brodie**.

## Origin story

This is probably the best small-model comparison in NSW.

Annette says the **concept began in 2018** when she researched what happened to pre-owned smartphones.

She discovered that some devices entering industrial shredding still had useful life if repaired.

The Reconnect Project:

- was founded in **2019**;
- publicly launched in **February 2020**;
- initially used a **shared workspace**;
- pivoted to **working from home almost immediately** because of COVID;
- opened the Penshurst repair shop in **early 2021**.

Source:
- https://www.linkedin.com/posts/annette-brodie_i-have-an-aversion-to-the-word-scale-the-activity-7444257939227340800-gAs1
- https://www.smallbusiness.nsw.gov.au/news-podcasts/news/annette-brodie-how-a-tech-repair-shop-is-making-a-difference-in-sydney

## Startup funding

In its Productivity Commission submission, The Reconnect Project says it began with only **$5,000 in crowdfunding**.

Source:
- https://assets.pc.gov.au/387583/sub134-circular-economy.pdf

That is very important for Dubbo:

> a working repair/reuse model can start with thousands, not hundreds of thousands, if intake is constrained and industrial processing is outsourced.

## The original problem Annette identified

The key insight was not "e-waste needs recycling."

It was:

> some older devices sent toward shredding no longer had much commercial value but still had **2–3 years of useful life** if repaired.

That is almost exactly the core Dubbo hypothesis.

## What it accepts

Current donation policy:

- mobile phones: all makes/models/ages/conditions;
- tablets: all makes/models/ages/conditions;
- laptops: currently **less than 8 years old**.

Source:
- https://thereconnectproject.com.au/donate/

This shows an important maturity lesson:

> acceptance criteria evolve.

The organisation historically described taking extremely broad device donations, but its current laptop rule is more selective.

## Triage / repair / parts

Current published process:

1. donated device received;
2. triage assesses function;
3. required parts identified;
4. technicians repair;
5. secure erase;
6. reuse/distribution/resale;
7. unrepairable devices become donor parts;
8. remaining broken parts/devices go to Australian recyclers.

Source:
- https://thereconnectproject.com.au/donate/

The Product Stewardship Centre of Excellence has described its hierarchy as:

1. **repair**;
2. **parts harvesting**;
3. materials recycling last.

Source:
- https://www.linkedin.com/posts/the-reconnect-project_annette-brodie-centre-of-excellence-activity-7311233696219504640-x6nW

That is virtually the same hierarchy adopted in this repo.

## Data erasure

The Reconnect Project uses **Blancco**.

It says a Certificate of Erasure can be provided and asks for a **$5 contribution per device** to cover that cost.

Source:
- https://thereconnectproject.com.au/donate/

## Repair economics / social-enterprise revenue

The Reconnect Project's public model is ingenious because **paid repair work helps fund the social mission**.

Its training page describes:

> **Profits = Progress** — repair fees support the organisation's work.

Source:
- https://thereconnectproject.com.au/training/

The NSW Small Business Commissioner reported in 2025 that the Penshurst shop had:

- ten staff;
- five technicians;
- a workshop supervisor;
- support/shopfront staff.

Source:
- https://www.smallbusiness.nsw.gov.au/news-podcasts/news/annette-brodie-how-a-tech-repair-shop-is-making-a-difference-in-sydney

## Refurbished resale

The organisation told the Productivity Commission that it sells some refurbished equipment through:

- **eBay**;
- **Reebelo**;
- direct customer sales.

Source:
- https://assets.pc.gov.au/387583/sub134-circular-economy.pdf

That makes it not merely a donation charity; it also participates in the second-hand/refurb market.

## Distribution

Refurbished devices are distributed via support agencies including:

- women's shelters;
- refugee services;
- youth outreach/support services.

Source:
- https://thereconnectproject.com.au/about-us/

## Real restoration cost

The donation page states an **average cost of about $200 to restore each device**.

Source:
- https://thereconnectproject.com.au/donate/

This is useful because it shows why "free donated laptops" are not economically free after:

- labour;
- parts;
- storage;
- data wiping;
- admin;
- support.

## Grants and sustainability

Early reporting says local-council funding helped establish the project.

A City of Sydney annual report records a **$33,000 Staying Connected** grant in 2019/20.

Source:
- https://www.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/-/media/corporate/files/publications/research-and-reports/annual-reports/statutory-returns-annual-report-2019-20.pdf

However, Annette explicitly said the organisation could not rely on grants forever and opened the repair shop to create an earned-income stream.

Source:
- https://www.frankie.com.au/article/the-reconnect-project-gives-old-technology-a-second-life-563637

## Skills / employment model

The organisation trains and employs neurodivergent young adults in repair.

Its Productivity Commission submission says it developed an Australian Mobile Device Repair training program and was piloting formal training.

## Barriers identified by Reconnect

Its Productivity Commission submission is extremely relevant to Dubbo.

It identifies barriers including:

- OEM parts pairing;
- Apple/Google account locks;
- donated devices becoming nonviable because locks cannot be removed;
- limited funding for upstream repair/reuse compared with downstream recycling.

Source:
- https://assets.pc.gov.au/387583/sub134-circular-economy.pdf

## What Dubbo could copy immediately

- selective device classes;
- triage before dismantling;
- donor-parts bins;
- no assumption that low-value = no useful life;
- repair services as an earned-income stream;
- use external recyclers for the residue;
- small workspace before industrial premises;
- Blancco/documented erasure later if clients justify cost.

## What should not be copied automatically

- broad public intake;
- charity structure;
- dependence on grants;
- expensive repairs where market value does not justify them;
- assuming every donated device has a social-use destination.

# 11. Practical service design this research suggests

A future Dubbo business service could look like:

## 1. Client sends an asset list

Preferably:

- make/model;
- rough age;
- quantity;
- device type;
- data-bearing status;
- known locks;
- location.

## 2. Dubbo pre-values the batch

Classify:

- likely resale;
- possible refurb;
- donor/parts;
- likely recycling liability.

## 3. Quote the whole job

Possible structure:

**service fee + collection fee + data service − residual buyback value**

That is more sustainable than promising "everything free".

## 4. At collection

Record:

- asset ID;
- serial;
- client;
- transfer authority;
- condition;
- custody time.

## 5. Secure data first

No resale/parts route until sanitisation is completed and recorded.

## 6. Fast triage

Within a defined time budget:

- A — resale whole;
- B — cheap repair;
- C — donor parts;
- D — social reuse if appropriate;
- E — downstream recycling.

## 7. Sell through multiple exits

- direct local;
- eBay;
- wholesale batches;
- social partner;
- parts;
- downstream recycler.

## 8. Give client a report

At minimum:

- items collected;
- data outcome;
- reuse/refurb count;
- donated/social count;
- recycled count;
- buyback/rebate;
- exceptions.

This is the most transferable common feature across the serious ITAD operators.

# 12. What all six companies collectively prove

## 1. The valuable business is upstream of shredding

Every useful comparison attempts to extract value **before** raw-material recycling:

- resale;
- refurbishment;
- repair;
- redeployment;
- social donation;
- component recovery.

That strongly validates the core Dubbo thesis.

## 2. Data is a commercial service

Data sanitisation is not just a compliance nuisance.

For business clients it is one of the reasons to choose a specialist operator.

## 3. Fleet refreshes are better than random waste

Businesses/government/education generate:

- consistent models;
- predictable age;
- documented ownership;
- batch volume;
- residual value.

This is why Renew IT, ITC, WorkVentures and LinkBytes are heavily fleet-oriented.

## 4. Free collection works only when something funds it

Possible funding mechanisms include:

- residual device value;
- paid data service;
- commercial service fee;
- scale/route density;
- grant/social funding;
- commodity value.

There is no evidence that "collect everything for free" is intrinsically sustainable.

## 5. Low-value equipment is the dangerous part of the model

Renew IT's origin stories about hard-to-process residuals and Reconnect's selective/current laptop rule both reinforce the same point:

> supply is not the problem; **good supply is the problem**.

## 6. Account/MDM locks destroy reuse value

Reconnect and WorkVentures both identify device locks as a major barrier.

A Dubbo donor/client checklist should require organisations to remove:

- Apple Activation Lock / Find My;
- Google/FRP;
- Windows Autopilot;
- MDM;
- BIOS/firmware passwords where possible.

## 7. Repair can be its own revenue stream

Reconnect demonstrates that repair revenue can subsidise the broader reuse mission.

This matters because resale alone can be lumpy.

## 8. Wholesale is an important exit

LinkBytes shows that once enough consistent assets are available, one-by-one local resale is not the only market.

## 9. Certification comes after process maturity

FlipTech and Renew IT both began tiny.

ITC's current four-ISO setup is a mature business-control state, not evidence that a garage pilot needs four ISO certifications before the first customer.

## 10. A regional operator can own the relationship without owning a recycling plant

The local value can be:

- sourcing;
- collection;
- custody;
- data;
- triage;
- repair;
- resale;
- reporting.

The industrial residue can go downstream.

That is the clearest overall lesson for Dubbo.

# Bottom line

The strongest NSW evidence supports this Dubbo path:

> **garage/small-workspace startup is plausible; repair/reuse should happen before recycling; business fleet refreshes are a better supply source than random dumping; secure data handling is a sellable service; residual value can subsidise collection; social reuse is viable but needs partner demand; wholesale resale can reduce inventory labour; industrial recycling should stay downstream.**

The two companies to study most closely **right now** are:

- **The Reconnect Project** for the small, low-capital repair/reuse operating model;
- **FlipTech** for the garage-to-professional-ITAD growth story.

The company to model the **future client workflow** on is:

- **ITC Asset Management**.

The company that best demonstrates the **scaled commercial destination** is:

- **Renew IT**.
