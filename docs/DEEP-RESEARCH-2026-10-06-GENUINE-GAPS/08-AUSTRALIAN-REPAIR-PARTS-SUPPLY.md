# 08 — Australian computer-repair parts supply chain

**Questions answered:** 24–26  
**Research date:** 6 October 2026

## 24. Which Australian suppliers cover useful categories?

This is not an endorsement list. It maps current public capability.

### EMPR Australia — strongest genuine/OEM business-laptop channel found

EMPR's current Buy NSW profile says it supplies genuine replacement parts for:

- HP;
- Lenovo;
- Dell;
- ASUS;
- HPE;
- Dynabook and others.

It advertises:

- authorised relationships;
- 12-month genuine-parts warranty;
- local stock;
- parts portal;
- tailored pricing.

HP's official Australian parts-reseller page names **EMPR Australia** as an authorised parts reseller.

Sources:
- https://buy.nsw.gov.au/supplier/profile/107033
- https://store.emprgroup.com.au/
- https://parts.hp.com/hpparts/Part_Resellers.aspx?cc=AU&geo_code=AP&lang=EN

This is the strongest source for enterprise/customer work where provenance matters.

### Lenovo Parts Lookup

Official Lenovo lookup can identify genuine FRUs by serial/model.

https://support.lenovo.com/au/en/parts-lookup

Use it to identify the correct FRU even when sourcing elsewhere.

### iFixit Australia

Current AU PC-laptop parts include:

- batteries;
- fans;
- screens;
- case parts;
- boards/cables;
- SSD/RAM related items.

It ships from Sydney and publishes a one-year guarantee for batteries, with broader guarantees for many other parts.

Sources:
- https://www.ifixit.com/en-au/Parts/PC_Laptop
- https://www.ifixit.com/Info/AU_Guarantee

This is attractive where repair documentation and part purchase can be linked.

## 25. Genuine/OEM vs aftermarket risk

### OEM/genuine advantages

- exact FRU/compatibility;
- traceable supply chain;
- stronger customer evidence;
- typically lower firmware/fit surprises;
- easier warranty/compliance narrative.

### Aftermarket can be rational for

- older equipment where OEM is unavailable;
- non-safety-critical plastics/hinges/keyboards;
- displays from identified panel manufacturers;
- well-supported supplier-branded replacements.

### Higher-risk aftermarket categories

- lithium batteries;
- mains chargers/power supplies;
- USB-C PD chargers;
- anything advertised with no real manufacturer/importer identity;
- parts affecting thermal/electrical safety.

A supplier warranty is useful but does not replace the refurbisher's obligation to sell safe goods.

## Parts-quality register

Score each supplier/part family on:

- exact manufacturer identity;
- Australian legal entity;
- OEM / authorised / aftermarket;
- safety compliance evidence;
- compatibility accuracy;
- DOA rate;
- return ease;
- warranty;
- average delivery days to Dubbo;
- freight cost;
- support quality;
- recall traceability.

Do not rate a supplier based on one successful order.

## 26. Stocking/freight strategy for Dubbo

### Stock locally

Only fast-moving/universal items initially:

- common DDR4/DDR5 SODIMMs after demand exists;
- common SATA/NVMe SSDs;
- thermal paste;
- cleaning consumables;
- common screws;
- Ethernet/USB cables;
- a small number of **compliant** common USB-C chargers only if repeated demand justifies them.

### Order on demand

- batteries;
- screens;
- keyboards;
- hinges;
- fans;
- exact chargers;
- touch assemblies;
- model-specific boards.

Why:

- battery ageing;
- model fragmentation;
- dead stock;
- warranty clock;
- cash tied up;
- uncertain failure mix.

### Harvested parts

Harvest only when:

- exact identity/compatibility can be recorded;
- condition can be tested;
- likely demand exists;
- storage location is assigned;
- data-bearing media remains under sanitisation control.

Set an ageing rule. A $30 keyboard kept for three years is not automatically value recovery.

## Pilot metric

For every repair part:

`landed part cost + sourcing minutes + install minutes + retest minutes + expected warranty risk`

Compare against:

- expected resale uplift; or
- approved customer repair price.

This tells you when ordering genuine/OEM is economically sensible and when the entire repair is uneconomic.
