# Corporate profile: "AMR" (Australian Metal Recycling), with focus on the Dubbo NSW site (as of late Nov 2026)

Research note: Bash egress to AMR/SMS domains was blocked and no AMR corporate website could be located or fetched. Findings rest mainly on public registers: ABN Lookup, which is authoritative, and the NSW Police Scrap Metal Industry Act public register dated 8 Sept 2026, which was read through a PDF-to-text fetch. Further sources are a Newcastle Herald advertising feature (Nov 2026), InfraBuild web pages and third-party directory listings. Directory listings are often stale. Where they describe the Dubbo yard, the wording is usually from the earlier "Matthews Metal Management" era.

## 1. Which company is "AMR Dubbo"? Does Australian Metal Recycling have a Dubbo site?

### Takeaway
The identification is confirmed with high confidence. "AMR Dubbo" is the yard at 34 Mountbatten Drive, Dubbo NSW 2830, which traded as Matthews Metal Management (MMM) until recently. It is now registered to Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd (ABN 51 640 765 091), which trades as "Australian Metal Recycling" (AMR) and as "Scrap Metal Services" (SMS). The company's ABN main business location has been Dubbo postcode 2830 since June 2025.

## 2. Who owns AMR? History, acquisitions, how SMS relates to AMR, and links to InfraBuild/GFG, Sims or others

### Takeaway
AMR is a brand of a small private NSW company, Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd, incorporated in 2020 and originally based in the Maitland area. In March–April 2025 it took on the Australian Metal Refiners (Central Coast) and Wilson Metal names. By mid-2025 it held the former InfraBuild-owned Matthews Metal Management regional yards, including Dubbo. Its shareholders and directors were not found publicly. No evidence links it to Sims, Sell & Parker or an overseas parent. Its tie to InfraBuild/GFG is that it took over InfraBuild's former MMM yards. The commercial terms of that hand-over (sale, lease or other) are undocumented.

### Cited Findings
- SMS is the legal entity, and "Australian Metal Recycling" is one of its registered business names:
  - Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd, ACN 640 765 091, registered 7 May 2020, Australian private company. — [ABN Lookup](https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View/51640765091)
  - Business names registered 30 Mar – 2 Apr 2025: Australian Metal Refiners, Metal Refiners, Wilson Metal Collections/Recyclers/Recycling, Australian Metal Recycling. — [ABN Lookup](https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View/51640765091)
  - Main business location was NSW 2323 (Maitland area) from 2020 to Apr 2025. — [ABN history](https://abr.business.gov.au/AbnHistory/View/51640765091)
- Brand roles: AMR is the yard and trade/business brand. SMS runs free booked household kerbside collections across Newcastle, Lake Macquarie, Maitland and the Central Coast. — [Newcastle Herald feature](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/9384032/turn-unwanted-metal-into-value-with-australian-metal-recycling/)
- "Australian Metal Refiners" was a Central Coast scrap dealer with yards at Wyong and Woy Woy, licence SM-0118. Its listing describes "a family business that has served the central coast for over 30 years". - InfraBuild (GFG Alliance / Liberty) ownership of MMM:
  - InfraBuild's ABN entity, OneSteel Recycling Pty Limited (ABN 28 002 707 262), registered the business names MATTHEWS METAL MANAGEMENT (12 Nov 2019), KEMPSEY SPARES and INVERELL SPARES (5 Nov 2019).   - An earlier InfraBuild page said: "our 21 InfraBuild recycling facilities are supplemented by **five sites in regional NSW** that operate as Matthews Metal Management – a brand of InfraBuild." — [InfraBuild (archived page id 18070)](https://infrabuild.com/?p=18070)

### Inferences
- InfraBuild appears to have acquired the Matthews Metal Management business around Nov 2019, judging by when the business names were registered. It ran five regional NSW MMM yards: probably Kempsey, Tamworth, Dubbo, Maclean and Metford/East Maitland. It kept East Maitland, rebranded as InfraBuild Recycling. The other four (Kempsey, Tamworth, Dubbo, Maclean) now appear under Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd.
- The registered manager for every SMS/AMR premises is named "Gordon Matthews". It is plausible, but **unconfirmed**, that the Matthews family behind the original MMM (est. 1985) is involved in AMR, either buying back the regional yards from InfraBuild or partnering in the business. Treat this as a hypothesis to check, e.g. by asking your AMR contact how AMR relates to the old Matthews business.
- The pattern looks like a regional roll-up: SMS (Hunter kerbside collections) plus Australian Metal Refiners (Central Coast yards) plus "Wilson Metal" (origin unknown) plus the four ex-MMM regional yards, all consolidated in 2025 under the AMR brand.
- AMR is a private regional operator. Nothing suggests private-equity backing or a listed or overseas parent, but this cannot be ruled out without an ASIC extract.

### Gaps
- Directors, shareholders and any holding company were not found. An ASIC company extract for ACN 640 765 091 (paid) would settle this.
- No press release, ACCC notice or trade-media story documents InfraBuild handing the MMM regional yards to SMS/AMR. The date, price and structure (asset sale, lease, licence) are unknown.
- The origin of the "Wilson Metal" names is unknown.

## 3. Size and footprint

### Takeaway
AMR is a small-to-mid regional NSW scrap metal operator with seven registered premises: Hunter, Central Coast, Mid North Coast, Northern Rivers, New England and Central West/Orana (Dubbo). It is NSW-only as far as found. Headcount and revenue are not public.

### Cited Findings
- Seven premises are on the NSW Police register: Wyong, Woy Woy, Kempsey, Tamworth, Dubbo, Maclean and Rutherford. — [NSW Police register, 8 Sept 2026](https://www.police.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/743500/Public_Register_8_September_2026.pdf)
- The AMR advertorial lists three public yards (Rutherford, Wyong, Woy Woy) for the Hunter/Central Coast. SMS kerbside collections cover Newcastle, Lake Macquarie, Maitland and the Central Coast. — [Newcastle Herald feature](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/9384032/turn-unwanted-metal-into-value-with-australian-metal-recycling/)
- For scale comparison:
  - InfraBuild Recycling has had 21–22 Australian sites handling about 1.4 Mt of scrap a year. — [Recycling Today](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/gfg-liberty-infrabuild-rebranding-uk-australia-steel-recycling); [InfraBuild](https://www.infrabuild.com/metal-recycling/)
  - Sims Metal says it has "40+" Australian facilities and "1,100+" employees. — [Sims Metal](https://www.simsmm.com/en-au/industries-we-serve/)

### Inferences
- With seven yards, AMR is far smaller than Sims or InfraBuild but larger than most single-yard independents. Dubbo is probably its only Central West/Orana site.

### Gaps
- No public employee count, tonnage or revenue figures were found. It is a private company with no public financial statements.
- No other states and no evidence of sites beyond the seven registered NSW premises.

## 5. What the Dubbo site does

### Takeaway
The Dubbo yard is a ferrous and non-ferrous scrap metal buying and drop-off yard. Older (MMM-era) listings show it took computers and accessories but not TVs, CRT monitors or printers. It also offered bin services, car bodies/car crushing, batteries and farm clean-ups. Current AMR-era services in Dubbo are not officially documented.

### Cited Findings
- Planet Ark's Recycling Near You listing for "Matthews Metal Management, 34 Mountbatten Drive, Dubbo":
  - Drop-off available; pick-up not available.
  - Residential and commercial quantities.
  - **Mon–Fri 8.00am–4.30pm; closed Sat, Sun and public holidays.**
  - "No TVs, CRT Monitors or Printers accepted".
  - Accepts aluminium (scrap and cans), cars and car parts, **computers and accessories**, copper, corrugated iron, electrical appliances, electrical cables, iron and steel, other metals and white goods.
  - Phone 1300 626 000; website matthewsmm.com.au (could not be fetched).
  - Listing date unknown, and it carries a "contact directly" disclaimer. — [Recycling Near You](https://recyclingnearyou.com.au/business/15852)
- The Localsearch Dubbo profile (MMM-era) says the yard collects "copper, brass, aluminium, batteries and a variety of e-waste". It invites people disposing of "computers, laptops, monitors, whitegoods, or IT products" to call.
  - Bins: scrap metal bins, NF bins, e-waste bins, crane bins, C/D bins and 20m hooklifts.
  - Services: car crushers, recycling of radiators, electric motors, stainless steel, car bodies and electronic waste.
  - Plant: a mobile car compactor, flat-top decks and tippers. — [Localsearch MMM Dubbo](https://localsearch.com.au/profile/matthews-metal-management/dubbo-nsw-2830/10E3)
- AMR network-wide services as of Nov 2026:
  - Yard drop-off of copper, brass, aluminium, steel, other metals, accepted e-waste and appliances.
  - Skip bins, plus a new free 1100-litre "Tradie Bin". It locks with the customer's own padlock, and AMR collects, weighs and pays for the metal. There is no sign-up fee, hire charge or contract.
  - SMS free household kerbside collections of scrap metal, appliances and accepted e-waste.
  - The advertorial describes these for the Hunter/Central Coast only. — [Newcastle Herald feature](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/9384032/turn-unwanted-metal-into-value-with-australian-metal-recycling/)
- Payment rules: the NSW Scrap Metal Industry Act 2016 bars dealers from paying cash for scrap metal, except aluminium cans. — [Localsearch MMM Kempsey FAQ](https://localsearch.com.au/profile/matthews-metal-management/south-kempsey-nsw/dD8u)

### Inferences
- For Josh's pitch: computers are a material the Dubbo yard already takes in, at least historically. The yard treats them as metal or e-waste feedstock, so a stream of whole units already exists that could be screened for reuse. TVs, CRTs and printers have historically been excluded, so the reuse pilot should focus on laptops and desktops.
- A scrap buyer values computers for their metal and circuit boards. Setting units aside for refurbishment could mean a small loss of material value for AMR. A clear "unusable gear comes back to AMR" loop, plus documented data erasure, answers the obvious commercial and liability concerns.

### Gaps
- Nothing confirms that current AMR-branded Dubbo hours, accepted items or e-waste policy match the MMM-era listings.
- No NSW EPA environment protection licence for 34 Mountbatten Drive was checked; the EPA register could not be reached. Whether the site needs an EPL depends on its throughput.
- Whether the Dubbo yard is an e-waste collection point under the National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme is undocumented.

## 7. Company culture and values

### Takeaway
No formal values statement was found, because no AMR or SMS website could be located. The only messaging is from the advertorial: accessibility, customers recovering value, keeping metal out of landfill, and a closed loop back into new steel, copper and aluminium.

### Cited Findings
- The advertorial says AMR "makes recycling more accessible", helps customers "recover value from unwanted metal" and keeps recyclable material out of landfill. It says collected metal enters the same recycling network and is reused to make new steel, copper and aluminium. The Tradie Bin pitch stresses transparency: owners can watch the weighing, with no fees or contracts. — [Newcastle Herald feature](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/9384032/turn-unwanted-metal-into-value-with-australian-metal-recycling/)

### Inferences
- The messaging focuses on value and convenience: getting paid for scrap, free bins and collections. It is not explicitly about circular-economy reuse. A pitch framed as "reuse first, and AMR still gets the metal back" fits that messaging better than one framed purely as environmental benefit.

### Gaps
- No official mission or values statement, ESG material, community or sponsorship programs, or reuse/repair partnerships were found.
