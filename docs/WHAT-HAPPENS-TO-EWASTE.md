# What happens to e-waste after it reaches AMR Dubbo?

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)  
**Scope:** TVs, computers/laptops, monitors, smartphones/tablets and printers/scanners accepted by Australian Metal Recycling (AMR), particularly the Dubbo yard at 34 Mountbatten Drive.

## Bottom line

We can now describe the **front of the chain** confidently, and the normal Australian **back end** in detail, but one crucial link is still not public:

> **AMR does not publicly identify the actual downstream e-waste recycler/facility that receives material from its Dubbo yard.**

### 2026 forensic follow-up

A deeper search of NTCRS/co-regulator records, historical Matthews contracts, current recycler partner lists and regional Central West processing routes still did **not** identify a current AMR Dubbo recipient.

What it did establish:
- historical Matthews Metal Management operated some e-waste collection under the **NTCRS**, with a historical Matthews listing pointing to **TechCollect** product rules;
- historical TechCollect NSW recycler pools included **Sims** and **TES-AMM**, but no Matthews load was mapped to one of them;
- current TechCollect/ANZRP NSW recyclers are **ACE Recycling Group** and **Sircel**;
- current TechCollect public NSW collection-partner information does **not** identify AMR/Matthews/Dubbo;
- **Sircel Parkes** is the strongest regional lead because Sircel currently processes Central West e-waste and has documented Parkes/Narromine/NetWaste activity, but **no AMR→Sircel evidence was found**;
- **ACE Recycling Group** is another credible current NSW processor, but again **no AMR→ACE evidence was found**;
- AMR's own **Rutherford** yard is a documented consolidation/processing hub, but AMR does not say Dubbo e-waste is routed there.

The full candidate-by-candidate evidence test is in [AMR-DUBBO-DOWNSTREAM-FORENSIC.md](AMR-DUBBO-DOWNSTREAM-FORENSIC.md).

The evidence therefore needs three labels:

- **[CONFIRMED — AMR]** AMR itself says it accepts the material / performs the step.
- **[AUSTRALIAN BENCHMARK]** this is how regulated/product-stewardship e-waste chains in Australia are documented to operate, but it is **not proof that AMR uses this exact downstream chain**.
- **[INFERENCE]** the evidence strongly points this way, but AMR has not published the missing operational detail.
- **[UNKNOWN]** requires AMR/downstream confirmation.

## 1. What definitely happens at the front end

### AMR takes these categories

**[CONFIRMED — AMR]** AMR's current material list says "Electronic waste excluding lithium ion" and explicitly lists:

- TVs
- computers / laptops
- monitors
- smart phones / tablets
- printers / scanners

AMR also says e-waste is recycled **for free**, while valuable scrap categories such as copper, aluminium, brass, lead, stainless steel, batteries, radiators and motors are categories it may pay for.

That means "free e-waste" should be read as **no disposal charge to the customer**, not "AMR pays for every e-waste item".

### Dubbo is one of AMR's regional yards

**[CONFIRMED — AMR]** Dubbo is at **34 Mountbatten Drive, Dubbo NSW 2830**, phone **(02) 6882 1033**. AMR says its regional yards provide door trade, weighing and material collection.

**[CONFIRMED — NSW Police]** Scrap Metal Services Pty Ltd / Australian Metal Recycling is registered as scrap metal dealer **SM-0759**, with 34 Mountbatten Drive listed as a registered yard.

### The first physical steps

AMR does not publish a Dubbo-specific process diagram for e-waste. Based on what it does publish:

1. Material arrives by customer drop-off or collection.
2. The yard accepts/rejects material against AMR's material rules.
3. Material is weighed where relevant and can be documented with weigh tickets.
4. Different scrap/material categories are segregated and consolidated.
5. Material leaves the regional network for processing / onward sale through recycling pathways.

Steps 1–4 are consistent with AMR's described door-trade and logistics operations. Step 5 is confirmed at company-network level: AMR describes recovered material being processed and returned to supply chains, and describes its Rutherford hub receiving consolidated material for "processing and onward sale".

**Important:** AMR has not published evidence that the **Dubbo yard itself** performs specialist e-waste dismantling, shredding, battery treatment, CRT treatment, precious-metal refining or data destruction.

## 3. What a proper Australian e-waste chain looks like after collection

This section is an **Australian benchmark**, not a claim that AMR uses TechCollect.

TechCollect, one of the approved National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme (NTCRS) operators, publicly documents the chain in unusually useful detail.

### Stage 1 — dismantle, shred and sort in Australia

**[AUSTRALIAN BENCHMARK]** First-stage recycling separates devices into streams such as:

- glass
- ferrous metals (steel/iron)
- non-ferrous metals (aluminium, copper etc.)
- plastics
- batteries
- printed circuit boards
- cables/wires
- ink and toner cartridges

This may involve manual dismantling, shredding and mechanical sorting.

### Stage 2 — specialised material processing

Separated fractions then go to specialist processors.

Some streams can be processed in Australia. TechCollect gives examples including:

- TV/monitor glass
- ink and toner
- laptop lithium-ion batteries
- selected metals and plastics

Other streams may go overseas because Australia does not have sufficient specialist downstream infrastructure for all fractions. TechCollect specifically says some plastics, metals and printed circuit boards go to approved facilities in Asia.

### The final outputs are commodities, not "old computers"

At the end of the chain, the original device has ceased to exist. Outputs can include:

- steel feedstock
- aluminium and copper alloys
- precious-metal-bearing refined products
- recovered lead
- glass aggregate / concrete inputs
- recycled plastics
- asphalt additives from some plastics/toner
- battery-material fractions

The useful endpoint is therefore not "the laptop is recycled into another laptop". It is **the laptop is separated into commodity streams which re-enter manufacturing**.

Typical recoverable streams include:

- printed circuit boards
- plastics

## 5. Why can AMR take e-waste "for free"?

There are three different economic mechanisms that can make free collection possible. Only one is directly confirmed for AMR.

### 1. Recoverable material value — confirmed principle

**[CONFIRMED — AMR]** AMR is a metal recycler. It says value varies with material type, grade, quantity, cost to collect/handle/process and commodity market conditions.

E-waste contains steel, aluminium, copper, circuit boards and other valuable fractions. Their recovered value can offset collection/processing costs.

But the amount of metal value does **not** mean every e-waste load is profitable by itself.

### 2. Product stewardship funding — how NTCRS works, but NOT confirmed for AMR

The Australian Government's **NTCRS** provides households and small businesses free recycling of TVs and computer products. It is **industry funded**: liable importers/manufacturers pay through approved co-regulatory arrangements for collection and recycling.

Under the scheme:
- approved co-regulatory arrangements must use recyclers certified to **AS 5377**;
- the material-recovery target is **90% by weight**;
- annual reporting includes collection, storage, recycling, exports and contracted service providers.

**Critical gap:** no current public source found in this research establishes that **AMR Dubbo is an NTCRS collection site or contracted AS 5377 recycler**.

So we should **not** claim AMR's current free service is NTCRS-funded unless AMR identifies the co-regulatory arrangement it works under.

### 3. Commercial/council contracts — plausible, not confirmed for current Dubbo e-waste

AMR works with businesses, councils and government clients and advertises compliant recycling pathways, weigh tickets and certificates of recycling for project work.

The old Matthews operation historically handled council e-waste in the region.

But Dubbo Regional Council's documented Matthews e-waste contract ended **30 June 2025**, and a replacement/current contract has not been found publicly.

**[UNKNOWN]** whether current Dubbo e-waste is subsidised by a council contract, NTCRS contract, commodity value, AMR absorbing the cost as part of its wider metal business, or a combination.

## 7. Data-bearing equipment: the important risk for Dubbo eWaste

AMR is advertising **material recycling**, not a secure IT asset disposition/data destruction service.

There is no current AMR page found that promises:

- NIST 800-88 sanitisation
- hard-drive shredding
- serialised destruction certificates
- chain of custody for drives
- destruction of residual customer data

Therefore Dubbo eWaste should:

1. sanitise HDD/SSD/NVMe itself using the agreed standard; or
2. physically remove data-bearing media and send it to a verified secure-destruction provider;
3. never rely on "it is going to recycling" as proof that data has been destroyed.

MobileMuster is different: its published phone recycling process explicitly says devices are physically destroyed and residual data is destroyed. That guarantee applies to **MobileMuster**, not AMR.

## 8. Overseas processing does not automatically mean dumping

A legitimate Australian recycling chain may export separated fractions for specialist recovery.

The Commonwealth says e-waste can be whole products, components or shredded material, and e-waste should be presumed hazardous unless shown otherwise. Hazardous e-waste exports are controlled under the **Hazardous Waste (Regulation of Exports and Imports) Act 1989** and Basel Convention arrangements.

Potential hazardous parts include:

- lead in CRT glass and solder
- cadmium
- mercury
- brominated flame-retardant plastics
- some batteries
- some photocopier/printer components

So a lawful downstream chain can include overseas processing, but it needs to comply with the relevant hazardous-waste/export controls.

TechCollect provides a useful example: it says first-stage recycling occurs in Australia, then some sorted fractions such as circuit boards and some plastics/metals go to approved Asian facilities because suitable Australian end-markets do not exist for everything.

Again, **AMR has not publicly disclosed its own export/downstream map**.

### The historical Sims/St Marys process is unusually explicit

NetWaste's 2023-2027 regional strategy records the then regional scrap-metal contractor as **Sims Metal Management**, with McCabe Transport as collection subcontractor. It says the e-waste option was **shredded at the St Marys depot**, with a trial involving E-cycle Solutions for further recovery/co-regulatory participation.

That gives a concrete historical chain:

**Council e-waste cage -> regional collection -> Sims St Marys -> shredding -> further material recovery.**

Do **not** label this the confirmed 2026 Dubbo route. The named contract dates have passed. NetWaste's current e-waste page still names Sims as the majority collector, but does not say that every current load still goes to St Marys.

### Parkes/Sircel: what specifically happens to the devices

Sircel's current published process is much more detailed:

1. **Collection and secure transport** to a Sircel facility.
2. **Receival and weighing**; each lot gets a tracking number.
3. **Triage**:
   - batteries, large plastics, steel and wood are removed/separated;
   - equipment suitable for reuse/repurposing is diverted to ITAD rather than destroyed.
4. **Data-bearing reusable devices** can be sanitised with Blancco and supplied with digitally signed erasure certificates.
5. End-of-life whole devices (without batteries) enter a **multi-stage mechanical size-reduction and separation process**.
6. Material is reduced as far as **<1 mm fractions**.
7. Separated commodities include **steel, aluminium, copper and other recoverable fractions**.
8. Sircel says it performs additional in-house metallurgical recovery of base, precious and platinum-group metals.
9. Recovered steel/copper and other commodities are sent back into manufacturing; plastics go to certified downstream manufacturing partners.
10. Batteries are handled separately through accredited downstream partners rather than through Sircel's standard mechanical line.

For a good laptop, the most important step is **step 3**. If it can be reused, the whole device can retain far more value than if it is mechanically reduced to metal/plastic/PCB fractions.

### What "recycled" looks like in real Australian NTCRS numbers

A useful audited-scale benchmark is **Ecycle Solutions' 2023-24 NTCRS annual report**. This is national Ecycle data, **not Dubbo-specific**.

- **45,617,361 kg** was recorded as recycled.
- **41,692,686 kg** became usable materials.
- **3,587,297 kg** went to landfill including downstream losses.
- **337,378 kg** remained as recycled material stored at recyclers at year end.
- The report states a **92.14% recovery/conversion rate**.
- It also reports **15,628,808 kg of reusable material exported** for use in new goods, including metals, circuit boards, plastics and hard drives.

So in a real large NTCRS chain, "recycled" does not mean every kilogram becomes a new product. Most becomes usable commodity feedstock, while a measurable residual still ends up in landfill.

### NTCRS sets the standard for TVs/computers

Under the National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme:

- approved co-regulatory arrangements must meet a **90% material recovery target** by weight;
- they must report collection, storage, recycling, exports and contracted service providers;
- scheme recyclers must meet the applicable **AS 5377** requirements.

That is the standard Dubbo E-Waste should seek from any downstream partner handling TVs/computers for business or school clients.

### Scrap metal and e-waste are now clearly separate contract questions

A late-2025 NetWaste tender for **Collection and Recycling of Scrap Metal (F4459)** was awarded by participating councils to **Access Recycling**. This is useful because it shows the regional metal contract changed.

However, this does **not** establish that Access Recycling replaced Sims for e-waste. NetWaste's current e-waste page still says Sims handles the majority of council e-waste. Treat **scrap metal** and **e-waste** as separate streams until a council confirms otherwise.

### The exact opportunity for Dubbo E-Waste

Council/recycler systems are very good at:

**collect -> consolidate -> shred/process -> recover commodities**

The missing layer is often:

**inspect -> test -> data-wipe -> repair -> redeploy/resell -> only recycle when genuinely end-of-life**

That is the strongest regional role for Dubbo E-Waste. A functioning 8 GB laptop with an SSD should ideally be captured **before** it becomes anonymous bulk e-waste.

### Reuse-first model deep dive

The most strategically relevant part of Sircel is not the industrial shredding line; it is the **decision system before destruction**. Sircel tracks incoming lots, triages devices for reuse, sanitises data with Blancco, hardware-tests reuse candidates, then routes passing devices to reuse/redeployment/charity/approved brokers. Its FY2023-24 ESG report records **2,350 items reused** and explicitly says significant numbers of incoming laptops and desktops were still working.

That workflow has now been researched separately as a potential core operating model for Dubbo E-Waste: [SIRCEL-ITAD-REUSE-MODEL.md](SIRCEL-ITAD-REUSE-MODEL.md).
