# Dubbo SME device-sourcing deep research — who is easiest to source retired IT from?

**Research date:** 6 October 2026  
**Scope:** Dubbo, NSW businesses in law, accounting, agribusiness, medical/allied health/dental and real estate.  
**Purpose:** rank realistic business-to-business sources of retired laptops, desktops and ordinary business workstations for a small reuse-first Dubbo ITAD/refurbishment pilot.

Companion dataset: [`../data/dubbo-sme-device-sourcing-prospects-2026-10-06.csv`](../data/dubbo-sme-device-sourcing-prospects-2026-10-06.csv)

This report complements the larger-institution research in [DUBBO-DEVICE-DISPOSAL-PROSPECTS.md](DUBBO-DEVICE-DISPOSAL-PROSPECTS.md) and the channel strategy in [DEEP-RESEARCH-2026-10-05-SOURCING-PARTNERSHIPS-BUSINESS.md](DEEP-RESEARCH-2026-10-05-SOURCING-PARTNERSHIPS-BUSINESS.md).

---

## Executive answer

Yes: **Dubbo SMEs and professional practices remain one of the strongest direct device-sourcing channels**, but the useful target is much narrower than “4,500 businesses”.

The best cold-outreach profile is:

> **locally controlled + desk-heavy + roughly 8–25 staff + commodity Windows/Mac endpoints + no obvious government/corporate disposal contract + an identifiable owner/partner/office manager.**

That combination usually beats a much larger branch of a national organisation. A 15-person independent accounting or real-estate office may be a better prospect than a 50-person corporate branch because the local principal may actually have authority to transfer title.

### Best first sectors

1. **Independent real estate** — best blend of local authority, endpoint density, business-class equipment and moderate data risk.
2. **Independent accounting** — excellent endpoint density and repeatability; stronger sanitisation expectations but usually reachable local principals.
3. **Independent agribusiness/property firms** — often locally controlled and relationship-driven; volumes vary widely.
4. **Local law firms** — good device quality and local control, but confidentiality raises the trust bar.
5. **Private medical/allied health/dental** — potentially useful fleets, but health information means a new operator should not lead with these until custody and sanitisation evidence is mature.
6. **National/corporate branches and banks** — fleet size can be attractive, but local staff may have no disposal authority at all.

### Best immediate prospect cluster

The strongest structural prospects found are **Dubbo Real Estate Agency, Bob Berry Real Estate, Martel Wheatley, Peter Milling & Company, Henderson McColl & Co, Western Plains Real Estate, Luka Group and Ray White Dubbo**.

This is **not evidence that any has equipment available**. No prospect is upgraded to “stock source” until the business confirms ownership, its incumbent route and the last/next refresh.

## What the numbers mean

Three different quantities are deliberately kept separate:

1. **Public staff evidence** — a team page, explicit employee count, or public company-size band.
2. **Estimated active commodity endpoints** — likely laptops/desktops/ordinary workstations used by the organisation.
3. **Annualised 3–5-year replacement equivalent** — a planning range only: endpoints divided across a 3–5 year cycle.

The annualised number is **not a prediction that devices arrive evenly every year**. SME refreshes are commonly lumpy: an office may replace nothing for two years and then release 8–15 machines at once.

Phones, monitors, docks, printers, servers, POS devices, dental/medical imaging systems, agricultural controllers and other specialist/vendor-owned hardware are **excluded** from the commodity endpoint estimate unless specifically known.

### Why a 3–5-year planning window?

It is used here as a **sensitivity model**, not as a claim about any named business. The ATO's effective-life materials illustrate rapid obsolescence for computers — including a two-year laptop effective life in employee guidance and four years in a desktop example — but tax effective life is not the same as an organisation's actual replacement policy.

Microsoft ended standard Windows 10 support on **14 October 2025**. Organisations can use paid commercial Extended Security Updates for a limited period, but the Windows 10 → Windows 11 transition creates a genuine present-day reason for organisations to inspect older hardware. It does **not** prove any named Dubbo firm is currently replacing PCs.

## Scoring model

Each prospect is scored from 0–100:

| Factor | Weight | What 5/5 means |
|---|---:|---|
| Local decision control | 25 | local owner/principal can plausibly authorise disposal |
| Procurement/disposal ease | 20 | no obvious central/government contract barrier |
| Usable volume | 20 | enough commodity endpoints to make a pickup worthwhile |
| Repeatability | 10 | likely recurring refresh rather than a one-off |
| Device quality | 10 | professional/business endpoints likely reusable |
| Data-handling ease | 10 | lower custody/sanitisation friction |
| Near-term refresh signal | 5 | business context makes near-term refresh plausible |

**Score is a prospecting score, not evidence of available stock.**

Data burden matters: accounting, legal and especially medical records should push the operating standard upward even where the local decision-maker is easy to reach.

# Sector deep dives

## 1. Independent real estate — strongest first cold-outreach sector

### Why it fits

A modern agency is unusually endpoint-dense for a small business:
- sales agents;
- property managers;
- administration;
- reception;
- principals;
- cloud CRM/property systems;
- email and document work;
- often mobile laptops in addition to phones.

The useful equipment is generally ordinary business IT rather than specialised industrial hardware. Client/property data still requires proper sanitisation, but the regulatory and contractual barrier is usually lower than health, banking or government.

### High-value local examples

**Dubbo Real Estate Agency** publicly presents 19 team members and describes itself as a family-owned independent agency after moving away from the Century 21 franchise model. That combination — meaningful staff count plus local ownership — makes it the strongest structural prospect in this study. Modelled endpoint stock: **17–24**; 3–5-year annualised equivalent: roughly **3–8**.

**Bob Berry Real Estate** publicly describes a local independent operation with a staff count around the mid-teens and a larger current team roster. Its public profile emphasises current ICT/database capability. Modelled endpoint stock: **16–22**; annualised equivalent **3–8**.

**Western Plains Real Estate** is much smaller but remains interesting because direct local authority may make a 5–9-device fleet easier to qualify than a larger corporate branch.

**Ray White Dubbo** has a substantial local team, but the franchise structure earns a lower control score until the Dubbo directors confirm who owns the devices and whether an MSP/head office controls disposal.

### First question

> When you last replaced staff computers, what happened to the old ones and who arranged it?

Then ask:
- office-owned versus leased;
- desktops versus laptops;
- local director authority;
- MSP name and whether disposal is included;
- next known hardware review;
- any Microsoft Intune/Autopilot or Apple Activation Lock;
- whether they need a serialised wipe report;
- whether they expect buyback/value return.

## 2. Accounting — nearly as strong, with a higher trust threshold

Accounting firms are excellent hardware prospects because most staff require a dedicated endpoint and multiple monitors, and the work remains computer-intensive.

### Martel Wheatley

Current public material shows **16 named team members** across Dubbo/Coonamble and a technology-heavy accounting environment with cloud software support. The business is privately held and regionally controlled.

Model:
- local/firm workforce relevant to device pool: **13–18**
- commodity endpoints: **13–19**
- annualised 3–5-year equivalent: **3–7**
- structural score: **89/100**

The unknown that matters most is whether an MSP already handles disposal.

### Henderson McColl & Co

A particularly interesting prospect because public staff material identifies an office-operations role that includes **managing IT systems**. This is exactly the kind of reachable internal decision-maker a small ITAD pilot needs.

Modelled commodity endpoints: **6–12**.

### Luka Group

The current public team roster is much larger, around the low 20s across several western NSW offices. That offers more volume but introduces multi-office coordination. Before treating the full fleet as a Dubbo opportunity, confirm whether procurement and disposal are centralised in Dubbo.

Modelled commodity endpoints: **15–25**, medium confidence.

### Wright Partners, Ryan & Rankmore, Pearce Martin

All are worthwhile secondary targets:
- Wright: boutique Dubbo firm, likely manageable local authority;
- Ryan & Rankmore: 25+ staff across multiple towns, but local Dubbo split is unclear;
- Pearce Martin: very easy local access but only a small 4–6 endpoint pool.

### Accounting barrier

Financial and tax records make “we wipe it later” an unacceptable pitch. The offer needs:
- signed chain of custody;
- serial manifest;
- defined media state at handoff;
- sanitisation standard/method;
- evidence/report;
- policy for failed drives;
- confirmation of Microsoft/Apple management release.

## 3. Agribusiness — highly variable, with some excellent independents

This sector should be split into **independent regional firms** and **branches of national agribusinesses**.

### Peter Milling & Company

The public company page names **8 Dubbo staff** and the firm is independently run. That is almost an ideal first agribusiness prospect: enough endpoints for a small batch, local leadership and ordinary office/property equipment.

Modelled commodity endpoints: **8–12**.

### IMAG Consulting

Public company-size data places the independent Dubbo consultancy at **2–10 employees**. The likely batch is small, but direct access may be excellent. Treat this as a relationship/early-pilot prospect rather than a volume source.

### Barlow & Peadon / Olsen C. Agencies

Both fit the local relationship profile but have weak public headcount evidence. They remain worthwhile qualification calls; do not manufacture a fleet count from the size of the livestock business.

### Elders Dubbo

The local branch page identifies around **10 local team members**, but its attractiveness falls sharply because Elders is a large national organisation. The devices may be centrally procured, managed and retired.

That makes Elders a **good question, not a good assumption**:
> Does the Dubbo branch have any authority over retired endpoints?

### Hutcheon & Pearce

The Dubbo branch is likely to have a useful number of endpoints, but a large multi-branch organisation plus specialist John Deere/ag-tech systems creates two complications:
1. ordinary office IT may be controlled centrally;
2. specialist/vendor systems should not be treated as generic resale stock.

### Delta Agribusiness

Potentially useful scale, but the Elders acquisition/integration adds ownership and procurement uncertainty. Qualify corporate authority before discussing pickup.

### Rabobank

The Dubbo branch publicly reports **14 employees**, so the raw volume looks good. It still ranks at the bottom of the cold-outreach list because a global bank's security/procurement controls make local title transfer implausible without central approval.

**Lesson:** employee count without disposal authority is almost worthless.

## 4. Law firms — good devices, but earn the trust first

Law is structurally attractive:
- dedicated endpoints;
- professional-grade hardware;
- locally owned/partner-led firms;
- repeat replacements.

The downside is obvious: client material can be acutely confidential. A law firm is not the place to test an informal wiping process.

### Duffy Elliott

Public material shows 5 lawyers plus support staff. Modelled active commodity endpoints: **8–15**. Strong local control, but approach only with a credible custody/sanitisation pack.

### Nelson Keane & Hemingway

The firm's public site states **17 employees** firm-wide and multiple western NSW offices. Modelled pool: **10–16** commodity endpoints. The main question is whether Dubbo controls disposal for all offices or only its local stock.

### Wilsons / Booth Brown / Macquarie Law

All merit direct qualification, but exact current public workforce data is weaker than the top accounting/real-estate prospects. Do not let a rough endpoint estimate become a claimed staff count.

### Minimum law-firm pitch

Do not lead with:
> Can I have your old laptops?

Lead with:
> I'm building a local retirement path for business IT with a serialised handoff, controlled custody and documented data sanitisation. I'm trying to understand what your firm currently does with machines after a refresh and what evidence you would require before changing that process.

## 5. Medical, dental and allied health — attractive stock, deliberately later

Health practices may have good PCs, but the **data barrier is categorically higher**.

The OAIC says a private health service provider is covered by the Privacy Act even if it would otherwise qualify for the small-business exemption. Health information is sensitive information.

Therefore a local practice's ownership does not make it an easy first source.

### DubboCare Family Practice

Public team material names around **10 clinical/reception staff**, with local ownership. Modelled ordinary endpoints: **8–14**.

Structurally this would otherwise be a strong target, but patient information and clinical-system access make it a **Tier D / mature-process prospect**.

### Dubbo Dental

Public material identifies roughly **11–12 team members** and describes the practice as locally owned. Modelled commodity endpoints: **10–15**, excluding imaging and specialist dental systems.

The first task is separating:
- ordinary office PCs/laptops;
- treatment-room endpoints;
- imaging workstations;
- vendor-owned or contract-controlled clinical equipment.

### Parkside Dental

Smaller, local and independent. That improves authority but not the privacy burden.

### Dubbo Medical & Allied Health Group

Potentially larger across two clinic sites, but the public structure includes visiting specialists and other services. It is unsafe to assume every visible endpoint belongs to one practice entity.

### Correct sequencing

Only approach private health as a serious intake prospect after the pilot can demonstrate:
- controlled storage;
- chain of custody;
- repeatable sanitisation evidence;
- failed-media handling;
- no casual access to live patient data;
- a clear answer to onsite wipe/destruction requirements.

# Refresh-cycle analysis

## What is actually known

For almost every named Dubbo SME, the exact current refresh cycle is **not public**.

That is the single most important direct-research gap.

A team page can establish likely scale. It cannot establish:
- purchase dates;
- lease terms;
- Windows versions;
- warranty state;
- device age;
- next budget date;
- whether old devices were traded in;
- whether an MSP already owns the downstream process.

### Near-term market signal: Windows 10

Microsoft ended Windows 10 support on 14 October 2025. Commercial Extended Security Updates can extend security coverage temporarily, but this is a current hardware-review trigger for machines that cannot move cleanly to Windows 11.

For a Dubbo prospect, ask:

> Did the Windows 10 end-of-support transition cause you to replace any machines, or are older PCs still being worked through?

Do **not** say:
> You must have a pile of Windows 10 PCs.

### EOFY

EOFY can influence business capital spending, but no named firm's purchasing calendar was established. Treat **July–September** as a period worth asking about, not as a proven local supply season.

### Best data to capture

For every qualified business:
- number of active laptops;
- number of active desktops;
- average fleet age;
- last replacement month/year;
- number replaced last batch;
- next intended refresh;
- owned/leased;
- standard make/model;
- MSP/vendor;
- warranty/lease-return requirement;
- current disposal destination.

After 10–20 calls, replace the modelling assumptions in this report with real Dubbo cycle data.

# Device-volume model

The most valuable discovery is not “which business is biggest?” It is:

> **How many owned commodity endpoints can the local decision-maker legally release in one controlled batch?**

Examples from this study:

| Office profile | Active endpoint model | 3–5-year annualised equivalent | What a real release may look like |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 4–6 person boutique | 4–7 | ~1–2/yr | often 3–6 at once |
| 8–12 person professional practice | 8–15 | ~2–5/yr | often 5–12 at once |
| 15–20 person office | 15–24 | ~3–8/yr | often 8–20 at once |
| 20–30 person multi-office firm | 20–35 | ~4–12/yr | potentially 10–30, but central approval more likely |

These are planning ranges, not verified inventory.

A 10-device batch of four-year-old Latitude/EliteBook/ThinkPad-class machines may be far more useful than 50 random consumer or specialised devices.

# One-question opener

The most productive neutral opener remains:

> **When you last replaced computers or phones, what happened to the old ones, and who arranged it?**

It avoids prematurely selling a service and reveals:
- whether the machines are actually owned;
- whether an MSP controls the route;
- whether an ITAD already pays for them;
- whether devices sit in cupboards;
- whether the real problem is data, logistics, value recovery or approval.

Then ask:

> If you could change one thing about that process, what would it be?

# What would make a prospect move up or down?

## Upgrade to HIGH-CONFIDENCE SOURCE only after evidence of:
- local or reachable authority to transfer title;
- an owned fleet;
- a known next/recurring refresh;
- acceptable models/ages;
- no exclusive disposal route;
- manageable data requirement;
- signed handoff possible.

## Downgrade if:
- all devices leased;
- national IT controls disposal;
- incumbent ITAD already provides strong buyback;
- specialist/vendor hardware dominates;
- only very old low-value equipment is released;
- business expects guaranteed value before inspection;
- secure onsite destruction is required and the pilot cannot provide it.

# Practical conclusion

The earlier repo statement that SMEs are a **high-value target segment** is supported, but it needed refinement.

The evidence now points to a narrower sweet spot:

> **Dubbo independent professional firms with roughly 8–25 desk-based staff are the best direct prospecting pool.**

They combine:
- enough devices to matter;
- local decision-makers;
- repeatable technology use;
- good reuse potential;
- less procurement friction than institutions.

The highest-value next research is no longer more web searching. It is **ten consistent real conversations** to replace estimated refresh cycles and device counts with observed Dubbo data.

---

# Evidence limits

- Public team rosters are snapshots and may include contractors, owners or staff from several offices.
- “Named team members” is not always identical to payroll headcount.
- Device estimates are explicitly derived, not claimed inventories.
- No named prospect has confirmed willingness to transfer equipment.
- No named prospect's current ITAD/MSP disposal contract was established unless stated.
- A Windows 10 market signal does not prove a particular organisation has incompatible machines.
- Public company-size bands such as LinkedIn are secondary evidence and may lag.
- Health, legal and financial data risk must be resolved operationally rather than inferred away from public research.
