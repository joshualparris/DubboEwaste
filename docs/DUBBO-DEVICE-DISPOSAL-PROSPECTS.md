# What Dubbo prospects do with retired devices

Research checked **2 October 2026**. Scope: the eight named private/community organisations and six institutional prospect groups discussed for a Dubbo collection and relationship-building role. “Government departments” is scoped to NSW because no individual agency was named. No organisation has been contacted; no equipment, contract or partnership is secured.

The strongest public evidence is for **CSU's controlled disposal process and historical resale channels**, **TAFE's historical recovery arrangement**, and **central NSW Health and Education rules**. For the eight private/community prospects, this research did **not establish a current device-disposal provider or the destination of the last retired batch**. That is a gap in public evidence, not evidence that they lack a provider or have free stock.

Sources are numbered below and linked in the [source register](DUBBO-DEVICE-DISPOSAL-SOURCES.md). Key positive claims are recorded as `EVD-DISPOSAL-001`–`025` in [the evidence register](../data/evidence.yaml). The [CSV tracker](dubbo-device-disposal-prospects.csv) contains all 14 prospects and the question needed to move each forward.

## 1. MAAS Group Holdings

**What was found.** MAAS publishes a Dubbo head-office contact [S01]. Heidelberg Materials' current announcement says it has completed the acquisition of MAAS's Construction Materials business, including more than 1,000 employees, with existing brands and contacts continuing [S02]. The FY2026 annual report described the sale as pending at its reporting date [S03]; the later completion announcement takes precedence for this ownership question. None of these sources establishes an IT-disposal provider.

**Implication — inference.** Historical group headcounts cannot be converted into a current MAAS-owned device pool. A construction-materials site's devices may now belong to the acquired business. A transition could create IT work, but no refresh or surplus batch was established.

**Ask Group IT/finance:** “For the Dubbo office and local sites, which entity owns the devices now, and what happened to your last retired laptop/desktop batch?” Then request the disposal supplier, whether the arrangement includes resale credit, and whether local collections are controlled centrally. Treat continuing MAAS and acquired operations as separate opportunities after ownership is clarified.

## 2. Fletcher International Exports

**What was found.** Fletcher's company history records a computerised commodity-trading-system development in 2015–16 [S04]. Its environmental-certificates page and the examined 2024 EPA reported-data document concern environmental monitoring; they do not identify an electronic-asset recovery service [S05–S06]. A freight capability is not evidence of an offered IT-collection service.

**Unknown.** Current device fleet, refresh cycle, disposal contractor, storage backlog, lease status and revenue-return arrangements.

**Ask IT and finance through the official contact page [S35]:** “Who signed off the last office-device disposal, who collected it, and was it sold, returned, donated or recycled?” Separate office laptops/desktops from production equipment. Request rough counts by device type only after the owner confirms a batch exists. This is an initial qualification prospect, not a verified source of volume.

## 3. Westhaven

**What was found.** Google's customer case study describes a 2019 Workspace migration supported by Deloitte and a replacement of ageing equipment with **70 HP Chromebooks supplied through Unitech Solutions** [S07]. It also describes Android devices and a further administrative Chromebook rollout as a plan at that time. The 2025 annual report describes a much larger regional workforce [S08], but employees are not a device count. The case study does not say where the replaced equipment went.

**Useful lead.** Unitech is a historically identified procurement supplier. It is not established as Westhaven's current managed IT provider or disposal contractor. Historical named IT leaders should not be assumed to hold today's role.

**Ask the current IT lead:** “When those older machines were replaced, did Unitech take them back, did another recovery company collect them, or did you dispose of them internally?” Follow with the most recent refresh outcome, current supplier, owned versus leased devices, phones/tablets, and whether retired Chromebooks remain useful under their support and management arrangements. Of the private prospects, this has the clearest published replacement clue and is a sensible first conversation.

## 4. Earlyrise Baking Company

**What was found.** Its sustainability page covers energy, refrigeration and packaging rather than a device-disposal arrangement [S09]. An undated historical case study on DSD Assist describes warehouse and mobile-delivery systems involving **Numeric Computer Systems (NCS)** [S10]. This supports asking about handheld equipment; it does not establish a large retired-laptop pool or an NCS disposal contract.

**Ask the IT/operations systems owner via the published contact page [S11]:** “Who owns the delivery handhelds and warehouse devices, and what happens when they are replaced?” Qualify office PCs separately. Ask whether retired handhelds go back to a vendor, have commercial resale demand, and can be released from device management. Decide whether that equipment fits the processing partner's accepted categories before proposing collection. No refresh date or available batch was found.

## 5. Dubbo RSL Memorial Club

**What was found.** The club publishes group venues and management roles, including a CFO [S12–S13]. Its environmental policy is generic and does not identify an IT recovery contractor [S14]. An annual-report URL previously presented as a 2026 report returned 404 during this research; it is not evidence of a current disposal practice. The Memorial Club should also not be conflated with a separate RSL sub-branch computer initiative.

**Ask CFO/finance and IT:** “Who owns and disposes of office laptops, desktops and phones across the Dubbo venues, and who collected the last batch?” Ask separately about supplier-owned POS, payment and gaming equipment. A group of venues may create collection efficiencies, but neither combined ownership nor device quantities was established. Use the switchboard to reach the asset owner rather than sending a device proposal to the functions contact.

## 6. Inland Petroleum

**What was found.** Inland's official site describes a locally based multi-site business [S16]. The terms page credits **Reliance Technology** in its body and **P.S. Websites and Design Dubbo** in its footer [S15]. These are website-development clues with inconsistent presentation; neither proves a current whole-of-business IT or disposal relationship.

**Ask business services/IT:** “Who manages your computers across the sites, and what did they do with the last retired batch?” If an MSP handles everything, ask whether disposal is part of that agreement and whether a local pickup route would be useful. Qualify office computers and company-owned mobiles separately from operational equipment. Do not approach Reliance claiming it is Inland's verified MSP.

## 7. Dubbo City Toyota

**What was found.** The dealership says it operates in partnership with Servco Australia [S17]. The public ABN record identifies **Servco Australia Dubbo Pty Ltd**, with business names including Dubbo City Toyota, Gilgandra Toyota and Lexus of Central West [S18]. This establishes an ownership/approval lead, not a disposal supplier.

**Ask dealer management/finance:** “Is retired office IT handled locally or through Servco's group IT team, and who owns the machines?” Follow with the most recent destination, leased equipment, resale return and supplier restrictions. Treat diagnostic equipment and other vendor-controlled systems separately. A local dealer relationship may open the door; it does not establish authority to release group assets or equipment from the other business names.

## 8. Midwest Foods & Liquor / Bidfood Dubbo

**What was found.** The current official branch page presents **Bidfood Dubbo**, at 2 Capital Drive, under a title that also references Midwest Foods & Liquor [S19]. Bidfood Australia's sustainability page describes broader environmental activity without identifying this branch's retired-device route [S20]. The branch page alone does not date or prove a legal ownership transfer.

**Ask the branch manager:** “Does national IT arrange your device replacements and disposal, or does Dubbo appoint its own contractor?” Then seek the responsible group asset contact, last collection outcome and any local collection subcontracting opportunity. Warehouse handhelds and office computers should be counted separately. Disregard disposal policies for unrelated overseas businesses called Midwest Foods and for Bidfood in other countries.

## 9. Dubbo Hospital / Western NSW Local Health District

**What was found.** NSW Health's August 2025 Privacy Manual, section 9.2.3.4, requires eHealth guidance and approved providers for disposal/destruction, appropriate removal of personal information before reuse/recycling/lease return, and retained destruction evidence for redundant computer storage [S21]. It does not say every computer chassis must be shredded. eHealth's 2026 sustainability account shows retirement activity in other LHDs [S22]; those examples do not establish Western NSW's supplier or Dubbo volume.

**Ask the LHD ICT asset/procurement team:** “Which approved provider currently collects Western NSW's retired devices, and does its contract permit a local collection subcontractor?” Request the current reuse versus storage-destruction requirements and who owns disposal proceeds. A processing firm's general certifications are not proof of approval under this particular contract. Position any proposal through the authorised provider/procurement route after confirming eligibility.

## 10. Charles Sturt University — Dubbo

**Current route.** CSU's current purchasing/disposal procedure routes surplus through the **Computer Shop**, or approved internal redeployment. Staff cannot independently donate or sell university equipment. Disposal uses asset paperwork and an IT service request; the Computer Shop removes personal/confidential information and university software [S23]. CSU's public e-waste page also directs campuses to that process [S24].

**Historical destination detail.** The Q3 2019 Computer Shop report describes donations, public auctions and e-waste. It names **ALLBIDS** and **Industry Trading** for sales, alongside council recycling at Bathurst/Albury and Kurrajong at Wagga. Industry Trading reportedly paid upfront and covered courier costs at that time [S25]. These are historical university-wide channels, not a current Dubbo supplier list.

**Ask the Computer Shop:** “Are ALLBIDS or Industry Trading still used, what currently happens to Dubbo-origin devices, and can a local collector support your approved process?” Establish ownership transfer, residual-value allocation, eligible charitable routes and the economics of any collection contract. This prospect already has a documented recovery system; the opportunity is improving local logistics or an authorised reuse route, not taking unapproved campus surplus.

## 11. TAFE NSW — Dubbo

**What was found.** A 30 August 2023 report describes Dell's statewide refresh and removal of displaced non-Dell equipment for recycling [S26]. A public project description by a Greenbox participant, headed “TAFE NSW – IT Refresh” and starting May 2019, describes **Greenbox** receiving/deploying equipment, collecting legacy assets, sanitising/testing them and remarketing with proceeds returned to TAFE [S27]. Its open-ended “Present” label is not proof of a current contract. Direct profile access was rate-limited; the project text was available in search results, so this is a **medium-confidence research lead**. Greenbox's official recovery offering is consistent with these services but does not independently confirm TAFE as a customer [S28].

The 2024–25 TAFE annual report mentions training employees of Green Technology Recycling [S36]. That is a training relationship, not evidence that it disposes of TAFE's own equipment. General waste contracts likewise do not prove an IT recovery contract.

**Ask Digital Solutions/procurement:** “Is the Dell/Greenbox recovery arrangement still active, does it cover Dubbo, and who controls local collections and resale returns?” Confirm campus authority before proposing equipment acquisition. Recycling and remarketing accounts should be reconciled by the contract owner; neither proves the fate of every device.

## 12. Dubbo Regional Council

**What was found.** Whylandra's public page describes an e-waste acceptance stream [S29]. It does not establish that Council's own staff computers use that stream, or name their corporate IT contractor. The June 2025 “Laptop to Launch” announcement concerns digital training [S30]; it does not document Council donating retired laptops.

**Ask corporate ICT and procurement:** “What was the disposal route for the last staff-device refresh, who collected it, and was any value recovered?” Request the relevant agreement/tender reference, contract renewal timing and whether local pickup or authorised reuse can be considered. Separately, Council/community teams may know recipients for refurbished devices, but that is a recipient lead rather than a verified supply source. Do not turn household drop-off arrangements into claims about Council-owned equipment.

## 13. NSW government departments

**What was found.** NSW's current **C9826** end-user-device contract includes disposal and sanitisation services and options involving reuse/sale/donation, recovery and recycling. Offsite handling requires customer consent and unsanitised devices cannot be sent offshore under its conditions [S31]. This is an available framework, not proof that every agency uses the same supplier or that specialised clinical equipment is covered.

The **NSW Device Bank**, announced 21 May 2026, is a 12-month surplus-government-laptop reuse pilot involving NSW Telco Authority and **WorkVentures**, with secure preparation and distribution through community organisations/charities [S32]. It is an established alternative reuse route; it is not evidence that all agencies must send all devices there or that Dubbo receives a particular allocation.

**Next step:** identify a specific agency before estimating supply. Ask its asset/procurement owner for its existing disposal framework, Device Bank participation, lease returns and authorised contractor. An authorised regional logistics role is more plausible than asking a local office to release government assets informally; that is a planning judgement, not a confirmed opportunity.

## 14. NSW public schools in Dubbo/Orana

**What was found.** The Department's current technology procedure provides internal redistribution of unused allocated devices and a **dedicated eWaste contract through EDConnect** [S33]. The eWaste application guide retrieved earlier in this research describes regional collection scheduling, vendor hard-drive sanitisation, a sanitisation certificate and a Settlement Report [S34]. A later direct open of the guide URL redirected to Technology for Learning, so its current location/applicability needs confirmation. Neither source names the contractor or the final Dubbo downstream destination. The documents do not establish that every laptop is physically destroyed.

**Important unresolved instruction difference.** The technology procedure requires configuration/storage wiping before disposal. The operational guide says schools no longer need to perform hard-drive wiping/destruction because the vendor sanitises collected drives. Confirm the applicable preparation steps with Education's Digital Field/EDConnect team; do not use the guide to override the policy or infer permission for an unwiped release. The [existing education report](NSW-EDUCATION-EWASTE-DUBBO-LAPTOPS.md) now carries this qualification.

**Ask the central service/contract owner:** “Who is the current vendor, where do Dubbo devices go, what reuse outcomes are recorded, and can an approved regional collection partner participate?” Schools are not independently verified donors. Private schools need their own ownership/policy assessment and are outside this public-school finding.

## Recommended qualification order

This order is a **research judgement** based on the quality of the published lead and likely approval path. It is not a forecast of stock, profit or willingness to work with Josh.

1. **Westhaven:** strongest private-sector replacement clue; resolve Unitech's current role and last-batch destination.
2. **Fletcher, RSL, Inland and Earlyrise:** establish the asset owner and current route through local business contacts. Prioritise office devices, then qualify specialist equipment.
3. **MAAS:** make the ownership/IT responsibility check early; pursue an actual batch only after the entity split is clear.
4. **Toyota and Bidfood:** qualify locally, then seek the correct group IT owner where required.
5. **CSU and Council:** request their formal current disposal process and whether regional logistics or approved reuse offers any opening.
6. **TAFE, Health, schools and named NSW agencies:** investigate incumbent/contract-owner arrangements as partner-led opportunities. Existing recovery programmes reduce the case for informal surplus access.

No volumes, purchase prices, collection fees or annual refresh dates were established for these local prospects. Don't build financial projections from staff numbers or historical statewide purchases.

## Evidence needed from the next conversation

For each prospect, capture: legal asset owner; current supplier and contract scope; what happened to the last batch; device types/counts/condition; owned versus leased; next known release date; storage/data requirements; current value return or fees; authority to transfer title; and whether a local collection arrangement is permitted. Ask for a redacted asset list, disposal report or relevant contract reference where available, rather than client data.

A first-call question that exposes the actual route is: **“When you last replaced computers or phones, what happened to the old ones, and who arranged it?”** Then ask what they would change about the current service. Record the answer as a new dated evidence entry; do not upgrade a historical supplier to a current incumbent without confirmation.

This report supports prospect qualification. It does not alter [CURRENT-STATE.md](CURRENT-STATE.md), establish processing-partner authority or prove that operational launch gates have been met.
