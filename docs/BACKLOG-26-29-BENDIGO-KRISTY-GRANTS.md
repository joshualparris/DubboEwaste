# 27. The Painted Brush & Co / Elise Parker

## Why this matters

Elise's history is valuable to this project because it is an unusually clear **microbusiness-to-premises** progression.

The key lesson is not furniture specifically.

It is:

> **hobby -> social proof -> small paid work -> use existing home space -> expand the product/service mix -> dedicated retail space -> warehouse only after demand**

## First-person business history supplied to this project

The Painted Brush & Co story supplied for this research gives the following chronology:

- **October 2015:** established as **Miss Scotts Furniture Adoptions**;
- initially just a Facebook page documenting a hobby;
- intended to earn a small amount to support the family;
- the activity grew from hobby into full-time work;
- **2017:** move from Melbourne to Bendigo;
- **September 2017:** rebrand to **The Painted Brush & Co** and begin stocking paints/finishes;
- business operated for about 12 months from a roughly **900 mm x 2 m hallway** in a rental;
- **2018:** move into first owned home and use an approximately **11 m x 3 m shed** as the studio;
- **July 2021:** open The Painted Brush & Co Studio in Eaglehawk;
- **2024:** move into a warehouse and, in the same family-business period, "kickstarted Bendigo E-Waste & Bendigo TV Repairs";
- **2025:** move into a bigger warehouse / take on a third warehouse on the site.

These details are useful **first-person source material**, but not every individual date/detail was independently recoverable from public web pages in this research pass.

## Independent corroboration

A 2021 local-business article identifies Elise Parker as the founder of The Painted Brush & Co and documents the business setting up a physical shop in Eaglehawk in 2021.

The current Parker family trust also has **THE PAINTED BRUSH & CO** as a registered business name from 1 March 2024.

Sources:
- https://www.3556magazine.com.au/category/local-business/
- https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View?abn=39160094928

## Important 2026 update: growth is not always one-way

The current Painted Brush site says:

> **Studio Closed - Online Only**

and states that from **1 February 2026** the physical studio closed for the foreseeable future, with the business continuing **online only on a much smaller scale**.

It also says local customers no longer have click-and-collect but can use flat-rate **$5 local delivery**.

Source:
- https://www.thepaintedbrush.com.au/

### This is strategically important

The full Painted Brush story is not merely:

> hallway -> shed -> shop -> warehouse -> bigger forever.

It is:

> **start tiny -> scale when demand/life supports it -> later deliberately reduce fixed overhead/physical retail when a smaller online model suits the business better.**

That is arguably even more relevant to a family microbusiness.

## What can be copied

- start with existing skill/interest;
- create a public record/social page before a store;
- let real sales justify further stock;
- use available home space before rent;
- add products/services only after customers exist;
- move into premises when home constraints become real;
- retain the option to shrink or return online if premises stop making sense.

## What cannot be assumed

Public sources do not establish:

- monthly revenue at each stage;
- profitability of the hallway/shed/shop;
- customer-acquisition cost;
- inventory investment;
- exact rent;
- how much of growth came from furniture versus paint/finishes/workshops;
- whether each move was forced by sales volume, family needs or another factor.

Those are business-history questions for Elise.

## Online selling economics

### Etsy

Current Etsy Australia fee components include:

- **6.5% transaction fee** on listing price plus charged shipping/gift wrap;
- Etsy Payments for an Australian bank account on domestic orders: **3% + AU$0.25**;
- additional fees may apply for listing, advertising, currency conversion and other services;
- currency conversion is **2.5%** where listing/payment-account currencies differ.

Sources:
- https://www.etsy.com/au/legal/fees
- https://www.etsy.com/legal/etsy-payments/

Therefore an ordinary Australian Etsy sale can lose materially more than 6.5% once payment processing and any other applicable fees are included.

### Madeit

Madeit is an Australian handmade marketplace.

Current published pricing:

- Hobby: **$15/month**;
- Maker: **$20/month**;
- Business: **$25/month**;
- higher tier: **$45/month**;
- flat payment/transaction fee: **2% + $0.35/order**;
- platform says zero commissions.

It requires handmade/original-maker compliance and no longer permits digital products from 30 June 2026.

Sources:
- https://madeit.com.au/pages/sell-handmade-on-madeit
- https://madeit.com.au/pages/seller-terms-and-conditions

### Implication

For a first six-piece test, neither Etsy nor Madeit is necessary.

Social/local direct sales remain the cheapest demand-validation channel.

## Children's workshops

If Kristy later runs children's creative workshops, NSW says a Working with Children Check is required where the work is **child-related work**, including services for under-18s that normally involve face-to-face contact that is more than incidental.

A paid WWCC currently costs **$112** and is valid for 5 years.

Sources:
- https://ocg.nsw.gov.au/working-children-check/who-needs-check
- https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/apply-for-a-working-with-children-check

Do not assume every family market stall needs a WWCC; the trigger is child-related work.

## Paint-and-sip / alcohol

If a future workshop **sells, serves or supplies alcohol**, liquor licensing and Responsible Service of Alcohol requirements can apply.

Current NSW on-premises licensing guidance says people involved in selling/serving/supplying alcohol generally need RSA.

Source:
- https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/liquor-and-gaming/liquor-licensing/licence-types/on-premises

The low-friction pilot should avoid alcohol and use an existing licensed venue if that model is explored later.

## Updated Kristy sequence

The strongest low-risk sequence is:

1. make 6–10 finished pieces;
2. sell directly/socially;
3. track time/materials/profit;
4. test one market/public selling day;
5. identify repeatable demand;
6. add prints/cards only from proven designs;
7. accept commissions selectively;
8. try an externally hosted adult workshop if she actually wants to teach;
9. consider children's workshops only with WWCC/child-safe requirements addressed;
10. consider grants only for genuinely eligible arts/community projects;
11. dedicated premises only when demand and enjoyment justify fixed overhead.

# 29. Grants and government support

## E-waste / circular economy

### Bin Trim Equipment Rebates — closed, but important precedent

NSW EPA says the Bin Trim Equipment Rebates program is **closed**.

Historically it provided:

- **$1,000–$50,000** rebates;
- up to **50%** of eligible capital cost;
- equipment/technology that avoids, reduces, reuses or recycles business waste.

Source:
- https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/Working-together/Grants/business-recycling/bin-trim-rebates-program

### Direct e-waste precedent

EPA recipient records show:

- **Green Technology Recycling** received **$24,995** for an **e-waste shredder**;
- **Revolve Recycling** received **$775** for e-waste fire-safety materials.

This proves NSW has previously funded e-waste-specific business equipment under Bin Trim.

It does **not** mean Dubbo E-Waste should buy shredding equipment or that the closed program is currently available.

Source:
- same Bin Trim recipient record.

### Bin Trim app/support

EPA's Bin Trim business tools remain available for businesses to assess waste and identify reuse/recycling opportunities.

Source:
- https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/Your-environment/Recycling-and-reuse/business-government-recycling/bin-trim

This is more relevant once Dubbo E-Waste has an operating site/business waste stream.
