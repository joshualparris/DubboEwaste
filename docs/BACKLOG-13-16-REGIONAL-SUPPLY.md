## 15.2 Later Dubbo annual reports

The 2024/25 Annual Report reports broader waste/recycling totals but the indexed report does **not** provide the same explicit e-waste category breakout found in 2021/22 and 2022/23.

Therefore:

**Do not estimate 2026 Dubbo e-waste tonnage by applying a ratio to total recycling.**

The public series is not comparable enough.

## 15.3 What the 18–27 tonne figures actually mean

They represent e-waste material recorded by Council and sent off site in those reporting years.

They do **not** equal:
- all Dubbo-generated e-waste
- all commercial e-waste
- Officeworks/retail take-back
- private scrap/recycler flows
- equipment retained in households
- reused/resold computers
- stockpiled devices.

They are therefore a **minimum visible Council-managed stream**, not total market supply.

## 15.5 National e-waste growth

Current Commonwealth e-stewardship guidance says:
- Australia generated **511,000 tonnes** of e-waste in 2019
- projected to grow almost 30% to **657,000 tonnes by 2030**.

An earlier detailed Commonwealth evidence model estimated:
- 521,000 tonnes in 2019
- 20.4 kg per capita
- 674,000 tonnes in 2030
- 23.4 kg per capita.

The numerical difference comes from different datasets/model versions. Use the current Commonwealth headline for simple communication and the evidence report for modelling context.

Sources:
- https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/protection/waste/e-waste
- https://www.dcceew.gov.au/sites/default/files/env/pages/d347d42b-d755-481b-bc38-71683b5d1958/files/e-stewardship-evidence-report-2021.pdf

## 15.7 Reusable share

Australian studies support reuse as a higher-order waste-hierarchy outcome, but no reliable source found gives a universal percentage of regional Council e-waste that Dubbo eWaste could profitably refurbish.

That percentage depends heavily on:
- source
- age
- battery condition
- activation/MDM locks
- whether valuable devices were removed before reaching the tip
- storage/weather exposure.

The first 20–30-item pilot remains the correct way to establish the local reusable share.

# 16. Potential supply sources

## 16.1 NSW public schools — volume exists, but the stream is contract-captured

This is one of the most important findings.

The NSW Department of Education's **Technology in schools procedures**, current in June 2026, says:

- principals must keep disposal/deactivation records
- memory/data must be correctly wiped
- configuration/storage must be securely wiped before disposal
- **the Department has a dedicated eWaste contract for secure disposal of Department digital assets**
- schools request e-waste collection through **EDConnect**.

Source:  
https://education.nsw.gov.au/policy-library/policies/pd-2024-0481-01

### Backlog evidence

A 2025 Technology for Learning update said there was a **backlog of schools requesting eWaste collection** and the Department was working with the vendor through the backlog.

Source:  
https://education.nsw.gov.au/teaching-and-learning/technology-for-learning/news-t4l/2025/issue122

### Meaning for Dubbo eWaste

NSW public schools should **not** be treated as an easy donation source.

A local school cannot simply bypass the Department's dedicated disposal process because Dubbo eWaste offers free collection.

Future opportunity would need to be one of:
- approved vendor/subcontractor
- formal Department arrangement
- a permitted reuse pathway inside Department policy
- future procurement.

### Supply implication

The backlog proves substantial redundant equipment exists, but it is already inside a controlled institutional stream.

## 16.2 Independent/non-government schools

The position is more flexible but governance matters.

AISNSW material discussing NSW not-for-profit school regulation notes that disposal of school assets below reasonable market value can raise not-for-profit compliance questions.

It gives a concrete example of a metropolitan school wanting to donate old operational computers to a lower-fee regional school and argues such low-value school-to-school transfers should be permitted.

Source:  
https://www.aisnsw.edu.au/Resources/WAL%204%20%5BOpen%20Access%5D/Non-Government%20Schools%20Not-for-Profit%20Regulatory%20Framework%20development%20and%20Not-for-Profit%20Guidelines%20review.pdf

### Meaning

Independent schools may be potential sources, but Dubbo eWaste should require:
- authorised asset disposal
- clear title transfer
- market-value/governance sign-off where required
- data sanitisation responsibilities
- MDM/Autopilot/Activation Lock removal.

Do not assume a staff member can simply give the devices away.

## 16.3 NSW Government agencies — Device Bank changes the landscape

The **NSW Device Bank** launched in May 2026 and is directly relevant.

It:
- takes surplus government laptops
- securely removes data
- refurbishes/quality-tests them
- distributes them to people facing digital exclusion
- is delivered by NSW Telco Authority with **WorkVentures**.

Source:  
https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/nsw-device-bank-to-help-people-and-communities-get-online

### Scale

The public tender for the pilot disclosed:
- about **2,800 laptops in storage** at contract start
- about **2,500 additional laptops** expected over the following 12 months
- predominantly HP EliteBook x360 1030 G3/G4
- some G7/G8
- most expected to be five years old or older.

Source:  
https://buy.nsw.gov.au/prcOpportunity/9A339589-49D9-448D-9A06E56260BBFA42

### Strategic implication

This is both:
- a **competitor/sink** for government surplus laptops; and
- strong validation of the same reuse-first idea Dubbo eWaste is pursuing.

The NSW Digital Inclusion Action Plan explicitly aims to create a framework for **all NSW Government agencies to donate end-of-use laptops to the Device Bank**.

Source:  
https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/nsw-telco-authority/publications/nsw-digital-inclusion-strategy/action-plan

So future government laptop supply is likely to become more structured, not more casually available.

Where a third-party vendor destroys hardware, NSW Health policy requires a **certificate of destruction**.

Source:  
https://www1.health.nsw.gov.au/pds/ActivePDSDocuments/PD2022_011.pdf

### Meaning

Health facilities are potentially high-value institutional ITAD clients later, but they are not a casual shed donation source.

A credible future service would need:
- secure chain of custody
- formal vendor relationship
- auditable wiping/destruction
- certificates
- insurance
- appropriate premises.

## 16.8 Dubbo Regional Council

No current public ICT asset-disposal tender was found during this research.

Council's current procurement page says:
- procurement over $225,000 including GST is publicly advertised through **VendorPanel**
- smaller procurement uses quote thresholds
- businesses can register relevant categories to receive opportunities
- an ABN is required.

Source:  
https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/Business-Investors/Networks-and-Opportunities/doing-business-with-council

### Action

Register/monitor VendorPanel once the business structure exists.

Do not assume Council will donate redundant computers.

## 16.9 Auctions / paid surplus channels

Government and institutional redundant IT also appears through commercial auction channels such as Grays.

This can provide inventory, but it changes the model:
- stock costs money
- auction fees/freight matter
- condition risk moves to Dubbo eWaste.

That is a **purchasing channel**, not free e-waste supply.

Phase 0 should prioritise donated/collection stock before paying for bulk lots unless the resale economics are already proven.

---

## 16.10 Private Dubbo businesses

Public web searches did not reveal a sufficiently reliable list of Dubbo businesses currently announcing fleet replacements.

That is expected: small-business IT refreshes are usually not public events.

### Best early targets

The most plausible Phase 0 supply sources are:

- households with worthwhile devices
- local SMEs
- farms/agri businesses
- accountants
- legal/professional offices
- clubs and community organisations
- charities
- private organisations replacing small batches of PCs
- independent schools where governance allows
- MSP/computer-service providers with lower-value retired stock they do not want to process themselves.

### Offer

The strongest supply pitch is not:

> Give me your old computers.

It is:

> We can collect approved redundant electronics, inventory them, securely handle data-bearing devices, prioritise reuse/refurbishment, and route the remainder responsibly.

That creates an organisational reason to use Dubbo eWaste.

## 16.11 Intake requirements for institutional supply

For business/institutional batches, require:

- legal owner/entity
- authorised contact
- signed transfer/disposal authority
- asset list where available
- serials
- MDM/Autopilot removal
- Apple Activation Lock removal
- Google/Android account removal
- BIOS/firmware passwords
- data-handling instructions
- known battery damage
- accessories/chargers
- clear agreement on certificates/reporting.

This prevents a “free pickup” from turning into a pile of locked or unsaleable devices.

# Recommended regional data request

A single standard request can be sent to each Council and NetWaste:

> For financial years 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26, please provide the tonnes of electronic waste collected at each waste facility; the current e-waste collection/recycling contractor; contract/RFQ number and commencement/expiry date; the first downstream processing facility; whether the arrangement is under the NTCRS and which co-regulator; whether reusable devices are triaged or diverted before material recycling; the point at which ownership transfers; and whether Council may partner with a local reuse/refurbishment organisation to recover suitable devices before recycling.

That dataset would provide a much stronger basis for estimating the addressable supply around Dubbo than population-based modelling alone.
