# Repair First Dubbo — 6-month pilot

**Purpose:** turn the deep research in [REPAIR-FIRST-DUBBO-DEEP-RESEARCH.md](REPAIR-FIRST-DUBBO-DEEP-RESEARCH.md) into a testable local program.

This is a pilot concept, not an existing Council program and not a quoted budget.

---

# Pilot objective

Increase the proportion of broken household goods in Dubbo that are:

**diagnosed → repaired → kept in use**

instead of:

**discarded / replaced without a repair check**

Initial focus:
- laptops/computers
- phones/tablets
- TVs
- small appliances
- selected whitegoods
- bicycles

---

# Core intervention bundle

## 1. Free Repair Check

Participating repairers/events provide a short diagnosis and:

- likely fault
- repair quote/range
- expected extra life
- repair-vs-replace recommendation

Behaviour target:

> **Before you replace it, get one repair check.**

---

## 2. Dubbo Fix-It Bonus

Suggested test design:

- customer receives **50% off eligible repair**
- subsidy deducted immediately at participating repairer
- scheme reimburses repairer
- capped by category

Possible test caps:

| Category | Example subsidy cap |
|---|---:|
| small electronics / small appliance | $50–$75 |
| phone / tablet / laptop / TV | $75–$100 |
| selected whitegoods | $100–$150 |

Final caps should be set only after interviewing local repair businesses.

---

# Why point-of-sale discount matters

Do not make residents:

1. pay full repair bill;
2. complete forms;
3. wait weeks for reimbursement.

That adds exactly the friction the intervention is trying to remove.

The participating repairer should process the discount.

---

# 3. Monthly Repair Café

One session per month.

Suggested format:
- bookings plus limited walk-ins
- one item per person initially
- computer/Linux station
- small appliance station
- bike/mechanical station
- textiles/mending station where volunteers exist
- basic furniture/toy repair
- referral route for licensed/specialist work

Repairs should be:
- collaborative where practical
- free volunteer help
- owner pays replacement parts
- unsuccessful items leave with owner unless another lawful intake pathway is separately agreed

---

# 4. Repair Dubbo directory

One public page showing:

- repair category
- business/service
- contact/booking method
- brands/items accepted
- diagnosis fee
- typical turnaround
- collection/call-out option
- repair warranty
- accessibility
- what happens if item is uneconomic to repair

Goal:

> remove the “I don't even know who fixes this” barrier.

---

# 5. Loan pool

Especially for:
- laptops
- phones

Use safe tested refurbished equipment as temporary loans while repair is underway.

This directly reduces the hidden cost of downtime.

---

# 6. Donor-parts bank

Dubbo eWaste can support commercial/community repair by retaining compatible parts from equipment that is genuinely not economical to restore whole.

Examples:
- chargers
- laptop keyboards
- RAM
- SSDs
- fans
- screens
- hinges
- TV boards
- speakers
- switches

This lowers repair cost and addresses the common “no spare part available” failure.

---

# 7. Repair stories campaign

Publish one local case each week.

Format:

**Item:** 6-year-old laptop  
**Problem:** battery + slow HDD  
**Repair:** battery + SSD  
**Repair cost:** $X  
**Equivalent new device:** $Y  
**Estimated extra life:** Z years  
**Outcome:** repaired locally

Use real customer permission.

Lead with:
- dollars saved
- convenience
- continued usefulness

Then add waste/environmental benefit.

---

# 8. Suggested pilot scale

## Very small proof-of-concept

- repair bonus pool: **$10,000**
- average subsidy assumption: **$60**
- theoretical maximum claims: about **166**
- one Repair Café monthly
- free diagnosis pilot

## Medium pilot

- repair bonus pool: **$25,000**
- average subsidy assumption: **$75**
- theoretical maximum claims: about **333**

## Larger regional test

- repair bonus pool: **$40,000**
- average subsidy assumption: **$80**
- theoretical maximum claims: **500**

These claim counts are simple planning arithmetic only; actual uptake depends on repair mix and caps.

Separate costs would include:
- administration
- insurance
- venue
- promotion
- volunteer support
- evaluation
- tools/consumables.

---

# Suggested 6-month timeline

## Month 0–1 — capacity mapping

Interview local repairers.

Find:
- spare capacity
- categories
- typical prices
- common rejected jobs
- parts problems
- turnaround
- warranty policies
- willingness to accept vouchers

Recruit Repair Café volunteers.

---

## Month 2 — baseline event

Run one repair event **before subsidies**.

Record:
- attendance
- item categories
- faults
- repair success
- repair quotes
- reasons people were going to replace

This creates a baseline.

---

## Months 3–5 — incentive pilot

Launch:
- Repair Check
- repair bonus
- directory
- monthly Repair Café
- local stories

Monitor capacity weekly.

If repairers become overloaded:
- pause promotion
- alter categories
- reduce voucher intake

Do not create waiting lists that teach people repair is inconvenient.

---

## Month 6 — evaluation

Compare:

- repair consideration before/after
- repair completion
- replacement avoided
- local repair business revenue
- average subsidy per saved item
- successful repair categories
- unsuccessful categories
- parts barriers
- time barriers
- customer satisfaction

Then decide whether to:
- expand
- change caps
- fund diagnosis instead
- add categories
- add Wellington/mobile events

---

# Participating repairer rules

Possible requirements:

- ABN/business details where commercial
- appropriate licences/competency
- transparent quote
- customer approval before extra charges
- itemised invoice
- clear warranty
- no voucher for a repair judged unsafe/uneconomic
- basic outcome reporting
- no double claiming
- compliance with ACL/electrical safety requirements

---

# Repair Café rules

Separate the volunteer event from commercial repair.

Key principles:

- clear liability/process rules
- electrical/lithium safety boundaries
- owner remains with item
- owner takes failed item away
- one item per visitor initially
- volunteers can refuse unsafe repairs
- parts charged at cost
- repair data recorded

Use Repair Café International starter documentation rather than inventing everything.

# Metrics dashboard

## Demand
- repair checks
- voucher applications
- repair café attendance

## Repair
- successful
- partially successful
- repairable later
- end-of-life

## Behaviour
- would customer otherwise have replaced?
- had they considered repair before campaign?
- repeat repair user?

## Money
- customer spend
- subsidy spend
- repair-business revenue
- estimated avoided replacement spend

## Operations
- turnaround
- parts delays
- repairer capacity
- waiting time

## Waste
- item count kept in use
- weight where practical
- donor parts reused
- residual items properly recycled

# Strongest funding pathway

The NSW EPA Local Government Waste Solutions Fund has already funded SSROC's:

**Let's Fix It Sydney — Mapping Repair Incentives for Southern Sydney**

with a 12-council rebate pilot.

Dubbo Regional Council should first:

1. obtain SSROC's Phase 1 methodology/results;
2. ask NSW EPA how the regional findings can be reused;
3. explore a future eligible Council/regional circular-economy funding round;
4. avoid paying consultants to rediscover what SSROC has already studied.

---

# Pitch to Council

> **Repair First Dubbo**
>
> Council's own 2025–2035 Waste Strategy says the circular economy should keep products in use through reuse, repair and remanufacturing before disposal.
>
> We propose a six-month pilot combining a free repair check, capped repair rebate, local repair-business network and monthly Repair Café.
>
> The pilot would measure whether reducing repair cost and hassle keeps more laptops, phones, appliances, bikes and household goods in use, while directing expenditure to local repair businesses and reducing waste.

---

# Recommended first action

Before requesting money:

> **Interview 8–15 Dubbo repair businesses/repairers and map actual spare capacity, prices and barriers.**

The subsidy amount should be designed around the **real local repair market**, not copied blindly from Europe.
