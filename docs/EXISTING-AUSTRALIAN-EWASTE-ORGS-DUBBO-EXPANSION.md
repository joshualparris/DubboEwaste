# Could an existing Australian e-waste organisation expand to Dubbo?

**Research date:** 2 October 2026  
**Question:** Could an existing Australian e-waste / ITAD / device-reuse organisation expand into Dubbo instead of Dubbo eWaste having to build every capability itself?

## Executive answer

**Yes — several already have operating models that could extend into Dubbo.**

But “expand to Dubbo” can mean very different things:

1. **serve Dubbo remotely** using existing trucks/reverse logistics;
2. **add a Dubbo collection partner/drop-off point**;
3. **use a Dubbo business as a local collection/refurbishment/subcontracting partner**;
4. **open a local depot/warehouse**;
5. **build a full processing plant**.

The first three are realistic now for several organisations. A full processing plant is much harder and would need enough regional feedstock, industrial premises, anchor contracts and capital.

**No organisation researched currently advertises a public franchise program or a confirmed plan to open a Dubbo branch.**

The strongest near-term opportunities are therefore partnerships rather than waiting for a national company to open a standalone branch.

---

# Why Dubbo is genuinely credible as a regional e-waste location

NSW Government's Invest Regional NSW site specifically promotes **e-waste recycling in Dubbo** as an investment opportunity.

It highlights:

- Dubbo's position as a major regional centre;
- Newell, Golden and Mitchell Highway connections;
- rail connection to east-coast cities and ports;
- air links;
- industrial-zoned land;
- the ability to aggregate e-waste outputs such as metals, plastics, glass and circuit boards.

The NSW Government fact sheet says Dubbo's location creates multiple opportunities for e-waste recycling.

Sources:

- https://www.investregional.nsw.gov.au/opportunities/dubbo
- https://www.investregional.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/publications/2024-08/Dubbo_ewaste_recycling.pdf

That does not prove a processor would be commercially viable, but it means the idea of a **regional e-waste hub in Dubbo is not speculative marketing invented by this project; it is an opportunity NSW Government itself promotes.**

# Best expansion candidates

## 1. WorkVentures — strongest reuse-first strategic fit

### What it already does

WorkVentures operates CircularIT/ITAD services that:

- collect end-of-life corporate technology;
- maintain chain of custody;
- securely wipe or destroy data;
- test and grade assets;
- refurbish devices;
- resell, redeploy or donate viable equipment;
- recycle failures through certified downstream partners;
- provide reuse/recycling/social-impact reporting.

Most importantly for Dubbo, WorkVentures says it provides **national collection coverage across metropolitan and most regional areas of Australia**.

It says collection costs can be subsidised or completely offset where devices have residual value or are donated in good working condition.

Sources:

- https://workventures.com.au/circularit/
- https://workventures.com.au/refurbishment/
- https://workventures.com.au/decommissioning/

### Could it expand to Dubbo?

**Already serviceable: HIGH.**  
It can already collect in regional Australia.

**Dubbo local partnership/hub: HIGH potential.**  
A local operator could potentially:
- aggregate business donations;
- pre-inventory assets;
- handle regional pickup coordination;
- act as a local logistics/refurbishment partner if WorkVentures agreed;
- connect local community organisations into the Device Bank/National Device Bank ecosystem.

**Full WorkVentures-owned branch: MEDIUM/LOW without anchor volume.**

### Why this is a particularly good match

This is the closest mature organisation to the proposed Dubbo eWaste model:

**reuse first + professional ITAD + community impact + resale/redeployment + recycling only when reuse fails.**

WorkVentures' public logistics data reports significant reuse: its logistics page states 40% of units collected were repurposed, 52% responsibly e-wasted and 8% in inventory in the reported dataset.

### Best approach

Do not ask:

> Will you open a branch in Dubbo?

Start with:

> Would WorkVentures consider a Dubbo/Orana regional collection or refurbishment partnership for corporate/SME IT assets, with local intake and consolidated transport into CircularIT or the Device Bank network?

# 2. Ecoactiv — effectively already expanded into Dubbo

### What it already does

Ecoactiv is an approved NTCRS co-regulatory arrangement and offers e-waste pickup across NSW.

Its current NSW service page **explicitly lists Dubbo** among the regional NSW locations it services.

It supports:
- NTCRS electronics;
- broader e-waste;
- household/business/school collection;
- pickup and drop-off logistics;
- AS/NZS 5377-certified processing networks.

Sources:

- https://ecoactiv.com.au/e-waste-ntcrs-sydney/
- https://ecoactiv.com.au/ntcrs/
- https://ecoactiv.com.au/e-waste-electronic-recycling-disposal/

### Could it expand to Dubbo?

**Service expansion: ALREADY PRESENT.**

Ecoactiv already advertises pickup in Dubbo.

The meaningful next step would be:

**Dubbo collection/drop-off partner: HIGH potential**, subject to Ecoactiv agreeing.

### Fit with Dubbo eWaste

Ecoactiv is much more **recycling/logistics-focused** than the proposed selective refurb/resale operation.

Best use:

**Dubbo eWaste handles reuse/refurbishment first → Ecoactiv handles eligible residual e-waste.**

### Why partnership may be better than competition

Ecoactiv does not need a whole processing branch in Dubbo to serve it. Its business model already relies on reverse logistics and downstream partners.

A local Dubbo node could reduce collection friction without duplicating industrial processing.

# 3. Ecycle Solutions — strong candidate for a Dubbo collection node

### What it already does

Ecycle Solutions is:

- an approved NTCRS co-regulator;
- a national e-waste operator;
- backed by QLS Group;
- operating **300+ collection locations**;
- using **150+ trucks on the road daily**;
- designed to service metropolitan, regional and remote communities.

It explicitly says its reverse-logistics model allows it to reach locations that many recyclers cannot.

Sources:

- https://ecyclesolutions.net.au/
- https://ecyclesolutions.net.au/ewaste-recycling/ewaste-recycling-solutions
- https://ecyclesolutions.net.au/assets/main/Ecycle-Solutions-Capability-Statement.pdf

### Could it expand to Dubbo?

**Regional servicing: HIGH — its model is already built for this.**

**New Dubbo drop-off/collection partner: HIGH potential.**

**Dedicated processing branch: LOW/MEDIUM.**

Because its advantage is an existing national reverse-logistics network, it may gain more from putting a cage/drop-off partnership in Dubbo than constructing another processing facility.

### Dubbo eWaste fit

Best as:
- NTCRS residual recycler;
- cage/pickup provider;
- downstream partner for TVs/computers/printers that fail reuse triage.

Less aligned with:
- resale;
- local refurbishment.

# 4. TechCollect / ANZRP — strongest free formal collection-partner model

### What it already does

TechCollect explicitly invites:

- local councils;
- waste-management organisations;
- selected retail/business partners

to become **collection partners nationwide**.

It states partnership is **free** and can include:

- signage;
- marketing support;
- staff training;
- collection units;
- transport;
- recycling.

Its current NSW collection list includes regional councils but **does not currently list Dubbo**.

Sources:

- https://techcollect.com.au/our-partners/partner-with-us/
- https://techcollect.com.au/our-partners/

### Could it expand to Dubbo?

**Dubbo collection point: VERY HIGH structural fit.**

There is a literal published partnership pathway for exactly this type of expansion.

### Important limitation

TechCollect is a **recycling stream**, not a resale stock source.

Existing project research found TechCollect directs still-useful equipment toward reuse before surrender; once material is surrendered into the TechCollect recycling stream, it should be treated as recycling material.

So the right sequence for Dubbo eWaste would be:

**intake → reuse triage → viable assets retained for legitimate reuse → genuine end-of-life NTCRS material enters TechCollect**

subject to TechCollect's agreement and all ownership/contract rules.

### Best target

A future approved commercial premises or Council/waste partnership is more likely to suit TechCollect than a small residential shed.

# 5. PonyUp for Good — strong commercial reuse-first partner

### What it does

PonyUp for Good describes itself as a social enterprise handling decommissioned corporate technology across Australia.

Its model:

- collect retired corporate technology nationwide;
- secure data erasure;
- remarket/refurbish reusable assets;
- recycle failures through accredited partners;
- report reuse vs recycling outcomes;
- donate 50% of profits to SecondBite.

PonyUp says its primary focus is technology about **2–5 years old** and publicly reports a reuse-first mandate.

Its published impact page says **32.5% of collected equipment was reused rather than recycled** in the reported program.

Sources:

- https://www.ponyupforgood.com/
- https://www.ponyupforgood.com/faq
- https://www.ponyupforgood.com/1-million-meals
- https://www.ponyupforgood.com/telstraenterprise

### Could it expand to Dubbo?

**Serve corporate clients in Dubbo: HIGH.**

It says collections occur across Australia.

**Dubbo local operating partner: MEDIUM/HIGH potential**, although no public franchise or regional-depot program is advertised.

**Standalone branch: LOW/MEDIUM.**

### Why it fits

PonyUp is extremely close to the future Dubbo commercial pitch:

**secure pickup + reuse first + resale + certified downstream + social impact reporting.**

A regional partnership could give PonyUp cheaper local collection/consolidation while Dubbo eWaste learns from a mature reuse-first operator.

# 6. Renew IT — strong candidate for regional ITAD, weaker case for a public drop-off branch

### What it already does

Renew IT:

- is headquartered in Lane Cove NSW;
- started from a garage;
- operates multiple facilities across Australia/NZ;
- offers IT asset buyback;
- sanitises data to NIST 800-88;
- refurbishes hardware for resale;
- processes older non-resale equipment through circular downstream systems;
- serves SMEs, corporations, schools and universities.

Renew IT says the 2021 acquisition of Verser enabled it to expand services **nationwide**.

Sources:

- https://renew-it.com/
- https://renew-it.com/about-us/
- https://renew-it.com/international/

UNSW's profile of founder James Lancaster says the original business model was collecting/refurbishing corporate laptops, wiping data and selling functional equipment to second markets before later adding advanced materials processing.

Source:

- https://www.businessthink.unsw.edu.au/articles/circular-economy-e-waste-recycling-renew-it

### Could it expand to Dubbo?

**Corporate ITAD service into Dubbo: HIGH.**

**Local consolidation/drop-off partner: MEDIUM/HIGH.**

**Dedicated Renew IT processing facility: MEDIUM only if enough institutional/commercial volume exists.**

Renew IT's facility growth has followed large-scale ITAD volume and acquisitions, not a public franchise model.

### Why Dubbo may eventually appeal

Dubbo could aggregate:
- government;
- education;
- health;
- SME;
- agriculture;
- regional business fleet replacements.

But the feedstock needs to be proven first.

# 9. MobileMuster — very easy to extend locally, but wrong for reuse stock

### What it does

MobileMuster allows organisations to join as registered collection partners.

A site may:
- become a public collection point; or
- remain non-public and send its own collected devices.

MobileMuster provides free pickups for registered sites.

Sources:

- https://www.mobilemuster.com.au/join-us/
- https://www.mobilemuster.com.au/book-a-pickup/
- https://www.mobilemuster.com.au/workplaces/

### Could it expand to Dubbo?

**Yes — extremely easily via collection partnership.**

### Critical limitation

The MobileMuster partner terms explicitly require the partner to transfer ownership of collected phones/components to AMTA and agree **not to reuse or resell them**.

Therefore MobileMuster should only receive phones that have already failed Dubbo eWaste's legitimate reuse/repair pathway.

Correct model:

**good phone → reuse/refurbish/resell**  
**true end-of-life phone → MobileMuster**

It cannot be a feedstock source for Dubbo eWaste.

# 11. Sircel — geographically compelling, but financially the riskiest candidate right now

### Why it looks ideal geographically

Sircel already operates a processing site at **Parkes, NSW** and says it:

- collects from clients using its own fleet or trusted logistics partners;
- serves councils, government and corporate clients;
- prioritises asset reuse through its ITAD team;
- processes end-of-life e-waste at its facilities.

Sources:

- https://sircel.com/contact-us/
- https://sircel.com/services/
- https://sircel.com/how-we-work/our-process/

It has also historically demonstrated physical expansion through acquisitions and new processing capability.

### But current corporate status matters

Sircel Limited and related entities entered **receivership and voluntary administration on 31 October 2025**.

Its November 2025 update said operations continued at all sites.

In June 2026, administrators/receivers were still working through sale/recapitalisation proposals; public insolvency reporting said a viable rescue outcome had not yet been settled at that point.

Sources:

- https://sircel.com/2025/11/17/update-sircel-operations/
- ASIC/administrator notices
- June 2026 insolvency reporting.

### Could it expand to Dubbo?

**Sircel service from Parkes: HIGH structural fit.**

**New Dubbo branch right now: LOW / too uncertain.**

**Future Dubbo satellite after restructuring/new ownership: MEDIUM/HIGH strategic logic.**

### Best near-term approach

Do not build the Dubbo plan around Sircel opening a new branch while its corporate future remains unsettled.

Instead ask whether:
- current operations can collect/consolidate Dubbo material;
- Dubbo could become a partner collection/triage point;
- a future owner would be interested in an Orana regional satellite.

# What expansion model is most realistic?

## Model A — collection partner / local node

**Most realistic immediately.**

Potential organisations:
- TechCollect
- Ecycle Solutions
- Ecoactiv
- MobileMuster (dead phones only)

Dubbo provides:
- secure local collection point;
- storage/cage;
- basic separation;
- consolidation.

National operator provides:
- transport;
- processing;
- compliance;
- reporting.

### Advantage

Very low capital.

### Problem

Most of these pathways are **recycling**, not resale/reuse businesses.

---

# Model B — local reuse/refurbishment + national downstream partner

**Best fit with the current Dubbo eWaste idea.**

Potential partners:
- WorkVentures
- PonyUp for Good
- FlipTech
- Renew IT
- Good360/Device Bank ecosystem.

Dubbo eWaste would do some combination of:
- local relationships;
- pre-screening;
- intake;
- inventory;
- basic test/triage;
- local refurbishment;
- local resale/donation.

National partner could provide:
- higher-assurance data processing;
- bulk resale channels;
- corporate reporting;
- downstream recycling;
- overflow logistics.

This is probably the most valuable partnership model to pursue.

# Model C — local subcontractor for national ITAD jobs

Potential partners:
- WorkVentures
- Renew IT
- SK Tes
- PonyUp
- FlipTech
- possibly Sircel after its corporate position stabilises.

Example:

A national company wins an asset-disposal job for:
- a Dubbo business;
- regional government office;
- bank;
- telco;
- health provider.

Instead of sending Sydney technicians for every job, an approved local partner could:
- attend site;
- inventory;
- decommission;
- palletise/secure assets;
- coordinate transport.

A mature Dubbo eWaste operation could eventually bid to become that **regional field partner**.

This may be more commercially realistic than opening a national operator's branded branch.

---

# Model D — actual industrial processing facility

Potentially capable organisations:
- Renew IT;
- SK Tes;
- a restructured/new-owner Sircel;
- Ecycle Solutions;
- another major recycler.

Dubbo has a strategic case because NSW Government explicitly markets it as an e-waste recycling opportunity.

But a processing plant would need evidence of:
- regional tonnage;
- commercial/institutional contracts;
- Council/NetWaste feedstock;
- industrial land;
- EPA/planning approvals;
- skilled staff;
- downstream/offtake;
- transport economics;
- capital.

**The current repo does not yet prove enough guaranteed feedstock for this step.**

- Sircel

# What would make a national operator say yes?

The pitch needs evidence, not just “Dubbo needs e-waste recycling”.

Build these first:

1. **Verified local supply**
   - first 20–30 device pilot;
   - enquiries;
   - businesses wanting pickups.

2. **Regional tonnage**
   - Dubbo/Wellington;
   - Narromine;
   - Parkes;
   - Forbes;
   - Warren;
   - Bogan;
   - Coonamble;
   - Gilgandra;
   - Mid-Western.

3. **Anchor customers**
   - SMEs;
   - MSPs;
   - corporate offices;
   - Council;
   - approved education/health/government work.

4. **Premises**
   - if scaling beyond home pilot, compliant industrial/storage site.

5. **Clear role**
   - local collection;
   - secure intake;
   - reuse triage;
   - field decommissioning;
   - data handling;
   - consolidated freight.

6. **Transport saving**
   - demonstrate why having someone in Dubbo is cheaper/better than sending staff/trucks from Sydney/other facilities for every small job.

# Strongest strategic idea

The most realistic path is not:

> **Dubbo eWaste versus the big Australian recyclers.**

It is:

> **Dubbo eWaste becomes the regional front end; a larger organisation becomes the back end.**

For example:

**Dubbo / Orana businesses**
↓
**Dubbo eWaste — relationships, pickup, intake, asset inventory, reuse triage, local refurbishment**
↓
**WorkVentures / PonyUp / FlipTech / Renew IT — overflow ITAD, resale markets, institutional reporting**
↓
**TechCollect / Ecycle / Ecoactiv / AMR / specialist processors — genuine end-of-life residual recycling**

That lets Dubbo keep more of the **reuse value locally** while avoiding the enormous capital cost of industrial recycling.

---

# Key caveats

- A local collection partnership does not automatically solve the residential-shed planning issue.
- Material collected under an NTCRS/MobileMuster scheme may become scheme-owned recycling material and should not be pulled back out for resale unless the agreement expressly allows it.
- Data-bearing business assets need documented chain of custody.
- Some national operators may prefer direct collection rather than subcontractors.
- A processing facility is a completely different planning/EPA/capital project from the current Phase 0 concept.
- Sircel's operational/corporate future should be checked again before relying on it for long-term commitments.
