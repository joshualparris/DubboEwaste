# Deep research prompt: EcoBatt, EcoCycle and Dubbo's electronic waste supply chain

**28 November 2026.** Canonical prompt, preserved as supplied. Executed results: [ECOCYCLE-ECOBATT-DUBBO-SUPPLY-CHAIN-RESEARCH-2026-11-28.md](ECOCYCLE-ECOBATT-DUBBO-SUPPLY-CHAIN-RESEARCH-2026-11-28.md). The preliminary summary that preceded this prompt has been claim-checked in section 1 of the results; several of its statements are only partly verified, so use the results rather than the preliminary summary.

## Primary objective

Conduct an exhaustive, evidence-led investigation into EcoBatt, EcoCycle Group and its subsidiaries, focusing on what happens to discarded electronic devices originating in Dubbo, NSW.

The ultimate objective is to determine whether functional and repairable electronic equipment could be lawfully redirected towards refurbishment, repair, community donation and resale through Dubbo E-waste, before entering irreversible material recycling.

## 2. Corporate investigation

Investigate the complete EcoCycle corporate network: EcoBatt, Eco E-Waste, EcoCycle, EcoBatt Safety, Recycal, Castings Tasmania, ACL Metal Powders.

Identify ownership, directors, executives, ABNs, subsidiaries, facilities, environmental licences, certifications, government funding, contracts, partners, revenue sources and business models. Distinguish EcoCycle from unrelated companies with similar names, particularly Ecycle Solutions.

## 3. Map the entire Dubbo collection network

Investigate every potentially relevant collection relationship involving BIG W, Woolworths, Coles, ALDI, Bunnings, JB Hi-Fi, The Good Guys, Officeworks, Harvey Norman, Telstra, Optus and Vodafone, Dubbo Regional Council, Whylandra Waste and Recycling Centre, Matthews Metals, and schools, hospitals, government offices and commercial businesses.

For every organisation, identify its actual recycling contractor, contractual arrangements, collection frequency, estimated volumes, collection points, regional consolidation facilities and downstream processing destinations. Separate confirmed Dubbo relationships from national partnerships that have not been locally verified.

## 4. Trace every device category

Desktop computers, gaming PCs and workstations; laptops, Chromebooks and MacBooks; iPhones, Android phones and tablets; servers and networking equipment; graphics cards, RAM, CPUs and motherboards; hard drives and SSDs; POS terminals, self-service checkouts and barcode scanners; printers, monitors and televisions; smartwatches, consoles and electronic toys; chargers, cables and batteries.

Determine whether each category is tested, graded, repaired, refurbished, dismantled, shredded, exported, resold or donated. Investigate whether fully functioning equipment is destroyed without first being tested.

## 5. Investigate separate waste streams

Do not assume all electronics follow the same disposal pathway. Investigate separately: customer recycling bins; retailers' own decommissioned IT equipment; customer returns; warranty replacements; damaged packaging and non-saleable stock; unsold clearance merchandise; insurance write-offs; commercial IT asset disposal; council-collected household e-waste; manufacturer-authorised recycling. Identify who owns equipment at every stage and who has authority to approve alternative disposal arrangements.

## 6. Reconstruct the physical recycling process

From Dubbo collection to final material recovery: collection containers and handling; transport companies and regional transfer facilities; NSW facilities including St Marys; Victorian facilities including Campbellfield; battery sorting and separation; battery-in-device shredding; computer and laptop shredding; robotic dismantling; data destruction and verification; metal, plastic and circuit board recovery; domestic and overseas downstream processors; residual waste and landfill rates. Investigate the Campbellfield BIDS facility, its funding, capacity, equipment suppliers and outcomes. Distinguish material recovery percentages from whole-device reuse rates.

## 7. Investigate the destruction-only policy

Eco E-Waste reportedly states that it does not resell or refurbish collected e-waste. Determine whether this applies across the group; whether functional devices are routinely destroyed; whether reusable components are recovered intact; whether contracts permit refurbishment exceptions; whether clients can select secure sanitisation rather than physical destruction; whether destruction is legally required or primarily commercial; whether reuse partnerships have been trialled; whether alternative collection streams could be created. Examine EcoCycle's submissions to the Productivity Commission, ACCC and parliamentary inquiries.

## 8. Legal and regulatory investigation

National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme; Recycling and Waste Reduction Act 2020; NSW Product Lifecycle Responsibility legislation; AS/NZS 5377 and applicable standards; battery stewardship and B-cycle; MobileMuster; environmental protection licensing; privacy and secure data sanitisation; ownership transfer and product liability; dangerous goods transport; consumer guarantees and refurbished electronics; right to repair. Establish whether a separate, consent-based reuse-first pathway is legally and commercially feasible. Do not assume certification automatically grants permission to recover equipment, access recycling containers or remove device locks.

## 9. Quantify the opportunity

Find actual data or construct clearly labelled low, medium and high scenarios for annual e-waste generated in Dubbo; devices collected by EcoBatt and related companies; proportion suitable for refurbishment; residual value; transport and processing costs; repair labour and parts; resale revenue; community donation; avoided emissions and material consumption. Model 100, 500, 1,000 and 5,000 devices annually. Do not present hypothetical volumes as verified collection statistics.

## 10. Develop realistic partnership models

Authorised pre-recycling diversion; separate repair and reuse donation streams; regional collection subcontracting; local IT asset disposal; secure refurbishment with documented sanitisation; corporate donation programmes; component harvesting with written approval; a Dubbo regional circular electronics pilot. Investigate whether Dubbo E-waste could become an approved upstream reuse partner while EcoCycle continues to process equipment unsuitable for reuse. Identify incentives, certification costs, insurance, contractual barriers and implementation steps.

## 11. Identify decision-makers

Publicly documented professional contacts for EcoCycle business development, EcoBatt regional operations, Eco E-Waste partnerships, Woolworths Group IT asset management, BIG W non-trade procurement, retail sustainability programmes, Dubbo Regional Council resource recovery and regional product stewardship. Prepare enquiry scripts and draft emails, but do not send correspondence without explicit approval.

Publish canonical research to DubboEwaste and cross-reference it from ResearchAtlas, ResearchLibrary and CircularEconomyDubbo where appropriate. Verify all repository changes and links.

## Research integrity

Distinguish independently verified facts, company claims, historical evidence, reasonable inferences and unknowns. Never fabricate contracts, collection volumes, transport routes, financial figures or partnerships. Respect property ownership, customer consent, privacy, battery safety and contractual commitments. The goal is not to interfere with existing recycling infrastructure, but to identify a lawful, economically viable pathway that preserves useful electronics before they become waste. Execute the research rather than merely returning another research plan.
