# Executive summary

## Core finding (confidence: moderate)
Dubbo has **recycling and disposal infrastructure that is well developed, and market-driven repair/reuse that is real but uneven**. What public evidence does **not** show is a *structured* reuse-before-recycling step at the Council waste facility, or a general-purpose community repair venue.

## What the evidence supports
1. **Council policy is circular-economy-flavoured.** The Waste Strategy 2025–2035 is adopted [VERIFIED]. Council said (2024 exhibition) it diverted ~45% of material from landfill and aimed for 80% by 2030 [VERIFIED]. These are *targets*, not services.
2. **Whylandra is a sorting-and-recycling site, not a reuse site, on public evidence.** Its Community Recycling Centre takes e-waste ("anything with a power cord"), batteries, textiles, EPS, paints, oils etc. free; scrap metal and degassed whitegoods free when separated [VERIFIED]. Council's A-Z guide tells residents to *themselves* "consider reuse" via op shops, and sends bicycles to depots for metal recycling [VERIFIED]. No reuse shop, salvage area or repair triage appears in any public page found [PARTIAL — absence of evidence].
3. **E-waste goes into a national recycling scheme.** Whylandra is listed as an approved participant in the National TV & Computer Recycling Scheme via DHL Drop Zone [VERIFIED (directory listing)]. That's material recycling, not reuse. Downstream destination: [UNVERIFIED].
4. **Council already supports charity op shops with a free textile recycling service** [VERIFIED] — meaning unsellable donated textiles have a Council route. Whether landfill is still the destination for other rejected goods: [UNVERIFIED].
5. **Men's Sheds are the clearest community repair/restoration capacity**, but they're men-oriented membership groups. Dubbo Community Men's Shed publicly offers furniture restoration to the wider community [PARTIAL — undated news]; they've built items from donated scrap/office furniture [PARTIAL]. Electronics/appliance repair: [UNVERIFIED].
6. **Scrap metal is a functioning commercial loop** (e.g. Orana Wool Scrap Metal, operating since 1986, takes whitegoods, e-waste, old cars) [PARTIAL — directory listing].
7. **No Dubbo Repair Café found** in search [PARTIAL — search limits; confirm locally].

## Biggest likely leakage points (INFERENCE unless noted)
- Household → Whylandra: working/repairable items enter recycling streams with no reuse triage.
- Charity rejection: nationally, Vinnies refuses broken/stained furniture and many shops refuse electrical or furniture [VERIFIED national]; Dubbo practice unverified.
- Business IT refresh → recycler/ITAD with data-handling friction.
- Cheap-appliance economics (labour > replacement cost).

## Biggest opportunity (INFERENCE)
A **reuse/repair layer sitting upstream of Whylandra** — Repair Café + refurb social enterprise + (longer-term) tip-shop style reuse area — supported by Council. Comparator councils (Wagga tip shop, Byron Re-Market with repair workshops, Waverley Repair Café) show it's feasible in NSW.

## What would change this picture
Evidence of contractor-level reuse at Whylandra, or active Dubbo op-shop testing/repair operations, or established independent repairers meeting most consumer demand.
