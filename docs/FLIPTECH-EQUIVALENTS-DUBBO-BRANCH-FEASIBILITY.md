# Fliptech equivalents and Dubbo branch / regional-hub feasibility

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)  
**Question:** Which Australian businesses are genuinely comparable to Fliptech's reuse-first / secure-ITAD model, and could Fliptech or an equivalent realistically establish a Dubbo presence?

## Executive conclusion

**Yes, a Dubbo presence is plausible. A full industrial recycling branch is not the most logical first version.**

The strongest near-term model is:

> **Western NSW collection + secure ITAD + reuse/refurbishment satellite**
>
> with final material recycling sent to an established downstream processor.

That is much more plausible than building a shredder/material-processing plant in Dubbo.

The evidence supports three progressively larger models:

1. **Regional collection/aggregation partner** — lowest cost and lowest risk.
2. **Secure ITAD/refurbishment satellite** — local serial capture, data sanitisation, testing, grading, repair and resale/donation.
3. **Full e-waste processing plant** — much higher capital/compliance burden and less obvious need given existing downstream facilities such as Sircel at Parkes and large Sydney processors.

No public evidence was found that Fliptech, Renew IT, G1, WorkVentures, Compnow or the other companies below currently offer a **franchise** model. A Dubbo branch would therefore require a direct corporate partnership, company-owned expansion, joint venture or contractor/collection arrangement.

# 2. Could Fliptech itself open in Dubbo?

## Structurally: yes

There is no obvious structural reason Fliptech could not establish a Dubbo satellite.

Reasons Dubbo is credible:

- Dubbo Regional Council describes the city as servicing a **catchment over 125,000**.
- Official/local-government data records around **5,593 active businesses** in the LGA.
- Dubbo is a major regional service centre at the intersection of three highways.
- Major employment sectors include health, education, government and other organisations that operate substantial IT fleets.
- The Central West/Orana region includes Dubbo, Narromine, Wellington and many surrounding towns with existing e-waste streams.
- Fliptech is already NSW-based.
- Fliptech is already a Buy NSW supplier.
- Its Royal Far West partnership is explicitly aimed at rural/regional digital inclusion.
- Fliptech has already shown geographic expansion outside Sydney.

Sources:
- Dubbo Regional Council: https://www.dubbo.nsw.gov.au/Business-Investors/Economic-development/regional-economic-development
- NSW Your Council: https://www.yourcouncil.nsw.gov.au/council-data/dubbo-regional-1787123466/
- RDA Orana: https://www.rdaorana.org.au/orana-region/dubbo-region

## But the word "branch" matters

A **full warehouse/processing branch** and a **regional ITAD satellite** are economically different things.

### Model A — Western NSW collection partner / depot

A secure local point could:

- receive approved business equipment;
- record client / chain of custody;
- serialise assets;
- place data-bearing equipment into secure storage;
- consolidate loads;
- dispatch to Fliptech's main processing facility;
- coordinate local business pickups;
- run collection bins/cages.

This requires much less capital than establishing a fully certified data-erasure/refurbishment warehouse.

### Model B — Dubbo ITAD/refurbishment satellite

A more useful local branch could perform:

- secure custody;
- asset manifests / serial capture;
- data sanitisation;
- hardware tests;
- grading;
- RAM/SSD/battery/charger work;
- refurbishment;
- resale preparation;
- donation preparation;
- component recovery;
- pallet consolidation of true end-of-life equipment.

This is the model most closely aligned with the current **Dubbo eWaste** concept.

### Model C — full recycling/processing facility

This would add:

- industrial dismantling;
- bulk material separation;
- shredding;
- hazardous streams;
- possibly battery processing.

There is currently much less evidence that Dubbo needs this.

Parkes already hosts specialist e-waste processing through Sircel, while major NSW processors operate closer to Sydney.

The business case for Dubbo is stronger at the **high-value beginning of the lifecycle** than at the industrial shredding end.

# 3. Why a Dubbo satellite may make more sense than sending everything to Sydney

A regional satellite could solve problems created by distance:

- small Western NSW collections may be uneconomic as individual Sydney pickups;
- businesses may leave retired IT in storerooms because collection is inconvenient;
- devices lose resale value while sitting unused;
- lithium-bearing equipment can become a storage hazard;
- chain-of-custody is easier to explain when a regional specialist can collect directly;
- local testing lets valuable devices be identified before unnecessary freight;
- low-value material can be consolidated into economical downstream loads.

The ideal purpose of a Dubbo site would therefore not be:

> "store lots of e-waste"

It would be:

> **reduce the amount that needs to be freighted as waste, while consolidating the material that truly does require downstream recycling.**

# 4. The main risk to a Fliptech Dubbo branch

The biggest commercial question is **not whether Dubbo generates e-waste**.

It clearly does.

The question is:

> **Is there enough recurring, high-quality business IT refresh volume to support permanent staff and premises?**

Public data establishes the size of the regional economy but does not disclose:

- annual retired laptop count;
- corporate refresh volumes;
- current ITAD contract spend;
- how many devices are already captured by incumbent national providers;
- how much residual value those devices contain.

That needs real sales discovery.

A branch should ideally be anchored by recurring sources such as:

- large employers;
- health and aged-care providers;
- schools/education;
- councils/government;
- banks/professional offices;
- MSPs;
- retail chains;
- mining/agriculture/renewable-energy businesses;
- organisations with many regional sites.

Without anchor supply, a national ITAD company can usually service Dubbo more cheaply through scheduled logistics than a permanent branch.

# 20. The branch model Dubbo should pitch

Do **not** start by asking a company:

> "Will you open a recycling plant in Dubbo?"

A much more commercially credible pitch is:

> **"Would you trial a Western NSW secure ITAD satellite in Dubbo?"**

### Proposed function

The satellite would:

1. source local organisations;
2. collect/receive only approved IT assets;
3. record transfer authority;
4. serialise/photograph;
5. secure unwiped equipment;
6. perform approved data sanitisation if authorised/trained;
7. test and grade devices;
8. refurbish straightforward equipment;
9. route high-value assets into the company's resale/broker system;
10. prepare social-use assets for approved partner pathways;
11. consolidate genuine e-waste;
12. send remainder to the parent company's nominated certified recycler;
13. provide outcome reports.

### Parent company supplies

Ideally:

- SOPs;
- client contract wording;
- chain-of-custody forms;
- erasure software/licences;
- certificate system;
- asset management portal;
- grading rules;
- approved reseller/broker channels;
- downstream recycler contract;
- insurance/compliance requirements;
- national-client referrals.

### Dubbo operator supplies

- local presence;
- business development;
- pickups;
- secure storage;
- bench diagnostics;
- refurbishment labour;
- regional relationships;
- local social partners;
- Western NSW route consolidation.

This is much closer to the economics of a **regional ITAD franchise/satellite**, even though none of the companies currently publishes a franchise program.

# 21. Could Dubbo eWaste become the local operator?

Potentially, but this should be approached carefully.

The current Dubbo eWaste project is still resolving:

- residential planning;
- premises suitability;
- Fair Trading / second-hand dealer position;
- insurance;
- downstream agreements.

A national ITAD company would likely require a much higher standard than the initial shed pilot for customer data-bearing assets.

Likely requirements would include:

- proper commercial/security premises;
- alarm/CCTV/access control;
- locked cages;
- chain-of-custody procedures;
- cyber/privacy controls;
- background checks;
- suitable insurance;
- WHS;
- data-erasure competency;
- auditable asset system;
- approved transport;
- lithium safety;
- customer contractual liability.

Therefore:

> **The home-shed pilot can prove local supply and technical economics. It should not be represented as automatically ready to become a certified national ITAD branch.**

The data collected by the pilot could, however, become the evidence used to win such a partnership.

# 22. What would prove a Dubbo branch is viable?

Before asking anyone to sign a lease, gather actual evidence for:

## Supply

- number of local organisations interested;
- asset volumes;
- refresh cadence;
- equipment age/spec;
- proportion with residual value;
- current disposal method.

## Route economics

- current cost of Sydney pickup;
- minimum volume for free/nil-cost collection;
- pallet/cage freight cost;
- how often regional collections are needed;
- value lost when assets sit for months.

## Client demand

Do clients want:

- certificates?
- serial reports?
- buyback?

# 27. Biggest unresolved questions

1. **Does Fliptech actually service Dubbo today?**
   No public service map or regional minimum volume was found.

2. **What is Fliptech's Melbourne operating structure?**
   Public evidence says it operates there, but no verified public branch address/process model was found.

3. **Does Fliptech use subcontractors/partners for regional collection?**
   No public evidence found.

4. **Would any ITAD company allow local wiping/refurbishment?**
   Chain-of-custody and certification may require central processing.

5. **What is the actual Western NSW corporate fleet volume?**
   This is the key branch-economics unknown and requires direct market discovery.

6. **Would large organisations already be locked into national ITAD contracts?**
   Likely in some cases, but not quantified.

7. **What site/certification/security standard would a parent company require?**
   Must be obtained from each company.

8. **Who bears resale warranty/ACL risk?**
   Critical if local assets are sold under parent-company branding.

9. **Can social-use devices remain in the Dubbo region?**
   Strong social case, but requires partner rules and demand.

10. **Which regional downstream recycler would be contractually approved?**
    Parkes/Sircel is geographically attractive, but each ITAD company may have its own certified downstream chain.

# 28. Overall finding for Dubbo eWaste

The Australian market already proves that the proposed Dubbo model is real.

There are many companies making a business from:

**collect → secure → wipe → test → value → refurbish → resell/donate → recycle failures**

What is unusual is not the model.

What is unusual is that **Western NSW appears to have no obvious publicly visible local specialist doing the whole Fliptech-style ITAD/value-recovery chain from Dubbo**.

That creates two possible paths:

### Independent path
Build Dubbo eWaste into the local version of that model.

### Partner path
Prove local supply, then become the **Western NSW satellite/collection/refurbishment partner** of an existing ITAD company.

The second path could provide:

- better credentials;
- national accounts;
- erasure software;
- reporting systems;
- resale channels;
- downstream contracts;

while retaining a local Dubbo operation.

**The most commercially sensible first ask is not "open a Dubbo branch".**

It is:

> **"Would you trial a Western NSW secure ITAD partner/satellite in Dubbo if we can prove the volume?"**
