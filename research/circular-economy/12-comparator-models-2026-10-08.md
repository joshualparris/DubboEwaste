# Circular Economy Comparator Models

Date: 8 October 2026

This is not a generic list of “good recycling towns”. Each comparator contributes a specific operating mechanic that could be copied in Dubbo.

## Bathurst — reuse centre integrated with waste facility

Bathurst Regional Council identifies the **Junktion Reuse and Recovery Centre** next to its Waste Management Centre.

HopeCare's description adds useful operating mechanics:
- established in 2010;
- open seven days;
- paid staff plus volunteers;
- accepts/resells large reusable items;
- working electrical goods are tested/tagged;
- electrical goods are sold with a warranty.

### What Dubbo can copy
A staffed reuse interception point physically adjacent to the waste facility, with an explicit electrical safety process.

---

## Bendigo — repair café grew into a broader sharing hub

Bendigo Share and Repair Shed traces its beginnings to a Repair Café in 2017 and now combines:
- repair café activity;
- tool library;
- useful-material “bin”/reuse;
- small-item recycling;
- swaps;
- workshops and skill sharing.

### What Dubbo can copy
Do not design a repair café as only “people fixing toasters once a month”. It can become a community skills/reuse hub with multiple low-cost services.

---

## Ballarat — regular repair café in a community precinct

Ballarat Repair Cafe operates from Barkly Square and reports twice-monthly sessions and accumulated landfill diversion.

Ballarat's broader Waste to Wellbeing/community ecosystem links repair and reuse with other community organisations.

### What Dubbo can copy
Regular predictable sessions + a host precinct + public reporting of repairs/weight diverted.

---

## Albury — council reuse shop + tool library

Albury Council operates an Upcycle and Recycle Shop selling second-hand household goods, furniture, bikes and related reusable items. It also operates a tool-library model through the broader reuse ecosystem.

### What Dubbo can copy
Combine a reuse shop with access-to-tools, not just resale. This tackles both waste and the cost barrier to repair.

---

## Armidale — second-hand shop at waste facility

Armidale's Waste Management Facility publicly includes a **Second-Hand Goods Shop**.

### What Dubbo can copy
Simple, understandable public pathway:
> reusable item at waste facility → reuse shop rather than disposal stream.

---

## Toowoomba — networked council reuse shops with repair/parts logic

Toowoomba Regional Council operates multiple reuse-shop pathways and publishes unusually practical acceptance language, including some broken/damaged items such as furniture, bikes, lawnmowers and whitegoods when easily repairable or useful for parts.

### What Dubbo can copy
A reuse shop should not use “perfect resale condition” as its only gate. A controlled **repairable / parts-harvest** category captures more value.

---

## Tamworth — useful cautionary comparator

Tamworth Council investigated concepts including a reuse/repair centre and tool library, while later action reporting placed emphasis on promoting existing op shops and reuse initiatives.

### What Dubbo can copy
Before building a new facility, test whether an **ecosystem-coordination model** can extract more value from existing charities, repairers and community groups.

---

## Wagga Wagga — active 2026 reuse-shop repositioning

Wagga Wagga's reuse shop at the Gregadoo Waste Management Centre was rebranded **Relove It** in March 2026. Council frames it explicitly as a circular-economy reuse operation beside the waste facility.

### What Dubbo can copy
Public identity matters. A reuse centre can be presented as a desirable second-hand destination, not merely “the tip shop”.

---

## Seymour — council + social-enterprise operator

In March 2026 Mitchell Shire Council opened a Reviva Reuse Shop at Seymour in partnership with not-for-profit social enterprise Resource Recovery Australia.

### What Dubbo can copy
Council does not necessarily need to operate the shop itself. A formal **Council + specialist social-enterprise/community operator** model is credible.

---

# Recommended Dubbo hybrid

The strongest model for Dubbo is not to copy one comparator.

Combine:

1. **Bathurst/Armidale/Wagga:** pre-disposal reuse interception at Whylandra.
2. **Toowoomba:** repairable/parts categories, not resale-only intake.
3. **Bendigo/Ballarat:** community repair sessions and skills development.
4. **Albury:** tool access/shared equipment.
5. **Seymour:** external/social-enterprise operator rather than requiring Council to run everything.
6. **AssetFlow:** serialised chain-of-custody, data sanitisation evidence and outcome reporting for electronics.

## Pilot before capital build

Before a permanent Whylandra shop opens, the lowest-risk experiment is:

- formal off-site or controlled pre-disposal collection;
- one electronics/refurb stream;
- monthly repair/reuse event;
- referrals to existing local repairers/charities;
- AssetFlow logging;
- 3–6 months of measured outcomes.

Measure:
- items presented;
- repairable/reusable percentage;
- kg diverted;
- labour minutes;
- parts costs;
- resale/donation outcome;
- residents who would otherwise have disposed/replaced;
- referral destination;
- safety/data exceptions.

That creates the local evidence needed to justify or reshape a permanent model.
