# 1. The overall conclusion after all research

The project has changed substantially from the original idea of "an e-waste business".

The strongest evidence now supports this model:

> **Dubbo eWaste should be a small regional IT asset recovery / refurbishment service that captures useful technology before it enters an end-of-life recycling stream.**

The core workflow is:

**source equipment**
→ prove authority/ownership
→ assign asset/serial record
→ safety triage
→ secure data sanitisation
→ test and grade
→ reuse as-is where possible
→ repair/refurbish where economic
→ redeploy / resell / donate
→ harvest useful parts if whole-device reuse fails
→ send only the genuine end-of-life remainder to a verified recycling route.

That is materially different from:

> "open a place where people dump old electronics."

Dubbo and surrounding towns already have multiple ways for residents to dispose of or hand over unwanted electronics. The unresolved commercial opportunity is **reuse before destruction**.

# 2. Top 10 takeaways from the whole project

## 1. Reuse before recycling is the central opportunity

Council, Officeworks, AMR and regional/NTCRS routes already solve much of the **disposal** problem.

The gap is earlier:

**identify useful device → protect data → repair/refurbish → recover whole-device value → recycle last.**

Sircel, PonyUp for Good, The Reconnect Project, Compnow, WorkVentures and the National Device Bank all independently reinforce that hierarchy.

Relevant files:

- [SIRCEL-ITAD-REUSE-MODEL.md](SIRCEL-ITAD-REUSE-MODEL.md)
- [REUSE-MODELS-PONYUP-RECONNECT-LAPTOP-INITIATIVE.md](REUSE-MODELS-PONYUP-RECONNECT-LAPTOP-INITIATIVE.md)
- [TECHCOLLECT-REUSE-DEEP-DIVE.md](TECHCOLLECT-REUSE-DEEP-DIVE.md)

## 2. Triage is probably the highest-value operation in the whole business

A functioning business laptop can still have hundreds of dollars of use/resale value.

Once it enters a normal end-of-life recycling stream, that whole-device value can disappear and the machine becomes:

- metal;
- plastic;
- glass;
- PCB/precious-metal feedstock;
- battery stream;
- residual material.

TechCollect is especially explicit: equipment surrendered into its normal recycling stream is **not sold as second-hand equipment for reuse**. It tells owners to divert good equipment to family, friends or charity **before** surrendering it.

Dubbo eWaste's most valuable decision may therefore be:

> **"Is this actually end-of-life?"**

before anyone puts it in an e-waste cage.

## 3. Good corporate fleets are a better supply source than random household e-waste

Compnow, PonyUp and WorkVentures all show the operational advantages of fleet-refresh equipment.

Ten similar ex-business laptops are usually easier to:

- identify;
- serialise;
- wipe;
- test;
- repair;
- source chargers/parts for;
- image/configure;
- sell or donate;

than ten unrelated household devices in unknown condition.

That shifts the preferred upstream targets toward:

- businesses;
- schools;
- professional offices;
- MSP/IT providers;
- government/community organisations;
- organisations refreshing fleets.

Household intake can still exist, but it should remain selective.

## 4. Data security can be a major commercial differentiator

Business clients do not only want equipment removed.

They may need evidence of:

- chain of custody;
- serial numbers;
- secure sanitisation;
- verification;
- failed-erase escalation;
- final asset outcome.

Mature ITAD operators such as Sircel and WorkVentures use Blancco and issue auditable records/certificates.

Dubbo eWaste should begin with accurate wording:

> **documented media sanitisation / secure data erasure**

and only use stronger "certified destruction" language if a genuinely certified process/provider supports it.

The long-term high-value business service is:

**collection → asset inventory → secure erase → test/grade → value recovery → final-outcome report.**

## 5. Commercial resale and social reuse can support each other

Good360/Compnow and the National Device Bank show that social reuse does not require every valuable computer to be donated.

Higher-value stock can be:

- resold;
- bought back;
- remarketed;
- redeployed;

and that value can help pay for:

- wiping;
- repairs;
- chargers/batteries;
- logistics;
- lower-value social devices;
- connectivity/support.

A mature Dubbo model could therefore have:

- **commercial reuse stream**
- **social reuse stream**
- **parts stream**
- **recycling stream**

instead of choosing between "business" and "charity".

## 6. "We take e-waste" is not enough of a Dubbo value proposition

Dubbo already has:

- Council household e-waste services;
- Officeworks technology trade-in/recycling channels;
- Australian Metal Recycling;
- repair/refurbished-device retailers;
- second-hand electronics channels;
- regional NetWaste / NTCRS pathways.

So the proposition should not be:

> "Dubbo finally has somewhere to take an old computer."

A stronger proposition is:

> **"Before you recycle old IT, we determine what still has useful value, protect the data, reuse what is viable and document what happened to the remainder."**

## 7. The biggest startup barrier is planning/legal classification, not EPA tonnage

A 20–30 device pilot is nowhere near the major NSW EPA licensing thresholds for scheduled waste facilities.

But being below an EPA threshold **does not create planning permission**.

The unresolved questions are:

- is the activity a low-impact home business?
- a home industry?
- a goods repair and reuse premises?
- a waste/resource activity?
- does the proposed property/lease permit the actual activity?

The repo deliberately narrows Phase 0 to make a home-business classification more defensible:

- <30 m²;
- one resident operator;
- pre-approved intake;
- no unattended public bin;
- no outdoor stockpile;
- low traffic;
- no industrial shredding;
- online sales;
- no walk-in retail showroom.

Council still needs to answer the classification in writing.

## 8. The right launch is tiny, selective and reversible

The cheapest defensible pilot is not a warehouse.

It is:

- selective intake;
- Tuesday/Saturday by appointment/pre-approval;
- no general public dumping;
- only items with a known exit route;
- no loose or damaged lithium batteries;
- no CRTs initially;
- physical shelf limits;
- low stock;
- online resale;
- measured outcomes.

This closely matches the real early sequence behind Bendigo E-Waste:

**technical experience → own equipment → friends/family → controlled activity → formalisation → warehouse later.**

Copy the **growth sequence**, not the current mature operation.

## 9. The downstream chain remains surprisingly opaque

The project now knows a lot about what legitimate electronics recycling can look like.

Examples:

- Parkes Shire → Sircel is directly evidenced.
- historical regional NetWaste material described Sims St Marys shredding.
- TechCollect currently uses ACE Recycling Group and Sircel as NSW recyclers.
- Coonamble documented a 2025 route to Matthews Metals Dubbo.

But the key local question remains unresolved:

> **After AMR Dubbo accepts e-waste, what exact physical facility receives it next?**

No defensible public evidence yet proves:

- AMR Dubbo → Sircel;
- AMR Dubbo → ACE;
- AMR Dubbo → Sims;
- AMR Dubbo → Rutherford;
- AMR Dubbo → TechCollect.

That must remain marked unknown until a written answer, transport document, certificate or contract closes the chain.

## 10. The project has reached the point where pilot data matters more than generic research

Many web questions are now exhausted.

The next major business unknowns are empirical:

- how much good equipment is actually available locally?
- what percentage is reusable?
- how much repair time is required?
- what does each device sell for?
- how quickly does it sell?
- how many become liabilities?
- how much does downstream recycling cost?
- what is gross margin per labour hour?

The pilot tracker should determine whether the model is worth scaling.

# 3. Officeworks — exact role in the Dubbo e-waste ecosystem

This point caused understandable confusion in chat and should be recorded precisely.

## What Officeworks has to do with e-waste

Officeworks is **not being treated as an industrial e-waste processor in Dubbo**.

It is relevant because it provides an **existing consumer technology handover channel**.

Officeworks currently operates:

### Tech Trade-in

Eligible devices can be traded for value such as an Officeworks gift card.

Relevant categories include selected:

- laptops;
- phones;
- tablets;
- smart devices;
- consoles and other eligible technology.

Suitable equipment can therefore be diverted toward another owner/reuse route rather than simply disposed of.

### Bring it Back / technology recycling

Officeworks also accepts selected unwanted technology for recycling through its recycling program.

Nationally advertised categories include various:

- computers/laptops;
- phones;
- chargers/cables;
- storage devices;
- keyboards/mice;
- consoles/controllers;
- networking devices;
- other approved streams.

Officeworks stopped in-store battery collection in February 2024.

## What the Dubbo store represents

Officeworks Dubbo is at **10 Erskine Street**.

Its existence means a Dubbo resident already has a retail channel where at least some unwanted technology can be:

- traded in;
- or handed into a recycling program.

It does **not** mean:

- Officeworks Dubbo has an industrial dismantling line;
- the Dubbo store itself shreds e-waste;
- every category on the national program is necessarily accepted locally without checking;
- Officeworks is equivalent to AMR/Sircel/ACE as a processor.

### Correct wording for this repo

Use:

> **Officeworks is an existing retail trade-in / technology collection channel and disposal substitute for some Dubbo households. It is not being described as the local downstream recycler.**

That distinction is important.

# 4. Phase 0 operating model preserved from prior chats

The current Phase 0 design is intentionally narrow.

## Intake

- Tuesday and Saturday only.
- Pre-screen before arrival.
- No unattended dumping.
- No public "leave it at the gate" pile.
- Rejected items leave with the person who brought them.
- No walk-in retail showroom.
- Sales principally online.
- Keep total operating footprint <30 m² if relying on home-business pathway.

## Preferred equipment

Priority:

- business-class laptops;
- quality consumer laptops;
- desktops / mini PCs;
- Windows 11-capable equipment;
- Macs with Activation Lock removed;
- useful monitors;
- selected phones/tablets with locks removed;
- useful PC components;
- selected consoles/network equipment;
- selected flat-screen TVs only by prior approval.

## Refuse / avoid initially

- CRT TVs/monitors;
- loose lithium batteries;
- swollen/damaged/wet/fire-affected batteries/devices;
- e-bike/e-scooter batteries;
- random low-value bulk e-waste;
- cracked-panel TVs without a known profitable parts route;
- unresolved Apple/Google/MDM/Autopilot locks;
- suspicious provenance;
- contaminated/pest-infested electronics;
- broad whitegoods;
- anything with no identified exit route.

## Storage principle

Every category gets a physical capacity.

> **Shelf full = pause intake.**

No miscellaneous pile.

# 5. Planning, lease and property findings from all chats

## Planning

The strongest public planning evidence suggests the proposed site is likely in an **R2 Low Density Residential** setting, but the repo deliberately retains one authoritative property-specific confirmation step.

If R2 is confirmed:

- home business/home industry may be permissible subject to applicable controls;
- a principal **goods repair and reuse premises** or waste/resource facility is a different and potentially prohibited/problematic classification.

The exact classification depends on the **actual operation**, not the business name.

## Lease

Review of the current residential tenancy agreement found no blanket clause simply saying:

> "no business"

but there are important restrictions.

The lease review found:

- illegal use/nuisance/neighbour-impact restrictions;
- a garage/car-space clause restricting its use to vehicle parking rather than general storage;
- storage-area wording stating the landlord does not warrant fitness, ventilation, security or watertightness.

This means:

> **planning permission and lease permission are separate gates.**

## Shed condition

Prior tenancy evidence documented water/moisture/mould concerns.

That matters directly to the business because a damp shed is a poor place to store:

- valuable stock;
- unwiped client drives;
- PCs and PCBs;
- chargers/batteries;
- cardboard packaging.

Current repo rule:

> **Do not store valuable resale stock or unwiped customer/business equipment there until the workspace is demonstrably dry, secure and suitable.**

# 6. Second-hand dealer / ownership issue

Electronic goods are prescribed second-hand goods under NSW guidance.

A second-hand dealer licence can therefore become relevant.

However current Fair Trading guidance also says a licence is not required to:

> engage in recycling and rubbish collection programs.

The unresolved question is whether this exact activity:

**collect free unwanted electronics → securely wipe → refurbish viable equipment → resell it → recycle the remainder**

qualifies for that exemption.

This must not be guessed.

If a licence applies, issues include:

- supplier identity;
- ownership statement;
- police reporting;
- electronic records;
- 14-day holding period;
- rule that regulated goods must not be altered during the hold.

A key unanswered sub-question is whether:

- wiping;
- opening;
- RAM/SSD replacement;
- repair;
- parts harvesting;

counts as prohibited alteration during that period.

Current rule:

> **Get Fair Trading's answer in writing before the operating workflow depends on an exemption.**

# 7. Data sanitisation findings

The repo's data approach has matured from "wipe the disk" into a real media-sanitisation program.

## Standards

Current research now refers to:

- **NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2** (2025);
- current ASD/ACSC ISM media guidance;
- IEEE 2883 where relevant;
- platform-supported cryptographic erase on modern Apple hardware.

## Operational principle

For every data-bearing asset record:

- device serial;
- drive/media serial if accessible;
- media type/capacity;
- selected sanitisation method;
- tool/version;
- start/end;
- verification result;
- PASS / FAIL / DESTROY;
- final route.

If sanitisation cannot be reliably completed/verified:

> **do not sell the storage medium with unknown data.**

Use a verified destruction route or retain/remove the media.

## Wording

Use:

> **documented secure data erasure / documented media sanitisation**

until independent certification/accreditation supports stronger language.

# 8. Lithium, WHS and workshop safety

Phase 0 should not become a battery collection business.

Key preserved rules:

- reject loose lithium batteries;
- reject swollen, punctured, leaking, wet, fire-affected or overheating battery devices;
- never charge questionable batteries;
- no unattended/overnight charging;
- compatible chargers only;
- non-combustible charging/testing area;
- separate damaged-battery emergency path;
- do not assume a generic metal battery bin solves the hazard;
- do not post damaged/recalled lithium devices.

From 1 July 2026, NSW WHS Codes of Practice have elevated significance as minimum performance standards expected of a PCBU.

# 9. What happens to e-waste after disposal

## Council / regional

NetWaste currently says Sims Metal Management collects e-waste from the **majority** of NetWaste member councils, while some councils have individual arrangements.

Current/known routes include:

- Parkes → Sircel (direct Council evidence);
- historical regional e-waste → Sims St Marys shredding (historical contract evidence);
- Coonamble → Matthews Dubbo in 2025 (Council evidence);
- Narromine has used Sircel/Parkes in regional activity;
- Mudgee has a visible reuse-oriented Tip Shop route.

But exact current council-by-council routing remains incomplete.

## Industrial processing

A proper end-of-life processor may:

- weigh/track loads;
- remove batteries;
- triage for reuse;
- dismantle;
- shred mechanically;
- separate steel/aluminium/copper;
- separate PCBs/precious-metal fractions;
- separate plastics and glass;
- send fractions to specialist refiners/processors;
- landfill unavoidable residuals.

Real Australian NTCRS annual reporting shows that "recycled" does **not** mean 100% of the device becomes a new product. High recovery rates still include residual landfill/downstream losses.

# 11. TechCollect / ANZRP lesson

TechCollect is primarily the **recycling back end**, not the reuse model to copy.

Important finding:

> Once equipment enters the normal TechCollect recycling stream, TechCollect says it is recycled in Australia and not sold second-hand for reuse.

Good equipment should be diverted **before** formal surrender.

ANZRP separately supports upstream reuse via:

- PonyUp for Good;
- The Reconnect Project;
- The Laptop Initiative.

This creates the best conceptual model for Dubbo:

> **local reuse-first operator → send failures into an approved recycling back end.**

Not:

> **put everything into a recycling cage and hope someone rescues the good items later.**

# 12. Sircel lesson

Sircel provided the clearest industrial example of a reuse gate before destruction.

Its published model includes:

- secure collection/logistics;
- unique load tracking;
- triage;
- reuse identification;
- Blancco data sanitisation;
- hardware testing;
- cleaning/refurbishment;
- charity / broker / client redeployment;
- destructive mechanical recycling only for failures.

Sircel reported **2,350 reused assets** in FY2023-24.

Its strongest lesson for Dubbo is:

> **the valuable intellectual process is the decision system before the shredder.**

Dubbo does not need industrial shredding machinery.

It can specialise in:

- intake;
- chain of custody;
- data;
- testing;
- repair;
- value recovery;
- final reporting;

and outsource final material recycling.

Important risk:

Sircel entered receivership/voluntary administration in late 2025. Its model is still useful, but Dubbo eWaste should not become operationally dependent on one downstream company without current commercial confirmation.

# 16. Economics / resale findings

## eBay

A major repo correction was required.

The old rough assumption of ~13% eBay fees is **not appropriate for the intended tiny Australian pilot** under the currently published seller rules.

Eligible Australian sellers with:

- ≤AU$25,000 eBay sales in the prior 12 months;
- no applicable Pro arrangement;

can currently sell without the old blanket final-value-fee assumption.

Still track:

- listing upgrades;
- promoted listings;
- postage;
- packaging;
- returns;
- international fees;
- actual account-specific charges.

## Facebook Marketplace

Primarily a local sale/lead channel in Australia.

## Gumtree

Current official Gumtree material has had conflicting fee wording, so the repo correctly treats seller fees as **verify at use**, not a hard-coded rate.

## Pilot economics

The business should measure:

- sale price;
- parts cost;
- labour;
- collection time/km;
- postage/packaging;
- returns;
- recycling cost;
- recycling rebate;
- days in stock.

Important metric:

> **gross margin per labour hour**

rather than headline sale price.

# 17. Consumer law / resale

Business sales of refurbished equipment remain subject to Australian Consumer Law.

Do not use:

- "as-is";
- "no refunds";
- "no warranty";

as if they erase statutory rights.

Keep:

- serial/asset records;
- test records;
- accurate descriptions;
- known-fault disclosure;
- charger/accessory disclosure;
- buyer/contact details sufficient for product recall where required.

For customer-owned repair work, use the current repair notices before accepting data-bearing products.

# 22. Practical business architecture supported by the research

A mature Dubbo model could ultimately look like:

## Stream A — commercial reuse

Business/school/MSP retires devices
→ collect/inventory
→ ownership transfer
→ sanitise
→ test/grade
→ refurbish
→ resale or client redeployment
→ client outcome report.

## Stream B — social reuse

Useful device with lower commercial value
→ sanitise/test/configure
→ pass to trusted local charity/community partner
→ partner selects recipient
→ digital support/connectivity where available.

## Stream C — donor parts

Whole device fails economic repair
→ harvest useful components
→ repair other equipment
→ record remaining material.

## Stream D — end-of-life

No meaningful whole/parts value
→ verified recycler/product-stewardship route
→ retain weight/certificate/receipt where available.

This is the strongest combined lesson from:

- Sircel;
- PonyUp;
- Reconnect;
- Compnow;
- WorkVentures;
- Good360;
- TechCollect/ANZRP;
- Bendigo E-Waste.

# 23. Immediate sequence before public launch

The repo's current best sequence is:

1. **Council classification**
2. **lease / workspace suitability**
3. **Fair Trading answer**
4. **insurance**
5. **verified downstream terms**
6. **small selective pilot**
7. **measure 20–30 devices**
8. **only then widen intake or spend materially**

Do not lease a warehouse because the concept feels exciting.

Lease only when:

- supply is consistent;
- stock actually turns;
- labour return is worthwhile;
- storage is the genuine constraint;
- downstream costs are known;
- business pickups are recurring;
- revenue can support fixed overhead.

# 25. Single-sentence summary

After all the research and all chats, the strongest version of the idea is:

> **Get useful retired technology before the recycling system does; prove ownership, secure the data, preserve whole-device value, use resale to fund the operation and some social reuse, track what happened to every significant asset, and recycle only the genuine end-of-life remainder.**
