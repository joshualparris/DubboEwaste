# Opening a Dubbo ITAD branch/hub — ranked pathways, exact public costs and process

**Research date:** 2 October 2026 (AEST)

## Question

Of the Australian companies most comparable to Fliptech, which offers the easiest, cheapest and highest-payoff path to establishing a real Dubbo presence, and exactly what would the process and cost look like?

## Critical distinction

There are **three very different things** that can be called a "Dubbo branch":

1. **local sales / white-label front end** — no local custody or processing;
2. **secure collection / consolidation hub** — local custody, but central processing elsewhere;
3. **full processing branch** — local intake, sanitisation, testing, refurbishment, remarketing preparation and residual recycling dispatch.

Those have radically different costs.

Also:

> **You cannot independently open a branded branch of Fliptech, WV, Renew IT, Shred-X, Iron Mountain, G1 or Greenbox without that company's written agreement.**

For some companies the realistic path is a formal **partner or white-label service relationship**, not a franchise.

No shortlisted company publishes a franchise fee or a guaranteed Dubbo territory.

G1 also:

Sources:
- https://g1.com.au/
- https://g1.com.au/contact/
- https://g1.com.au/service/corporate-and-education-it-asset-buybacks/

### Why this is easiest

You can test the Dubbo market **without leasing a branch** and without first recreating G1's security/certification stack.

A potential first version is:

**Dubbo client**
→ local relationship / quote
→ G1 performs approved collection or backend service
→ G1 processes / sanitises / reports
→ local operator keeps the customer relationship under the agreed commercial structure.

### Exact public process

1. Have an ABN/business entity.
2. Contact G1 through its IT Service Providers page or quote form.
3. State that you want a **white-labelled Western NSW ITAD service**.
4. Ask G1 to define:
   - wholesale/service pricing;
   - local markup/reseller rules;
   - customer ownership;
   - who invoices the end client;
   - buyback settlement;
   - minimum regional volume;
   - whether you can do first-mile collection;
   - chain-of-custody requirements.
5. Start with **no local processing and no warehouse**.
6. Win 3–10 local business accounts.
7. Route jobs through G1 and record:
   - assets/month;
   - kilograms;
   - pickup cost;
   - G1 charge;
   - local revenue/margin;
   - resale/buyback value;
   - repeat work.
8. Only after recurring volume is proven, ask G1 to approve:
   - local secure staging;
   - serial capture;
   - local sanitisation;
   - eventually a dedicated Dubbo node.

### Cost

**G1's commercial price is quote-only. No public franchise/partner fee is published.**

The lowest public setup cost can therefore be extremely small:

- 1-year business name: **$47** from 1 July 2026.
- New proprietary company if desired: **$636** registration, plus **$342 annual review**.
- No local Blancco licence required if G1 performs sanitisation.
- No warehouse required initially.
- No staff required initially.

Sources:

- https://business.gov.au/news/changes-for-businesses-from-1-july-2026

Insurance and G1's wholesale/service pricing are quote-only and cannot be truthfully inserted as exact figures.

### Payoff

No public G1 reseller margin is published.

The attraction is **capital efficiency**:
- no processing facility;
- no certification build;

This is the best route to prove whether Dubbo customers will actually pay.

# 2. Greenbox — best formal partner program

Greenbox is the strongest company on the list for a **documented formal partnership pathway**.

## Exact public partner process

Greenbox currently says:

1. Contact its sales team / partner page.
2. Receive a **two-page Partnership Application**.
3. Complete a **12-month business plan** showing expected volume/business.
4. Supply **three relevant references**.
5. Supply **three months of credit information**.
6. Greenbox reviews the application.
7. It aims to notify applicants within **14 business days**.
8. Accepted partners complete mandatory online training and quizzes through Greenbox's LMS.
9. Partnership operates under:
   - a Partnership MOU;
   - the agreed business plan;
   - Greenbox's partner rulebook;
   - preferred pricing.
10. Greenbox says the partner program itself does **not** require a long-term contract; a partner can leave the program.

Sources:
- https://www.greenbox.com.au/faqs/
- https://www.greenbox.com.au/partners/

### Eligibility issue

Greenbox publicly says its program is for:
- distributors;
- resellers;
- OEMs.

A new stand-alone e-waste startup is therefore **not automatically eligible**.

The exact question is whether a Dubbo ITAD/refurb reseller would be accepted as a "reseller" for the program.

### Published partner fee

**No membership/application fee is publicly listed.**

Do not write "$0 partner fee" as a fact.

The correct statement is:
> **No public partner fee was found; commercial pricing appears inside the partner membership pack after approval.**

### Cheapest Greenbox path

**Partner / local reseller, no warehouse.**

Use Greenbox's national backend and preferred pricing first.

A physical Dubbo hub should come only after the 12-month business plan proves recurring volume.

### Known public launch cost

Same business setup floor:
- ABN: $0;
- business name: $47/year;
- company if newly incorporated: $636 + $342 annual review.

Additional costs:
- insurance: quote-only;
- Greenbox partner/service pricing: not public;

### Why this is #2

Advantages over Fliptech:
- actual partner program exists;
- application process is published;
- partner training exists;
- preferred pricing exists;

4. What can be done locally versus at your facility?

Speak to 20–30 potential business clients.

## Days 31–60 — first white-label/pilot collections

Use the first company willing to approve the model.

No branch lease.

Measure:
- collection count;
- assets;
- kilograms;
- service revenue;
- buyback value;
- parent-company charge;
- transport;
- hours.

## Days 61–90 — branch decision

Only move to a $125–$220/month storage hub if:
- multiple repeat clients exist;
- local staging materially reduces logistics;
- parent company approves it;
- insurance/planning/storage terms are clear.

Only move to a $20k+ industrial lease after:
- recurring monthly revenue;
- enough asset flow to justify local processing;
- parent company approves local sanitisation/refurbishment;
- rent is a small enough share of gross margin.

2. price per pallet/bin;
3. price per asset for sanitisation;

5. reseller/partner discount;
6. local operator commission/markup rights;
7. freight cost Dubbo → processing facility;
8. minimum free-pickup threshold;
9. share of remarketing proceeds;
10. rejected-asset/recycling charge;

12. warranty responsibility;
13. required insurance limits;
14. minimum monthly volume for a dedicated hub;
15. whether Dubbo gets geographic exclusivity.

With those 15 numbers, a genuine profit-and-loss model can be calculated.

Without them, exact profit is unknowable.

# Bottom line

If the goal is to make a Dubbo ITAD operation real with the **least money and least risk**, do **not** start by opening a branch.

Start:

> **G1 white-label or Greenbox partner → win local accounts → prove asset volume → secure aggregation → local processing → branded hub only when the economics justify it.**

A full industrial branch before customer evidence creates at least **$24,315.30 of identifiable annual cost** before the major expenses are even known.

A white-label market test can start with essentially only the business/insurance structure and no warehouse.

That difference is why the partnership-first path has the highest risk-adjusted payoff.
